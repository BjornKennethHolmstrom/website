# European Subsidiarity Protocol — Frequently Asked Questions

**Version:** 1.0 (Briefing Companion)
**Target:** European Policy Centre, Committee of the Regions, institutional stakeholders

---

## 1. The GSI and the Yellow Card

**Q: How does the Global Subsidiarity Index (GSI) relate to the existing Yellow Card mechanism?**

The Yellow Card allows national parliaments to object to a proposal if they believe it violates subsidiarity. Currently, objections are political arguments—persuasive but not empirical.

The GSI provides a **shared evidentiary baseline**. When a GSI analysis shows that a proposal would be optimally implemented at regional level, national parliaments can attach that data to their Yellow Card reasoning. The Commission, in turn, can respond with its own GSI analysis. The debate becomes structured around measurement rather than competing political claims.

The mechanism remains unchanged. The evidence improves.

## 2. Legal Basis and Treaty Change

**Q: Does this require a treaty change?**

**No.** Phase 1 requires no legal framework at all—it is a non-binding analytical exercise.

Phase 2 deployment uses **Enhanced Cooperation (Article 20 TEU)** , the same mechanism used for the European Public Prosecutor's Office (EPPO) and the Unitary Patent. A coalition of nine or more member states can adopt the protocol without unanimous approval.

The protocol operates within existing EU law. No treaty change is required.

## 3. Funding and Cost

**Q: Who funds Phase 1, and how much does it cost?**

**Estimated Phase 1 cost:** €20–35 million over 24 months.

**No new EU budget allocations required.** Phase 1 can be funded from existing envelopes:
- Horizon Europe (Pillar 2) — research and innovation
- Digital Europe Programme — digital infrastructure
- Structural Reform Support Programme — technical assistance

The pilot is designed as a **methodological consultation and feasibility assessment**, not a new programme. Full GSI co-development with Eurostat is a Phase 2–3 undertaking.

## 4. The Civic Value Registry and Privacy

**Q: Isn't a "civic registry" just surveillance by another name?**

**No.** The Civic Value Registry is designed with privacy as the first principle:

- **Voluntary participation** — no one is forced to register
- **Zero-knowledge proofs** — users can prove "I have Level 4 reliability" without revealing their transaction history, trading partners, or income
- **Local storage** — raw data never leaves the municipality
- **Aggregated only** — EU institutions receive anonymized statistical proofs, never individual records
- **GDPR compliant** — designed to exceed EU data protection standards

The registry makes visible the **informal care work** (eldercare, community support) that currently sits outside policy models. It does not create a surveillance system.

## 5. Conditional Service Credits vs. Digital Euro

**Q: How are Conditional Service Credits (CSC) different from the Digital Euro?**

| | Digital Euro (ECB) | Conditional Service Credits (Protocol) |
|--|-------------------|----------------------------------------|
| **Legal status** | Central bank digital currency | Not legal tender |
| **Function** | General-purpose digital cash | Targeted fiscal instrument (voucher-like) |
| **Scope** | Eurozone-wide | Pilot regions only |
| **Convertibility** | Fully convertible to Euro | Non-convertible |
| **Use cases** | Any legal transaction | Restricted: care work, community services |
| **Expiry** | None | Time-limited (forces circulation) |
| **ECB role** | Issuer | Technical observer (non-issuer) |

CSC is explicitly **not a currency**. It is a conditional claim on specific services, analogous to a sector-specific voucher. The ECB is invited as an observer to validate that the instrument operates outside monetary policy space.

## 6. Evidence and Falsifiability

**Q: What happens if the GSI is wrong?**

The protocol is designed to be **falsifiable**.

Phase 1 runs in **Parallel Diagnostic Mode**: GSI recommendations are compared against actual outcomes in pilot regions without changing policy. If the GSI consistently recommends routing that produces worse outcomes than existing processes, the hypothesis is falsified.

Negative results are published through the same academic channels as positive results. The data collected still constitutes the EU's first systematic empirical study of subsidiarity implementation—valuable regardless of outcome.

---

## 7. Subsidiarity Drag

**Q: What is "Subsidiarity Drag"?**

When a policy is implemented at a different governance tier than the GSI recommends, the protocol publishes the estimated cost—in administrative overhead, budget impact, and outcome terms—as **Subsidiarity Drag**.

**Example:** If GSI analysis shows that a care policy would be 35% more efficient if implemented at regional rather than EU level, that 35% inefficiency is the Drag.

This mirrors the European Semester's transparency on fiscal deviations: non-binding, but visible. No member state is required to follow GSI recommendations. But no member state can ignore them without explanation.

## 8. Relationship to Existing Tools

**Q: How does this relate to Better Regulation, REFIT, and impact assessments?**

| Tool | Function | GSI Relationship |
|------|----------|------------------|
| **Better Regulation** | Quality of legislative process | Adds empirical subsidiarity assessment to impact analysis |
| **REFIT** | Regulatory burden reduction | Identifies whether burden stems from governance-tier mismatch |
| **Impact assessments** | Costs and benefits of proposed policy | GSI asks a prior question: which level should make this policy? |

The GSI does not replace existing tools. It arms them with a new diagnostic layer.

## 9. Pilot Timeline and Scope

**Q: Where would Phase 1 happen?**

The current pilot design is anchored in **Västra Götaland, Sweden**—a region with:
- Deep municipal autonomy (kommunalt självstyre)
- Strong administrative capacity
- Zero secessionist tensions
- Existing interest in care sector innovation

**Phase 1 scope:** European Care Strategy domain (care policy, eldercare, informal care activation). Three to five pilot regions across different member states.

**Timeline:** 24 months for the Parallel Diagnostic Phase, with evaluation and scaling blueprint produced at conclusion.

## 10. What Member States and the Commission Gain

**Q: Why would national governments or the Commission support this?**

| Actor | Gains |
|-------|-------|
| **Member States** | Data to argue for flexibility where it's efficient; reduced budget pressure through activated informal care |
| **European Commission** | Measurable performance metrics; evidence of programme effectiveness; reduced litigation over allocation |
| **European Parliament** | Empirical basis for subsidiarity oversight; strengthened role in Better Regulation |
| **Committee of the Regions** | Operational tool to represent regional and local interests with data, not just political argument |

The protocol does not force centralization or decentralization. It makes the trade-offs visible and quantifiable.
