## **European Subsidiarity Protocol**

*A pilot-ready framework for reducing governance latency in the EU*

---

## The problem

The European Union is the most advanced polycentric governance system in the world. But it is currently facing a structural challenge:

> **Decisions are often made at the wrong level.**

As highlighted in the Draghi competitiveness analysis, managing 27 diverse economies through uniform regulation creates **governance latency**—a mismatch between:

* where knowledge exists
* and where decisions are made

This is not a failure of subsidiarity as a principle.

It is a failure of **measurement**.

Today, subsidiarity is politically interpreted—but not empirically diagnosed.

---

## The proposal

The **European Subsidiarity Protocol** adds a missing layer:

> A way to *measure*, *test*, and *improve* subsidiarity in practice.

It does not replace existing EU mechanisms.
It upgrades them from political tools to **evidence-based instruments**.

---

## Core mechanism

### 1. Global Subsidiarity Index (GSI)

A diagnostic system that identifies:

* where policy decisions should be made
* based on measurable characteristics of each domain

It evaluates governance across four dimensions:

* Decision proximity
* Knowledge inclusion
* Resilience capacity
* System cohesion

👉 Output: **Optimal Governance Tier (OGT)**
(e.g. EU level, national, regional, municipal)

---

### 2. Routing logic (OGT Matrix)

Instead of assuming centralization or decentralization:

> The system *routes* each policy domain to the level where it performs best.

Examples:

* High local knowledge → municipal/regional level
* High coordination need → EU level
* Mixed cases → hybrid structures

---

### 3. Subsidiarity Drag

When decisions are made at the wrong level, the protocol quantifies the cost:

> **Subsidiarity Drag = governance mismatch cost**

Measured in:

* administrative overhead
* fiscal inefficiency

This makes invisible inefficiencies **visible and comparable**.

---

## The pilot (Phase 1)

### Scope: European Care Strategy

The pilot focuses on care policy because:

* it is highly sensitive to local variation
* it carries major fiscal pressure
* it reveals the limits of uniform policy design

---

### Location: Sweden (proposed)

Initial pilot region:

* Västra Götaland (proposed reference case)

Selected for:

* strong institutional capacity
* high-quality data environment
* relevance to broader EU demographics

---

### What is tested

The pilot is explicitly empirical and falsifiable:

* Can subsidiarity be measured reliably?
* Do GSI-based routing decisions improve outcomes?
* Can local care capacity be activated more efficiently?

---

## Supporting infrastructure

### European Civic Value Registry

A voluntary, GDPR-compliant system that makes **informal care capacity visible**.

Examples of recognized contributions:

* eldercare
* community support
* local coordination
* social resilience work

Key properties:

* user-owned data
* locally governed
* privacy-preserving (zero-knowledge architecture)

---

### Function in the system

The registry provides data that traditional statistics cannot capture:

> The **real capacity of communities**

This enables:

* better policy routing
* reduced fiscal pressure
* recognition of currently invisible work

---

## Deployment model

The protocol is designed for **low-risk institutional testing**.

### Phase 1 — Parallel Diagnostic (Year 1)

* Non-binding
* Runs alongside existing EU processes
* Uses existing data (Eurostat + pilot inputs)
* Produces measurable results

---

### Phase 2 — Enhanced Cooperation (Years 2–3)

* Coalition of member states (Article 20 TEU)
* Expansion to additional domains
* Increased data precision

---

### Phase 3 — Gradual integration (Years 4–5)

* Integration into:

  * Better Regulation
  * REFIT
  * subsidiarity monitoring processes

---

## Institutional compatibility

The protocol operates fully within existing EU frameworks:

* **Article 5 TEU (Subsidiarity)**
* **Article 20 TEU (Enhanced Cooperation)**
* **Early Warning Mechanism (Protocol No. 2)**

No treaty change required.

---

Funded through existing instruments:

No new budget allocation required.

## Risk profile

The protocol is explicitly designed to minimize institutional risk:

* Non-binding pilot phase
* No monetary policy impact
* GDPR-compliant architecture
* Full transparency of methodology

## Falsifiability

This is not a theoretical proposal.

It is a **testable hypothesis**:

> If GSI-based routing does not improve outcomes, the model is falsified.

Even a negative result produces:

* the EU’s first empirical study of subsidiarity performance
