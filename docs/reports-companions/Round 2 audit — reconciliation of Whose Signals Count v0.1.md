## Does the episode design discriminate?

Partly. The signal–decision episode is the right unit for a high-volume service pathway and the wrong one for the country-level thesis. Of the accounts in your section E, four become separable at least in part, one only weakly, and one cannot be tested.

### What the unit can and cannot separate

| Account | Separable from the others? | What does the work, or why not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Information and operations | Yes | Receipt timestamps and a blind score of how complete and usable each signal was. |
| Authority and capacity | Yes, in part | Mandate and budget differ between units and change slowly. Use between-unit variation. Whether low capacity is itself a political choice lies upstream and outside episode data. |
| Attention | Yes, in part | Workload changes week to week inside one unit. Use within-unit variation in open cases per worker and vacancies. |
| Intervention efficacy | Yes, descriptively | Follow outcomes after the response and record whether it was delivered as decided. Causal efficacy is confounded by who gets which measure. |
| Selective influence | Weakly | Sources differ in report quality for legitimate reasons, so a raw source effect is an upper bound. Influential actors also create focusing events, so part of any "attention" effect may be influence. |
| Success-induced blind spots (Competence Trap) | No | Needs many units with independently measured prior performance and an independent date for when the anomaly existed. A few volunteer municipalities give neither, and high performers face a different problem mix. |
| Rights | Not a rival mechanism | Audit compliance separately, as you propose. |

### Residual observational equivalence

- **Attention and influence.** A response after media coverage fits both. Only load shocks unrelated to the signal's source separate them: a staffing gap, a simultaneous emergency, a system outage.
- **Influence and information quality.** A professional's report may be acted on faster because it is better, not because the sender matters more.
- **Capacity and priority.** "We lacked staff" and "we chose not to staff this" leave the same trace in a case file.
- **No response and wise restraint.** Not opening an investigation is sometimes the correct decision. Delay alone is not failure.

### Selection bias

- **Unrecorded signals.** An episode exists only if someone wrote the signal down. Warnings never sent because the sender expected no response are selected out. That is the form selective influence and the Competence Trap would most likely take. The 2024 audit found that reports of concern are not filed for all children suspected of crime.
- **Hindsight.** Coders who know the outcome will rate severity higher in cases that ended badly.
- **Volunteer sites.** Municipalities that agree to open their files are not typical.
- **Record survival.** Files are weeded and systems change, unevenly across units.

### Hard measurements

| Variable | Difficulty | Workable proxy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Severity at receipt | Judged by the same body whose response is studied | Blind double-coding of the original report text against a fixed rubric |
| Source influence | Circular if inferred from results | A score fixed before data extraction: source category only |
| Agenda load | Feasible | Open cases per caseworker and vacancy rate by week |
| Prior performance | No independent anomaly date | None at pilot scale |
| Omission harm | Requires a counterfactual | A new report on the same child within 12 months after a decision not to act |

### Minimum feasible evidence

One pathway in at least two municipalities; on the order of a thousand episodes, with the number set by a power calculation at registration; dated receipt and decision; source category; a blind-coded subsample for severity and report quality; a weekly workload series; twelve months of follow-up; and a staff survey on concerns not reported, to bound the first selection problem.

Policy-level cases such as NATO, housing or a single permit yield a handful of episodes each. They support qualitative process tracing. They cannot discriminate between the accounts, and the paper should say so.

## The attention hypothesis

Yes, Round 1 overstated it. I presented disproportionate attention as fitting the verified Swedish evidence. That evidence does not discriminate between attention, competence and selective influence.

### What Round 1 got wrong

- **The audit.** I read organisational friction as support for attention. Friction supports the operational account. The audit reports no time series of delay and catch-up, which is what the attention account predicts.
- **NATO.** One external shock followed by one policy change is predicted by all three accounts.
- **Generality.** That lag and lurch occur elsewhere is a claim from the comparative literature, which I did not re-verify. It raises the prior for attention. It is not Swedish evidence.

What survives is narrower: attention is the most general and least assumption-laden rival, so the burden is on the other two to show something it does not predict.

### Your point about agenda access

You are right that who gets onto the agenda is partly a question of influence. Attention and influence are then not rivals but layers: scarcity explains why something must wait, influence may explain what waits. A design must ask the second question conditional on the first.

### What would favour or disfavour it

| Finding | Reading |
| --- | --- |
| Within one unit, time to response rises with workload, for all sources alike | Favours attention uniquely |
| After a load shock unrelated to any source, delays rise evenly across sources | Favours attention uniquely |
| Response sizes cluster as many small adjustments and a few very large ones | Consistent with attention, but also with influence and with institutional friction. Not discriminating. |
| At equal workload and equal severity, delay differs by source category after report quality is controlled | Disfavours attention as a sufficient account; favours selective influence |
| Workload has no effect on delay | Disfavours attention |
| Responses track severity proportionately without any triggering event | Disfavours attention and the Drift Loop together |
| Delay is longer in units with better prior performance, at equal workload | Favours the Competence Trap over attention |

Only the first two rows are unique to attention, and both need within-unit workload data over time. Neither paper has such data.

### Statistical learning

Neither instrument is a route to it. Statistics secrecy has its own exception: data needed for research or statistics may be released where it is clear that no harm follows. That is the regime a learning use would have to pass through, together with ethics review where it applies and a data-protection basis for each flow. [SCB secrecy policy](https://www.scb.se/contentassets/99b579ae03d245c1a5700c12947746cf/sekretesspolicy_2025-11-04.pdf), read as an extract. The police guidance on 15 a § is silent on analysis, statistics and research.

- **"The 2025 reforms opened data for sensemaking."** They did not. The purposes are control purposes.
- **"The 2027 school change is only about transparency."** It also brings independent providers inside 15 a §. This is the reconciliation's M2 error.
- **"The 2027 change permits linkage of independent-school data."** It extends a case-level permission. Nothing more.
- **"15 a § covers routine bulk transfers."** The police guidance says interests must be weighed case by case, even for routine transfers.
- **"Schools may now share with social services under the April Act."** That Act runs only toward law enforcement.
- **"Health data can be linked through either gateway."** Both exclude health-care secrecy.
- **"A permission to disclose is a basis to process."** The receiving body still needs its own lawful basis. The privacy authority's 2025 opinion stresses that data-protection principles govern transfers between authorities.

## Was the lens critique fair?

Not entirely. Round 1 judged the lenses by whether they bring a method of their own. A lens can earn its place earlier, by changing what is measured, for whom, and against which baseline. The fair test is whether each one yields a hypothesis or a boundary that a conventional design would have left out.

### What each lens can add that is testable

| Lens | Hypothesis a conventional design would likely omit | How to test it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intersectionality | Disadvantage is not additive: the effect for a combination differs from the sum of its parts. | An interaction term that is specified before the data are seen. Needs national-scale data. |
| Intersectionality | Some needs cannot be recorded at all because the case-management categories have no place for them. | Compare free-text content of reports with the coded categories available to the caseworker. |
| Critical theory | Signals are withheld in advance because the sender expects no response. | A staff and resident survey on concerns not reported, and why. This applies equally to the parent's claim about frontline warnings. |
| Critical theory | Decisions about what to measure shape what can later be contested. | Trace specific measurement decisions and their stated reasons, as with the 2025 asset-data decision. |
| Decolonial analysis | Permit-by-permit assessment understates harm that accumulates across projects. | Compare the impacts assessed in individual permits with landscape-level change in the same herding area. The Truth Commission's call 35 asks for exactly this assessment. |
| Decolonial analysis | The affected unit is a collective rights-holder, not a set of residents. | Check whether evaluations sample individuals where the right belongs to a sameby. |

### What they add that is not testable

These are boundary questions. They cannot be settled by data, and the paper should declare its position on each instead of embedding it in a metric.

- **Constraint or endpoint.** Whether a right limits the set of acceptable outcomes or is one outcome to be traded against others.
- **Baseline.** Which state of affairs counts as the reference point for harm.
- **Standing.** Who is entitled to define what counts as harm to a livelihood or culture.

### What still stands from Round 1

- **v0.1 derives none of the above.** It names the lenses and then runs ordinary rival-hypothesis reasoning. The value was asserted, not shown.
- **Symmetry.** Withheld signals and unrecordable needs can affect any group. The lenses suggest where to look first; they do not license looking only there.
- **Critical realism and ecological economics** do no identifiable work in the paper. Either give each a hypothesis or drop the label.

A workable rule for v0.1.1: keep a lens only if it contributes at least one registered hypothesis or one declared boundary question, and say which.

## A pre-registrable pilot

One design is feasible: a registered cohort study of reports of concern about children to municipal social services. It tests three accounts and describes two more in one pathway. No feasible design tests the country-level thesis, and none tests the Competence Trap.

### Why this pathway

- **Volume and logging.** Each report has a recorded source, a receipt date and a first decision. Socialstyrelsen's handbook describes an immediate protection assessment and a preliminary assessment that ends in a decision to investigate or not. [Socialstyrelsen handbook, 2026](https://www.socialstyrelsen.se/contentassets/ec50f6207c1d4ad6a9cbc81c3c63edeb/2026-4-10193.pdf), read as an extract.
- **Deadlines.** Secondary sources give 24 hours for the protection assessment and 14 days for the decision to investigate. These refer to the previous Act. The section numbers and limits under the Social Services Act (2025:400), in force 1 July 2025, are unverified.
- **A recorded decision not to act.** This makes omission harm measurable.
- **Continuity.** It is the municipal end of the pathway the 2024 audit examined from the state side.

### Design

| Element | Specification |
| --- | --- |
| Type | Retrospective cohort, protocol registered publicly before any data are extracted |
| Sites | Two to four municipalities of different size; reasons for refusal by others published |
| Unit | One report of concern, nested in child and municipality |
| Window | 24 consecutive months, fixed at registration; the July and December 2025 law changes entered as covariates |
| Size | On the order of a thousand reports per site; final number from a power calculation in the protocol |
| Outcomes | Decision to investigate (yes or no); days to that decision; measure decided; new report on the same child within 12 months |
| Predictors | Source category; blind-rated severity; blind-rated completeness of the report; open cases per caseworker and vacancy rate in the week of receipt; child's age and earlier reports; area type |
| Coding | Two coders rate severity and completeness on a random subsample from the report text only, without seeing the decision; agreement threshold fixed in advance |
| Selection check | Survey of school and police staff in the same municipalities on concerns they did not report, and why |

### Registered hypotheses

| Account | Prediction | Status in this pilot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Information and operations | At equal severity, more complete reports are investigated more often and sooner | Tested |
| Attention | Within a municipality, higher workload in the week of receipt lengthens time to decision at equal severity, for all sources alike | Tested |
| Selective influence | At equal severity, completeness and workload, investigation rates differ by source category or area type | Tested as an upper bound; a residual difference is not proof of influence |
| Intervention efficacy | New reports within 12 months, by decision and measure | Described, not causally estimated |
| Authority and capacity | Reasons recorded in the file for not providing a measure | Described; too few sites to test |
| Competence Trap |  | Not testable |
| Rights | Share of decisions inside statutory limits; documented disclosures | Audited separately |

### Conditions and stop rules

- **Access.** Case files fall under social-services secrecy. Access needs each municipality's secrecy assessment and, for research on sensitive data about children, ethics approval. Whether an author without a research institution can be the applicant is unverified; plan for a university partner.
- **Lower-intrusion variant.** Municipal analysts run a fixed script and export only aggregates. This loses the blind coding, so it tests attention only.
- **Stop rules.** Declare the design infeasible, and publish that, if no municipality grants access, if coder agreement falls below the threshold, or if workload data do not exist at weekly resolution.

### What it cannot show

It says nothing about Sweden as a whole, about policy-level cases, or about the Sámi case. For the last, the feasible study is a document audit, not a causal one: sample decisions in the reindeer-herding area by decision type, and record whether the Act applied, whether consultation was requested and held, and whether the outcome changed. The records are public. The audit classifies; it does not rule on legality.
