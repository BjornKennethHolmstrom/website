## Executive Summary

**Research draft — dependency correction v0.2 (25 November 2026).** The original report is dated May 2026. This revision is under AI correction review; it is not peer reviewed. Company-specific descriptions below are historical research leads, not established findings. Primary-source verification and human domain review with disclosed conflicts are binding before release of company-specific findings. No community participation or legal authorization is claimed.

**The Paradox**

Frontier AI organisations can be understood as governance systems as well as technology companies: they make choices about risk, deployment, access and the allocation of resources. The original report asked whether two important ambitions — sustained alignment coherence and competitive viability — can be maintained together as capabilities change. It named the possible incompatibility the **Coherence–Velocity Trap**. The name is retained as a hypothesis, not a theorem or inevitable future. Technical AI-assisted research, autonomous recursive improvement and institutional learning are different processes; their rates and effects must be established separately.

**The Variety Gap in AI Organisations**

The earlier count-based interpretation of the Variety Gap is withdrawn. An objective is not an observation channel, and neither the number of objectives nor the rank of a channel establishes company-level blindness. A task may fail because evidence is absent, because a signal is misinterpreted, because incentives discourage response, because authority is absent, or because an authorized action cannot be implemented. Accurate evidence can be ignored. The report's five organisational archetypes are dated interpretive leads, with possible strengths and failure mechanisms rather than rankings. The following are the original's historical characterizations, not findings about current arrangements:

- **OpenAI:** a nonprofit/commercial hybrid, raising questions about formal board rights and effective executive authority.
- **Anthropic:** an alignment-first architecture, raising questions about the durability of restraint under competitive pressure.
- **Google DeepMind:** research within a large commercial organisation, raising questions about coordination across research and operational units.
- **xAI:** founder-centred decision-making, raising questions about rapid action, independent feedback and institutional memory.
- **DeepSeek:** a hypothesized state-coupled architecture, raising questions about policy constraints and independent scrutiny. This label is not a verified finding of state control.

All five are assessed under the same evidence and release requirements (§2.10). Appendix C states the conditional formal results.

**The Alignment–Deployment Oscillation Loop**

Competitive pressure may accelerate deployment; new concerns may prompt intervention, crisis, accommodation and recurrence. Each transition is a hypothesis. Successful quiet restraint may be much less visible than public disputes. Capital arrangements may favour particular time horizons; founder-centred decision-making may concentrate attention and authority; organisational fragmentation may impede handoffs; and public commitments, published safety research and advisory bodies may accumulate without a corresponding change in operational practice. The latter is the safety-washing hypothesis, not a presumption about sincerity. Cultural expectations may reinforce or counteract these pathways. Neither coexistence nor a particular public crisis establishes the loop. A separate slow hypothesis asks whether viable alternative governance arrangements are depleted or replenished. The **Recursive Governance Deficit** is a further hypothesis concerning whether governance adaptation keeps pace with specified capability changes, with organisational learning as a rival explanation. H0–H5 in §1.10 define tests independent of adoption of the proposed reforms.

**What Building Adaptive Coherence Would Look Like**

At the organisational level the report proposes independent safety boards with defined decision rights, oversight, protected dissent and audit. Across organisations it proposes shared evaluation and evidence infrastructure with independent interpretation, alternatives and failover. At the societal level it proposes advisory deliberation and expert assessment, with a response requirement only where legitimately adopted. Internationally it proposes coordination at different timescales, subject to jurisdiction, authorization and comparison with existing institutions. These are options, not necessary or sufficient conditions for alignment. Open-weight release presents distinct benefits and risks; it requires release-specific analysis rather than a universal rule.

The original seven participation incentives remain proposals to compare: liability arrangements, compute access advantages, regulatory fast lanes, procurement preferences, insurance advantages, reputational certification and treaty-linked benefits. They are not entitlements or a guarantee of competitiveness. Certification is a signal, not safety. Neither membership nor rapid adoption warrants reduced safety obligations or penalties for lawful nonmembership.

**A Concrete First Step: The AI Commons Governance Protocol**

The Protocol is a candidate intervention for particular observation and coordination gaps. It would share authorized evidence, evaluation resources and interoperable protocols while retaining independent challenge. Appendix D.6 expresses its guiding maxim: **consolidate the substrate, diversify the interpretation**. Evaluation infrastructure itself can embed a common threat model; the shared substrate therefore also needs challenge, alternatives and failover. Its observational component does not by itself repair an authority or implementation deficit. Participating institutions would need separately authorized escalation, decision and response pathways. The proposal openly contemplates pre-deployment evaluation and cannot confer statutory powers on a private body. It must be compared with adequate existing arrangements, targeted coordination and no change.

**The Honest Conclusion**

The reported 2023 OpenAI board episode and the reported April 2026 Anthropic Mythos access decision are research leads subject to the same evidentiary rules, not respectively proof of failure and proof of success. The latter may challenge a unilateral impossibility claim if durable adequate restraint and viability are established; it does not establish the need for a Commons. Later developments are separately queued for verification. It is possible that safety and viability are complementary, that only coordinated restraint is feasible under specified conditions, or that neither arrangement suffices. The challenge is to discover which conditions hold, and to develop authorized ways of responding where evidence warrants change.
