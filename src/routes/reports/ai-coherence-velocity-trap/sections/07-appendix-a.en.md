## Appendix A: Value Systems and Policy Mindsets — A Guide for the AI Governance Context

### A Note on This Appendix

The original draws on Spiral Dynamics as an interpretive vocabulary. The following portraits are historical, unaudited interpretive hypotheses, not validated developmental stages, diagnostic categories or evidence about any company's employees. They describe possible preferences and trade-offs, not a ranking of persons or institutions. Evaluate their explanatory value against more direct evidence about incentives, authority and practice.

### A.1 The Basic Insight

Institutions may emphasise different values and understand risks differently. These emphases can be useful in some contexts and limiting in others. The same person or institution may combine several, or not fit any of the categories. The vocabulary is one way of generating questions; it does not imply an inevitable progression or a preferred culture's superior governance capacity.

### A.2 The Value Systems in the AI Governance Arena

**Achievement and Efficiency (sometimes called "Orange") — the Deployment Imperative.** The original associates this lens with innovation, measurable performance, efficiency, entrepreneurship and competition. Potential strengths include rapid problem-solving and resource mobilisation. A potential limitation is that easily measured milestones may dominate less tractable or longer-horizon concerns. The original also asks whether safety is treated as a constraint to minimise rather than a design parameter to integrate, and whether attention narrows to the signals amplified by funding arrangements. Those are testable incentive and decision hypotheses, not inevitable properties of an achievement orientation. These are questions about specific incentives and decisions, not a diagnosis of everyone engaged in deployment.

**Inclusion and Care (sometimes called "Green") — the Safety and Ethics Movement.** The original associates this lens with concern for affected people, inclusion, ethical reflection and attention to harms. Potential strengths are independent perspectives and scrutiny of externalities. Potential limitations include difficulties translating diverse concerns into timely authorised action or resolving disagreement. The original specifically raises proposals for constraints that do not account for competitive dynamics, capture by the organisations the safety community seeks to influence, and purity dynamics that may suppress diversity or dissent within that community. These are possible mechanisms to investigate, not attributes of all advocates or researchers; effective challenge, plural methods and workable responses under competition would count against them. They need evidence rather than stereotyping. Safety research and ethical commitments can have technical as well as deliberative dimensions.

**Integrative and Systemic (sometimes called "Yellow") — the Adaptive Governance Perspective.** The original advocates a preference for functional fit, systemic awareness, plural methods and revisability. These are design values, not evidence of an empirically superior developmental stage. The original identifies comfort with uncertainty and a focus on designing institutional mechanisms as possible strengths, and the risk of being perceived as detached, overly analytical or politically unrealistic as a blind spot. Such perceptions and actual design limitations must be distinguished. A system-oriented proposal may itself become overconfident, impose a common framing or ignore rights and local knowledge. The Commons must be subject to independent challenge rather than being justified by the label.

### A.3 The Coherence–Velocity Trap as a Value-System Clash

The original interprets deployment and safety as a clash between achievement and care, and proposes an integrative translation layer. This is an attributed interpretation to compare with alternatives, including cases where safety and achievement are complementary and where conflicts reflect legal authority or resource constraints rather than culture. The candidate design links distinct perspectives without requiring one unified value system. H0 and H1 test adequacy and authority, not developmental typology. No endorsement of these portraits by any organisation or community is claimed.
