## Appendix E: About this report and how it was made

### How it was made

This report was generated by AI language models (Claude, ChatGPT, DeepSeek) from prompts by Björn Kenneth Holmström. He chose the topic, posed the questions, set the framework the models were asked to apply, and decided to publish the result. He wrote little of the text himself.

The report has not been systematically fact-checked. Descriptions of specific organisations, events, figures and sources may be inaccurate or invented, and should be verified against primary sources before being relied on or cited. Its arguments are best read as a structured hypothesis generated with a particular framework, not as findings from independent research.

### Interests

Björn Kenneth Holmström is not employed by, and has no financial interest in, any of the organisations discussed. Readers should note, however, that the report was largely written by AI models built by frontier AI companies — the kind of organisations it analyses. That is a structural limitation the report cannot correct from the inside.

### The organisational reports series

This report opened the Organizational Reports Series, which applies the Variety Gap Framework used in the Country Reports for Systemic Change series to organisations rather than nation-states: AI developers, universities, healthcare systems, central banks, courts and standard-setting bodies. The country reports covered Germany, France, Sweden, India, the European Union, the United Kingdom, Brazil, Russia, the United States, Finland, China, Japan, Nigeria, Israel and Spain.

Both series were produced in the same way as this report. They argue that a shared set of concepts — observation channels, latency, signal fidelity, requisite variety and institutional "immune systems" — helps explain governance failure across very different institutions. Whether that holds up is an open question that would need testing by people with domain expertise and real data.
