## 7. Coda: The Keeper of the Whole at the Frontier of Speed

---

### 7.1 The Wealth That Matters

The original report’s account (unverified):

> Bhutan is rich in something most states never attempt to build: a shared, explicit and carefully measured account of what a country is for. It did not arrive at that account by accident. It chose, deliberately and against the grain of its century, to define national progress as the securing of conditions for a good life across many dimensions at once—health and education, but also time, community, culture, governance, the natural world and the inner life of its people. It wrote that choice into its constitution. It built instruments to measure it, and it kept those instruments honest by designing them around sufficiency rather than accumulation.

The original’s normative aspiration and reported history are retained; no comparison of national sincerity, clarity or achievement is established.

The original contrasts multidimensional GNH with the compression diagnosed elsewhere in the historical series. The accepted Korea report instead treats the Tournament Trap as a domain-level hypothesis, not an established national objective collapse. A declared objective is not observation or legitimate action; W1/W4 apply to GNH itself.

The aspiration is to keep legitimate objectives connected to new capabilities while respecting voluntary mobility, not to presume that institutional growth or retention is inherently required.

### 7.2 The Shift

The original report’s account (unverified):

> For half a century, Gross National Happiness was carried by guardianship. Successive monarchs articulated it. A small public service shared it. A culture shaped by Buddhist ideas of balance gave it meaning. The institutions that could change the country were few, closely connected and slow enough for personal judgement and shared purpose to hold them to the whole.

This is the original guardianship interpretation, not a national-character finding, inferred unanimity or evidence of effective review. Assess institutional history and actual decision rights, including plural perspectives and independent criticism.

Education, perceived alternatives and domestic conditions may influence migration and public strategy, as H2 proposes. Earlier mining investment, civil-service reform, reopening borders and independent development objectives remain rivals; leaving and returning are legitimate choices.

The original proposes a shift from guardianship to architecture. This is a conditional institutional design, not proof that effective informal arrangements are inadequate, that GNH is sincerely held by everyone, or that new arrangements keep pace. The prospective question remains open.

---

### 7.3 The Wider Significance

Bhutan motivates a question that may arise elsewhere: as institutions acquire new capabilities, do task-specific responsibilities and interfaces remain adequate? Transfer requires comparative evidence and contextual judgment, not a universal claim.

The original advances the stronger general argument that every state is now building institutions that act faster and at larger scale than their accountability arrangements were designed for: sovereign funds and state investors, special economic zones with their own laws, central banks with expanded balance sheets, and “automated systems that decide at machine speed”. This is an attributed research hypothesis, not an established universal trend. Task-specific mandates, protective constraints and timely effective existing interfaces may defeat it in particular settings.

The original connects this pattern to the series’ frontier-AI report, where competitive velocity may pull against the coherence needed to steer development. Under the accepted R4 correction, strategic feasibility and effective response are conditional; neither that report nor this analogy supplies empirical evidence for Bhutan. Central bank responsibilities may also be legitimately distributed (R5).

The explicitly described GNH objective offers a distinctive lead for investigating objective breadth, measurement, legitimacy and action separately. No national comparison of clarity or observational capacity follows.

The original sees a successful Bhutanese interface architecture as potentially producing a model “that states with far vaguer objectives will need”; failure, in its account, would be “sobering for everyone”. These are attributed conditional scenarios, not a uniquely clean test, national superiority or a binary verdict. Successful task-specific arrangements might offer transferable lessons subject to independent comparison; adverse or mixed results might expose particular limits, including limits of the objective itself. Neither outcome establishes universal feasibility or impossibility.

The historical mirror comparison with Korea motivates two different hypotheses. The accepted Korea correction examines the configuration of university hierarchy, labour market and capital-region opportunities without asserting cultural predisposition or general state strength. Bhutan motivates an interface hypothesis. Neither is a measured national ranking, a demonstrated objective collapse or proof of superiority.

---

### 7.4 The Honest Conclusion

This report has proposed a diagnosis, and it must be honest about what the evidence establishes and what it leaves open.

The original reports a constitutional commitment, multidimensional measures, migration, pay revision, reserve deployment and later institutional changes. These are unverified leads. Neither the stated purpose nor the public chronology establishes actual motives, review timing, causal effects or fiscal sustainability.

The reported DHI ESG launch and Gelephu legal, integrity and audit arrangements motivate a test of institutional maturation. Actual scope, competent legal interpretation, independence, decisions and outcomes are open. No evidence of pace or lag is inferred merely from dates or absent public records.

Open questions concern DHI’s pre-investment decision procedures, actual Gelephu constraints, protected evidence and the scope of screening, fiscal and energy exposure, plural legitimacy and the population boundary. Appropriate approvals and effective confidential review may weaken concerns; no costly veto is required. Voluntary migration and justified nonadoption remain legitimate.

This report proposes an investigable race, preserves the original predictions and offers a candidate Register. It does not diagnose national failure, validate the hypotheses or authorize action. Default unknown; independent empirical, legal and human review is required.

### 7.5 A Final Word

In a hydropower-rich valley, machines run through the night, turning water into computation and computation into revenue. In Thimphu, a teacher opens a pay slip that is larger than it was a few years ago, and wonders whether it is large enough. At Paro, a young engineer boards a flight to Australia. At Gelephu, surveyors mark out a city that does not yet exist, in a forest that has always been there.

These are illustrative scenes from the original’s closing narrative, not documented individual cases or verified observations.

The scenes express the original’s concern about connecting economic capabilities to legitimate commitments and opportunities. No claim of uniquely thoughtful national purpose or inevitable machinery is established; people may freely choose where to live.

The original report’s account (unverified):

> Bhutan's first great achievement was to decide what a country should value. Its next may be harder, and more widely instructive: to show that a society can build fast, powerful institutions without letting them forget what they are for. If it succeeds, the world will have learned that a rich objective can survive the building of a modern economy. If it does not, the objective will not disappear from the constitution or the speeches. It will simply stop steering.

The original refrain, including its “It will simply stop steering” ending, is preserved as attributed aspiration and warning, not a causal prediction or claim about national superiority.

The original report’s account (unverified):

> The keeper of the whole now stands at the frontier of speed. What it does next will matter not only to the teacher deciding whether to stay, but to every society learning, as Bhutan is, that knowing what it values is only the beginning of making its institutions serve it.

This is the original concluding metaphor. The outcomes remain unknown.
