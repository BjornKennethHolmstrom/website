## Appendix A: Value Systems and Policy Mindsets — A Guide for the Bhutanese Context

### A Note on This Appendix

The main body of this report speaks the language of governance architecture: objectives, actuators, interfaces and latency. This appendix offers a complementary lens for readers who wish to understand the value-system dynamics beneath the Interface Race. It draws on Spiral Dynamics integral theory, simplified. It is optional, but it makes the report's underlying logic transparent.

This is an optional attributed heuristic from the original, not validated cultural classification or a scale of human development. None of the portraits identifies an individual or population, proves motives or causal mechanisms, or privileges the integrative portrait. All are open to plural interpretation and empirical challenge.

### A.1 The Basic Insight

Institutions and political cultures operate from different centres of gravity in how they understand governance, resources and change. These are not personality types or party affiliations. They are underlying value systems—ways of constructing what feels real, legitimate and important—each a coherent response to particular life conditions, each with characteristic strengths and blind spots. What follows is a simplified map of the systems most relevant to Bhutan.

### A.2 The Value Systems in the Bhutanese Arena

**Kinship and the Sacred Landscape (sometimes called “Purple”).** The original associates village community, household ritual and sacred mountains, lakes and forests with attachment to place, reciprocity and possible ecological restraint. It also proposes that belonging may become exclusionary. Verify actual practices, forest outcomes and diverse accounts; reverence is neither a causal explanation by itself nor a national trait.

**Order and Sacred Duty (sometimes called “Blue”).** The original associates monastery, monarchy, *driglam namzha* and constitutional order with continuity, discipline and shared identity. It hypothesises possible risks of uniformity and deference discouraging scrutiny, including in the history of integration policies. Neither cohesion nor deference is inferred from a cultural label. Independent evidence, protected disagreement and minority perspectives matter.

**Achievement and Strategy (sometimes called “Orange”).** The 10X Journey, Gelephu, mining, the reported civil-service aspiration of an “enlightened entrepreneurial bureaucracy” and the High-Income GNH Economy illustrate the original’s strategy lens: ambition, speed, measurable growth and possible opportunity creation. Potential blind spots are narrow financial proxies and the perception of review as delay. These are conditional mechanisms, not motives or a claim that migration must be stopped.

**Wellbeing and Care (sometimes called “Green”).** GNH’s declared psychological, time-use, community and ecological concerns motivate a lens on development serving wider goals. Its possible strength is keeping multiple concerns salient; its potential limitation is difficulty translating abstract commitments into commercial decisions. Actual implementation and plural objectives are empirical and normative questions.

**Integrative and Systemic (sometimes called “Yellow”).** The original proposes examining how growth and wellbeing might coexist through institutional design, including the explicit GNH weights and emerging interface proposals. This is a possible orientation, not a privileged evaluator, more advanced culture or proof of adequate integration. Legitimate conflict and separate responsibilities may be preferable to synthesis.

### A.3 The Interface Race as a Value-System Question

The original interprets GNH as a historical synthesis of Blue order, Green wellbeing and Purple attachment to landscape and community. It describes economic transformation as an expansion of Orange and the proposed interface architecture as a Yellow integration. The analogy is a configuration hypothesis, not a necessary developmental sequence. GNH materiality uses commercial instruments to consider selected wellbeing concerns; it must disclose omissions and test its own validity. Codified coupling is a possible means of institutional continuity, not inherently superior to effective informal coordination. Voluntary mobility remains legitimate.

The original also interprets the boundary question through the interplay of belonging, authority and nation-building. Such labels cannot establish historical causes, settle citizenship or establish the correct cultural interpretation. Minority and refugee voices, rights and legitimate independent challenge must shape any inquiry. No value-system portrait ranks people or grants authority over them.
