## Appendix G: The Gelephu Mindfulness City — Legal and Accountability Architecture

### G.1 Scale and Autonomy

The original reports that the Gelephu Mindfulness City was established by Royal Charter as a Special Administrative Region of approximately 4,046 square kilometres, about a tenth of Bhutan. It describes executive and legislative authority of its own, an independent judiciary and extensive investment, financial, regulatory and commercial powers. It identifies the GMC Authority as the highest decision-making body and the King as its chair and appointing authority. These particulars are unverified.

Verify the Royal Charter, territorial extent, scope of powers, appointment arrangements, judicial independence and actual jurisdiction with competent Bhutanese and Gelephu legal review. The description does not establish autonomy or adequacy in practice.

### G.2 Purposes

The original describes the Charter as incorporating wellbeing, happiness, sustainability and Gross National Happiness into the city’s purposes. It reports a City Development Department mandate to integrate sustainability, resilience, cultural values and community wellbeing into infrastructure decisions, and a preliminary alignment assessment in business registration. These are unverified accounts of purposes and procedures.

Verify the Charter’s purposes, departmental mandate and registration procedures; distinguish stated objectives from actual measurement, review, authority and outcomes. A preliminary assessment need not constitute a binding constraint.

### G.3 Legal Architecture

The original reports that Gelephu began enacting its own legal system in December 2024, adopting commercial laws from established international financial centres. The original reports that the Application of Laws Act provides for Kingdom of Bhutan law to continue to apply where Gelephu law does not otherwise govern. It reports no separate environmental statute in the legislation published at the original’s time of writing. On its reading of the published framework, the national Environmental Assessment Act, described as kingdom-wide and requiring pre-commitment assessment, would continue to apply in Gelephu unless displaced or adapted by its law. This is the original’s conditional analysis, not verified law. It also reports national transport assessments already modelling Gelephu’s cumulative impacts on forests, water and wildlife corridors. All particulars require verification.

The default-law conclusion is an unverified interpretation, not legal advice. Competent legal review must determine applicable, displaced and adapted law, territorial scope, timing, enforceability and rights. Check actual transport assessments and pre-commitment practice rather than inferring enforcement from a statutory text.

### G.4 Accountability Architecture

The original reports a constitutional power for the Royal Audit Authority to audit all public authorities and bodies administering public funds, and describes preparations for statutory audit arrangements for Gelephu in 2026. It reports that Gelephu’s own Audit and Corruption Investigation Bureau signed a cooperation agreement in June 2026 with the national Anti-Corruption Commission while retaining an independent mandate. The original interprets this as nested rather than isolated accountability, with national and internal institutions connected. These are unverified institutional and legal claims.

Verify the constitutional audit power, statutory arrangements, dates, the Bureau’s mandate and the cooperation agreement. Nested architecture on paper is not evidence of independence, effective review or correction. Assess appointments, resources, jurisdiction, protected access and actual follow-up.

### G.5 The Open Questions

The original’s three open questions are retained: will multidimensional purposes be measured and published alongside economic results; will authorized constraints bind in actual decisions, including where costly; and will alignment become visible through law, standards and procedure as well as shared leadership? P2/P5 preserve these prospective questions. No costly veto, public disclosure of protected material or formalization is required to demonstrate adequacy; appropriate approvals, existing effective procedures and legitimate plural voices matter. No conclusion about leadership or national consensus.
