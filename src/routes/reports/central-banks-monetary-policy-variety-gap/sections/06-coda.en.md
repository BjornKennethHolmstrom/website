## 6. Coda: The Engineers at the Table

### 6.1 The Wealth That Matters

The original begins its coda by recognising a considerable institutional inheritance: economic expertise, decades of institutional memory, analytical infrastructure, an aspiration to evidence-based judgment, international coordination through the Bank for International Settlements and other forums, and operational independence. The claims that these resources exceed those in other domains or have caused a generation of price stability are comparative and causal leads, not findings. In particular, the reviewer-supplied 2021–23 episode must be considered under the same standard as the earlier favourable account.

These assets matter in crises. The original cites the Federal Reserve's lending in 2008, the ECB's 2012 “whatever it takes” commitment and the responses to the COVID-19 pandemic as illustrations of potentially valuable speed, scale and expertise. The institutional details, legal authority, fiscal consequences, alternatives and causal effects need verification. The same euro-area and US interventions motivate the constitutional-drift critique in §2.9. An intervention can have a stabilising effect and still raise questions about mandate, distribution or public risk. Conversely, a large intervention is not in itself an illegitimate exercise of power. Evaluate both sides on the same evidence and preserve demonstrated crisis capacity rather than assume that reform must replace it.

The wealth that matters for a future monetary framework includes the capacity to recognise important limitations and respond to them, with appropriate responsibilities across institutions. Technical competence, legitimate authority, coordination and independent challenge are complementary assets. The original's appeal for greater monetary observability is best understood as an inquiry into sufficient evidence for specified tasks, not a demand to make the entire economy legible to a single institution. Dispersed knowledge, privacy and public authorization matter too.

The original contrasts the ability of an operational institution to respond swiftly with the deliberative time and different responsibilities of elected bodies. That comparison deserves care. The Fed and Congress, or the ECB and European Council, do not have identical tasks or legal powers. A faster response is not in itself superior to a slower decision about public resources; coordination may be indispensable. Preserve the ability of authorised institutions to act at the time a task requires, while assessing who bears the risks and who can subsequently contest the decision. This is a more useful interpretation of institutional wealth than a ranking of entire institutions by their speed or sophistication.

### 6.2 The Shift

The original proposes a shift in the relationship between institutions and their own knowledge: from the Pretence of Knowledge to model humility; from exclusive reliance on narrow representations to the consideration of relevant financial, distributional and climate risks; from uniform responses to differentiated instruments where they are justified; and from independence conceived as insulation to independence compatible with democratic scrutiny. These are proposed directions, not evidence that current institutions are universally closed or that they possess historically unprecedented data yet historically minimal capacity. Actual model portfolios, decision pathways and outcomes must be established.

Such a shift need not require a central bank to become the government's economic policy arm. Where the problem is an information gap, improved evidence may suffice; where the problem is authority or an instrument constraint, a different owner or a lawfully revised mandate may be needed. If price stability can be pursued alongside adequate distributed responses, a narrow monetary mandate may be the appropriate architecture. If additional public powers are proposed, their legitimacy, efficacy and risks must be examined rather than inferred from the existence of a concern. Model humility constrains those who propose reforms as much as those who defend existing practices.

The original contrasts confidence in a baseline forecast with the explicit admission that its structural assumptions might fail. A reform may therefore consist of revising a model, adding an independent scenario, clarifying the division of authority, or improving the procedure for challenge. It need not consist of a new macroeconomic control programme. The distinct aims are better evidence, appropriate instruments, and accountability for the actual use of authority. Their success criteria should be as explicit as the policy objective they are intended to support. A new analytic platform that supplies apparently precise numbers without reliable identification could be a step in the wrong direction.

### 6.3 The Global Significance

Central banking is a useful setting for testing a governance-as-engineering approach because policy rules, models, institutional mandates and responses are often explicit. This does not establish that central banks are the limiting case, that no other domain is as consequential or that a failure here demonstrates a universal architectural law. A Taylor rule is a stylised model, not the whole decision apparatus. The original's suggestion that inability to build the proposed architecture confirms the framework while ability to build it provides a template is withdrawn. Either an adequate current arrangement or a contrary causal episode may disconfirm the relevant diagnosis under H0–H5. Adoption, rejection or failure of an intervention does not by itself establish those hypotheses.

The original envisages adapting an impact assessment to healthcare resource allocation, model challenge to university rankings and deliberation to AI governance. Those are research possibilities, not established transfers. The healthcare and university diagnoses are uncorrected historical leads. The accepted AI correction R4 v0.2 distinguishes strategic feasibility, observation, authority and action, and does not establish that a Commons or any particular deliberative infrastructure is necessary. A useful comparison across domains would identify which tasks, evidence, legitimate owners and correction mechanisms are comparable and where they differ. There is no basis for declaring the framework validated everywhere or invalid everywhere based on a central-bank pilot.

### 6.4 The Honest Conclusion

The original concludes that entrenched professional and institutional arrangements may obstruct reform and describes the risks of recurrent financial crisis, distributional backlash and climate shocks. These are important questions, but the future trajectory is unknown. Crises may recur without the predicted mechanism, institutions may learn, and existing or distributed arrangements may prove adequate. The spiral is conditional, not a forecast of cumulative deterioration.

The report also identifies possible resources for experimentation: analytical expertise, existing data and research, the ability to develop distributional scenarios, challenge functions and facilitated deliberation. It cites the Bank of England's distributional research and independent statistical agencies as possible precedents for a protected analysis unit. These are leads to verify rather than demonstrations of technical or legal feasibility. The original names the Bank of England, Riksbank and Reserve Bank of New Zealand as possible early candidates; that is a proposed research direction, not a finding of political willingness or actual capability. Their mandates, capacity and existing practices need separate evaluation. The reviewer-supplied New Zealand changes are especially relevant to the historical remit example.

The Assessment could be a bounded experiment if an evidence or accountability gap and lawful proportionate design are established. Statutory independence and required responses may need legislation; uncertainty about distribution, possible communication costs and narrow mandate objections can be legitimate. A pilot could demonstrate incremental value, demonstrate no value, or identify a different institutional owner. The Red Team and Assembly face similar tests. A reasoned decline and genuine procedural absorption are different outcomes; neither is predetermined. The original's ambition of generating evidence for reform can be preserved without assuming that further reform is the correct outcome.

The original's wager was that one institution might pioneer a bounded Assessment and other institutions might learn from the experience. That remains a concrete experimental possibility. The lesson could be a better way of explaining uncertainty, a more independent analysis function or evidence that the costs of this proposal outweigh its incremental benefits. International diffusion could reflect learning, but also correlated assumptions or pressure to adopt a fashionable standard. The experiment must preserve alternatives and avoid judging other institutions by whether they copy it. The relevant outcome is the quality of the resulting decisions and accountability, not a movement toward the author's preferred architecture.

### 6.5 A Final Word

The original opens its final word with a distinction central to this report: “The central bank is not a failing institution. It is an institution that has succeeded brilliantly at the task it was designed to perform—maintaining price stability—and that is now being asked to perform tasks for which its architecture was never designed.” (Original §6.5; the same framing appears in §1.5.) That historical judgment is a proposition to investigate rather than an established result. As IC-t requires, assess the performance of a particular institution on its assigned tasks separately from the adequacy of its architecture for proposed additional tasks. The favourable account and the critical account both face the same evidence standard, including the post-2021 leads. New responsibilities require lawful democratic assignment; evidence of an economic problem does not by itself imply that a monetary institution should own its solution.

The report's central aspiration is not to dismiss central banking expertise. The fictional young economist who reports what a DSGE model predicts illustrates a question about the distinction between model output, available evidence and a decision. Her statement is not evidence of dishonesty, institutional blindness or inability to think beyond her training. A capable institution can combine technical models with judgment, alternative evidence and correction. The proposed Distributional Impact Assessment is not a panacea: it cannot by itself resolve financial crises, wealth inequality or climate risk, and it may not be needed for some tasks. Its contribution depends on the evidence it adds and the legitimate response pathway it serves.

The original also makes a precedent argument: an institution “that can be required to perceive one excluded dimension can be required to perceive others” (original §6.5). A successful bounded Assessment might establish reusable methods for independent analysis, lawful data access, reason-giving and review; it might also demonstrate that acknowledging a limitation need not undermine a legitimate mandate. Those capabilities could lower the cost of investigating other demonstrated information gaps, whether within the central bank or through interfaces with other institutions. This is a conditional institutional-learning hypothesis, not an entitlement to expand observation or a guarantee that one reform makes another necessary. The rival is scope creep: a precedent may encourage unnecessary data collection, broaden discretion without democratic authorization, or divert capacity from assigned tasks. Evaluate any proposed transfer against a specified gap, demonstrated incremental benefit, lawful task ownership, privacy, proportionality and alternatives. A precedent that merely induces further adoption, or that fails to improve outcomes, provides no warrant for extending the architecture; legitimate stopping and nonadoption remain available.

The engineers are at the table. That refrain is retained as an invitation to examine the relation between expertise, evidence, authority and action. Whether the inherited architecture is adequate is the question to test, not the answer assumed in advance. The capacity to recognise and correct a consequential limitation, including a limitation in this report's own proposals, is an aspiration worth preserving.
