## Appendix B: International Analogues and Precedents

The following examples were cited by the original as precedents. They remain dated verification leads, not verified implementations, existence proofs or recommendations for particular jurisdictions. Their effectiveness, legal scope, transferability and limitations must be established with primary sources and human domain review.

### B.1 The Bank of England: Prototype Distributional Analysis

The original describes Bank of England studies using the NMG Consulting Survey and Wealth and Assets Survey to examine the distributional effects of interest-rate changes and quantitative easing. It highlights differences between the cash-flow channel, employment effects and asset-price effects across groups. The actual datasets, methods, horizons, counterfactuals, findings and use in committee decisions require verification. Do not assert that the bank has the most advanced analysis or that its Monetary Policy Committee never receives the results. These studies, if appropriately documented, could motivate comparison between existing research and the proposed operational Assessment. A new independent unit is not automatically required by the existence of a prototype analysis.

### B.2 The Reserve Bank of New Zealand: The Remit as Multi-Dimensional Mandate

The original cites the 2019 remit as recognising employment alongside price stability and supporting government economic objectives. Verify the quoted wording, legal status, priorities and subsequent remit and legislative changes. Claude's audit review adds post-2019 changes concerning the employment objective as a research lead not present in the original. A multi-objective remit, if verified for a particular period, demonstrates only that such a legal arrangement existed, not that it succeeded or that it preserved independence. Compare performance, accountability and trade-offs across periods.

### B.3 The Swedish Riksbank: Accountability Through Published Minutes

The original cites attributed votes, detailed meeting minutes and reasoning for dissenting views as a possible model of transparency. Verify publication practices and the actual coverage of financial, international and distributional considerations. Examine comprehension, market effects, reason quality, challenge and correction rather than assume that publication improves credibility. Distinguish legitimate confidential deliberation from unjustified withholding. The precedent may inform an Assessment or parliamentary analysis but does not establish the need for either.

### B.4 The Network for Greening the Financial System (NGFS)

The original describes the NGFS as a network established in 2017 with more than one hundred central banks and supervisors, developing climate scenarios, portfolio recommendations and risk-assessment frameworks. Dates, membership, remit and actual use require verification; do not infer that most members have or have not integrated its work operationally. A shared scenario can improve coordination but can also create common-mode assumptions. The audit reviewer adds ECB and BoE climate-related actions to the queue. Compare their legal basis, analytical use and outcomes; participation and publication do not demonstrate effectiveness. Different actors may own climate-risk and emissions-policy tasks.

### B.5 Citizens' Assemblies on Complex Policy Questions

The original cites Irish assemblies on marriage equality, abortion and climate change and France's Citizens' Convention on Climate. Verify selection, facilitation, recommendations, implementation and public legitimacy. The proposed transfer concerns the ability of citizens to deliberate on complex trade-offs with time, information and support, not an authority to decide interest rates. Assess distinct timing and confidentiality requirements, representativeness and potential burdens. A successful example in another domain would justify investigation, not prove that the monetary-policy pilot is needed or effective.
