## Appendix C: The Governance as Engineering Connection

### C.1 The Architectural Foundation

The original drew on control theory, information theory and cybernetics. The accepted dependency audit GA-R02-R5 v0.2 binds the corrected VI, VIII and XX results, the capstone and subsidiarity corrections and the accepted AI correction for transferred claims. Papers I–V are historical and have not been independently re-audited here. The witnesses below are stipulated mathematical or logical examples, not empirical monetary models or legal findings. No universal compression-to-crisis principle, objective-equals-observation identity, count-based index or universal multi-scale necessity is retained.

### C.2 The Seven Primitives

The original mapped seven primitives to monetary governance. Retain the mappings as analogies, not a formal equivalence: **nodes** are committees and institutions (MPC, FOMC, Governing Council); **state** is the task-relevant economic condition (output, employment, prices, stability and distribution); **flows** are transmission channels (interest rates, asset values, exchange rates and credit); **latency** is the delay between information, decision and effect (the original invokes “long and variable lags”, attribution to verify); **constraints** include bounds on instruments, fiscal interactions and legal mandates; **feedback loops** include expectations, financial accelerators and possible political backlash; **signal fidelity** concerns how accurately evidence represents the distinctions relevant to the task. These primitives do not identify the entire economy as a single central bank's legitimate control task.

### C.3 Task-relative observation and action (W1–W3)

#### W1 — Task-relative observation (VI)

In the static noiseless linear model, disturbances d in subspace D, observations Cd, admissible residuals L (subspace), and all compensating actions −D available, exact task adequacy is equivalent to K=D∩ker C⊆L. Necessity holds because indistinguishable disturbances require a common action; sufficiency here uses all −D. Hidden gap dim K−dim(K∩L); lower bound max(0,dim D−dim(D∩L)−rank(C|D)) is not sufficient. Let D=R², L=span(e2), C1=(1,0), C2=(0,1). Equal ranks, hidden gaps 0 and 1. In R³ with L=span(e2,e3), observing x1 gives gap 0; observing x2,x3 gives gap 1 despite higher rank. A Taylor-rule input count is neither this rank nor a measure of a central bank. A CPI objective does not identify C. No empirical gap estimated.

#### W2 — Observation, mandate, action (VI, XX, capstone/subsidiarity)

For `d∈{0,1}`, y=d and required action a=d: one authority can act on the observation, another is authorized only a=0; a third has authority but failed implementation. Same C, different outcomes. Another actor may have a different legitimately defined task for which a=0 is sufficient. If an authorized fiscal or prudential actor observes and handles the relevant risk, the system can meet its task without the monetary committee optimizing every dimension. Specify accountability and coordination rather than equate narrow mandate with blindness. The corrected XX action-compatible cover bound is necessary, not sufficient: exact adequacy requires at least as many admissible actions as the minimum number of action-compatible sets covering the admissible states.

#### W3 — Scalar versus vector action: feasibility, not count

In one-period linear illustrative residual r=d+Bu, acceptable residual `L={0}`. With d=(1,2), B=(1,2)^T and scalar u=−1, both residuals vanish. With d=(1,0), same B, no u eliminates both. With B=I₂, u=−d works only if admissible; e.g. if u₂=0 and d₂≠0, two nominal instruments do not suffice. With B columns (1,2)^T and (2,4)^T the response span is still one-dimensional. For unconstrained controls exact cancellation iff −d∈im B; with bounds require −d∈BU, or more generally (d+BU)∩L nonempty. No central-bank transmission matrix estimated; instruments may overlap, interact or have delayed effects. Compare distributed ownership and coordination with mandate expansion. No universal necessity or independent targeting follows from instrument count.

### C.4 Conditional mechanisms and intervention limits (W4–W9)

#### W4 — Proxy, compression, and outcomes (XX)

Original universal Legibility Compression Principle withdrawn. A scalar can encode four binary states (2x+y); omitted state can be irrelevant to a stated task. A missing feature is neither necessary nor sufficient for crisis; even fully observed risk can be ignored or unmanageable. XX witness `X={(0,0),(0,1),(2,0),(2,1)}`, M=a, T=a+b. Every strict deterministic improvement in M improves T, but ties differ. Lottery on (2,0) with probability 1/4 and (0,0) otherwise has E[M]=E[T]=1/2 versus (0,1) giving M=0,T=1. The metric improves while the target worsens. Do not identify M with inflation or T with welfare without a specified model; price stability is a legitimate objective, not inherently a defective proxy. If noise is discussed, scalar AWGN capacity 0.5 log₂(1+SNR) gives 0.5 bit at SNR=1 under its assumptions; no universal cutoff.

#### W5 — Timescales and actuation

Two independent disturbances updated at different rates, observed by one controller sampling at the faster rate, with sufficient bandwidth and authorized immediate actions u_i=−d_i in a stipulated static per-step model: exact rejection. A multilevel controller with missed deadlines or missing actuator can fail. Not a dynamic stability theorem; specify timing, persistence, feedback, delays, information, emergency authority and costs. A fast market's anticipation of a policy may implement the expectations channel rather than defeat it. No universal arbitrage or need for real-time rate changes follows.

#### W6 — Interactions and crisis trajectories (early-paper bridge, capstone)

Under sequential normalized retentions r_i=1−f_i∈[0,1], loss 1−∏r_i≤∑f_i. Two half-retentions lose 3/4≤1, remaining 1/4 passes a 1/5 threshold but fails 1/3. No categorical failure. Common-baseline outcomes 10, 12, 13, 14 have interaction 14−12−13+10=−1; joint 17 gives +2. No universal reinforcing sign. A hypothetical crisis response that reduces leverage from 2 to 1 with stable output is a counterexample to logical inevitability of increasing fragility, not historical evidence. H2 tests every transition, including recovery.

#### W7 — Distributional incidence and counterfactual

Stylized two households, one asset holder H and one wage-dependent L. Suppose a policy relative to a stated no-change baseline raises H's asset income by 2 and L's labor income by 3, all else equal. Both gain and the absolute income difference H−L decreases by 1. If L gains only 1 instead, both gain but the difference increases by 1. If L loses 1, distributional harm differs again. No net transfer or welfare judgment follows from positive asset-price effects alone. Stocks versus flows, inflation, housing, debt, savers, employment, taxes, risk, horizons and heterogeneity must be modeled; counterfactual matters. Distribution metrics and normative weights are not interchangeable. No real-world causal estimate.

#### W8 — Sensing, reporting and decision effects

An Assessment correctly reports harm but the authorized policy set contains only one admissible action; publishing the report cannot change that action. Alternatively two feasible actions exist and decision-makers respond to evidence; no universal sign follows. A mandated public reason may increase accountability while the decision stays the same. Compare information available before decision, reason quality and independent outcomes; publication counts are process measures. A pilot that cannot lawfully obtain data may be redesigned or declined; refusal is not blindness. Transparency timing, confidentiality and market-sensitive information need separate tests.

#### W9 — Common-mode model risk and reviewer limits

Conditional on a specified hazard, two binary miss indicators with equal marginals with marginal probability p have joint miss p²+ρp(1−p), bounded by max(0,2p−1) and p. With any-flag escalation, independent p=.1 gives .01 joint miss; shared p=.05 and ρ=1 gives .05, worse despite marginal improvement; threshold ρ>3/19. At p=1/20, the minimum feasible correlation is −1/19, giving zero joint misses. This is failure to flag only, not response failure without reliable action. Both-must-flag miss 2p−joint miss; false alarms separate. Subsidiarity W5's agreement events are not hazard-conditioned misses. Shared data and model assumptions can correlate errors; diversify challenge and record dissent. Apply to AI review too; no claim of peer review or actual correlation estimates.

#### W3 refinement — counting rule and dynamics

The original counting intuition resembles the Tinbergen rule (historical attribution to verify). In this static linear model independent exact target directions require adequate response span and admissible controls, not simply a count. In a dynamic model, lags, expectations, horizon, path constraints and sequential choices change reachable target paths; a single instrument may trade off objectives over time. No universal dynamic impossibility or sufficiency is derived.

#### W7 refinement — disagreement between metrics

Take baseline incomes H=10 and L=5; after changes +2 and +1 they are 12 and 6. Absolute gap rises from 5 to 6 while H/L stays 2. These are stipulated values and not a causal or welfare estimate; choose measures and normative priorities transparently.

#### W9 alignment

The accepted R4 Appendix C.6 is the cross-report reference, with its distinct aggregation rule, feasible correlation bounds and explicit caution about reviewer dependence; no numerical transfer to monetary-policy institutions.
