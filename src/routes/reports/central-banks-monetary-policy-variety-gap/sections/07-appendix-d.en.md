## Appendix D: Anticipated Objections

### D.1 “The inflation target has served us well for decades. Why risk undermining it by expanding the mandate?”

The objection identifies a serious design concern: clarity, credibility and protection from short-term political instruction may be valuable. The historical claim about performance needs evaluation including the reviewer-supplied post-2021 episode. Expansion is not a default recommendation. A narrow mandate can be adequate alongside other authorities with appropriate responsibilities. Compare additional evidence within the existing mandate, distributed fiscal/prudential responses, authorized mandate change and retention of current arrangements. New objectives require democratic authority and a demonstrated case; the potential cost of weakening a clear mandate belongs in that comparison.

### D.2 “Distributional analysis is too uncertain to be operationally useful.”

The original notes uncertainty in transmission, potential output and estimates of the natural rate. Uncertainty need not imply uselessness, but the fact that committees use uncertain forecasts does not establish the value of another analysis. Compare scenario quality, calibration, relevance to decisions and costs against actual practices. The proposed Assessment presents counterfactual ranges and explicit assumptions, including possible structural error. Some consequences may not be reliably identifiable; a decision-maker may reasonably assign limited weight or an appropriate authority may address them separately. No precise transfer claim follows from a model alone.

### D.3 “Publishing distributional assessments alongside rate decisions would confuse the public and undermine the clarity of the inflation target.”

An Assessment might help people understand consequences through mortgages, employment, savings and other channels. It might also confuse readers, amplify false precision or be interpreted as forward guidance. Test different communication formats, market effects and comprehension. Secure predecision delivery and public release serve different functions; a release after a decision cannot inform that decision. Protect confidentiality, information fairness and lawful independence. Publication is a candidate supplement, not proof of clarity or accountability.

### D.4 “Central bank independence is essential to credibility. Democratic deliberation on monetary policy would politicise interest-rate decisions.”

Distinguish democratic goal-setting, lawful oversight and operational discretion under the mandate. The proposed assembly produces public nonbinding reasons, and the parliamentary office supplies independent analysis rather than directing the committee. Whether those arrangements improve legitimacy without political capture requires evidence; their design needs legal safeguards, conflicts and evaluation. Existing hearings and minutes may be sufficient. No participant or assembly can authorize a new policy instrument merely by recommending it.

### D.5 “The models are the best we have. Criticising their limitations without offering a superior alternative is irresponsible.”

The original's proposal is complementary rather than an abandonment of DSGE or inflation forecasts. A protected Red Team could supply alternative assumptions and scenarios, and the Assessment could supply distributional analysis. Actual models may already include such features. Compare model portfolios, forecast performance, consequential error detection, uncertainty and decision value. More models, challenge or information do not automatically improve outcomes; correlated errors and false alarms matter. A proposed alternative is subject to the same humility it demands from incumbents.

### D.6 “This analysis is interesting, but it will never be implemented. The Pretence of Knowledge is too strong.”

The original cites BoE distributional research, NGFS work and Riksbank minutes as possible precedents; verify their actual scope and outcomes. These may motivate a pilot, but feasibility and authority must be established separately. Legitimate decline, genuine procedural absorption, adaptation and successful reform are all possible. The future trajectory is unknown. H5 tests obstruction observationally; adoption or nonadoption is not a test of the diagnosis. Institution-specific findings remain on HOLD until applicable primary-source factual and legal verification and human domain review with disclosed conflicts are complete. No AI review substitutes for that requirement.
