## Between Parts I and II: A Conditional Diagnostic Schema

The earlier canonical formula and the prose accompanying its diagram asserted a universal path from compressed observation to crisis. That argument and the diagram’s use in the active edition are withdrawn. The original diagram was not supplied for this revision and is not reproduced in the active edition. The following text schema replaces the old image dependency; it does not certify that the old artwork or any cover is current.

| Investigation stage | Distinction to preserve |
|---|---|
| Task and affected people | Outcomes, legitimate rights, acceptable errors, time horizon and feasible alternatives |
| Evaluation and observation | What is valued versus what information is available, to whom and when |
| Modelling and decision | Which explanations are distinguishable; which actions are authorised and chosen |
| Actuation and outcomes | Which interventions take effect, with what distribution of consequences |
| Correction and review | How results, complaints and external evidence can change the model, objective, decision or action architecture |

Possible cross-cutting constraints include authority, resources, timing, incentives, institutional resistance and interactions with other systems. The arrows in a case-specific causal diagram should be hypotheses until supported by evidence. Failure at one stage does not mechanically imply failure at the next. Feedback can be informative but ignored; an apparently missing observation may be unnecessary or supplied through another channel. A suitable robust action may not need to distinguish every state.

Use revised Paper VIII's task-relative profile and Appendix F below to operationalise the schema. The point is to locate consequential constraints and feasible interventions rather than to infer a universal deficit or an inevitable crisis.
