## 8. What the Series Contributes and What It Has Not Established

### 8.1 The series as a research architecture

The historical series deliberately brought different models and sources into a shared process of inquiry. Initial responses from several language models, comparison, cross-feeding, authorial synthesis and later critique were used to develop report structures and terms. This process may generate useful hypotheses and notice disagreements. It does not establish statistical independence, reliability of particular models, comprehensive source coverage or superiority to other research approaches. Models may share training material, reproduce a prompt's assumptions and converge through cross-feeding. Authorial judgement is a further selection point. The original corpus has twenty traceable case entries; it is a discovery corpus, not a prospectively sampled validation set.

### 8.2 The methodological implication

The framework should be evaluated with the same distinctions it asks institutions to make. A claim can be salient without being true, well sourced without establishing a causal explanation, or formally valid within a model without transferring to a real institution. Separate model reasoning, reported historical interpretations, hypotheses and design proposals. Maintain prompt and source records, model identifiers and dates, changes to the coding vocabulary, alternative hypotheses and disagreement logs. Where those records are absent, state the limitation. Compare independent initial analyses before cross-feeding, and use independent evidence rather than model agreement to adjudicate contested claims.

The acceptance of specific corrections in VI, VIII and XX is a source-level event. It does not certify that the capstone's original case classifications or all earlier formal claims are valid. The historical tables remain available precisely so researchers can identify where the proposed categories came from and test them independently.

### 8.3 The invitation grounded in method

A useful next step is to use the same framework on tasks not used to develop it, with inclusion and exclusion rules established beforehand. Independent coders should be able to disagree, classify a case as unknown or not applicable, and identify a mechanism missing from the catalogue. Compare the quality of resulting explanations or decisions with simpler task-specific methods. A report that finds no deficit or identifies a non-architectural cause is a valid outcome. The methodology should be improved when it fails rather than treating every counterexample as a disguised instance of its existing categories.
