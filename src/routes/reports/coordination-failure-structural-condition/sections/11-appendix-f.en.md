## Appendix F: Methodology and Limits

### F.1 Development of the primitives

The original method combined research and AI-assisted interpretation of country and organisational cases, with comparison and cross-feeding of model outputs and authorial integration. Its vocabulary evolved during the investigation. It was not a fixed, independently coded protocol; later cases were examined using concepts formed on earlier cases. It would therefore be misleading to claim that all categories arose independently from the material rather than being partly imposed by earlier theory. This is exploratory theory construction [HR/H]. The original records available in this package do not establish full prompt provenance, independent coding or a representative sample. New work should preserve dates, source excerpts, model versions, prompts, initial outputs, coding changes, disagreement and decisions to reject hypotheses. AI outputs are leads, not evidence of factual accuracy or independent confirmation.

### F.2 What the framework cannot explain

**Deliberate malice and power.** Institutional constraints do not explain away corruption, predation, strategic deception or deliberate choices. A well-observed problem may remain unaddressed for political reasons. Some structural mechanisms interact with interests; identifying the interaction requires separate evidence, as §3.5 explains.

**Exogenous shocks.** Some events may be unanticipated even with a feasible, well-designed observation architecture. Distinguish forecast limits, preparedness, robustness, action constraints and the effects of shocks. The historical report's interpretations of the financial crisis, the pandemic and war as crossings of a variety gap are contested hypotheses, not conclusions supported by this revision.

**Timing and form of collapse.** No universal gap trajectory, probability of collapse or predictive clock has been established. The framework cannot derive crisis timing from dimensionality or infer vulnerability from a single score. A case-specific prediction requires a dynamic model and calibration appropriate to that outcome.

**Political economy and normative disagreement.** The catalogue may help identify questions of authority and interests, but it does not resolve them. Neither formal adequacy nor better coordination determines legitimate goals. Coalitions, institutions, material resources, historical context and rights matter, including evidence that conflicts with the proposed diagnosis.

### F.3 Prospective testing and falsification

A possible staged validation programme, aligned with corrected VIII, follows. It is proposed, not performed.

1. Specify the task, outcome, affected population, legitimate constraints, deadline, observation set and feasible action alternatives. Define the episode start and end, sampling frame, missingness and outcome reference. Include routine successes, unresponded cases and negative examples; for latency, document right censoring and use appropriate estimable medians or restricted means under stated assumptions.
2. Define each candidate primitive, inclusion and exclusion examples, possible alternatives, and observable implications before evaluating held-out cases. Record the five diagnostic axes separately with sources, uncertainty and codes for unknown and not applicable. Do not force a deficit or use the number of labels as a severity score.
3. Separate discovery from evaluation. Use cases and episodes not used to form the vocabulary, independent coders blind to previous labels where feasible, and an adjudication log. Compare agreement and explanatory or decision usefulness with a simpler task-specific baseline. Preserve disagreements, nulls and cases that require other categories. No recurrence rate estimated from the original discovery set is an independent test.
4. Specify mechanism-specific tests: for lock-in, examine changes in the four candidate components and their timing; for ontological capture, distinguish reporting and behavioural changes; for resistance, identify the rule, authority and alternatives. Prespecify what evidence counts against each hypothesis. A successful ordinary reform under the predicted conditions challenges a claim of architectural necessity. A case without the predicted relationship should not be redescribed post hoc as a hidden instance.
5. Test candidate interventions against feasible comparators, specifying costs, harms, rights, distributional effects, monitoring and stopping rules. Where causal identification is not credible, report associations and uncertainty. For a retention model test the sequential product against additive and interacting alternatives using independently measured inputs and outputs. The mathematical inequality in E.5 precludes the earlier fractional-loss superadditivity prediction under that model. Its conditional breadth result can motivate a cost-controlled comparison; it does not establish a policy preference absent the assumptions.
6. For the modernity and simultaneity hypotheses, use dated comparable task windows, dependencies and performance measures, including periods and cases where the relationship reverses. For auto-immunity, test both benefits and resource costs of protection. No numerical critical gap or global historical dimensionality series is assumed. Record all protocol changes and publish negative results.

### F.4 Epistemic status of the claims

| Status | Meaning here | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Exact conditional result [M] | Valid within explicitly stated assumptions | Static VI condition; retention inequality |
| Historical report [HR] | Original interpretation or unverified reported fact | Appendices B–D and G |
| Hypothesis [H] | Proposed empirical mechanism requiring testing | Lock-in; acceleration asymmetry |
| Interpretive/design proposal [IP] | Candidate framework, intervention or research direction | Five-axis profile and six design families |

These local labels distinguish evidence types; they do not amend the central series taxonomy. No historical claim becomes a formal result by appearing next to one. No validation is inferred from the existing reports or agreement across language models. All six design families require task-specific evaluation. Earlier engineering paper claims remain subject to separate audit; the corrected results are not a re-derivation of the whole series.

### F.5 The framework's own blind spots

The original author remains an integration and selection point. AI systems may share errors, reinforce prompt assumptions, omit local context and generate plausible but unsupported explanations. The case selection and sources may privilege visible institutions and the experiences of people whose evidence is accessible. The approach can miss outcomes not represented in the selected categories, forms of governance outside formal institutions, the constraints of marginalised groups, and disagreement about what counts as success. The absence of a recorded signal does not determine the direction of bias. Some proposed interventions may create harms that the framework fails to notice.

Independent source verification, participation of affected people, disagreement records, comparison with alternative lenses and the ability to reject the framework are safeguards, not guarantees. The research programme remains open to revision. No procedure can certify comprehensive self-knowledge; particular missing evidence can sometimes be identified and addressed.
