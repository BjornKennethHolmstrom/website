## Appendix G: Candidate Case Studies of Adaptive Coherence

These six examples are retained from the original capstone as historical descriptions and research leads [HR/H]. Their institutional arrangements, outcome claims and dates have not been independently checked in this correction. No current numerical usage, outcome advantage or causal effect is certified. Before empirical use, verify descriptions and dates against primary sources, define a comparator and test the proposed mechanisms. This is not a set of proofs that the design families work or that all six share a single structural property.

### G.1 Finland's foresight institutions

**Historical description.** The original report describes Sitra, the parliamentary Committee for the Future and the basic-income experiment as examples of anticipatory functions and learning-oriented institutional arrangements. The proposed distinction is between the capacity to investigate future conditions and the capacity to act on what is learned.

**Questions to investigate.** How do foresight findings enter actual policy decisions, and with what authority and delays? What evidence changes a decision rather than merely being published? Compare projects with and without the relevant foresight arrangements. Examine institutional learning and the opportunities for experimentation, including whether results changed policy.

**Limitations.** Foresight is not delivery; the original Throughput Constraint hypothesis remains untested here. Consensus, budget limits and authority may affect decision speed. Neither the presence of foresight organisations nor the existence of an experiment establishes adequacy or successful transfer. Local and national outcomes and their distribution need separate examination.

### G.2 Kaiser Permanente

**Historical description.** The original example describes an integrated managed-care arrangement combining payer and provider functions, salaried physicians and a shared information system. The proposed mechanism concerns coordination of clinical and organisational information and the relation between payment incentives and care decisions.

**Questions to investigate.** Compare continuity, decision quality, resource use, patient outcomes, access and equity with appropriate alternatives. Distinguish the effects of shared records, payment rules, staffing, enrolment selection and local practice. Which clinical distinctions are actually accessible at which decision points, and who has authority to act?

**Limitations.** Integration within one organisation does not solve the fragmentation of the broader health system; care outside the network may not share the same channels. Integration can introduce barriers, incentives or data risks of its own. The original outcome and quality assertions are not reverified, and no clinical conclusion or universal model of care follows.

### G.3 The Basque Concierto Económico

**Historical description.** The original report points to the fiscal arrangement under which Basque institutions collect taxes and negotiate a contribution to shared Spanish costs. The proposed research question concerns the relationship between territorial authority, local information and coordination of common functions.

**Questions to investigate.** Specify the legal responsibilities, actual fiscal flows, distributional effects, revenue risks and coordination mechanisms; compare against relevant alternative arrangements. Distinguish the effects of fiscal autonomy from economic structure, historical capacity and political institutions. Which outcomes are affected and who can contest them?

**Limitations.** The arrangement is politically contested and dependent on its legal and historical setting. Distributional consequences, negotiations and the capacities of other regions matter. The original claims about better outcomes and transferability are not established here. No necessary conclusion about constitutional settlement follows.

### G.4 Ireland's citizens' assemblies

**Historical description.** The original report refers to deliberative bodies on constitutional and policy questions, including marriage equality, abortion and climate. The proposed mechanism concerns giving a selected group of citizens time and support to hear evidence and formulate recommendations outside ordinary partisan competition. The distinct constitutional convention and citizens' assembly arrangements should not be conflated; verify membership, selection and mandate in each case.

**Questions to investigate.** Compare participation, representativeness, access to evidence, the quality and distribution of recommendations, subsequent decisions and legitimacy. Examine agenda-setting, selection and the authority of the body separately. A recommendation is not the same as binding decision or implementation.

**Limitations.** Such bodies have bounded mandates; governments or other authorities can shape referral and follow-up. Selection, nonresponse, facilitation and expert presentation can affect results. They are not a universal replacement for representative institutions. No causal or cross-national outcome advantage is asserted.

### G.5 Brazil's PIX

**Historical description.** The original example describes the instant-payment system built by the Central Bank of Brazil as a shared payment infrastructure. It proposes that a payment rail may supply a valuable function even when other aspects of financial governance remain difficult.

**Questions to investigate.** Verify accessibility, reliability, costs, adoption, inclusion, fraud, privacy and distributional outcomes relative to alternative payment methods. Distinguish transactional records from the information actually needed for monetary or other governance tasks. Examine the governance of infrastructure, participation and data access, not only speed or volume.

**Limitations.** A payment rail is not the whole credit system. The original report identifies concerns about concentration and credit costs, which require their own evidence and analysis. Payment success would not establish improved governance in unrelated domains or show that the central bank's entire observation architecture has changed. The original numerical claims are withheld from the active case argument.

### G.6 India's UPI

**Historical description.** The original example describes UPI, a shared digital payments interface with competing applications. It contrasts this arrangement with the report's concerns about legal and administrative infrastructure. The proposed mechanism concerns interoperability and the ability to supply a bounded service without replacing every underlying institution.

**Questions to investigate.** Establish access, cost, reliability, privacy, inclusion and the role of regulation and participating institutions. Compare the outcomes with alternative rails and account for selection and infrastructure differences. Investigate whether the interface reduces or displaces costs and whether its success affects incentives to reform other institutions.

**Limitations.** A payments interface does not resolve land disputes or general administrative constraints. It may depend on the underlying legal, financial and technical infrastructure. The bypass-trap mechanism is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated consequence of UPI's existence. The historical transaction and individual-case figures are not independently verified and are not reused as evidence.

### G.7 Candidate shared features and contrasts

Multi-scale observation, matched authority, integration preserving relevant distinctions and designed revisability provide questions to compare across the cases. Each must be coded separately and may be absent, inapplicable or counterproductive. Payment interoperability is not the same as deliberation; fiscal negotiation is not the same as clinical coordination. A common vocabulary is useful only if it preserves these differences. Prospective comparison should assess whether a feature improves a specified outcome relative to a feasible alternative and identify cases where it does not. This is a research agenda, not a finding of common causation.
