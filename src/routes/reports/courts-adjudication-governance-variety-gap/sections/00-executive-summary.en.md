## Executive Summary

**The Paradox**

Courts can be highly sophisticated institutions for determining the facts of disputes, applying law and granting remedies. Rules of evidence, opportunities to challenge claims, reasoned judgments, appellate review and the continuity afforded by precedent are valuable features of legal governance. The original report credited these features with protecting individuals against arbitrary power. That favourable account is a historical proposition requiring the same dated, jurisdiction-specific evidence as its criticisms; not every litigant receives a fair or accurate decision.

The paradox is that decisions in individual proceedings may also shape the conditions under which many other people live. The original identified administrative authority, digital-platform competition, surveillance, environmental protection and electoral representation as examples. Its references to *Loper Bright*, *West Virginia v. EPA*, major questions, Google, Microsoft and AT&T are unverified research leads, not findings about the holdings, records, or consequences of those cases. A decision may alter a regulatory framework even where the question legally before the court is narrow. Whether wider effects are relevant to that legal question, were evidenced, and can lawfully influence the remedy are distinct questions. A court need not possess legislative competence to be an adequate court.

**The Core Diagnosis: The Adjudication–Governance Variety Gap**

This is a candidate task-relative mismatch between the evidence, legal authority, feasible remedies and implementation available to institutions and the requirements of a specified legal or governance task. The original's universal claim of structural blindness and its numerical dimensionality inference are withdrawn. Missing information, inadmissible information, justified exclusion, lack of attention, lack of authority and failed enforcement are different mechanisms. Aggregate behavioural, distributional and long-run effects may warrant inquiry, but their existence does not assign their governance to a court. Legislatures, regulators, appellate mechanisms and existing research may already supply adequate complementary functions. Individual rights and procedural guarantees must be satisfied independently; they are not costs that can be offset by aggregate improvement.

**Resolution Lock‑In — A Cross‑Series Insight**

Resolution Lock‑In is a hypothesis that successful institutional specialization may make adaptation to another task difficult. Earlier healthcare and university diagnoses are uncorrected historical leads. The accepted AI correction (GA-R02-R4 v0.2) treats the Coherence–Velocity Trap as a conditional strategic feasibility hypothesis, and the accepted central banks correction (GA-R02-R5 v0.2) distinguishes objectives, evidence, attention, mandate and action. Both are research drafts on publication hold, not empirical authority. The courts report does not establish a limiting case or universal constitutional prohibition on other scales. Actual constitutional duties and procedural rules vary.

**The Signature Pattern: The Case‑by‑Case–Doctrinal Fragmentation–Systemic Blindness–Legislative Intervention Loop**

The original contrasted accumulation with other reports' drift and lurch metaphors. These are conditional historical pattern labels, not mutually exclusive or universal classes. Its proposed sequence starts with individual decisions that may exhibit high case fidelity; decisions may accumulate without integration, doctrinal fragmentation may follow, relevant adverse systemic consequences may emerge, legislative or regulatory intervention may respond, and the revised framework may again fragment. Each of five links and the favourable starting premise must be tested separately. Appellate synthesis, coherent distinctions, stable expectations, ordinary democratic lawmaking and durable clarification are rival pathways. H2 predeclares indicators and disconfirmers; recurrence and inevitability do not follow from the model.

**The Cultural Anchor: Adversarial Epistemology**

The original names an institutional hypothesis: reliance on contest between opposing advocates before a neutral arbiter may privilege evidence parties have incentives and resources to present. Challenge can also expose weak arguments and protect impartiality. Relevant systemic analysis may be introduced through lawful channels, and a decision not to rely on it may be justified. Test actual professional practices, independent challenge and reasoned correction, rather than attributing a belief to every lawyer or judge. The Myth of the Neutral Arbiter remains a metaphor for a possible conflation of impartiality with a particular evidentiary arrangement, not a claim that restraint is a vice.

**The Twin Deficits**

| Aspect | Outer (Hardware) | Inner (Operating System) |
|---|---|---|
| Candidate strength | Reliable evidence procedures, judicial independence, reasoned decisions, precedent and adversarial testing | Rule of law, impartiality, fair hearing and institutional memory |
| Candidate deficit | Relevant information absent or inaccessible, diffuse harms difficult to bring, unrepresented interests, inadequate lawful response or enforcement | Independent challenge may be deflected, professional routines may privilege familiar methods; neutrality may be mistakenly equated with exclusion of legally relevant knowledge |
| Scoped from original leads | Market consequences of antitrust doctrine, case-selection effects in constitutional litigation, missing outcomes among settled or unpublished cases | Accountability of consequential constitutional decisions, strategic delay and resource asymmetries, insufficient coordination with other task owners |

The hardware examples are not interchangeable. If a case record omits evidence of the market structure affected by a particular competition rule, that is a candidate information gap. If a constitutional review procedure selects a different question from the one that public debate would select, that may be a legitimate difference of institutional task. If a litigant cannot afford to challenge a report, formally admissible evidence may still undermine fairness. The corresponding operating-system question concerns actual institutional responses to independent challenge, not adherence to a particular legal tradition. Judicial independence can both protect parties and raise questions about appropriate forms of accountability; its value must be judged against the purpose it serves, not against a requirement for electoral responsiveness.

Neither side of the table is an established generalization. Evaluate the strengths and deficits on comparable evidence. Adverse outcomes do not demonstrate illegality, and legitimate constraints on judicial action are not deficiencies merely because a broader problem remains.

**The Structural Mechanisms**

Chapter 2 retains the interrupt-driven activation analogy, rules of evidence, standing, adversarial presentation, precedent, the Epistemic Black Hole, remedial fragmentation, weaponised latency, document volume, jurisdictional boundaries, constitutional adjudication, Adversarial Epistemology and their interactions. Case filing, later procedural stages and publication are different events. The Epistemic Black Hole is a missingness and selection hypothesis, not a universal settlement rate or a finding of intentional concealment. Delay can be a financial burden or a serious rights harm. The accepted conditional formal witnesses in Appendix C do not measure actual courts.

**What Building Requisite Judicial Governance Would Look Like**

The design aim is to preserve ordinary courts' retrospective stabilising role while investigating whether better information, remedies or coordination are needed. Three ordinary reforms are considered: standing, intervention rights and systemic evidence. Specialist tribunals with defined lawful powers, cross-border coordination compacts, precedent review commissions and a Digital Rights Assembly remain candidate designs. Existing arrangements, modest reforms and other institutional owners are real comparators. No necessary institutional sequence follows.

**A Concrete First Step: The Systemic Effects Registry**

The Registry is a possible independent research mechanism to study the consequences of doctrines. Compare independent research institutions with a statutory body, and the original three-domain pilot with its later four-domain variant adding plea bargaining. A public report need not enter proceedings. Any incorporation, mandatory reason-giving or judicial notice requires separate legal authority, relevant evidence, notice, meaningful rebuttal, independence and review. Where fair challenge would require protected data, exclude reliance rather than compel disclosure. Criminal research defaults to aggregate analysis with no use in individual cases. Plural assessments, explicit selection bounds where possible, and independent support for parties address the Registry's own risks of correlated error, resource asymmetry and selection. No empirical benefit is established.

**The Honest Conclusion**

The original's aspiration was to supplement adjudication without abandoning its safeguards. 

The default outcome is unknown. The original identifies research expertise, law reform institutions, peer learning and public interest as possible resources for a bounded experiment (§6.4); their availability, legal feasibility and value remain unverified. Existing arrangements or modest reforms may prove adequate.

**The Series Context**

This fifth organizational report retains that motivation, but the hypothesis may be wrong and the proposed intervention may fail, be unnecessary or be unlawful. Adoption and nonadoption do not themselves test the diagnosis. The English correction is a research draft for dependency review only. Institution- and case-specific findings remain on HOLD pending primary-source verification and binding human comparative, constitutional, procedural and empirical legal review, including criminal procedure, privacy and judicial ethics. No legal authorization, affected-public participation or conventional peer review is established.
