## 1. The Adjudication–Governance Variety Gap

### 1.1 Opening: Individual adjudication and systemic consequences

The original opened with *Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo*, a 2024 US Supreme Court case that it described as arising from a dispute over whether commercial fishing vessels could be required to carry federal observers and as transforming the administrative state's interpretive framework through the treatment of *Chevron* deference. The case, its companion proceeding, the precise holding and subsequent application require primary-source verification. The original asked whether a dispute about a particular regulatory requirement can become the vehicle for changing the relationship between courts and agencies across many domains. The question is important even if the original's account of the record proves incomplete.

Separate the particular regulatory dispute, the legal interpretive question, the authority of the court and agencies, and the empirical consequences of the resulting rule. The original claimed that the court lacked systematic evidence of aggregate regulatory effects. That is a lead to check against the actual record, including amici, not a demonstrated absence. Claude's v0.1 audit review supplied the companion *Relentless*, the industry-funded at-sea monitoring issue and amicus submissions as unverified verification leads. A legally relevant rival is that the applicable interpretive method might not authorize balancing aggregate policy effects in deciding the question. That would be a distinction about legal purpose, not necessarily blindness or bad faith.

Antitrust, privacy, environmental administration and constitutional law can similarly link a particular dispute to effects across a wider population. An order affecting one party, a precedential rule, a generally binding constitutional determination and a structural remedy are different routes by which that can happen. Verify each by jurisdiction. A decision may lawfully resolve a question without designing a replacement policy, even when the consequences are extensive. The report investigates whether the wider system has suitable evidence and legally assigned responsibility to understand and respond to those consequences.

### 1.2 The Case-by-Case–Doctrinal Fragmentation–Systemic Blindness–Legislative Intervention Loop

The original says, "Courts do not drift or lurch. They accumulate." This is an attributed pattern metaphor rather than an exclusive empirical classification. A candidate accumulation process begins with individual contract, tort and administrative disputes. Case fidelity is the favourable initial hypothesis: the record is developed carefully, the relevant rule identified, and the remedy reasoned. Verify it on the same standards used to evaluate shortcomings, including cases not decided on the merits and unequal access to representation.

First, decisions may **accumulate without integration**. Case A influences case B and subsequent distinctions condition case C. A hierarchy of courts, appellate review, persuasive authority and deliberate reconciliation can change that sequence; not every decision makes binding precedent. The hypothesis is that sufficiently important unresolved differences persist where available integration mechanisms fail.

Second, unresolved accumulation may produce **doctrinal fragmentation**. The original's examples are antitrust doctrine developed in older industries and applied to digital platforms, and privacy doctrine shaped by earlier surveillance technologies. New distinctions may be appropriate responses to changed facts rather than contradictions. Test conflicts in legally comparable cases, not the mere number of decisions or exceptions.

Third, fragmentation may produce **adverse systemic consequences**: uncertainty for firms and agencies, altered incentives, uneven access to rights or unanticipated behavioural responses. Evidence must distinguish these effects from changes in technology, markets, politics or legislative design. A consistent doctrine can have adverse effects too; uncertainty may reflect evolving knowledge rather than institutional failure.

Fourth, these consequences may motivate **legislative or regulatory intervention**, possibly after reporting, research, affected-public advocacy or crisis. The original treated intervention as a forced restoration of coherence. Ordinary democratic revision, independent political priorities and the legitimate division between law application and policymaking are rival explanations. The absence of an intervention need not mean that harms were hidden.

Fifth, a new framework may itself generate **renewed fragmentation** as courts interpret it. But it may also resolve conflicts durably. Statutory clarification or authoritative appellate synthesis can reduce the number of unresolved conflicts while the total stock of precedent grows. No monotone worsening follows from accumulation. H2 specifies observations that would weaken each link. The loop is a hypothesis about specified doctrines over time, not a prediction that all courts enter a stable trap.

### 1.3 The Adjudication–Governance Variety Gap Defined

The gap refers to a failure, if demonstrated, to meet a specified legally and normatively justified task with the available evidence and response architecture. It is not a measured difference in the number of dimensions observed by a court and the number of dimensions of society. A small set of relevant observations can be sufficient; many observations can be irrelevant. Appendix C W1 and W2 illustrate this distinction under explicit assumptions.

The original praised rules of evidence, adversarial testing and reasoned judgment for their case fidelity. That favourable claim must be checked rather than treated as true by definition. A fair process may legitimately exclude unreliable, privileged or legally irrelevant information. Standing concerns who may invoke jurisdiction and is not a sensor's sensitivity. The purpose of adjudication, access to evidence, legally admissible use of evidence, attention and interpretation, jurisdiction, remedial authority and enforcement require separate assessments. A correct legal decision may be followed by adverse aggregate outcomes for which a legislature or regulator, rather than the court, has responsibility.

The original identified market structure, innovation, consumer welfare and international competitiveness in competition policy; atmospheric physics, energy economics, intergenerational interests and international coordination in climate governance; and the behaviour of state actors and long-run democratic effects in constitutional law. These are possible domains for evidence gathering, not a mathematical proof of excluded dimensions. Their relevance depends on the precise legal question and authorized task. The inquiry is whether material evidence or feasible responses are missing, whether the omission matters, and which institution has the responsibility and ability to address it.

### 1.4 Resolution Lock‑In — A Cross‑Series Concept

The original proposed that the architecture enabling success at one resolution might constrain adaptation to a new one. Departmental structures, tenure and disciplinary journals in universities, and payment systems, records and dashboards in healthcare were offered as earlier analogies. These remain historical unverified leads, not established findings of the present report.

For central banks, the accepted R5 correction distinguishes monetary-policy adequacy from system-wide economic-governance adequacy: inflation targeting is an objective, not an observation channel, and distributional or climate evidence need not be absent merely because it has no direct policy role. For frontier AI, the accepted R4 correction treats the trap as a conditional feasibility hypothesis and distinguishes observation from authorized implementation. Neither establishes that a proposed institutional supplement is necessary. These accepted dependency corrections are not empirical authority; findings remain on HOLD.

Courts are an instructive possible instance because many constraints are legally constituted and protect valuable purposes. But the original's universal constitutional-prohibition and limiting-case claims are withdrawn. Some legal systems already use collective claims, referrals, constitutional review, expert input and remedies extending beyond parties; the extent and legal basis of such mechanisms are verification questions. Adaptation need not mean expanding the court's role: a complementary institution can address a different task. Test the hypothesis against successful adaptation, effective existing divisions of labour and legitimate non-expansion.

### 1.5 The Genuine Strengths

The original describes courts as retrospective stabilisers, protecting continuity and deliberation against impulsive power. This is a favourable historical proposition, not a comparative ranking or universal finding. Judicial independence can shield adjudication from electoral or executive pressure; precedent can support planning and equal treatment; a fair hearing and reasoned judgment can expose defects in evidence and arguments. Time for careful challenge may protect rights, but delay can itself threaten them. In criminal cases, family separation, eviction and other urgent matters, protection against irreversible delay is part of the task, not an optional efficiency gain.

Apply the same evidence standard to the positive and critical accounts. Distinguish success on assigned legal tasks from adequacy for newly proposed tasks, following R5's accepted IC-t treatment. Establish who assigned those tasks, under what law and when. A finding that a court is not authorized to regulate an entire market cannot by itself establish institutional failure. Equally, a narrow mandate does not excuse a failure to protect rights already within that mandate. The broader governance system may be adequate, or may need change elsewhere.

The goal is not automatically to make courts faster, turn them into regulators or replace fair adversarial testing with technocratic assessment. The retrospective function may be preserved while evidence or coordination improves; whether new forms are warranted depends on comparison with existing practices and modest reforms.

### 1.6 The Real Question

The original contrasts judicial restraint with judicial engagement. Restraint can protect constitutional limits, impartiality and the separation of powers; engagement can protect rights and constrain unlawful power where the law authorizes it. These are real principles, not proxies for indifference or virtue. Their appropriate balance varies with the legal system and question. An engaged or restrained court can possess relevant knowledge yet lack lawful means to act; neither stance establishes the quality of its evidence.

The original's reframing in §1.6 is a hypothesis about institutional design: a restrained court may still produce significant consequences it does not examine, while an engaged court may miss aggregate effects despite its willingness to act. Neither approach by itself determines what information is relevant, available or legitimately usable. Test both against the actual legal question, procedure and consequences, rather than treating judicial philosophy as the explanation in advance.

The research question is: under which conditions do judicial and complementary institutions lack task-relevant evidence, lawful procedures or effective responses, and what proportionate change, if any, can improve outcomes without compromising individual rights, independence and the rule of law? A research registry, a procedural change, a legislative amendment and a regulator's action address different bottlenecks. Compare them, including no new intervention.

### 1.7 Observational diagnosis protocol
Before case selection, specify jurisdiction, time, legal task, applicable law, legally relevant evidence, permissible remedies, rights floors, comparison baseline and outcomes. Use dated records, comparable case populations and rival explanations; distinguish missing records from absence. Do not demand confidential records or infer wrongdoing from refusal. Do not classify any claim as demonstrated from toy math. Each hypothesis may be weakened or rejected independently of all interventions.

- H0 — Task/authority fit: a specified legal task is inadequately served by current court or institutional system. Disconfirmers: court adequately performs its assigned role; existing regulator, legislature, ombudsman, law reform or appellate mechanism effectively addresses broader harm. Test legal responsibility and coordination, not simply existence of a social problem. Avoid assuming every adverse policy outcome is legally remediable.
- H1 — Evidence-to-response gap: relevant available evidence is missing, inadmissible, ignored, misinterpreted or unacted on, with distinguishable mechanisms. Trace actual record, submissions, procedural rulings, reasons, legal power and enforcement. Disconfirmers: relevant systemic evidence is already introduced and fairly tested, reasons reflect it, or exclusion is justified and outcome/task adequate. A legally correct judgment is not necessarily a failed response when it has adverse aggregate effects.
- H2 — Accumulation loop: define units (doctrine/jurisdiction/domain) and follow five links: (1) case decisions→accumulation without integration (disconfirmer: coordinated appellate synthesis), (2) accumulation→fragmentation (coherent distinctions or consolidation), (3) fragmentation→adverse systemic consequences (stable expectations or no identified effect), (4) consequences→legislative/regulatory intervention (ordinary remedies or no demonstrated need, independently motivated policy change), (5) intervention→renewed fragmentation (durable clarification/reduced conflicts). Also test first stage's claim of high case fidelity and repeat cycle. Predeclare candidate indicators: circuit/chamber splits, inconsistency on matched facts, outcome forecastability and time to clarification; verify existing integration mechanisms (certiorari or referral to resolve splits, en banc review, Restatements) as reviewer-supplied leads, not universal practices. Establish temporal order, causation, competing mechanisms (new facts, technological change, political shifts), distinguish legal disagreement from incoherence. Crises and published judgments more visible than absorbed disputes and successful quiet corrections; missing evidence not confirmation.
- H3 — Selection and missingness: nonfiling, filing, standing, settlement, sealing, publication, appeal and private ADR (including arbitration, a reviewer-supplied lead absent from the original) can systematically bias the accessible population. Compare stage-specific denominators, case types, records and lawful sources; W4 bounds and sensitivity where the eligible dispute frame is defensible; if nonfiling leaves frame unknown, report it rather than claim population identification. W5 bilateral bargaining, exit routes and selection changes. Disconfirmers: accessible data give representative or adequately bounded conclusions for the task; alternative sources fill the relevant gap; no association with predicted selection after credible adjustment. Do not infer confidentiality, merit, concealment or counterfactual precedent from settlement alone. Include access-to-justice effects of data policies as separate intervention outcomes.
- H4 — Delay, processing, jurisdiction and remedies: test separately strategic resource advantage vs justified time/congestion; input overload vs usable evidence and review tools; cross-border enforcement gaps vs existing coordination; available remedies vs implementation. Disconfirmers: matched outcomes show no predicted resource gradient; capacity keeps pace without reduced accuracy; effective recognized/enforced remedies or appropriate alternative owners address specified harms. No actor motive inferred from latency or forum choice alone. Independently assess applicable rights and serious or irreversible delay harms (detention, custody, eviction, removal, continuing injury), interim relief, priority listing and proportionate deadlines. No offset by aggregate efficiency or monetary gains. Separate status-quo advantage from resource exhaustion.
- H5 — Adversarial Epistemology/closure: test whether independent, relevant and legally admissible challenge is systematically deflected without substantive justification; professional incentives may contribute, but genuine procedural objections, lack of relevance or validity, limited jurisdiction, and resource constraints are rivals. Disconfirmers: meaningful challenge leads to justified revision or reasoned rejection, professional groups support evidence-based correction, alternative procedural cultures do not differ as predicted. No sincere belief, adverse judgment, nonadoption or rejection of Registry counts as confirmation by itself. Evaluate durability/legitimacy pathways and communication risks symmetrically.
