## 5. A Concrete First Step: The Systemic Effects Registry

### 5.1 The Logic of the First Step

The Registry targets a possible evidence deficit: the information available to study the effects of accumulated doctrine may omit relevant outcomes, or existing studies may be inaccessible or insufficiently scrutinized. The first step is bounded because it can be tested as a research function independently of whether the original's wider reform programme is justified. It is not the demonstrated master mechanism of the Adjudication–Governance Variety Gap. If the bottleneck instead lies in authority, enforcement, access, or justified legal exclusion, new research may not improve the outcome. Compare existing court research, law reform, regulators, legislative analysis, targeted procedural submissions and no additional intervention. Modest reforms may be sufficient.

Public research does not by itself change what courts are required to do or see. The original proposed a mandatory case-record provision; this is a distinct, legally consequential option, not an observation-only measure. Establish competence and safeguards before adopting it. A useful Registry may remain outside the adjudicative record entirely. An unsuccessful pilot may test the intervention claim but does not by itself confirm or refute H0–H5.

### 5.2 The Systemic Effects Registry

The original proposed a formally maintained, publicly accessible database of assessments of the consequences of major doctrinal decisions, updated on a defined schedule. There are two original ownership options: a network of independent research institutions (§3.4) and a statutory body (§3.8 and this chapter). Compare them rather than silently select one. Each would require a transparent research remit, lawful data governance and plural independent research. Establish the actual knowledge gap and relevant task in each domain, and predeclare coverage, deadlines, comparison cases and evaluation criteria. The Registry cannot promise a representative census of all disputes or a causal estimate from observation alone.

For each doctrine, retain the five original assessment questions:

1. What are the aggregate effects across the class of cases in which the doctrine has been applied? Define that class, denominators, missing cases and uncertainty.
2. How have regulated actors responded behaviourally, including compliance, avoidance or adaptation? Distinguish conduct from inferred intent and consider other causes.
3. What are the distributional consequences: who benefits and who bears costs? Specify outcomes and counterfactuals, and do not trade individual rights for aggregate gains.
4. What are the effects on the wider domain, such as market structure, regulatory capacity, institutional legitimacy or democratic accountability? Distinguish associations, causal claims, legal holdings and normative assessment.
5. What trajectory is observed, and are the doctrine's legally defined purposes being served or are unintended effects emerging? Specify the horizon and comparison, and identify which institution has authority to act.

Research teams could combine legal scholars with economists, data scientists, sociologists and domain specialists. Publish methods, assumptions, limits, selection frames, versions, correction records and proportionate aggregate findings. Independent review and public comment can improve scrutiny but do not guarantee validity. Where evidence is missing, report uncertainty. Do not confuse policy recommendations with description or causal findings. Research methods and assessment outputs must remain genuinely plural; no single authoritative statutory assessment should be mandatory across all cases.

The original §3.8 identifies three candidate pilot domains, while §5.2 adds a fourth. Compare the three- and four-domain alternatives, rather than presume the domains with the widest demonstrated gap have already been identified:

- **Digital-platform antitrust:** research the effects of the consumer welfare standard and relevant doctrinal alternatives on competition, innovation, market structure and consumers. Establish actual legal scope and selection of cases.
- **Environmental administrative law:** investigate the effects of major questions, nondelegation and evolving judicial review standards on regulatory capacity and relevant outcomes. Distinguish legal constraints from empirical effectiveness.
- **Campaign finance:** investigate *Citizens United* and related doctrines, electoral competition, political equality and legitimacy. Verify actual holdings, attribution and other influences.
- **Plea bargaining:** investigate aggregate administration of criminal justice, selection, disparities, defence rights and relevant outcomes. Default to aggregate research with no use in individual cases. Any departure requires separate competent authorization and criminal-procedure and defence-rights review; no aggregate inference of individual guilt or pressure on defendants.

These named cases and legal characterizations are manuscript leads, unverified. Subsequent cycles might be justified by evidence of benefit and a new specified gap, not merely by improved institutional credibility or demand for expansion. Stop when the original scope is sufficient, when additional research is not proportionate, or when protections cannot be met.

### 5.3 Core Design Features

**Independence and governance.** The original envisaged a board of judges, academic researchers, practising lawyers and stakeholders (consumer organizations, civil society, industry associations and regulatory agencies), appointed for fixed non-renewable terms, choosing a director and approving the agenda. Multidisciplinary staff would conduct or commission assessments. These are options, not evidence of representation or impartiality. Compare independent appointments by competent institutions, separation between agenda-setting and findings, conflict disclosure, recusals, limits on involvement of active judges and litigants, transparent budgets and independent oversight. Sitting judges' participation could compromise impartiality or create recusal burdens. A stakeholder voice does not authorize representation of every affected person. Repeat players and funders must not be able to select a favourable assessment or suppress a result.

**Statutory and funding design.** The original body corporate would hire staff, contract for research and publish without prior departmental approval, funded by public appropriation and philanthropic support with protection against budgetary retaliation. Compare statutory independence, pooled/core funding, an independently overseen appropriation, donor firewalls and independently assigned research. Evaluate residual capture risk, continuity, competence, cost, accountability, lawful enforceability and plural research. The network alternative can have conflicts and coordination burdens too. Disclosure alone does not cure structural conflicts. No presumed legal power or immunity follows from the proposed institutional form.

**Data access and publication.** The original proposed court records, anonymised and aggregated settlement data, regulatory filings and economic statistics. Statutory access is not currently established and may be inappropriate. Identify lawful data owners, purpose, minimum fields, retention, security, access controls, privilege, commercial sensitivity, safety, redress and deletion or withdrawal where applicable. Compare aggregated, sampled, federated and no-new-data approaches. Anonymisation may fail through linkage; do not publish protected material or compel waiver. Measure effects on filing, settlement, private ADR and willingness to seek remedies. Where a defensible external eligible-dispute frame exists, report W4 bounds or justified sensitivity analyses; where unknown, report unknown coverage. Distinguish a public report from the underlying data. Access to protected information for research does not establish admissibility or a right to public disclosure.

**Procedural alternatives and the original mandatory judicial notice provision.** A public research-only design leaves assessments outside the adjudicative record. A second design makes relevant assessments available through ordinary or appropriately authorized evidence and argument rules. A third, corresponding to the original proposal, would require formal incorporation or reasoned acknowledgment when a court is asked to extend, limit or overrule an assessed doctrine. The third may require legislation or rule change and may be unlawful in some systems. Public availability, evidence offered by parties, legal argument, admissibility, judicial notice and a reason-giving duty are distinct. Contested empirical findings must not become indisputable adjudicative facts. Verify judicial authority, notice rules and permissible reasons requirements for each jurisdiction; Claude's v0.1 audit review supplied FRE 201 and 706, legislative facts and Brandeis briefs as unverified US leads, not universal procedures.

For any authorized case-specific use, require advance notice of the version relied on, a reasonable preparation cutoff, access to lawful underlying data and methods, the opportunity to examine authors where findings are relied upon, meaningful rebuttal and supplementation, independent expert assistance, time and resources, judicial discretion over relevance, admissibility and weight, and applicable review and appeal. No ex parte contact. Establish how postdecision revisions are handled without silently changing the record or reopening final judgments. Where fair challenge would require access to protected material that cannot lawfully be supplied, exclude reliance rather than compel disclosure. Privacy, privilege and defence rights prevail. Acknowledgment does not mean agreement, and a justified refusal to rely on a report is not capture. Test whether the new challenge process increases delay or advantages repeat players even after assistance; no case-use model is justified if procedural fairness cannot be met.

### 5.4 Selection Criteria: Why This Intervention?

The original gave three reasons for selecting the Registry. First, it could make a previously excluded dimension visible without dictating the response. This is a candidate benefit only if an actual knowledge deficit is identified and research fills it more reliably and proportionately than alternatives. Evidence may already exist; the relevant problem may be attention, authority, enforcement or a justified legal boundary. The correction does not assume the Registry targets a primary cause.

Second, the original argued that an independent statutory research body is a familiar bounded institutional form that could supplement rather than replace adversarial process. Verify the legal and empirical claims about existing forms, actual costs, independence, appointments and statutory powers; case incorporation and mandatory reasons are separate legal changes. Compare a network, existing law reform and research-only design. No claim that constitutional amendment is unnecessary or that objections can be bypassed. Transparency about reform aims is required.

Third, the original anticipated feedback: public research could give affected populations, legislators and civil society more reliable evidence, and successful methods could become a precedent for further inquiry. That is an institutional-learning hypothesis, not a mandate for political pressure on judges. Scope creep, distorted incentives and publicity affecting pending matters are rivals. Further use requires a separate demonstrated gap, incremental benefit, lawful authority, independent rights protection and comparison of alternatives. Stop when warranted. The Monetary Policy Distributional Impact Assessment is an analogous historical proposal under the accepted R5 correction, not a proven equivalent; healthcare and university audits remain uncorrected historical leads.

### 5.5 How to Measure Success

Predeclare a comparator and stopping criteria. Compare the Registry against existing court research and law reform, public research without case-record use, targeted expert or amicus submissions where lawful, regulator and legislative review, and no new intervention. Evaluate the owner and pilot scope separately. Define the task and legal rights floors before setting metrics. Measure net effects for different parties, including one-shot litigants, repeat players, defendants, nonparties and people who never file.

The original's metrics—establishment, funding, data access, timely assessment completion, publication, court engagement, acknowledgment, evolution of doctrine, legislative citations and diffusion to other jurisdictions—are potentially useful implementation or process indicators. They do not measure justice or determine success. Court engagement can be appropriate or inappropriate; reasoned disagreement is not capture; doctrinal change is neither necessary nor sufficient evidence of improvement. Further reform is not the ultimate metric.

Independent evaluation should investigate research reliability, scope, uncertainty, relevant decision quality as legally defined, actual enforcement and downstream outcomes, costs and delay, access to justice, procedural equality, privacy and confidentiality, conflicts, recusal and accountability. Evaluate the Registry's own selection and correlated errors, the availability of genuinely independent alternative assessments and the net costs of contesting findings. Cases where protected data preclude fair rebuttal are excluded from reliance. Rights floors are independent of all proxy gains. Publish null or adverse results, including differential access effects, and correct methods and estimates when warranted. Legitimate stopping, narrowing or nonadoption is available. Adoption, rejection or null results do not by themselves test H0–H5.

### 5.6 The Honest Acknowledgment

The original anticipated obstacles: professional commitments to adversarial procedure, limited engagement, pro forma acknowledgment, disputed data access, funding and resistance from groups with material interests. These are possibilities, not predictions of motive or capture. A judge may properly reject a finding, a party may lawfully withhold privileged information, or an authorized institution may determine that the proposal is inappropriate. Evaluate reasons and outcomes, including genuine professional support and effective existing alternatives.

The wager is that a proportionate independent research mechanism might help institutions and the public understand the consequences of doctrine. It may fail because it adds costs, reproduces selection, concentrates authority, compromises independence, or does not address the relevant bottleneck. The default outcome is unknown, not inevitable accumulating crisis. A useful research-only pilot may be all that is warranted; a pilot that cannot meet rights and legal constraints must not proceed. Appropriate participation of affected litigants, defendants and parties to confidential settlements is required before pilot design and implementation; none has been established here. No legal authorization or human review is claimed.
