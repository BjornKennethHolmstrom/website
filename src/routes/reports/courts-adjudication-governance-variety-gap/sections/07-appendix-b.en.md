## Appendix B: International Analogues and Precedents

The original offers six analogues as possible sources of design knowledge. Each is an unverified dated research lead, not an established finding or proof that the proposed architecture is necessary, effective or transferable. Verify the precise institutional form, actual competence, outcomes and limits against primary sources and relevant human review.

### B.1 The United Kingdom: Law Reform Commissions and Systemic Doctrine Review

The original describes the Law Commission of England and Wales (establishment dated to 1965) as a statutory law-reform body using inquiries, consultation, empirical research and draft legislation. Verify the date, mandate, independence, funding, actual scope of common-law and statutory review and effect of recommendations. Compare a Registry with existing work rather than assume the proposed review function is novel. A law reform report does not thereby become admissible evidence or a binding instruction to a court.

### B.2 Australia: Systematic Law Reform and Specialist Courts

The original identifies the Australian Law Reform Commission, federal and state law reform, family and federal specialist arrangements, and the NSW Land and Environment Court. Verify their institutional forms, specific legal powers, procedures, multidisciplinary expertise, remedies and outcomes. Specialized adjudication may provide useful domain knowledge or create duplication and forum conflicts. Compare actual tasks and safeguards; no proof of the proposed tribunal design follows.

### B.3 The United States: Multidistrict Litigation as Systemic Aggregation

The original identifies federal MDL as a way to coordinate cases with common factual questions for pretrial proceedings, using pharmaceutical liability, product defects and environmental contamination as illustrative domains. Verify the legal basis, transfer and remand rules, scope of common discovery and evidentiary rulings, party protections, settlement and actual merits authority. Coordination of filed cases is not automatically class representation or population identification. Test costs, access, delay and fairness.

### B.4 The European Union: Preliminary Reference Procedure as Cross-Scale Coordination

The original refers to Article 267 TFEU and national courts' references to the Court of Justice of the European Union. Verify referral duties and discretion, scope, binding effects and subsequent implementation. Such a mechanism may support coherent interpretation without expanding every national court's task. It is not a Registry and does not establish the adequacy of other multi-scale designs.

### B.5 Ireland: Citizens' Assemblies on Constitutional Questions

The original names assemblies concerning marriage equality, abortion and climate. Verify the actual topics, dates, selection, testimony, recommendations and subsequent referendum or legislative routes separately. Deliberation may inform political choices, but no direct mandate to courts or override of rights follows. Compare the Digital Rights Assembly proposal against existing consultation and representative mechanisms, including minority protections and nonadoption.

### B.6 The International Criminal Court: Complementarity as Coordination Mechanism

The original names the Rome Statute's complementarity principle and the question of genuine domestic proceedings. Verify treaty jurisdiction, admissibility prerequisites and actual decisions separately. The division between national and international responsibility is a potential coordination analogue; it is not an equivalent civil research registry or general authorization for cross-border adjudication. Preserve defence rights, legal authority and jurisdiction-specific differences.
