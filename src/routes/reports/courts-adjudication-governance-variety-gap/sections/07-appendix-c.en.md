## Appendix C: The Governance as Engineering Connection

### C.1 The Architectural Foundation

This report draws on the Governance as Engineering series, which models specified governance tasks using concepts from control and information theory. The accepted corrections to Papers VI, VIII and XX, capstone and subsidiarity delimit the formal dependencies; the earlier I–V bridge is historical and model-dependent, not re-audited here. The objective-as-observation equivalence, universal legibility compression, numerical dimension inference and universal single-scale necessity are withdrawn. No quantitative gap is estimated for a court. The following seven primitives and nine witnesses distinguish analogies and conditional mathematics from empirical and legal claims.

### C.2 The Seven Primitives

Nodes: the court (trial, appellate or constitutional) or complementary institution. State: the relevant facts and the governance domain, such as market structure or a regulatory framework. Flows: evidence, decisions, behavioural responses, later interpretations and legislative or regulatory responses. Latency: filing, observation, hearing, remedy and implementation delays, including delay harms. Constraints: legal standing, jurisdiction, evidence, authority, remedies, procedural and substantive rights. Feedback loops: precedent, appeal, legislative revision and other authorized correction. Signal fidelity: the accuracy of evidence relative to a specified question, not a measure of legal authority or fairness. These are analogies, not identified state variables or measured signals.

### C.3 Ashby's Law of Requisite Variety and conditional formal witnesses W1–W9

The report's variety-gap vocabulary draws on Ashby's Law of Requisite Variety (original C.3). In specified regulatory models, bringing disturbances into a task-defined acceptable set requires adequate distinguishable responses to the disturbances that matter for that task. W1 gives a conditional observation criterion; W2 distinguishes observation from lawful response and implementation, and the Paper XX action-compatible cover supplies a necessary response-category bound, not a sufficient condition. W3 illustrates why timing and the assumptions about dynamics and observation also matter. The original analogy remains useful: the court's decisional framework (evidence rules, adversarial procedure and precedent) plays the role of a controller, and governance challenges such as market concentration, environmental degradation, surveillance and democratic erosion play the role of disturbances. This analogy does not assign all such tasks to courts. Relevant objectives, acceptable outcomes, disturbance sets, information, admissible actions, rights and legitimate institutional ownership must be specified before assessing adequacy. No comparison of actual institutional dimensionalities, constitutional unobservability, universal stability condition or universal need for architectural expansion follows. The law's historical formulation and attribution are not independently checked here.

Reading note: the delayed-trigger variant, the bilateral settlement examples and the arbitration lead described as reviewer-supplied in the witnesses below originate in Claude's v0.1 audit review; they are stipulated illustrations or unverified research leads, not original manuscript claims. W9's reference to subsidiarity W5 concerns agreement between evaluators, not the hazard-conditioned joint miss event or a court's decision rule. The accepted witness text is retained below for traceability.

These are deliberately small counterexamples and design distinctions, not fitted legal-system models, legal propositions or empirical findings. VI, VIII, XX, capstone and subsidiarity are accepted corrections; the I–V bridge is historical/model-dependent, not a re-audit of those papers. The numbers below are stipulated. No substantive right is treated as a compensable welfare loss.

#### W1 — Task-relative observation (VI)
In a static noiseless linear model, disturbances d∈D (subspace), observation Cd, acceptable residual subspace L and unrestricted compensating actions −D, exact adequacy iff K=D∩ker C⊆L. Necessity: indistinguishable disturbances need a common action; sufficiency uses all −D. Hidden gap dim K−dim(K∩L), at least max(0,dim D−dim(D∩L)−rank(C|D)); this lower bound is not sufficient. In R² with L=span(e2), observing x1 or x2 gives equal rank but gaps 0 and 1. In R³ with L=span(e2,e3), observing x1 gives gap 0 while observing x2,x3 gives gap 1. The number of legal categories, litigants, evidentiary filters or social outcomes is not this rank. A court's lawful purpose is not its observation map. No institutional gap measured.

#### W2 — Evidence, jurisdiction, lawful remedy, implementation (VI/XX/capstone/subsidiarity)
For a stipulated task `d∈{0,1}`, y=d and acceptable action a=d, a body with authority and effective implementation can act; a body authorized only a=0 cannot when d=1, despite the same y. A third body with authority but failed implementation also fails. For a different legitimate task, a=0 may suffice. An authorized other institution could handle d=1 without expanding the court's mandate. Specify actual division, coordination and accountability. XX: number of distinguishable response categories/actions must be at least the minimum action-compatible cover where each observation class must share an acceptable action; necessary not sufficient. A wrong action can follow perfectly accurate evidence. No assumption that actual legal adjudication is binary.

#### W3 — Event-driven and multi-scale adequacy (I–II bridge)
Two independent disturbances change on different schedules. In a stipulated static per-step model, one controller samples at the faster rate, receives both current components and can immediately apply u_i=−d_i. Residuals vanish on every step. Separate levels with a missing actuator or missed deadlines can fail. Event-triggered observation can also be sufficient for a task: `d∈{0,1}`, signal fires exactly when d changes and reports the new value; with known initial state, reliable instantaneous delivery and piecewise-constant d, state is known between events. Remove reliable triggers and adequacy may fail. Delayed variant (reviewer-supplied): over discrete steps 1–10, changes at steps 3 and 7, reliable delivery exactly two steps later and known initial state, the held estimate is wrong at steps 3,4,7,8 (4/10), assuming each change flips the binary state. Filing triggers are endogenous to resources, standing and expected outcomes; W4/W6. Neither illustrates an actual court or proves dynamic stability. Case filings are not guaranteed to be complete triggers; specify deadlines, legal activation conditions, alternative institutions and emergency measures. No universal fractal or continuous-monitoring necessity follows.

#### W4 — Selected cases and identification
Suppose 100 eligible disputes exist, 20 have observed binary outcome Y and 12 of those have Y=1. Without assumptions on the other 80, the population fraction lies in [12/100,(12+80)/100]=[.12,.92]. Two populations with identical observed records can yield opposite majority conclusions. The 60% observed fraction is not a population estimate absent a selection model. The 100-dispute frame is stipulated; nonfiling may make that frame unknown. Bounds require a defensible external frame, or explicit assumptions about coverage; do not substitute filed cases for all eligible disputes. A filing rate, settlement rate, publication rate and observed outcome rate have different denominators; dispositions other than settlement are possible. Missing records neither prove harm nor prove absence of harm. Even complete outcomes do not identify a causal effect of doctrine without counterfactual assumptions. Require bounds, ascertainment and sensitivity, legal access; no forced disclosure to solve missingness.

#### W5 — Settlement and privacy trade-off (stipulated)
For a simplified party, trial value net of cost/risk is 3 and confidential settlement net value is 5. If mandated disclosure reduces the latter to 2, trial becomes preferred; if reporting is safely aggregated and costs .5, settlement still yields 4.5. This is an incentive illustration, not a prediction or a justification for breaching a privilege. Selection and willingness to seek remedies can change with the reporting rule. Public-interest information benefit must be tested separately from party outcomes and non-negotiable privacy/fairness constraints. A settlement need not erase evidence; a judgment need not create published precedent. No universal rate or direction follows.

Bilateral extension (two reviewer-supplied stipulated witnesses): plaintiff trial payoff 3, confidentiality benefit 1; defendant trial cost c, confidentiality benefit 2. With transfer S from defendant to plaintiff and weak voluntary participation, confidential settlement requires S+1≥3 and S−2≤c, hence S∈[2,c+2]. Under disclosure both benefits vanish, S∈[3,c]. For c=6, intervals [2,8] and [3,6] both nonempty; bargaining set changes, not a determined price. For c=5/2, intervals [2,9/2] and [3,5/2] (empty) show settlement feasibility lost. No equilibrium price or actual effect follows; trial, abandonment, nonfiling and other private ADR including arbitration are possible exits (arbitration is reviewer-supplied, absent from original). Rules may change the selection frame itself. Legally protected confidentiality, privilege, safety and victim identity are noncompensable constraints, outside this payoff calculation.

#### W6 — Latency and heterogeneous burdens
At a stipulated net cost of 2 per period, a litigant with resources 3 can fund one full period, while another with 9 can fund four. A two-period proceeding exhausts the first but not the second (abstract no-credit budget). Conversely a one-period deadline can exclude evidence arriving at period two; allowing the second period can make both sides' evidence available if resources and rights permit. Time alone does not identify strategic intent or fairness; compare funding, legal aid, urgent relief, case complexity, congestion and proportional deadlines. Distinguish procedural delay from observation delay, remedy implementation and long-run effects. Separate benefit of continuing the status quo from exhausting an opponent’s resources; neither establishes intent. Independent rights constraints: detention/liberty, custody/family separation, eviction, removal and continuing injury may make delay itself a serious or irreversible harm, regardless of resources. A stylized proceeding requiring two periods but subject to an independently justified one-period maximum without interim protection is infeasible even for a litigant with unlimited funds. Actual limits depend on law and facts; evaluate interim relief, priority listing, proportionate deadlines and fair hearing independently, not as monetized offsets or aggregate gains.

#### W7 — Information volume, proxy and rights (XX)
With a fixed review capacity of 2 documents, a queue [relevant A,relevant B] reveals both; placing two irrelevant documents first and processing only the first two reveals neither. With an accurate relevance filter the same larger queue can yield both. This is a processing/ordering example, not a general SNR or bandwidth theorem. Scalar codes can represent four binary states via 2x+y; omission may be irrelevant to the specified task. XX lottery witness: `X={(0,0),(0,1),(2,0),(2,1)}`, metric M=a, target T=a+b. Strict deterministic M improvements improve T, but ties differ. Lottery 1/4 on (2,0), 3/4 on (0,0) gives E[M]=E[T]=1/2 versus (0,1) giving M=0,T=1. Do not optimize judicial throughput as justice. No universal legibility compression principle; AWGN SNR=1 gives .5 bit/use only under scalar Gaussian assumptions, not a universal threshold. Rights constraints must be independently satisfied, not traded away for a better proxy.

#### W8 — Conditional interactions and doctrinal accumulation
For sequential retentions r_i=1−f_i∈[0,1], combined loss 1−∏r_i≤∑f_i. Two half-retentions lose 3/4≤1; remaining 1/4 satisfies a 1/5 criterion but fails 1/3. Common-baseline outcomes 10,12,13,14 imply interaction −1; joint 17 implies +2. Neither supports universal reinforcing mechanisms. A toy doctrine graph with two apparently conflicting rules can be integrated by a later authoritative clarification; number of cases increases while unresolved conflicts decrease from 1 to 0. No claim that actual courts did so. H2 tests integration as a rival to monotone fragmentation.

#### W9 — Correlated assessment errors and contestability
For two binary hazard-conditioned misses with equal marginal p and feasible correlation ρ, joint miss J=p²+ρp(1−p), max(0,2p−1)≤J≤p. Under any-flag escalation independent p=.1 gives .01; shared p=.05 and ρ=1 gives .05; threshold ρ>3/19; minimum correlation at p=.05 is −1/19. Both-must-flag miss is 2p−J. These are detection rules, not court decision rules; false alarms and response require separate tests. Distinguish subsidiarity W5 agreement events. Shared Registry data/model assumptions may create correlated errors across courts and experts, including common benchmark gaming; preserve plural methods, independent rebuttal, corrections and provenance. Apply reflexively to model-family review; no measured correlations or claim of independent human peer review.

### C.4 The Variety Gap and Resolution Lock-In

W1 concerns a static noiseless linear model with explicit subspaces and unrestricted actions; it does not establish constitutional unobservability. W2 distinguishes evidence from authority and implementation, and an institution's assigned objective from the observation map. W3 concerns particular static per-step models, not universal dynamic stability. W4–W9 identify counterexamples and conditional risks, not measured causal effects. Resolution Lock-In remains a testable specialization and adaptation hypothesis, not a theorem that the court cannot operate at any other scale. No new index or composite ranking is introduced. Individual rights are independent feasibility constraints, not aggregate losses.
