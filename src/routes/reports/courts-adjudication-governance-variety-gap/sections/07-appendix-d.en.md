## Appendix D: Anticipated Objections

### D.1 "The adversarial process is the foundation of the rule of law. Systemic evidence mechanisms would compromise judicial neutrality."

The original recognizes the value of adversarial testing. The objection may be correct for particular designs: an independent report can prejudice the arbiter or create a practically unchallengeable expert record. A research-only Registry is distinct from case incorporation; the latter requires lawful relevance, prior notice, access to methods and lawful underlying data, examination of authors where relied upon, rebuttal, independent assistance, adequate time, judicial discretion, no ex parte contact and review. If protected information prevents fair challenge, exclude reliance. In criminal proceedings the plea-bargaining pilot is aggregate research only by default; any departure needs separate authorization and defence-rights review. Preserve the possibility of justified rejection. Compare outcomes against existing procedures rather than assume supplementation is necessary.

### D.2 "Courts should interpret the law, not make policy. The Systemic Effects Registry would transform courts into regulators."

Courts' decisions may have policy consequences. The original points to antitrust, administrative and constitutional doctrine, but a governance consequence is not legislative authority. Distinguish individual relief, precedent, general binding effects and structural remedies by jurisdiction. A court may be obliged to apply the law without weighing aggregate welfare. Public research may support the legislature or regulator rather than the court. Any new role requires lawful assignment, rights and review; neither an assessment nor an assembly can confer authority. Test whether existing divisions work before proposing a change.

### D.3 "The Epistemic Black Hole is a feature of settlement, not a bug. Settlement resolves disputes efficiently and reduces the burden on the court system."

Settlement can protect privacy and provide timely, flexible relief. It may also produce missing or selective records. The original's universal rate and intentional signal-destruction claim are withdrawn. W4 bounds require an eligible-population frame, and W5 shows how a rule may alter bilateral feasibility and filing or private ADR choices. A lawful aggregate research mechanism might narrow uncertainty but may also chill legitimate settlements, change who seeks relief or be unable to protect sensitive information. No forced disclosure or waiver. Compare no-data alternatives, evaluate access and delay, and respect independent rights and confidentiality protections. A settlement may itself create accessible evidence; the reviewer-supplied archive leads require verification.

### D.4 "Specialised systemic tribunals would fragment the legal system, creating inconsistent doctrines and forum shopping."

This is a serious rival, not a problem solved by adding another specialist layer. Existing specialist institutions differ from regulators and ordinary courts; verify legal status, competence and effects. Clear lawful task allocation, conflict resolution, review, remedies, rights, enforcement and coordination are necessary. Compare existing arrangements and modest reform. A new tribunal can worsen fragmentation or concentration of power; reject it where the evidence does not justify its incremental benefit or rights cannot be preserved.

### D.5 "This analysis is interesting, but it will never be implemented. Adversarial Epistemology is too strong."

Professional objections, material incentives, costs, legal restrictions and justified refusal are real alternatives. The default outcome is unknown. H5 tests actual challenge and reasons, not adoption. The Registry pilot must be evaluated independently; null or adverse findings justify revision or stopping rather than confirming the diagnosis. Further use requires incremental benefit, lawful authority and rights. The English dependency correction does not establish any case-specific factual or legal finding. Before release of institution-specific findings, binding human review and primary-source verification are required (comparative, constitutional and procedural law, empirical legal studies, criminal procedure and defence rights, data protection/privacy, judicial ethics and recusal, with disclosed conflicts). AI review is supplementary only. Appropriate affected-public participation is not established and is required before pilot design. All findings remain on HOLD.
