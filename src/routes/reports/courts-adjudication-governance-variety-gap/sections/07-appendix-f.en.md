## Appendix F: Key Concepts and Abbreviations

Terms retained from the original; the definitions below are conditional diagnoses, analogies or proposed designs, not established findings or legal rules.

**Adjudication–Governance Variety Gap:** A possible task-relative mismatch between relevant evidence, lawful authority and feasible response and the requirements of a specified legal or governance task; not a numerical dimension count.

**Adversarial Epistemology:** The hypothesis that professional reliance on contested presentations may sometimes inhibit justified alternative evidence channels; test against legitimate procedural objections and independent correction.

**Case‑by‑Case–Doctrinal Fragmentation–Systemic Blindness–Legislative Intervention Loop:** A proposed sequence from decisions through unresolved accumulation, fragmentation, adverse effects, intervention and renewed fragmentation; each link is independently testable, including rival integration and durable clarification.

**Epistemic Black Hole:** Potential selection and missingness across disputes, filing, settlement, confidentiality, sealing, publication and private resolution. No universal rate, concealment or counterfactual precedent follows.

**Interrupt‑driven governance architecture:** An event-triggered activation analogy, not an inference of universal ignorance. Adequacy depends on triggers, delivery, legal task, deadlines and other institutions.

**Myth of the Neutral Arbiter:** A metaphor for a possible conflation of impartiality with a particular evidentiary arrangement, not a claim about judges' motives or a rejection of neutrality.

**Resolution Lock‑In:** Conditional specialization and adaptation hypothesis. Earlier cross-series leads are not established; the R4 and R5 accepted corrections distinguish strategic feasibility, evidence, objectives, authority and action.

**Specialised systemic tribunals:** Candidate lawful specialist institutions for defined tasks, subject to competence, independence, rights, review, coordination and comparison with existing arrangements.

**Systemic Effects Registry:** Candidate independent research mechanism to assess doctrinal consequences; public research, evidence use, notice and mandatory reasons are distinct, separately authorized options, with selection, privacy and fair-challenge constraints.

**Weaponised latency:** Hypothesis of strategic advantage through duration or resource exhaustion, distinct from warranted time and congestion. Serious delay harms require independent rights protection.

**Westphalian Boundary Gap:** Potential mismatch of cross-border tasks and jurisdiction, applicable law or effective enforcement; not a universal territorial limit or inference of intent.
