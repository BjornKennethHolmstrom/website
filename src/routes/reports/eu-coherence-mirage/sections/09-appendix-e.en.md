### Appendix E: About this report and how it was made

#### How it was made

This report was generated by AI language models (Claude, ChatGPT, DeepSeek) from prompts by Björn Kenneth Holmström. Several models were prompted in parallel to approach the EU's situation from different angles, applying the systems-thinking framework he had set out. He chose the topic, posed the questions and decided to publish the result, but wrote little of the text himself. The voice of the report is the models', not his.

The report has not been systematically fact-checked. Statements about EU institutions, treaties, legal procedures, events, figures and sources may be inaccurate or invented, and should be verified against primary sources before being relied on or cited. Like any work of synthesis, it is provisional. It makes no claim to finality. It claims only that the lens it offers merits testing against reality — and that the testing, in the end, is what matters most.

#### Perspective and limits

Neither the models nor the person who prompted them write from within the EU's institutional core. Björn Kenneth Holmström has studied engineering physics and has a long-standing personal interest in systems thinking, complexity and governance. He has no formal training or professional experience in EU affairs, EU law or political science, and has never worked in or with EU institutions.

That distance limits access to the granular, day-to-day texture of European policy-making — the unwritten norms, the informal power structures, the lived reality that no formal framework can capture. An outside lens can sometimes raise questions that insiders have learned not to hear, but it can just as easily miss what insiders know. The report does not claim insider knowledge. It offers a lens that may or may not prove useful to those who hold institutional positions and are searching for frameworks that make sense of what they are experiencing. Feedback, criticism and corrections are welcome.

The report refers to the **EU Subsidiarity Protocol**, a proposed specification for a subsidiarity routing mechanism intended to determine the appropriate governance level for specific policy domains. It is available on this website and was produced in the same way as this report. It has not been reviewed by EU practitioners or legal experts.

#### The Country Reports Series

This report was the fifth in the series of Country Reports for Systemic Change, all produced in the same way; later reports have since extended the series. The first examined Germany through the lens of an execution deficit. The second examined France through the lens of an integration deficit. The third examined Sweden through the lens of a feedback deficit. The fourth examined India through the lens of a synchronisation deficit. Together, the first five reports propose a diagnostic framework built around five dimensions of adaptive capacity: the ability to *decide*, *integrate*, *sense*, *synchronise*, and *cohere* — which the series suggests determine whether governance systems can meet the complexity of the 21st century. The series does not claim to be complete. It is meant as a foundation on which further analysis, deeper testing, and better design can be built.
