## Appendix A: Value Systems and Policy Mindsets — A Guide for the Finnish Context

### A Note on This Appendix

This appendix retains the original Spiral Dynamics-inspired interpretive vocabulary as an optional lens, not a validated measure or national stage assignment. The main report's task-specific timing hypothesis does not depend on it.

### A.1 The Basic Insight

Order, achievement, care and integrative thinking may illuminate different institutional priorities. The original treats each as a coherent response to conditions, not a personality type or party label. It says none is better in any absolute sense; strengths and blind spots depend on context. Governance, it argues, must integrate their legitimate concerns without capture by any one. Do not infer motives, hierarchy, national psychology or adequacy from these categories. Test actual institutional decisions and plural experiences; the framework itself is not a validated explanatory model here.

### A.2 The Value Systems in the Finnish Arena

The original four portraits are preserved as interpretive lenses: **Order and Stability (Blue)**, constitutional and rule-of-law institutions, procedural reliability and potential rigidity; **Achievement and Efficiency (Orange)**, innovation and knowledge economy, enterprise and possible narrow performance proxies; **Inclusion and Care (Green)**, welfare and social solidarity, protection and possible rigidity in defending existing programmes; **Integrative and Systemic (Yellow)**, anticipatory thinking, coordination and attention to functional fit. No institution or society is assigned a stage; each lens may highlight protective or problematic features depending on task. The portraits do not measure Finnish capacities or establish a need for velocity.

The historical Blue portrait cites low corruption, professional civil service, institutional memory and fairness; Orange cites Nokia-related startups, R&D, global orientation and technology as a possible substitute for institutional change; Green cites dignity, willingness to fund shared services and concerns about legacy programmes and pluralism; Yellow cites Sitra, cross-party foresight, basic income experimentation and the wellbeing-economy discourse, with the possible blind spots of detachment, intellectualism and political infeasibility. The claim that Finland has more Yellow capacity than nearly every other case, but a predominantly Blue–Orange–Green operational apparatus, is a historical interpretation, not a validated ranking or a psychological gap. The original links insufficiently integrated Blue to Stability Bias and institutional inertia; Orange to post-Nokia risk aversion and the Innovation Diffusion Gap in domestic uptake; and Green to the claimed Lutheran collective-responsibility shadow affecting pluralism and immigration and to Welfare State Rigidity limiting reallocation. These are unverified interpretive links, not established causes or judgments of people or religion. Verify specific institutional descriptions and favorable and adverse claims equally.

### A.3 Value-System Clash as an Interpretive Hypothesis

The original credits Blue with trustworthy legal scaffolding, Orange with innovation funding welfare, and Green with solidarity legitimating high taxation and welfare. It argues that procedural delay, Pilot Purgatory and rigid programme defence persist while Yellow foresight remains outside operational machinery. It proposes binding foresight to bring long-term consequences into Blue procedure, portfolio management to take Orange innovation from pilot to scale, and sunsetting to preserve Green’s commitments by retiring ineffective instruments. The constraint, it explicitly argues, is not failure of any single value system but absence of mechanisms allowing all three to evolve together at the pace external conditions demand. These are historical hypotheses; Chapter 3’s conditions, safeguards and alternatives, including retention, remain binding on the candidate proposals. This is an advocacy and design hypothesis, not a developmental diagnosis. Lawful procedures, innovation caution, social protection and valid disagreement can improve adequacy. Test task-specific costs and outcomes and the legitimacy of remedies with affected people.
