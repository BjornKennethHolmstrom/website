## Appendix C: The Governance as Engineering Connection

### C.1 Historical Mathematical Foundations and Scope

The original draws on the first five Governance as Engineering papers, which use feedback-control and information-theoretic models to discuss institutional design. Their historical model assumptions and derivations have not been audited here. The companion reports *Coordination Failure as Structural Condition* and *The Subsidiarity Deficit* (Appendix E, especially E.1, E.4 and E.5, of the first report; Appendix C, C.1–C.4, of the second), set out narrower mathematical examples: losing information irrelevant to a particular task need not impair that task; for deterministic controllers required to meet every specified case without residual inadequacy, the minimum number of actions needed to cover those cases gives a necessary lower bound on distinguishable controller states, not a guarantee that a particular observation channel distinguishes those cases or that the actions are authorized and feasible. A counterexample to an unrestricted institutional inference is not a refutation of an unspecified original model. Separate legitimate objectives, observation, modelling, decision and actual response. The five historical paper arguments below motivate testable questions, not a single mathematical theorem, a national score or a demonstrated formal extension.

### C.2 The Five Papers in Brief

**Paper I — Governance Stability Simulator.** The historical summary claims aggregation destroys spatial information and makes subsidiarity a structural requirement. For a sum task, observation of the sum can suffice even where individual coordinates cannot be recovered; for a coordinate task, the same observation can fail. Test task-relevant distinctions, actual authority and feasible responses. Municipal decentralisation or consolidation is not mathematically required. The original describes Finland’s municipal architecture as highly developed and attributes local-information loss to the Municipal Capacity Gradient and digital–physical asymmetry; that ranking is unverified and the proposed loss is a task-specific empirical hypothesis.

**Paper II — Fractality as Stability.** The original claims no single-scale controller can stabilize simultaneous fast, medium and slow disturbances, motivating shock absorbers. This unrestricted transfer is not established: in an additional illustrative scalar dynamic plant with known parameter, exact state observation and unrestricted action, a single feedback policy cancelling the state term leaves the next state equal to the bounded disturbance. Boundedness is not arbitrary task tolerance, and delays, constraints and uncertainty may defeat the policy. Historical delay and gain expressions remain model-dependent, not universal bounds. The original argues that municipal, regional, national and EU layers are not synchronized for velocity, proposing shock absorbers and binding foresight as a missing fast-response layer. Test these hypotheses against actual timescales, deadlines and standing preparations; no missing layer is proved.

**Paper III — Observability–Democracy.** The original claims preferences cannot be reliably transmitted through more than two or three layers, motivating deliberation. Chain depth alone cannot determine information loss; a chain of identity channels is an illustration of retained information, and in the stipulated scalar Gaussian case signal-to-noise ratio one gives half a bit, not a universal cutoff (Coordination Failure as Structural Condition, E.4; The Subsidiarity Deficit, C.3). Actual errors may be correlated, nonresponse selective and authority absent. The original proposes citizens' assemblies and experiment portfolios with public participation to shorten the chain and restore observability of preferences; test that hypothesis rather than presume improved representation. Citizens' assemblies cannot infer a public mandate from selection.

**Paper IV — Requisite Variety and the Commons.** Neither the historical inference of inadequacy from low-dimensional observation nor the favorable high-variety Finnish foresight rating establishes its claimed result. Observation dimension, rank or action count alone certifies neither adequacy nor inadequacy: a lower-dimensional observation can suffice when it preserves the distinctions that matter for a specified task, tolerated residual and authorized feasible response. The necessary counting bound described above does not guarantee the suitability of the actual observation partition. In the linear example, a bound derived for disturbances spanning the whole plane must be recomputed if disturbances lie in a smaller subspace. The original attributes the gap to the Foresight Silo, where existing foresight does not reach decision machinery, and proposes binding foresight as the connector. This is an empirical and institutional hypothesis; communicating signals cannot itself create authority or resources.

**Paper V — Coordination Failure Tax.** The original says four modes multiply, jointly producing categorical incapacity, and that Finland's velocity loss compounds after first-order success. Conditional sequential retention can be modelled by a product, but the combined fractional loss is no greater than the sum of stage loss fractions in that model; this inequality alone does not establish incapacity, and may be uninformative when the summed losses exceed one. In the stipulated scalar example, four half-retentions leave 1/16: enough for a requirement of 1/20 but not for 1/10. Task-specific retained capacity must be compared with an explicit minimum, and meeting it alone does not establish actual institutional adequacy (Coordination Failure as Structural Condition, E.5; The Subsidiarity Deficit, C.4). Interactions and recurrence can improve, worsen or leave outcomes unchanged. No national incapacity, necessary deterioration or first-order exemption follows. All five historical model claims remain distinct from the hypotheses.

### C.3 The Throughput Hypothesis and Limits of the Formal Transfer

The original describes Finland as having high gain, high observability, adequate controllability and insufficient bandwidth. These terms were used analogically for response accuracy, sensing, ability to act and speed, not as defined control-theory properties. All four ratings are withdrawn as established national measurements, favorable and adverse alike. The claim that foresight, portfolio and shock absorbers expand bandwidth without altering fundamental design parameters is an unverified design hypothesis. Distinguish first-order performance, legitimate revisability, tasks, authority, observations, resources and outcomes. The original claimed the report extends Governance as Engineering beyond its original scope and establishes a second-order binding constraint; neither a formal extension nor theorem nor developmental sequence is established. Temporal adequacy is a research question. Compare ordinary successful responses, beneficial deliberation and plausible alternative bottlenecks; no national scalar proxy or necessary acceleration.
