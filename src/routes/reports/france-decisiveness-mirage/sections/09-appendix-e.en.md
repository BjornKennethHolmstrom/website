## Appendix E: About this report and how it was made

### How it was made

This report was generated by AI language models (Claude, ChatGPT, DeepSeek) from prompts by Björn Kenneth Holmström. Several models were prompted in parallel to approach France's situation, and their outputs were combined into a single text. He chose the topic, posed the questions and decided to publish the result, but wrote little of the text himself.

The report has not been systematically fact-checked. Statements about French institutions, laws, events, figures and sources may be inaccurate or invented, and should be verified against primary sources before being relied on or cited. Like any work of synthesis, it is provisional. It makes no claim to finality. It claims only that the lens it offers merits testing against reality—and that the testing, in the end, is what matters most.

### Perspective and limits

Neither the models nor the person who prompted them write from within the French policy establishment. Björn Kenneth Holmström has studied engineering physics and has a long-standing personal interest in systems thinking, complexity and governance. He has no formal training or professional experience in French public administration or political science. The framework applied here draws on complexity science, developmental psychology and governance theory.

That distance limits access to the granular, day‑to‑day texture of French policy‑making. An outside lens can sometimes raise questions that insiders have learned not to hear, but it can just as easily miss what insiders know. The report does not claim insider knowledge. It offers a lens that may or may not prove useful to those who hold institutional positions and are searching for frameworks that make sense of what they are experiencing.

Feedback, criticism, and corrections are welcome. The argument is offered in the spirit of collaborative sense‑making, not definitive pronouncement.

### The Country Reports Series

This report was the second in a series of Country Reports for Systemic Change, all produced in the same way. The first examined Germany through the lens of a twin execution deficit and proposed Adaptive Governance Pilot Regions. Later reports went on to examine Sweden, India, the European Union, and others, each asking the same underlying question: *Is the real bottleneck money and decisions, or is it the system's capacity to use them wisely?* Together, the series aims to build a comparative, integrative framework for diagnosing and addressing the adaptive capacity deficits that hold nations back.
