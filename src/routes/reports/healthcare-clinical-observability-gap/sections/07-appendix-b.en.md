## Appendix B: International Analogues and Precedents

The original presents five examples as potential implementations of clinical observability mechanisms and possible cases of partial resistance to the proposed spiral. These are unverified comparative hypotheses (HC83). The existence of a design does not establish its causal benefits, necessity or transferability. Verify dated primary evidence, definitions, actual institutional arrangements, selection and applicability, with favourable and adverse claims assessed symmetrically. The comparisons below preserve the original's leads; they are not institution-specific findings or clinical recommendations. All such findings remain on HOLD.

### B.1 Kaiser Permanente: Integrated Payer-Provider Architecture

The original describes Kaiser Permanente as an integrated managed-care model in the United States, contrasting coordination between financing and provision with fragmented fee-for-service arrangements. It emphasises integrated records, cross-speciality coordination and physician payment less directly tied to activity volume. These are potentially relevant institutional features, but the precise organisations, contractual relationships and remuneration require verification. Claude's v0.1 audit review supplied the Permanente Medical Group contracting structure as an unverified lead concerning the original's salaried-physician claim.

The original's favourable claims about outcomes, cost relative to comparable systems, and lower documentation burden or burnout are also research leads. Compare populations, selection, geographic context, case mix, benefit coverage, costs and quality outcomes. Integration may facilitate information exchange while concentrating organisational discretion or creating barriers to external services. Verify the original's qualifications about historical circumstances, continuing workload and burnout. Neither lower cost nor a particular contract alone establishes clinical observability. Compare other integrated and distributed arrangements; do not assume transferability.

### B.2 Singapore: Mixed Financing and Active State Management

The original identifies MediSave, MediShield Life and active public management of supply as potential elements of a mixed financing design. It hypothesises that patient-level cost awareness, protection against large costs and supply planning may reduce low-value utilisation or support efficient allocation. Verify the financing arrangements, eligibility, subsidies, coverage and burden in the relevant period; Claude's v0.1 audit review supplied MediFund as an unverified lead. Cost awareness may deter necessary care as well as unnecessary care, depending on affordability, risk and support. Patients' choices are not reducible to price signals.

The original's comparative claims about efficiency, outcomes and universal access require evidence and a definition of access. Consider financial hardship, distribution, unmet need, public spending, demographics and actual clinical outcomes rather than infer causality from the existence of financing instruments. Household financial circumstances and institutional context constrain transfer; the original itself notes this limitation. Mixed finance is a candidate comparator, not a necessary solution or proof that administrative incentives have been aligned with care.

### B.3 Veterans Health Administration: Turnaround Through Decentralisation and Integration

The original describes a transition in the US Veterans Health Administration in the 1990s towards primary care, decentralised service organisation, shared electronic records and performance management. It attributes improved quality, satisfaction and efficiency by the early 2000s to these changes. These are dated claims to verify, including the baseline, comparators, selection, outcomes and attribution to distinct reforms. An integrated record and local autonomy may be complementary but neither alone establishes better clinical care.

The original states that the record system was the same as that used by the Department of Defense. Claude's v0.1 audit review supplied VistA, separate DoD systems and later shared commercial EHR arrangements as unverified leads; the blanket equivalence is not asserted here. Verify actual system histories, implementation, interoperability and consequences. The original also cites the 2014 waiting-list scandal and later access and quality concerns. These may challenge a favourable account without negating all earlier gains. Study both periods and alternative explanations rather than claim an inevitable reassertion of a spiral. Evaluate whether observed changes are attributable to organisational arrangements, resources, data practices or other causes.

### B.4 Nuka System of Care (Alaska): Community-Owned, Relationship-Based Care

The original describes the Southcentral Foundation's Nuka System of Care serving Alaska Native and American Indian people, with “customer-ownership”, relationship-based continuity, integrated teams including clinical and community workers, and a proposed description of financing. These are attributed historical leads, not findings endorsed by this correction. The original cites a population of approximately 65,000, emergency utilisation decreasing by over fifty percent, admissions by over thirty percent, and improvements in satisfaction, outcomes and costs. All figures, definitions, periods, populations and causal attributions require verification against the community's own accounts and appropriate primary evidence. Neither the figures nor the description establishes a controlled comparison or universal model.

Community ownership is not simply a technical means to improve information collection; it concerns legitimate self-governance. No inference of permission, endorsement, participation or consent to transfer follows. Before Nuka is used beyond an attributed historical lead, require Indigenous health-governance expertise designated with or by the relevant community authorities. Respect Southcentral Foundation's published accounts, data-governance terms and community authority; involve the relevant community in any substantive interpretation or proposed application. Claude's v0.1 audit review supplied tribal self-governance compacting (ISDEAA) and the CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance as unverified research leads. No community-specific data are sought or used in this correction. Context, history and ownership cannot be reduced to a transferable score or technical template. No transfer without legitimate permission and evidence of fit.

### B.5 The NHS: The Standardisation Spiral in a Universal System

The original contrasts the NHS's founding commitment to access free at the point of care, claimed comparative efficiency and equity, and successive waves of targets, payment and regulatory reforms with concerns about workload, waiting, morale, clinical discretion and prevention. The founding history and actual service arrangements, which differ across the United Kingdom and over time, require verification. Favourable and adverse claims are evaluated on the same evidence standard. The original's characterisation of a paradigmatic spiral is a hypothesis, not a conclusion.

Compare particular periods, services, standards and reforms, controlling where feasible for staffing, funding, demand, demographics, case mix and external shocks. Targets may improve access, reveal inequity or encourage harmful prioritisation. The urgent suspected-cancer and surgical-priority leads supplied by Claude's v0.1 audit review require examination alongside the original's waiting-list concerns. Do not infer actual triage from aggregate reporting. Look for beneficial standards, successful adaptation, unmet needs and counterexamples to the proposed loop. The original recognises that the NHS is not simply a failed system. Its successes and problems may coexist and require different explanations. No necessity of structural redesign follows from the comparative label.
