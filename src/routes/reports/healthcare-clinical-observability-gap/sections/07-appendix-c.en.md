## Appendix C: The Governance as Engineering Connection

### C.1 The formal scope

The original draws on engineering and cybernetic analogies. They can help distinguish a task, relevant disturbances, information, feasible responses, feedback and constraints. They do not establish clinical diagnoses, empirical effect sizes, legal authority or a dimensionality measure of people or institutions. Papers VI, VIII and XX and the accepted capstone and subsidiarity corrections require task-relative claims and distinguish objectives from observation. The examples below are stipulated mathematical witnesses, not clinical recommendations. No scalar governance index or universal stability result follows.

### C.2 The Seven Primitives

**Nodes** are entities that receive and interpret information or participate in decisions. The original names patients, clinicians, teams, hospital administration, payers and regulators. These actors differ in legitimate responsibilities, knowledge and capacity; a person is a rights-holder, not merely a processing node. Processing capacity must be assessed for the particular task rather than inferred from a count of dimensions.

**State** denotes the relevant condition of the system in a specified model. The original maps this to physiological and psychological condition, functional status and social circumstances. Relevant aspects can be uncertain, interdependent and changing. This is an analogy, not an assertion that the entire patient is a measurable state vector or that all aspects should be observed.

**Flows** are the movements of information and resources, including referrals, results, prescriptions, reimbursement and handover. Evidence quality, timing, access, legitimacy, responsibility and implementation constrain their contribution. Observation channels carry evidence; actuators are available responses, which may belong to different lawful owners. Information does not confer authority.

**Latency** concerns the elapsed time between relevant changes, detection, interpretation and action, illustrated by deterioration, referral and delayed effects of preventive investment. Sampling, reporting and response intervals and causal effect horizons are distinct; independent urgency limits cannot be offset by aggregate throughput.

**Constraints** include safety, rights, consent, lawful authority, resources and clinically justified limits. Some are independent conditions of admissibility, not compensable costs. **Feedback loops** connect observations of consequences to future decisions; follow-up appointments, repeat tests and patient accounts can support correction without establishing a causal effect by themselves. **Signal fidelity** concerns reliability, relevance and accuracy relative to a specified task. More records do not necessarily provide better evidence and protected information need not be collected. The original's candidate losses through documentation, payment, waiting and defensive medicine require separate tests. Patient agency is not simply a feedback signal.

### C.3 Ashby's Law of Requisite Variety

The original maps the controller to the governance architecture of payment, regulation, information systems and institutional culture, and the disturbances to the clinical reality of patients and changing needs. Preserve this as an analogy: in specified models, regulating to a task-defined acceptable set requires adequate distinguishable responses relative to relevant disturbances. W1 concerns information, W2 admissible response and implementation, and W3 timing. The action-cover bound in Paper XX is necessary under its assumptions, not sufficient. No comparison of total patient and institutional dimensionality, constitutional unobservability or universal stability follows. The historical formulation and attribution of Ashby's law are verification leads. The analogy confers no authority to regulate a person or expand a mandate.

### C.4 The Fractality Principle

The original distinguishes fast clinical tasks, medium-scale coordination and slow investment and prevention. This is a candidate division of responsibilities, not a proof that no single-scale controller can perform a given task or that all levels must be expanded. Specify the actual disturbance and response horizons, interfaces, legal ownership and capacity; compare existing mechanisms and distributed alternatives. An institution may handle several timescales and an aggregate reporting channel can coexist with timely clinical escalation.

### C.5 The Variety Gap

The original imported the claim that objective functions are observation architectures from Paper VI. This equivalence is withdrawn, as are universal legibility compression and unsupported dimension counts. W1–W2 distinguish evidence, objectives, acceptable responses and authority. A proxy may be inadequate even when the desired outcome is observed, or a service may record evidence without being rewarded or authorised to act. The task-relative gap is a hypothesis about a particular evidence or response bottleneck, not a universal index. W4–W9 show further limits on inference from proxies, burden, missingness, bundles, interaction and correlated errors.

### C.6 The Healthcare System in the Country Reports Series

The original refers to Germany (execution), France (integration), Sweden (feedback), India (synchronisation), the European Union (coherence), the United Kingdom (control-delivery mismatch), Brazil (accumulation), Russia (legibility), the United States (integration), Finland (throughput constraint), Japan (continuity trap), Nigeria (substrate deficit), Israel (boundary deficit), and Spain (integrative closure deficit). These remain historical research leads, not evidence for healthcare. The original C.6 says sixteen preceding reports but lists fourteen, while Appendix E lists fifteen including China; verify publication history rather than silently reconcile the count. Appendix E retains the historical record verbatim. The corrected R4 AI report scopes the strategic feasibility hypothesis and does not establish necessity of a Commons. ### C.7 Conditional witnesses

The following W1–W9 reproduce the accepted dependency audit's examples. Reviewer-supplied clinical and institutional leads are explicitly unverified elsewhere; the witnesses are mathematical stipulations, not estimated outcomes. The subsidiarity W5 agreement-event distinction means that different decision rules produce different agreement and error events; agreement does not establish accuracy.

#### W1 — Task-relative observation
Let D be a linear disturbance subspace, C a linear measurement map, and L a subspace of clinically/task-acceptable residual ambiguity. Invisible task-relevant ambiguity has quotient dimension h = dim(K) − dim(K∩L), K = D∩ker C. Thus h ≥ max(0, dim D − dim(D∩L) − rank(C|D)). For exact identification modulo L, K⊆L. In R³ let D=R³, L=span(e2,e3). Measuring x1 alone suffices for this task; measuring x2 and x3 leaves h=1. A higher count does not imply greater adequacy. Neither the count nor L may be assigned to patients empirically without evidence and legitimate task definition.

#### W2 — Objectives, observations, response and implementation
A service can record (need, cost) while paying for cost only, or pay according to need while observing only a noisy proxy. Objective ≠ sensor. For each observation-equivalence class E, a deterministic policy requires an admissible response in the intersection of acceptable response sets A(x) over x∈E; where empty, observation or response architecture may be inadequate. If A(x) includes only lawful and rights-preserving actions, information cannot confer authority. In a static example one team observes two needs but is authorized to address only the first; a second team can address the second. The integrated system can respond if coordination and implementation work. The Paper XX response-category cover bound is necessary, not sufficient; no universal single-scale failure follows. Ashby's intuition concerns requisite distinguishable responses to specified disturbances for a specified task, not total societal or patient dimensionality.

#### W3 — Temporal fit and urgency
A reliable event-triggered system observing at state changes can suffice for piecewise constant state if notification and response are timely. With 10 periods, state flips at 3 and 7 (zero-based), notifications arrive two periods later: stale estimate at 3,4,7,8 (4/10). Patient-initiated reporting is an endogenous trigger: access, symptoms, burden, preferences, trust and legitimate refusal can affect whether and when a report arrives. No report does not establish absence of need; link W3 to W6, without treating refusal as a defect. A fast sample clock is not equivalent to fast action. A separate aggregate report may be slow without impairing a functioning clinical alert path. For two hypothetical jobs of length one, one due at t=1 and one at t=2, both orders complete two jobs by t=2, but only urgent-first meets both deadlines. These are abstract scheduling examples, not clinical triage rules. Independent clinically and legally justified delay limits cannot be offset by throughput. Prevention: observed event probability 0.1 under an intervention is compatible with untreated probability 0.1 or 0.2; avoided events are estimable only under identification assumptions, not individually observable counterfactuals.

#### W4 — Proxy, multidimensional outcomes and score reversal
Two stipulated designs have (time ratio, independently assessed quality) = (0.8,0.4) and (0.6,0.9). Optimizing direct-contact time alone selects the first; this says nothing about the empirical relation. For equal weights the composite is 0.6 versus 0.75; weights (0.8,0.2) reverse the ranking (0.72 vs 0.66). Weight choices are normative and measurement uncertainty matters. No score compensates for failing a hard safety/rights constraint. Objective-as-observation and the universal legibility-compression transfer are withdrawn. No validated Clinical Observability Score or Signal Preservation Index follows. Preserve the six components as separate candidate indicators; compare independently measured outcomes and gaming, not the index against itself.

#### W5 — Net burden and alert capacity
An illustrative team has 100 time units: 40 essential indirect care, 20 other administrative work, 40 direct care. Removing 10 units of administrative work but adding 6 for new reporting and 7 for reviewing alerts leaves 37 units for direct care, down from 40. This counterexample does not establish actual workloads. For one review interval with 8 new actionable or nonactionable alerts and capacity 5, an initially empty queue has at least 3 outstanding absent other dispositions; accuracy, prioritisation, escalation, timeliness and staffing all matter. Do not infer neglect from a queue or assume every alert should trigger treatment. Patient burden and shifts to nurses, caregivers and other services also count. Mandatory care-time quotas can crowd out essential indirect work; no prescriptive staffing ratio follows.

#### W6 — Selection, patient voice and inference
In a stipulated frame of 100 patients, 20 respond and 12 report a specified experience; observed fraction 3/5, population fraction bounded by [12/100,92/100] absent assumptions on missing responses. If the frame itself omits unregistered patients, these bounds do not cover them. Device ownership, disability, language, illness, consent and refusal can change the sample. Refusal is legitimate and not evidence of poor care or noncompliance. Patient-reported experience, clinical outcome and preference are different variables. Non-digital alternatives, accessible methods and data minimisation required; never compel disclosure to narrow a bound.

#### W7 — Sandbox identification
Let mean outcome be Y(d,p) with d=documentation relief and p=payment change (0/1); compare Y(1,1)−Y(0,0). In two stipulated potential-outcome tables ordered (00,10,01,11), (0,2,0,2) and (0,0,2,2) both yield bundle difference 2, but documentation-only effect differs (2 vs 0). Even random assignment of the bundle cannot identify its components without further assumptions or arms. A matched clinic contrast additionally requires exchangeability, positivity, consistent intervention and handling interference; matching alone does not establish causality. Cluster allocation, pretrends, common outcome ascertainment, implementation fidelity, contamination and uncertainty matter. Safety and care cannot be suspended to identify effects. A small pilot may assess feasibility rather than efficacy; no general proof or guaranteed savings follows.

#### W8 — Interaction and feedback
Two changes alone improve a stipulated outcome by 1 each, but jointly improve by 1 or 4; interaction term −1 or +2. Standardisation, documentation, staffing and integration may amplify or offset one another. A cycle can be broken by a correction that reduces needless reporting while retaining useful standards; no inevitability follows from a feedback diagram. Preserve both original spirals as hypotheses with link-level rivals. Apply the same disconfirmation standards to reform bodies.

#### W9 — Correlated error, human and AI checks
For two reviewers with hazard-conditioned miss probability p and correlation rho, joint miss J=p²+rho p(1−p). With p=1/20, independent J=1/400; J≤1/100 iff rho≤3/19. Feasible rho lower bound −1/19, upper bound 1. If either flags, miss J; if both must flag, miss 2p−J. These are not accuracy claims about any system. False positives, downstream review and action separately matter. Shared data, model training, clinical workflows or common standards may correlate errors; human override does not ensure independence. Assess human-only, AI-only and combined designs, calibration and subgroup harms. Agreement-event distinctions from subsidiarity W5 must not be conflated with accuracy. R4/R5/R6 acceptance is dependency correction only, not empirical evidence. Model-family overlap in this audit creates analogous risks.
