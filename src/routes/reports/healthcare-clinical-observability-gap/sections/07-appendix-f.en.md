## Appendix F: Key Concepts and Abbreviations

These terms preserve the original glossary vocabulary with task-relative or historical definitions. No validated composite or clinical standard is introduced.

**Administrative Imperative:** Candidate mechanism whereby payment, regulatory and performance arrangements encourage control, reporting or benchmarking in ways that may impede justified correction. Test actual decisions, legitimate oversight and counterexamples; not a claim of shared motives.

**Clinical Observability Gap:** A hypothesised task-relative deficiency in relevant available and reliable information, distinct from interpretation, attention, authority, capacity and implementation. Not an empirical dimension count or inevitable conflict between clinical and population tasks.

**Clinical Observability Audit (Requisite Variety Audit):** Candidate assessment of six information and response questions. The historical Clinical Observability Score is unvalidated and withdrawn as a composite ranking. Independent validation, burden and outcomes matter.

**Clinical Signal Preservation Index:** Historical name for six separate candidate monitoring indicators: protected clinical time, continuity, cross-speciality observability, administrative abstraction, patient-reported signal fidelity and temporal depth. Not a validated index or compensatory score.

**Clinician's gaze:** Contextual clinical observation and reasoning, including conversation, examination and experience. Valuable but fallible and complementary to other evidence and patient agency.

**DRG (Diagnosis‑Related Group):** Classification of cases into groups for specified payment, allocation or reporting purposes. Uses and rules vary by jurisdiction; a coded diagnosis does not establish the full clinical need or the payment mechanism.

**EHR (Electronic Health Record):** Digital clinical record infrastructure, potentially supporting clinical communication, reporting, safety, reimbursement and other functions. Design and actual use determine task relevance, reliability and burden.

**Healthcare Administrative Complex:** Historical label for hypothesised convergence of institutional incentives among payers, administrators, regulators and vendors. No collusion or motives inferred; professional and reform interests must face the same scrutiny.

**Information Sandbox:** Candidate bounded clinic study of specified payment and documentation changes, subject to causal design, protected records, cross-setting interoperability, independent safety review, patient participation, authorisation, stopping and rollback. No blanket exemption or efficacy inference.

**Moral injury:** A proposed construct concerning distress related to conflict with deeply held moral commitments. Clinical and workforce definitions and measurement require verification; not interchangeable with burnout or turnover and not attributed to a single cause by this report.

**Requisite variety:** Task-relative regulatory intuition that adequate distinguishable responses are needed for relevant disturbances in specified models. Observation, admissible action, timing and implementation matter; historical attribution requires verification.

**Signal fidelity:** Reliability, relevance and accuracy of information for a specified decision. Additional data or recording does not necessarily improve it; legitimate privacy and refusal constrain collection.

**Solidarity principle:** Normative commitment to equitable access and the equal worth of people, including collective responsibility for care. Equal treatment need not be identical treatment; justified standards and individualisation may complement one another.

**Standardisation–Signal Destruction Spiral:** Historical name for a hypothesised sequence linking cost pressure, standardisation, information or time loss, care failure, adverse outcomes and renewed pressure. Each link is separately testable and can be broken; beneficial standards and successful adaptation are genuine rivals.

**Variety gap:** Task-relative potential inadequacy of evidence or distinguishable admissible responses for specified relevant disturbances. Not a count of clinical complexity, a universal stability theorem or a mandate to expand observation or control.
