## Appendix E: About this report and how it was made

### How it was made

This report was generated by AI language models (Claude, ChatGPT, DeepSeek) from prompts by Björn Kenneth Holmström. Several models were prompted in parallel to approach India's situation from different angles, applying the systems-thinking framework he had set out. He chose the topic, posed the questions and decided to publish the result, but wrote little of the text himself. The voice of the report is the models', not his.

The report has not been systematically fact-checked. Statements about Indian institutions, laws, schemes, events, figures and sources may be inaccurate or invented, and should be verified against primary sources before being relied on or cited. Like any work of synthesis, it is provisional. It makes no claim to finality. It claims only that the lens it offers merits testing against reality — and that the testing, in the end, is what matters most.

### Perspective and limits

Neither the models nor the person who prompted them write from within India. Björn Kenneth Holmström is not Indian, does not live in India and has no lived experience of Indian governance. He has studied engineering physics and has a long-standing personal interest in systems thinking, complexity and governance, but no formal training or professional experience in Indian administration, development economics or political science. The framework applied here draws on complexity science, developmental psychology and governance theory.

That distance limits access to the granular, day-to-day texture of Indian administration — the unwritten norms, the informal power structures, the lived reality that no formal framework can capture. An outside lens can sometimes raise questions that insiders have learned not to hear, but it can just as easily miss what insiders know. The report does not claim insider knowledge. It offers a lens that may or may not prove useful to those who hold institutional positions and are searching for frameworks that make sense of what they are experiencing. Feedback, criticism and corrections are welcome.

The report refers to the **DPI 2.0** proposal — a proposed technical specification for a governance routing protocol on India Stack, intended to reduce disaster response latency through precision subsidiarity. It is available on this website and was produced in the same way as this report. It has not been reviewed by Indian practitioners, India Stack developers or disaster-management experts.

### Acknowledgment of Intellectual Debt

This report draws on themes from the work of many scholars and practitioners who have thought deeply about Indian governance. The diagnoses of institutional hollowing associated with **Pratap Bhanu Mehta**, of state capability at the last mile associated with **Jean Drèze**, and of welfare state architecture associated with **Yamini Aiyar** inform the argument. The framework is also indebted to the cybernetic tradition — Ross Ashby, Stafford Beer, Norbert Wiener — and to the contemporary scholars who have extended their work into governance design. The text was generated by AI models, which may have simplified or misrepresented this work; any such errors are not the fault of the scholars named.

### The Country Reports Series

This report was the fourth in a series of Country Reports for Systemic Change, all produced in the same way. The first examined Germany through the lens of an execution deficit. The second examined France through the lens of an integration deficit. The third examined Sweden through the lens of a feedback deficit. Together, the first four reports propose a diagnostic framework built around four dimensions of adaptive capacity: the ability to *decide*, *integrate*, *sense*, and *synchronise* — which the series suggests determine whether governance systems can meet the complexity of the 21st century. Later reports extended the series to the European Union, the United Kingdom, and beyond. Each asks the same underlying question: *Is the real bottleneck money, decisions, legitimacy, or sensing — or is it the system's capacity to align what it already has?*
