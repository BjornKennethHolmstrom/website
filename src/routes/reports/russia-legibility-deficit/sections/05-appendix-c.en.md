## Appendix C: About this report and how it was made

### How it was made

This report was generated by AI language models (Claude, ChatGPT, DeepSeek) from prompts by Björn Kenneth Holmström. Several models were prompted in parallel to approach Russia's situation from different strategic angles—institutional architecture, political economy, cultural dynamics, and comparative governance—and to apply the systems-thinking framework he had set out. He chose the topic, posed the questions and decided to publish the result, but wrote little of the text himself.

The report has not been systematically fact-checked. Statements about Russian institutions, laws, events, figures and sources may be inaccurate or invented, and should be verified against primary sources before being relied on or cited. Like any work of synthesis, it is provisional. It makes no claim to finality. It claims only that the lens it offers merits testing against reality—and that the testing, in the end, is what matters most.

### Perspective and limits

Neither the models nor the person who prompted them write from within Russia's institutional core. Björn Kenneth Holmström is not Russian, does not live in Russia and has no lived experience of Russian governance. The framework applied here draws on complexity science, developmental psychology (Spiral Dynamics), governance theory, and control‑theoretic approaches to institutional design.

That distance limits access to the granular, day‑to‑day texture of Russian policy‑making—the unwritten norms, the informal power structures, the lived reality that no formal framework can capture. An outside lens can sometimes raise questions that insiders have learned not to hear, but it can just as easily miss what insiders know. The models were also trained mainly on Western sources about Russia, which may introduce systematic bias. The report does not claim insider knowledge. It offers a lens that may or may not prove useful to those who hold institutional positions and are searching for frameworks that make sense of what they are experiencing. Feedback, criticism, and corrections are welcome.

The report refers to related material on this website—the Global Governance Frameworks, the Governance as Engineering working papers and the Country Reports for Systemic Change—which were produced in the same way.

### The Country Reports Series

This report is part of the series of Country Reports for Systemic Change, all produced in the same way, and was conceived as its boundary case. Other reports in the series examined Germany through the lens of an execution deficit, France through an integration deficit, Sweden through a feedback deficit, India through a synchronisation deficit, the European Union through a coherence deficit, the United Kingdom through a control‑delivery deficit, Brazil through an accumulation deficit, the United States through an integration deficit, and Finland through a throughput constraint. This report examines Russia through the lens of a legibility deficit. Together, the reports propose a global diagnostic framework covering a wide range of adaptive capacity failures—from the most functional systems in the series to a system that the report describes as having deliberately eliminated the preconditions for adaptive governance. The series does not claim to be complete. It is meant as a foundation on which further analysis, deeper testing, and better design can be built. It also tries to be honest about its own limits—the boundary beyond which, in the report's view, diagnosis is possible but prescription is not. The Russia report was conceived to mark that boundary. The work beyond it, the report suggests, belongs to history.
