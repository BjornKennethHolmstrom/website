## Appendix C: The Governance as Engineering Connection

### C.1 The Architectural Foundation

This report draws on the Governance as Engineering series, a set of formal analyses that model governance institutions using feedback control, information theory and cybernetics. This appendix summarises parts that motivate the Tournament Trap hypothesis, distinguishing scoped results from report-level inferences. [HR] denotes a historical, unaudited source or model claim, not an accepted theorem of this correction.

### C.2 The Papers in Brief

**Paper II — Fractality as Stability [HR].** The historical model motivates investigation of disturbance timescales and controller bandwidth. Its derivation and uniqueness claims are not audited here. A single-scale controller's inadequacy cannot be inferred from the mere presence of several timescales. Compare institutional arrangements for cohort-scale changes with task-specific delays, mandates, costs, rights and review; long-term funding or protection is a proposal, not a theorem.

**Paper VI — The Variety Gap (v0.2).** Evaluation and observation are distinct. In its static noiseless linear model, with disturbances D, acceptable residual subspace L and observation C, K = D ∩ ker C must be contained in L for adequate deterministic correction; this is sufficient when every correction in −D is available. The hidden gap dim K − dim(K ∩ L) is task-relative. Equal observation ranks may yield different hidden gaps. The Korean consequence-coupling hypothesis concerns dependence among opportunities, not this formal gap or an estimated rank. Here C and L denote the observation map and acceptable residual subspace in VI, not consequence coupling and rank legitimacy in §6.2.

**Paper VIII — Measuring the Variety Gap (v0.2).** The original pilot score, interval, bands, trajectory and pilot-validation claims were withdrawn. No active Korean estimate is retained. The former qualitative claims concerning sophisticated observation, adequate static capacity, disturbance growth, polarisation and a declining structural-to-symbolic reform ratio are unverified and withdrawn from this report's active argument. Use task-specific diagnostic profiles: evaluation, observation, modelling, decision and actuation. A retrospective coding study must test whether the proposed reform pattern exists before explaining it.

**Paper XIII — Legitimacy [HR].** The historical model treats legitimacy as operational gain; no audit of its derivation or calibration is supplied. Perceived fairness, reporting and compliance may affect institutional performance, but no scalar multiplier or necessary effect of adding criteria is inferred. The Legitimacy Lock remains a separately testable hypothesis.

**Paper XXV — Where Reform Pushes Down, Strategy Pushes Back [HR].** Its historical account motivates asking whether a relocation claim identifies a bounded quantity, mechanism and boundary. The source has not been audited in this correction. No conservation of competitive effort follows from the existence of a tournament. Whether changing a discriminator leaves competitive incentives intact, or changes total effort and its distribution, is a question for empirical testing.

### C.3 The Tournament Trap and the Distinction Between Controller and Objective

Earlier series diagnoses are historical hypotheses, not evidence that any national system has solved its first-order problems. The Korean report motivates a distinction between the capacity to correct the *controller* and the capacity to revise what the system *optimises*.

Rapid political correction and slower opportunity restructuring are distinguishable hypotheses, not estimates of control gain or observation latency. A task-level investigation should specify who knows what, who can decide, what actions are feasible, and how their outcomes are evaluated. Corrected Paper XX v0.2 distinguishes proxy–target ordering on a feasible set from mere omission of relevant dimensions: neither narrower selection nor more criteria alone establishes harmful or beneficial optimization.

The original report described two channels as having empirical support: persistent opportunity structure and legitimacy-constrained adaptation. Those supporting studies are not independently fact-checked here; the two channels remain hypotheses for this correction. The third channel, transition load, was originally proposed as a hypothesis without comparable empirical support. Coding reforms and comparing independent outcomes may strengthen, narrow or refute these proposals. None explains an established national gap trend.

The diagnostic grammar used in the report, *T = f(P, S⁻¹, C, E, L)*, is a decomposition rather than an estimated equation. Its terms do different work: prize steepness, effective supply, consequence coupling and effort responsiveness govern the tournament's intensity, while rank legitimacy governs above all its durability. Formalising the grammar—and in particular identifying whether any of its terms defines a quantity that is bounded in the sense Paper XXV requires—is a natural candidate for a future paper in the series.

The potential contribution is to investigate whether an allocation rule can be protected by the perceived legitimacy of its own procedure, and how this interacts with consequence coupling and reform. This is a research hypothesis, not a mathematical consequence of VI or XIII. Credible revision of evaluation and decision rules requires transparent authorization, evidence, review and safeguards.
