## Appendix C: The Governance as Engineering Connection

### C.1 The Architectural Foundation

The formal connection is a scoped research foundation, not a proof of the national diagnosis. The historical I–VI papers are antecedents with distinct assumptions and corrections. Task-relative information, authority, feasible action, implementation, legitimate objectives and outcomes are separate. The formal witnesses that follow are stipulated examples, not measurements of Spain.

**Original argument — attributed historical record, unverified (not the revised conclusion).**

> This report draws on a deeper body of work: the Governance as Engineering series, a set of formal analyses that model governance institutions as feedback control systems using standard mathematics from control theory, information theory, and cybernetics. The series is technical; this appendix summarises its core findings in non‑technical language and shows how they underpin the Integrative Closure Deficit diagnosis.

### C.2 The Six Papers in Brief

Paper I: spatial aggregation loses consequential distinctions only relative to the task and representation (W1). A pooled signal can suffice for a pooled response; decentralisation does not guarantee local observation. Paper II: timescale multiplicity does not prove single-scale impossibility (W3); the political, economic, demographic, monarchy and climate examples require evidence of deadlines, response, attribution and actual ownership. Paper III: chain length does not entail attenuation (W4); inspect each transformation and error, including correlated reports and valid representation. Paper IV: conditional requisite variety is about consequential information/action under defined assumptions, not a national observation dimension (W1–W2). Paper V: no universal multiplication or accumulation (W8); the capstone bound is conditional, not empirical causal independence. Paper VI: normative objectives are not observations; a stability emphasis does not prove a one-dimensional value architecture or a Variety Gap. GA-F01 distinguishes assumptions; GA-F02 withdraws the universal scalar gap, there is no I–V bridge, VIII is not invoked, XX's cover is necessary only under its premises, and the subsidiarity result separates agreement from outcome. All six original paper summaries are retained below solely as historical arguments whose unconditional formal inferences are withdrawn. The four-stage loop name is historical, not an analytic requirement.

**Original argument — attributed historical record, unverified (not the revised conclusion).**

> **Paper I — Governance Stability Simulator** demonstrates that centralised governance systems operating on aggregated signals destroy spatial information. Spain's territorial architecture, in its current form, exhibits exactly this dynamic: the vertical flow of fiscal and political authority through Madrid aggregates the diverse realities of the autonomous communities into a single national framework, destroying the spatial information—the specific grievances, the distinct identities, the local knowledge—that a genuinely multi‑level system would process at the appropriate scale. The horizontal governance mechanisms proposed in this report—inter‑regional compacts, networked municipalism, bioregional governance—are designed to restore the distributed information processing that the current architecture suppresses.
>
> **Paper II — Fractality as Stability** demonstrates that no single‑scale controller can stabilise a system facing simultaneous fast, medium, and slow disturbances. Spain faces disturbances across all three bands: fast political crises (the 2017 Catalan referendum, the 2023 amnesty law confrontation), medium economic and social pressures (the tourism‑heavy equilibrium, the labour market dualism, the generational lock‑out), and slow secular trends (the demographic hollowing of the interior, the climate‑driven water crisis, the declining legitimacy of the monarchy). The current architecture is calibrated primarily for fast political crisis management—Article 155, judicial interventions, coalition negotiations—while the medium and slow disturbances that are steadily intensifying the territorial pressures go largely unaddressed. The transition architecture is designed to create the multi‑scale response capacity that the fractality principle requires.
>
> **Paper III — The Observability‑Democracy Connection** demonstrates that citizen preferences cannot be reliably transmitted through deep representation chains. Spain's representation chain is particularly attenuated by the territorial dimension: a Catalan citizen's preferences must pass through autonomous community politics, national party structures, parliamentary coalition negotiations, and the judicial filter of the Constitutional Tribunal before reaching the policy layer. The citizens' assemblies, municipal networks, and inter‑regional compacts proposed in this report are designed to shorten the observation chain, connecting citizen preferences to governance decisions through channels that bypass the attenuated, multi‑layer representation architecture.
>
> **Paper IV — Requisite Variety and the Commons** demonstrates that governance systems with low‑dimensional observation cannot stabilise high‑variety resource systems. Spain's governance system has low observational variety with respect to its territorial reality: it processes territorial dynamics primarily through the single dimension of centre‑periphery conflict, missing the tripartite reality of *La España Vaciada*, the cross‑boundary ecological interdependencies of water systems, and the functional economic geographies that the constitutional map obscures. The networked municipalism and bioregional governance compacts are designed to increase the observational variety of the governance system, making visible the dimensions of territorial reality that the current architecture cannot register.
>
> **Paper V — The Coordination Failure Tax** demonstrates that the four failure modes do not add—they multiply. Spain exhibits a specific interaction between the spatial blindness generated by the vertical centre‑periphery architecture (Paper I), the frequency gap generated by a system calibrated for fast political crises rather than slow structural pressures (Paper II), and the low observational variety generated by a constitutional framework that cannot register the full dimensionality of the territorial reality (Paper IV). The "tax" in Spain is the compounding cost of permanent ambiguity—each cycle of the oscillation erodes trust, deepens polarisation, and consumes political capital, making the integrative closure that would resolve the underlying tensions progressively more difficult to achieve.
>
> **Paper VI — The Variety Gap** extends Ashby's Law of Requisite Variety to the architecture of institutional value, demonstrating that low‑dimensional value architectures systematically exclude the disturbance dimensions that eventually destabilise them. Spain's constitutional value architecture is effectively one‑dimensional—optimising for stability and consensus—and the excluded dimensions (territorial justice, plurinational recognition, fiscal fairness, generational equity) are the ones that periodically destabilise the system through the Crisis–Centralisation–Peripheral Mobilisation–Accommodation Loop. The transition architecture is, in essence, an attempt to increase the dimensionality of the Spanish value architecture—to build the institutional capacity to recognise and respond to the multiple, irreducible dimensions of the plurinational reality that the 1978 settlement acknowledged in principle but never operationalised in practice.

### C.3 The Integrative Closure Deficit and the Limits of Constructive Ambiguity

First-order performance within a constitutional framework and second-order ability to legitimately revise it are conceptually distinct. The original's favorable claims of adequate observability, high gain and accurate crisis response, and its adverse claim of deficient adaptive capacity, are unverified and not established by the formal papers. Gain and stability require a defined model and data; legal authorization and protective thresholds are not mathematical disturbances. Adequate existing arrangements, justified constraints, effective adaptation or a different legitimate owner can defeat a claimed deficiency. No measured Spain-wide score or second-order theorem is supplied. The formal witnesses provide scope and counterexamples, not confirmation of an institutional diagnosis.

**Original argument — attributed historical record, unverified (not the revised conclusion).**

> The Governance as Engineering series identifies the structural requirements for first‑order governance—observability, controllability, requisite variety, and feedback integrity. Spain satisfies these requirements within the existing constitutional framework: the system can perceive threats, respond to them, and maintain stability. What it cannot do is evolve the framework itself. The Integrative Closure Deficit is a second‑order governance challenge: not a failure of the architecture to perform its basic functions, but a limitation on the architecture's capacity to complete itself—to transform the ambiguity that enabled transition into the institutionalised pluralism that sustains settlement.
>
> In control‑theoretic terms, the system has high *gain* with respect to crisis management—it responds accurately and forcefully to territorial challenges. It has adequate *observability* within the existing constitutional frame. What it lacks is *adaptive capacity* with respect to the constitutional frame itself—the ability to reconfigure its own foundational parameters as the conditions that justified those parameters change. The frozen amendment procedures, the prohibitive consensus requirements, and the Consensus Machine's immune response are the mechanisms that suppress this adaptive capacity. The orthogonal interventions proposed in this report are designed to build that capacity from the bottom up—creating, through parallel subsystems, the adaptive governance that the constitutional architecture cannot provide directly.
>
> The Spain report thus extends the Governance as Engineering framework to the specific challenge of constitutional evolution under conditions of frozen ambiguity. It demonstrates that the transition from ambiguity to integration requires not merely better institutions within the existing framework, but institutions capable of questioning and revising the framework itself—a meta‑governance capacity that the 1978 settlement, for all its achievements, never developed.

### C.4 Conditional witnesses (W1–W9)

These stipulated models are counterexamples and scope illustrations, not estimates for Spain. The historical I–V papers are cited as research antecedents, not inherited general theorems. GA-F01 consolidates bounded representation, Ashby counting, Goodhart ordering and certification accounting under distinct premises; it does not derive them all from finitude or establish an I–V bridge. GA-F02 separates objectives from observations and withdraws a universal scalar gap. Paper VIII is not explicitly invoked in the original; its withdrawn index is not used here. XX's action-compatible cover is necessary under its stated assumptions, not a general sufficiency theorem. The capstone retention bound concerns stipulated conditional losses, not independent causal effects; the subsidiarity correction distinguishes agreement events from substantive outcomes. No measured national gain, rank, gap, attractor, stability or second-order theorem is established here.

#### W1 — Spatial compression, task relevance and value
Let D be a disturbance subspace, L⊆D task-irrelevant directions, C a static noiseless linear observation and K=D∩ker C. With unrestricted compensating responses modulo L, task adequacy of the observation requires K⊆L; hidden consequential dimension h=dim K−dim(K∩L). The necessary lower bound max(0,dim D−dim L−rank(C|D)) need not be sufficient. In R³ with L=span(e2,e3), C observing x1 gives h=0; observing x2,x3 gives h=1 despite higher rank. For two territories d=(1,0),(0,1), pooled sum 1 suffices for a task requiring only total response, but not for a task requiring distinct local actions. A central owner may receive both components; local owners may observe neither. Neither aggregation nor decentralisation alone determines adequacy. Objective dimensionality is independent of C (VI). Spain's objectives and local channels require evidence, not dimension counts.

#### W2 — Responsibility, feasible response and legitimate constraint
For a fixed information class E a common feasible authorized acceptable response in the intersection over E is necessary; XX does not establish sufficiency. In d∈{0,1}, y=d, required a=d, an owner authorized only a=0 fails d=1. If a second owner lawfully can implement a=1, the distributed system may suffice; if implementation fails, permission does not suffice. For task requiring only a=0 there is no deficiency from the constraint. For constitutional amendments the legal threshold is part of legitimate feasibility, not a disturbance to be suppressed; no algebra can establish normative legitimacy.

#### W3 — Multi-rate response
A single fast controller with exact current state and instantaneous unrestricted compensation handles fast and slow disturbances in a static model; hence timescale multiplicity alone cannot prove impossibility. A binary state initially zero flipping at periods 3,7 with reliable two-period delayed observations is stale at 3,4,7,8 in periods 0–9. Zero-delay event reporting with known initial state suffices in this example. Real deadlines, authorization, endogenous reporting, budgets and dynamics require separate evidence; crisis response is not proof of long-term incapacity. II's universal impossibility is withdrawn.

#### W4 — Representation and proxy ordering
A chain of three identity maps preserves a binary preference exactly; a single constant map loses it. If two reports have unbiased unit-variance errors, their average has variance 1/2 for independent errors but 1 for identical errors; common bias 2 can give unanimous wrong reports. The same estimand and independence assumptions matter. III does not imply universal attenuation with chain depth. For profiles X=(.8,.4), Y=(.6,.9), equal weights yield .60/.75; weights (.8,.2) yield .72/.66. A hard minimum .5 on each component excludes X regardless of its score. With proxy M=x, target T=x+y, a lottery (2,0) with probability 1/4 else (0,0) gives E[M]=E[T]=.5 versus deterministic (0,1) with M=0,T=1. Ordering claims need declared reachable sets; stochastic ordering not inherited from deterministic comparisons. Assembly satisfaction, uptake, funds and project counts cannot validate closure or override rights.

#### W5 — Fiscal incidence, eligibility and administrative burden
A stipulated existing budget 100 and earmark 10 leave 90 for original purposes unless additional revenue or savings finance it. Preserving nominal baseline does not prove additionality: if all 100 are already committed, a 10 earmark requires 110 total or displacement. A region with benefit 4 and application/co-financing cost 5 declines even though nominal access exists; another with cost 1 participates. A two-region project net benefit 6 becomes net 4 if a third partner adds benefit 1 and coordination cost 3. A third partner may instead add net value; threshold must be task justified. For capacity 100, essential work 40, admin 20, removing 10 and adding 6+7 leaves 37 not 40 for other task work. Count shifted costs, baseline funding, equalisation, fiscal risk and lawful obligations; no Spanish forecast or recommended percentage.

#### W6 — Assembly representation and population boundaries
Of 100 eligible people, 20 participate and 12 favor an option: participant share .60; without missingness assumptions eligible-population proportion lies in [12%,92%]. This holds even before considering excluded residents, noncitizens or other affected persons. A random invitation is not equivalent to random acceptance; stratification cannot guarantee representativeness, preference change or authority. Separate descriptive estimand, normative standing, legal franchise, deliberation outcome and elected decision. Protect voluntary participation and dissent.

#### W7 — Causal identification and reversible experimentation
Outcome tables for (status quo, funding only, assembly only, both): (0,2,0,2) and (0,0,2,2) both yield bundle improvement 2 but funding effect 2 or 0. Even a randomized bundle cannot identify separate mechanisms. Define counterfactual, selection, spillovers, pretrends, interference, implementation, time horizon and independent endpoints; distinguish process and substantive results. Adoption/expansion/nonadoption neither establishes nor refutes the diagnosis by itself. A finite pilot can create irreversible land, fiscal, labor or environmental obligations; stop/rollback must protect affected people.

#### W8 — Interaction, recurrence and retention
For losses ℓ_i in [0,1], product Π(1−ℓ_i)≥max(0,1−Σℓ_i) algebraically; this is not an empirical assertion that mechanisms are independent or amplify. Outcomes for neither/A/B/both (10,12,13,14) have interaction −1, while (10,12,13,17) has +2. A recurrence x_{t+1}=x_t+c_t with c_t=-1 and x_t>0 can reduce unresolved burden after accommodation; c_t=+1 increases it. No universal compounding or national attractor from metaphor. V and capstone only within premises.

#### W9 — Independent review and reflexivity
For two reviewers with equal miss probability p=.05 and Bernoulli correlation ρ, joint miss J=p²+ρp(1−p). Independence gives 1/400; J≤.01 requires ρ≤3/19 with feasible lower bound −1/19. If both must flag to trigger action, miss probability 2p−J=39/400 at independence. Independence cannot be assumed from different offices, models, identities or agreement. Fréchet bounds apply. Require structural independence, access and challenge, report disagreement, compare decisions to independent endpoints. Agreement is not evidence of truth or legitimate authority; criticism of the framework may be correct.
