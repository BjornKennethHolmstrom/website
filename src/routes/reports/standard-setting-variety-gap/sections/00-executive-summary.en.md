# Executive Summary

**The Paradox**

Standard-setting bodies such as the IASB, FASB and ISO are rarely discussed as governance institutions, yet the categories they specify influence which evidence is available and admissible to many other institutions. Their mandates and authority differ: accounting standards, technical standards, certification and jurisdictional enforcement are not the same thing. The paradox is that a reporting architecture can be professionally rigorous for its intended task while leaving other users or purposes underserved. This is not an accusation of bad faith.

**The Core Diagnosis: The Standard-Setting Variety Gap**

This report asks whether particular reporting arrangements omit distinctions needed for specified decisions about viability, risk and impacts. Counting capital categories cannot answer that question. A reporting standard, notes, voluntary disclosures, independent evidence and internal records can together provide relevant information; even adequate information does not confer the authority or means to act. Earlier universal dimensionality and compression claims are withdrawn; Appendix C describes the narrower formal results. The original May 2026 institutional narrative is a set of leads for verification, not an empirical certification.

**The Signature Pattern: The Standards-Setting Spiral**

The proposed spiral links crisis, demands for reform, due process, supplementary expansion, pressure relief, preservation of the core and renewed exposure. Each link is a hypothesis: consultation may also improve standards, and complementary reports may be sufficient. H1–H5 in Chapter 1 define opportunities for disconfirmation independently of the proposals.

**Representation, transactions and institutional memory**

Financial reporting selects and organizes evidence. Transaction-based accounting can retain useful comparable records while leaving relationships, knowledge, ecological dependencies and other phenomena to different evidence systems. Whether such distinctions are lost for a given task, and whether losses matter, depends on the actual information pathways and decisions. Failure to recognize something in a balance sheet is not erasure from all records.

The distinction between transactional and relational evidence is especially significant. A record of a purchase can be reconciled against a contract and payment; trust, tacit knowledge and the condition of a shared ecosystem may require a history, contextual description or independent evidence. These differences are reasons to investigate methods of representation, not reasons to transform all relationships into transactions. Capitalizing an asset can maintain its carrying amount in the balance sheet across periods, while expensing a cost changes the financial record in a different way. The corresponding organizational knowledge may still be preserved elsewhere. That is the limited sense in which accounting can be described as a memory-allocation protocol.

**The Structural Mechanisms**

- The Conceptual Framework can constrain changes to the purpose of reporting, although its mandate and revision procedures need to be distinguished from wider public policy.
- Due process can slow reform or improve it; representation, resources and outcomes matter more than elapsed time alone.
- Financial materiality and impact materiality are different purposes, not counts of independent dimensions.
- Recognition, measurement uncertainty and assurance can favour evidence that is easier to verify; this may be legitimate or may leave decision-relevant gaps.
- Harmonization can propagate common errors but also reduce error and improve comparability.
- Reporting boundaries can create incentives for arbitrage; enforcement, genuine ownership boundaries and alternative supervisory channels matter.
- Periodicity and format can affect usability; machine readability and speed do not guarantee relevant, accurate or authorized information.

**The Political Immune System**

Investor primacy, claims of technical neutrality and professional identity are interpretive lenses. Investors, large audit firms, preparers and institutions that rely on common standards may have reasons to value stability; they may also support change. The question is which incentives and procedures produce observable effects, not which actors are presumed to obstruct reform.

**What a different architecture might look like**

The report advocates testing multi-capital stewardship, integrated reporting, double materiality, measurement innovation, distributed sensing, longer horizons, machine-readable exchange and networked translation against narrower or complementary alternatives. These are proposals, not necessary or certified designs. Their own categorization and sensing can reproduce the ontological reduction criticized in §2.14. Where Indigenous lands, knowledge or data are involved, collective free, prior and informed consent through peoples' own institutions, community control, refusal, non-legibility and benefit-sharing are proposed safeguards distinct from individual privacy and stakeholder consultation. No imposed capital classification or monetisation of sacred or relational knowledge. Other communities' authority must be established on their own terms; legal obligations and scope require separate verification. An authorized interface may leave distinctions unmapped.

**A Concrete First Step**

- The independent Multi-Capital Materiality Audit would produce a prospective task-relative profile, with unknowns and alternative channels recorded, not a universal Gap Score.
- The Integrated Reporting Sandbox would compare existing, additive, integrated and targeted options, initially alongside all existing obligations; actual waivers require lawful authorization. Outcomes include independent decision performance, costs and community-defined harms, with non-participation and null results legitimate.
- The Conceptual Framework Review Procedure would submit evidence and a reasoned petition to competent bodies. No automatic amendment or imposed IASB obligation; retaining existing arrangements is a valid result.

The three proposals are deliberately complementary. The Audit can identify a specific evidential constraint without advocating a change in mandate. The Sandbox can test whether a different representation improves a decision without assuming that wider disclosure is desirable. The Review Procedure can provide a reasoned response if a competent body adopts it, without binding that body's substantive decision. Each could be worthwhile without the others, or fail independently. The original bypass-catalyst idea is retained as the question of whether authorized experimental evidence can inform later institutional decisions; concealment and circumvention are withdrawn. A useful complement need not become a replacement.

**The Honest Conclusion**

Standards can be a protocol layer through which information travels to other institutions. Reform may offer leverage where a specific dependency and benefit are demonstrated, but other sources and legitimate boundaries may make expansion unnecessary or harmful. The challenge is to preserve rigor and comparability while testing whether different representations support better decisions, without claiming authority over what others have a right to keep outside the reporting system.
