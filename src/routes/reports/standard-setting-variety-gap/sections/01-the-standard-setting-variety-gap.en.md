# 1. The Standard-Setting Variety Gap

## 1.1 Opening: The Invisible Architects of Visibility

Consider a hypothetical corporation reporting assets of fifty billion dollars, liabilities of thirty billion, and shareholder equity of twenty billion. Its financial statements might comply with applicable standards, be audited and faithfully represent the financial position within their scope. That would be a considerable achievement, not a deception.

A reader interested in twenty-year viability may also ask about water access, workforce skills and tacit knowledge, trust, supply-chain resilience and climate exposure. Some of this information may be in notes or sustainability reports, some in independent records, some not available at all. An asset-recognition decision is different from whether evidence of a dependency exists. The example is a composite, not evidence about a named company or a claim that all these factors are absent from compliant reports.

The IASB and FASB need not have set out to exclude ecological or human concerns for gaps to arise. Dedicated professionals may reasonably see their work as serving the public through reliable capital allocation. What deserves investigation is how their reporting rules influence the evidence used by other decision-makers, and whether existing alternatives suffice.

## 1.2 The Core Diagnosis: The Standard-Setting Variety Gap

The Variety Gap is used here as a research question about task-relative adequacy, not an estimate of the rank of an economy. For each user, identify the decisions, relevant distinctions, available evidence, response authority and feasible actions. The formal conditions in Appendix C require defined models and assumptions; they cannot be read off the names of accounting categories.

Financial reporting commonly organizes information around assets, liabilities, equity, income and expenses. The original report cited the IASB's definition of an asset as a present economic resource controlled as a result of past events and quoted older expected-benefit wording. The exact version and recognition rules require verification. A controlled resource may receive different accounting treatment from a workforce's skills or a community relationship; a present obligation differs from a diffuse future impact. This distinction can be appropriate for financial position, while another task might require complementary evidence.

Operational efficiency, innovation, workforce health, supply chains, ecosystem dependencies, social legitimacy, regulatory change and physical or transition risk are potential variables. They are not necessarily independent, and counting them does not measure effective rank or establish a widening deficit. H1 asks whether particular missing distinctions affect actual decisions.

## 1.3 The Epistemic Constitution

The metaphor of an *epistemic constitution* draws attention to the rules that govern admissible evidence. A constitution specifies authority and procedures; accounting frameworks specify the purposes, definitions and forms of evidence for particular reports. Such conventions influence how an asset or liability enters decisions based on those reports. They do not constitute all economic reality, nor do they determine every regulator's, court's or tax authority's evidential rules.

A change to recognition criteria may require more than adding a field; it may require reconsideration of reporting purpose, definitions, measurement and jurisdictional adoption. A central bank or hospital may similarly face mandate constraints, but the comparative difficulty of these reforms is an empirical question. Supplementary disclosure and independent records need not require changing a financial accounting constitution.

The original narrative quotes the IASB's decision-usefulness objective for investors, lenders and other creditors. Verify its wording, status, revision procedures and relationship to specific standards. The report's political question is whether that purpose should be retained alongside complementary purposes, or broadened by competent authorities. It is not a finding that the Framework is immutable.

## 1.4 The Signature Pattern: The Standards-Setting Spiral

The proposed Standards-Setting Spiral is a sequence to investigate rather than a law:

**Crisis → demand for reform → due process → possible limited expansion → possible pressure relief → preservation of core arrangements → renewed exposure.**

The original May 2026 narrative cites Enron (2001) and the 2008 financial crisis as leads involving off-balance-sheet entities, consolidation, valuation and risk disclosure. Accounting treatment is only one possible contributor alongside leverage, incentives, supervision, fraud and underlying exposures. Neither chronology nor causation has been independently checked here.

Agenda papers, exposure drafts, hearings, field tests and re-deliberations can take time. Delay may dissipate pressure or may improve reliability, legitimacy and implementation. The creation of the ISSB in 2021 and the initial sustainability standards in 2023 are further historical leads: a separate reporting track could represent limited change, substantive complementary improvement or both. Verify mandates, dates and adoption before drawing conclusions.

H2 specifies how to test the loop: include successful and unsuccessful reforms and no-change cases; distinguish consultation from implemented change; examine recurrence and competing explanations. The spiral is weakened by durable change through ordinary due process or lack of the predicted recurrence.

## 1.5 The Withdrawn Legibility Compression Principle and Selective Representation

The earlier Legibility Compression Principle asserted universal irreversible loss and crisis accumulation. Those claims are withdrawn. Representation can be lossless for the relevant task even when other distinctions are omitted; the relevant information may also remain accessible elsewhere (Appendix C).

A multinational's operations, innovation, workforce, emissions, supplier relationships and geopolitical exposure cannot all be described exhaustively in a convenient report. Selection is part of producing comparable, auditable and useful information. It is not automatically a defect. A credit analyst may nevertheless need reliable hydrological evidence that a financial report does not contain. A biodiversity impact or a worker safety problem may continue independently of whether it is recorded. Omission is neither necessary nor sufficient for physical harm; decisions, enforcement and counterfactual response matter.

The original cited Volkswagen emissions and Boeing safety failures as examples of crises arising outside a narrow accounting perspective. Treat these as causal questions, not established examples of accounting-induced failure: what was known, by whom, through which channels, what authority existed and whether better reporting would have changed the outcomes? The same questions apply to stranded assets and supply-chain disruptions.

## 1.6 Transaction Primacy vs. Relational Reality

Accounting's transaction-event model is particularly useful for ownership transfers, contracts and monetized flows. Double-entry bookkeeping provides an enduring way of recording interrelated transactions. A sale, an obligation and an acquisition can be recorded and reconciled. This does not mean all accounting information is transactional; estimates, accrued obligations and other disclosures can represent more than discrete exchanges.

Trust, cohesion, biodiversity, legitimacy and tacit knowledge develop through interactions over time. They may not fit the same recognition and assurance criteria as cash. Their relevance and possible representation depend on the decision and evidence: turnover records, independent ecological observations, worker accounts and community-controlled records might answer different questions. Merely adding a balance-sheet line need not address a relational issue; a separate record or action may be preferable.

The proposal to build channels that register the continuous, emergent and relational must respect §2.14 and the safeguards in §3.1. Relations are not automatically capital, and legitimate knowledge refusal is not missing data to be extracted. Communities may decline measurement, categorization, valuation or translation; no claim of entitlement follows from the fact that evidence would be useful.

## 1.7 Standards as Compressed Civilization-Memory Systems

Financial statements can be understood as one of civilization's memory systems. Accounting conventions shape what is retained in a comparable form over time. Capitalizing an item can keep a carrying amount visible across reporting periods, while an expense enters the period's costs. Neither treatment determines whether the organization retains training histories, research notes or community investment records in other systems. Capitalization and depreciation depend on the applicable asset and rules; not every asset is depreciated.

The memory question matters when an investor or manager must distinguish current expenses from investments whose benefits or harms emerge over a longer horizon. An accounting convention could influence incentives to invest in training or restoration, but this requires evidence about actual decisions, other records and causal alternatives. Not recording an ecosystem service as a corporate asset does not imply that ecosystem loss cannot be tracked, valued or addressed through independent environmental accounts or public action.

The standard-setter is therefore a curator of one strand of economic memory, not the owner of civilization's memory. Expanding that strand may be useful only where authorized and proportionate. Collective memory and sacred or relational knowledge must remain under appropriate community control; recognition may help or harm, and refusal must not be treated as an invitation to infer protected contents.

## 1.8 The Observability Threshold in Capital Markets

The earlier Observability Threshold asserted a universal failure threshold at SNR = 1. That claim is withdrawn. For a specified real scalar additive Gaussian channel the capacity per use is one half log base two of (1 + SNR), hence 0.5 bit at SNR = 1; this is not a universal decision threshold (Appendix C).

Intangible assets, human skills, network effects, brand and ecosystem dependencies may matter for future corporate performance. The original cited a change from approximately 17% in 1975 to over 80% in a later period; this is an unverified historical lead whose source, sample, dates and denominator require checking. A market-to-book residual is not a direct estimate of intangible assets, let alone signal power, noise power or the percentage of relevant information observed. No inference about investor channel fidelity follows from it.

The useful question is whether particular data improve a defined forecast or decision relative to the best existing information. Neither a difference between market and book value nor a high-quality audit of a financial statement establishes the adequacy of the wider evidence environment.

## 1.9 The Cascading Variety Gap

Accounting data can travel through banks, supervisors, investors, rating agencies, courts and statistical offices. A common error or omission may propagate when users depend on the same inputs and cannot substitute other evidence. H3 asks whether such a chain actually exists for the task in question.

A bank supervisor may draw on borrower financial statements alongside exposure reports and physical-risk models; a court may use accounting evidence alongside expert testimony and other legally admissible evidence. Sustainability ratings may supplement financial reports effectively or introduce inconsistency and burden. National accounts have their own purposes and rules; their treatment of unpaid care or ecosystem services cannot be inferred directly from corporate accounting. These pathways require separate investigation.

The historical Compounding Failure Tax does not establish that failures necessarily multiply or that combined harms exceed the sum of individual harms. Under the specified sequential retention model the retained fractions multiply, and total fractional loss is bounded above by the sum of individual fractional losses. Interaction in outcomes is a different question and must be measured against a common baseline (Appendix C).

## 1.10 The Twin Deficits

The twin-deficits framework distinguishes outer arrangements from inner cultural commitments. The following is a diagnostic heuristic, not measured deficits or professional diagnoses.

| Aspect | Outer arrangements | Inner operating system |
|---|---|---|
| Strengths | Comparable standards, assurance, transparent revision procedures, coordinated technical expertise | Commitment to rigor, stability, public service and protection from arbitrary interference |
| Questions | Are certain relevant distinctions unavailable, unreliable or late? Would linked disclosures, changed rules or independent evidence help? | Do mandate commitments, notions of neutrality or professional incentives impede a justified change, or protect a useful boundary? |
| Observable tests | Compare evidence pathways, costs, impacts and response across cases | Compare consultation access, decisions, reasons, actual changes and competing explanations |

The ISSB, financial and impact materiality, and investor needs research are potential cases for investigating these questions. Their status alone does not prove lock-in or a generalized deficit.

## 1.11 The Genuine Strengths

The achievement of internationally comparable financial reporting deserves recognition. Different national frameworks historically made comparisons more difficult; the IASC/IASB development and IFRS–US GAAP convergence are leads for verifying concrete changes in comparability and costs. Adoption and enforcement remain jurisdiction-specific. The technical skills needed to draft, implement and audit standards, and the transparency of due-process documentation, are genuine resources for institutional learning.

Those resources may explain why existing arrangements command support even when some users seek additional evidence. A stable common language has value and may be costly to replace. Professional pride can sustain rigor and is not itself evidence of resistance. A system designed for a particular task need not be obsolete merely because another task has emerged. The appropriate comparison includes the option of retaining the core with complementary evidence.

## 1.12 The Real Question

How can standards provide sufficiently reliable evidence for specified purposes, while preserving comparability, legitimate authority and plural knowledge? Should the task be served by a broader reporting objective, a linked disclosure, a targeted independent record, or no change? The answer depends on evidence rather than on a count of capitals. The mechanisms in Chapter 2 motivate these alternatives; Chapter 3 advocates designs and Chapter 5 outlines how to compare them.

## 1.13 Diagnosis tests (separate from intervention evaluation)

**H1 — Reporting adequacy.** Specify sector, user, decision, horizon, loss and relevant distinctions before observation (for example water stress and credit exposure). Compare mandatory reports, notes, voluntary disclosures, external sources and proposed additions. Assess prediction and decision performance against independent outcomes. Adequate current evidence or no incremental benefit weakens the scoped hypothesis. Separate missing evidence, access, inference, incentives, authority and action.

**H2 — Standards-setting spiral.** Predefine episodes and event coding (pressure, consultation, actual changes, implementation, outcomes). Compare successful, unsuccessful and no-change episodes, including legitimate due process, measurement difficulty, mandate scope and resources as rival explanations. Substantive reform without crisis, durable changes through consultation or absence of recurrence under unchanged arrangements disconfirm the predicted links. Rejection or delay alone is not capture.

**H3 — Cascading dependency.** Trace actual downstream data, substitution and decision pathways; compare reliance, outcomes and harms. Observational disconfirmers include traced downstream decisions that use substitute evidence and documented errors or omissions in shared inputs that do not propagate to those decisions. Compare otherwise comparable existing users with and without alternative evidence: no difference attributable to the proposed dependency weakens the specified pathway, subject to confounding and adequate evidence. Intervention effects belong in §5.3, not this diagnosis. Do not infer economy-wide fragility from shared inputs.

**H4 — Ontological colonization.** Define cases, eligibility, outcomes and permitted evidence with affected Indigenous peoples or other affected communities, distinguishing their legal status and self-defined institutions. Collective authorization is required, with no penalty for refusal, withdrawal and independent remedy. Compare community-controlled knowledge and decision channels with voluntarily authorized standardized recognition; no imposed treatment or collection of protected data to construct controls. Prespecify community-defined outcomes (knowledge authority, categories, land and resource decisions, continuity, distribution of benefits and harms), baselines and rival explanations. Recognition strengthening authority or accountability, inclusion sought on communities' own terms without loss of control, or unaffected independent records and practices can disconfirm the scoped harm hypothesis. Conclusions apply only to consenting cases, not all communities. Refusal or unavailable evidence is unknown, not evidence of harm or adequacy. Representation and disagreement within communities may be contested; the report cannot adjudicate legitimate representation. If authorization is unresolved, abstain or redesign. H4 is not a test of the Sandbox's success.

**H5 — Legitimacy–complexity inversion.** Measure complexity, reliance/trust and task adequacy separately for specified users, compare matched episodes or time periods, account for legal mandates, assurance quality, sector and alternative evidence. Null, negative or adequacy-explained associations weaken the hypothesis that reliance rises independently of adequacy. Correlation does not establish causation.

None of these protocols has been executed. Diagnosis and intervention evaluation remain separate: a successful intervention does not prove every diagnosis, and non-adoption does not establish obstruction.
