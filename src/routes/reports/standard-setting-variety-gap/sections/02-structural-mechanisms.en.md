## 2. Structural Mechanisms: Hypotheses About Reporting and Decision Boundaries

The following eighteen mechanisms are proposed explanations, not established causes. Each component of standard-setting serves legitimate functions. Whether it produces a harmful constraint depends on the task, other information pathways, authority, incentives and context. H1–H5 specify tests; no number of mechanisms constitutes proof.

### 2.1 What “Standard‑Setting Observability” Means

In control theory, observability refers to reconstruction of a specified state under a specified dynamics and observation model. A standard is not itself a controller, and a reporting system need not reconstruct the entire state of an enterprise. A user may require only a particular distinction or prediction to make a sound decision. Specify the available observation history, the task and the user's authority; a lack of full-state observability does not establish failure of that task.

For an investor assessing solvency, an audited balance sheet and cash-flow forecast may suffice. For a water authority deciding on extraction limits, physical water use and basin conditions may matter independently of financial position. For a worker concerned about safety, inspection and incident records may be appropriate. The same report can be adequate for one purpose and inadequate for another. The question of whether the reporting standard should change is separate from the question of which actor should obtain and act on the information. Appendix C gives conditional models, not a numerical score for these users.

### 2.2 Financial Statements as a Narrowband Sensor

Financial statements have a relatively specialized purpose. The balance sheet, income statement, cash-flow statement, changes in equity and notes supply structured information about financial position, performance and transactions. That specialization enables comparison and assurance; the narrowband sensor metaphor describes its limited remit, not a finding that it receives no information about other variables. Financially material sustainability risks can enter forecasts, impairment assessments or disclosures, depending on actual requirements and use.

Consider water scarcity in a supplier region. A financial statement may show cost increases or provisions after the effects have occurred. Hydrological records, supplier data and forward-looking disclosures might indicate the risk earlier. A useful test compares timeliness, accuracy and decisions with each source and their combination; a new metric is not necessarily better than independent evidence. Financial statements do not need to be a universal sensor. If an authorized supplementary channel supplies the necessary distinction reliably, a new recognition rule may add burden without benefit.

### 2.3 The Conceptual Framework as Constitutional Lock

The Conceptual Framework gives a common basis for accounting concepts and the objective of general-purpose financial reporting. The original report treats its focus on capital providers as a constitutional lock. A change in the intended users or purpose may indeed require more than a new disclosure, especially where it conflicts with existing definitions and measurement principles. But the exact relationship between the Framework and authoritative standards, the processes for amendment and the powers of each standard-setter and jurisdiction require verification.

An independent impact report could coexist with financial reporting, and a financial report may already incorporate some longer-term risks. The narrower objective is not itself a sensor, nor is it intrinsically illegitimate. The lock hypothesis would be supported by cases where a justified change is prevented specifically by the foundational objective, not merely delayed by measurement uncertainty or a decision that another institution is competent. A revision through ordinary procedures or complementary evidence that meets the task would weaken the diagnosis.

### 2.4 Due Process as Absorption Mechanism

Agenda consultation, research, exposure drafts, hearings, field tests and re-deliberation allow preparers, auditors, investors and others to raise objections and improve a proposed standard. The hypothesis is that, in some cases, the duration and distribution of influence in these procedures dissipate pressure without resolving the problem that prompted it. The original cites the path from early sustainability-reporting initiatives through the IIRC (2010), ISSB (2021) and initial standards (2023). Dates, causal sequence and actual implementation are verification leads.

Time is not a sufficient indicator of absorption. A hurried accounting rule can create costly or misleading results; consultation may reveal a substantive problem and legitimately change or defeat a proposal. H2 compares participation, access, reasons, milestones and outcomes, including durable reforms outside crisis periods. A concern about resource asymmetry is distinct from an allegation of obstruction. Examine whose evidence was heard, what changed and whether expedited or complementary measures could have achieved better results with appropriate accountability.

### 2.5 Single Materiality as Dimensionality Filter

Materiality connects a disclosure requirement to its intended users and decisions. The original contrasts financial materiality (information relevant to capital providers) with impact materiality (effects on people and the environment). The exact IASB, FASB and ISSB definitions and legal scope must be verified; not all financially material sustainability information is excluded from financial reporting. The distinction is about purpose, not a dimensionality count.

An impact may be important to affected people even if it has not yet become a financial risk to the company. For instance, a slow change in water quality or a diffuse ecosystem impact might matter to a public authority independently of whether a provision is recognized. Conversely a financially material climate risk can be relevant within the existing purpose. The EU's double-materiality approach under CSRD/ESRS is a lead for comparing disclosure of both types of relevance; current scope, amendments and dates need verification. Test whether separate or integrated disclosure improves actual decisions, including impact outcomes, without presuming that either should be the sole global standard.

### 2.6 The Measurement Trap and the Verification Asymmetry

Recognition, measurement, assurance and disclosure are distinct steps. The original's quoted “measurable with reasonable certainty” formulation needs checking against applicable rules and versions; recognition does not invariably require the same type of precision. Cash, contractual claims and physical assets can often be supported by different evidence from workforce health, reputation, ecosystem dependency or social license. The evidence also varies within those categories.

Verification asymmetry is the hypothesis that practices optimized for familiar evidence make it more difficult for alternative evidence to gain legitimate recognition, even where it would improve a task. Rigorous assurance is itself valuable. Some requested quantities may simply not be reliable or appropriate; the answer could be ranges, physical measures, qualitative accounts, an independent record or no disclosure. The cost of false confidence is part of the comparison. No new measure should imply ownership or require a monetary equivalent of protected relational values (§3.1). The test is whether an alternative evidence and assurance procedure supplies useful information at defensible cost and risk, not whether rigor is reduced.

### 2.7 The Reflexivity Trap

Investor surveys, market-reaction studies and consultations provide important information about user needs. The reflexivity hypothesis is that familiarity with existing disclosures can influence what users request and what researchers can measure. If a valuation model uses only the familiar accounting inputs, a survey of that model's users may understate demand for a genuinely useful alternative. It does not follow that investors cannot imagine or access other evidence.

Compare elicited needs before and after exposure to credible independent evidence; observe actual decisions, predictive performance and burden. Include sophisticated users who already employ external sources and users who find existing disclosures sufficient. A lack of incremental benefit from additional evidence weakens the proposed gap. The Audit would observe aspects of the reporting process that current studies might not capture, not assume the observer is epistemically closed. Its own methods are subject to the same critique, including H4 and legitimate refusal.

### 2.8 The ISSB as Symbolic Expansion

The creation of the ISSB in 2021 was a significant institutional development in the original narrative; IFRS S1 and S2 in 2023 are leads for verification. A sustainability standards board alongside the IASB can expand information without changing the financial reporting objective. The question is whether that separation produces an ineffective supplement, an effective division of labour, or additional costs and inconsistencies. Verify jurisdiction-specific adoption, scope and later developments before treating any May 2026 description as current.

Symbolic expansion describes the possibility that apparent reform relieves pressure while the underlying problem persists. It is not a label that follows automatically from a separate board or an investor-oriented standard. Financially material sustainability information may change decisions even if impact reporting remains a separate task. Compare implementation, actual use, outcomes and reasons for keeping distinct mandates. H2 permits substantive improvement without constitutional change; such evidence would weaken the hypothesized spiral.

### 2.9 Audit as Observation Channel Gatekeeper

Auditors provide assurance within a defined engagement and applicable standards. The original names Deloitte, PwC, EY and KPMG as major firms; market concentration, jurisdictional coverage and liability incentives are leads for verification, not findings of misconduct. The audit opinion is not a guarantee of viability or sustainability, and the profession does not alone determine reporting law or decisions made from audited information.

The gatekeeper hypothesis is that feasibility of assurance influences what standard-setters require. Expertise, training, liability exposure, verification costs and demand may create incentives for retaining familiar measurements, but those incentives could also support expansion into new assurance services. Compare evidence for both mechanisms. An audit practice that rejects unreliable ecological valuation may be performing its proper function; an alternative assurance method might nevertheless validate specific physical or qualitative evidence. The appropriate comparison includes independent specialists and community-controlled evidence with appropriate authorization, not simply a demand that financial auditors certify every domain.

### 2.10 The Monoculture Fragility Paradox

Harmonization can reduce cross-border comparison costs and provide a shared language for investors and regulators. The original cites widespread IFRS adoption and the potential effect on the cost of capital; exact coverage and causal evidence need checking. The same shared language can create common exposure to a particular mismeasurement if downstream users all rely on it and lack independent checks.

The 2008 crisis is a lead for examining consolidation, fair value, structured instruments and common exposures. It is not an established accounting monoculture event: investigate leverage, supervision, incentives, asset correlations and differing standards or implementation. A shared reporting rule could also have improved recognition relative to fragmented alternatives. Compare common errors, local errors, actual substitution pathways and containment. Harmonization and diversity each have costs and benefits. Local differences may serve as firewalls in a particular pathway, or make comparisons less reliable; H3 requires evidence about the pathway rather than a claim of synchronized global blindness.

### 2.11 The Boundary Shell Game

Reporting rules define the perimeter of an entity and the conditions for recognition of assets and liabilities. Consolidation is necessary to produce interpretable accounts, and distinct entities may reflect real economic and legal boundaries. Where benefits or risks remain with an entity while a structure changes their reported treatment, the boundary may create opportunities for arbitrage.

The original cites off-balance-sheet joint ventures, special purpose entities, shadow banking and transfers of emissions-intensive assets as leads. A reduction in disclosed exposure after a transfer is not necessarily a reduction in physical emissions or economic risk, but it may be a genuine change in responsibility. Trace ownership, contractual commitments, retained risk, enforcement and other supervisory records; compare before and after and identify the affected decision. A new disclosure can close one opportunity while another emerges, or enforcement and incentive changes can remove the gradient. Treat boundary migration as a hypothesis, not a universal law or accusation against a firm.

### 2.12 The Medium is the Bottleneck

Periodic annual and interim statements, written standards and explanatory notes serve important purposes: a stable record, human review, reconciliation and assurance at known times. Some economic activities, such as logistics, clearing and operational monitoring, produce more frequent digital data. A mismatch in reporting latency or format may matter for decisions that require timely information, but it need not matter for every purpose.

Machine-readable filings, including XBRL, and APIs may reduce transcription errors and permit automated comparison. They can also amplify errors, impose security and assurance burdens, create privacy risks or make a weak metric appear more precise. Compare current filing regimes, reporting frequency, data quality and user decisions rather than equating PDF with blindness. Forward-looking estimates need explicit uncertainty and appropriate verification regardless of format.

The original's Stage Yellow versus Blue/Orange metaphor expresses a preference for adaptable, interoperable architecture (Appendix A); it does not validate an API design. A static document may be the right public record; a restricted data interface may serve a separate task. Authorization, community control and refusal apply even when information is technically machine-readable.

### 2.13 The Permanent Frontier Dynamic

Precise boundaries can create an incentive to structure transactions just inside or outside a criterion. A rule that changes recognition costs may affect how a contract is written, and better-resourced actors may respond more readily. Structured investment vehicles and synthetic instruments are historical leads for studying that process, not proof that all standards create systemic arbitrage.

The permanent frontier metaphor directs attention to monitoring changes in behaviour after a rule is introduced. Track the relevant exposures, enforcement and outcomes, including cases where a revised standard eliminates an incentive or closes a loophole without a harmful replacement. A more detailed framework may create more categories to exploit, but may also improve supervision or eliminate ambiguity. Do not infer inevitable migration or crisis. The practical implication is to design revision, feedback and sunset mechanisms with a specified monitoring purpose, not to seek unbounded observation.

### 2.14 Ontological Colonization

The original uses *ontological colonization* to criticize the possibility that externally imposed reporting categories strip knowledge of context and authority. That is a hypothesis about specified coercive uses of standardization, not the essence of every standard. Standards can be voluntary, layered, scoped or defined by the people using them. The absence of a category in one official record does not erase the knowledge, its holders or other records.

Consider a reporting rule that re-expresses a community's relationship with land solely as a monetary asset or an ecosystem-service metric. If the category is imposed, the authorized decision-makers may lose control of how their knowledge is interpreted, who uses it and for what purpose. The original used the metaphors of the “voice of the forest” and long-standing Indigenous stewardship to make this argument; they are the report's metaphors, not testimony or a representation of particular peoples. There is no documented affected-community consultation in the available source. Recognition can also strengthen land-rights claims, accountability or authority where sought on communities' own terms. Whether particular Indigenous organizations seek inclusion in a framework is a question to verify with them, not presume.

The critique applies to the report's own remedies. Turning relationships into capitals, requiring SEEA valuation, sensing lands or supply chains, and automatically mapping knowledge to a global taxonomy could reproduce the same reduction. Neither omission nor forced inclusion is presumptively beneficial. For Indigenous lands, knowledge or data, the proposed safeguards require collective FPIC through the peoples' own institutions, distinct from individual privacy and consultation, together with refusal, non-legibility, community control, benefit-sharing and remedy. Other local communities have distinct status and appropriate authorization must be established rather than assumed identical. Representation and internal disagreement can be contested; this report does not decide them. If authority is unresolved, redesign or stop. §3.1 describes the design constraints, H4 tests the scoped harm hypothesis with consenting communities only, and Chapter 5 tests the proposals separately.

### 2.15 ISO as Hidden Governance

ISO standards exemplify another route by which technical specifications affect governance. The original cites ISO 9001 quality management and the figure “over a million” organizations, alongside environmental and other management standards. Verify the denominator, year, certification figures and committee and national-member governance. ISO standard-setting is not the same institutional process as IASB or FASB accounting standard-setting.

A nominally voluntary specification may become consequential through procurement, certification, contractual requirements or incorporation into law. This can enable coordination, safety and quality; it can also make the cost of compliance and participation unequally distributed. Study the actual chain from committee to adoption to enforcement. A certification does not itself establish every aspect of quality, and a noncertified organization may meet the relevant task. The hidden governance question concerns which actor authorizes which obligation and how affected parties can challenge it, not a finding that all technical standards operate covertly.

### 2.16 The Legitimacy‑Complexity Inversion

The legitimacy–complexity inversion is a proposed counterintuitive mechanism. As firms become harder to assess, a simplified, certified representation may become more attractive because it reduces the burden of independent investigation. At the same time, some task-relevant distinctions may be harder to infer from it. These are separate empirical propositions, not an inverse law of legitimacy and adequacy.

An auditor's opinion is carefully scoped to financial statements under the applicable framework. Users may misread it as a general signal of corporate health, or they may understand the limitation and rationally combine it with other evidence. Increasing reliance may reflect actual improvements in reporting quality or legal requirements, not misplaced trust. A decline in reliance as complexity increases is also possible.

H5 measures complexity, reliance and adequacy independently. Compare comparable users or episodes and control for assurance quality, sector, legal mandates and alternatives. A null or negative relationship, or one explained by adequate information, weakens the proposed mechanism. Do not infer causal lock-in from reliance alone.

### 2.17 The Cultural Operating System: Investor Primacy, the Pretence of Objectivity, and Professional Identity

Three cultural lenses may help investigate the institutional response to proposed changes. They are interpretive hypotheses about practices, not a diagnosis of the psychology of professional groups.

**Investor primacy as anchor narrative.** The argument that efficient capital allocation serves society is a coherent public-interest rationale for a specialized financial reporting mandate. It may give less attention to distributional and external impacts that do not enter financial decisions. Test how the rationale is used in actual reasoning, and distinguish the reporting objective from company law and political goals. A limited mandate may be a legitimate division of labour.

**The pretence of objectivity.** Technical rigor is real, but choices about purpose, definitions and acceptable evidence have normative implications. The phrase identifies the risk of treating contestable purposes as purely technical necessities. It does not establish that practitioners deny values or act in bad faith. A response may be to disclose and deliberate over purposes while preserving independent assurance.

**Professional identity as perceptual constraint.** Training and accumulated expertise can make unfamiliar approaches difficult to assess. They also provide the capacity to improve standards and identify unreliable innovations. Evidence of both responses matters; a reasoned objection may reveal a design flaw. The hypothesis is that these narratives and incentives can interact with institutional procedures to impede warranted changes, not that the profession necessarily resists change.

### 2.18 How the Mechanisms Reinforce Each Other — and Fuel the Spiral

The mechanism list suggests a feedback structure. A defined reporting objective influences the information requested and the evidentiary rules. Those rules shape the information used by some analysts and researchers; their experience may feed back into future agenda choices. Consultation may modify a proposal, delay it or produce substantive improvement. Shared reporting and assurance may spread a common error where users rely on it without alternatives. Boundaries may change the incentives to report an exposure. Timing and format may influence whether users can act on evidence.

These links can fail independently. A separate sustainability standard can improve decisions, an auditor can support new evidence, and a supervisor can compensate for a reporting omission. Professional norms may reinforce stability or enable adaptation. The complexity–reliance hypothesis and the ontological-colonization hypothesis do not follow from one another. The latter can constrain even an otherwise effective intervention. No general multiplication law, inevitable tightening spiral or timetable for crisis follows from the list.

Compare the predicted feedback and alternative explanations using H1–H5. If evidence identifies a specific problem, match the remedy to it: delayed information, missing evidence, distorted incentives, insufficient action authority and coercive collection are different problems. More reporting is only one possible answer.
