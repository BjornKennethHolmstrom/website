## 5. A Concrete First Step: The Multi-Capital Materiality Audit and the Integrated Reporting Sandbox

The three proposals are a research and deliberation programme. They target reflexivity, reporting design and review procedures at different levels. A transparent parallel initiative may be useful where authorized, but it must not evade existing duties, manufacture pressure for a predetermined amendment or conceal its aims. No outcome is required to be positive.

### 5.1 Proposal: Three Parallel Innovations

**The Multi-Capital Materiality Audit (Requisite Variety Audit for Standard-Setting).**

The Audit would be an independent, securely funded assessment of reporting adequacy for specified sectors, users and decisions, with public methods and scheduled reporting. The original Multi-Capital Materiality Gap Score (MCMGS) is withdrawn as an unvalidated count-based instrument, distinct from the withdrawn Paper VIII index. Neither a count of capitals nor an estimated dimensionality difference is a certificate. Instead record a prospective profile on five axes: evaluation, observation, modelling, decision and actuation (corrected VIII §5). For example, credit exposure to water scarcity might require an independently observed outcome, actual evidence channels, a calibrated prediction, a lending decision and an available response. Each axis may be adequate, inadequate, contested or unknown against predeclared task criteria.

Develop the methodology with standard-setters, preparers, auditors, investors and civil society, and where relevant with collectively authorized community participants; document disagreements and conflicts rather than presume a common purpose.

For each sector and entity class, specify the users, decisions, relevant distinctions and uncertainty; record mandatory requirements, notes, voluntary and external evidence separately from actual availability and use. Identify alternative channels and gaps, evidence quality, costs, affected parties and rights. The six-capital framework is a candidate organizing device, not a required record for each entity or community. An independently measured improvement in decisions or impacts must be distinguished from additional categories, recognition or uptake. A profile can identify a question without prescribing an amendment. No universal trend or composite score is used; any proposed task-specific metric would require separate validation and preference choices.

A statutory entity-data access right could be considered only within a defined lawful mandate, necessity and purpose limitation, minimization, confidentiality, access controls, retention limits, independent oversight, appeal and applicable consent. The Audit cannot acquire authority by naming itself independent. Publish aggregate methods and results only with authorized disclosure and re-identification safeguards. §3.1 applies before collection: corporate or statutory permission is not collective permission. Include community-defined indicators of knowledge authority, coerced categorization or valuation, extractive sensing, unauthorized reuse and distribution of benefits and harms with consenting communities. Respect refusal; missing evidence is unknown. No protected data is demanded to demonstrate harm. Benefit-sharing, independent complaint and remedy, withdrawal and stop procedures must be agreed before relevant work proceeds. The Audit targets the reflexivity hypothesis, but its own evidence practices are subject to the same tests.

**The Integrated Reporting Sandbox.**

A cohort of willing corporations, public-sector organizations and financial institutions would test alternative disclosure arrangements. Initially participants retain all existing reporting obligations; any later safe harbour or actual waiver requires specific lawful authorization from the competent authority, transparency, time limits and protection of investors, workers, communities and nonparticipants. This resolves the original contradiction between exemption and continuing obligations. The experiment does not require integrated reporting to become primary.

- **Cohort:** aim for at least fifty diverse entities within two years, across jurisdictions, sectors, size and exposure categories, subject to feasibility and voluntary participation. Specify a proposed minimum observation period for useful longitudinal comparison, agreed before enrolment, without restricting free withdrawal or imposing penalties. Specify selection and exit rules; no participation pressure.
- **Reporting alternatives:** compare existing arrangements, additive or linked disclosures, integrated presentation and targeted measures. Draw where justified on the Integrated Reporting Framework, validated SEEA-derived measures, impact materiality, scenarios, human-capital and social-impact reporting approaches, and supply-chain due diligence; verify their applicability. No compulsory six-capital classification, monetization or community sensing. No additional reporting is a legitimate comparison.
- **Authorization:** apply §3.1 before enrolment or data collection. Entity consent is distinct from collective FPIC for relevant Indigenous lands, knowledge or data and appropriate authority for other communities. Establish community-defined harm endpoints, baselines, monitoring, confidential complaint, independent remedy, benefit-sharing, withdrawal and stop conditions. Unresolved representation or authority requires abstention or redesign; do not collect protected data to create controls or expose it for replicability.
- **Evaluation:** an independent consortium with relevant accounting, financial, environmental, social and community-authorized expertise would preregister decisions and outcome metrics, including decision accuracy against independent results, early risk detection and false positives, verified impacts, costs, assurance, trust and distributional harms. Compare baselines; use randomized assignment where practicable and defensible quasi-experimental designs otherwise. Matching alone does not establish causation. Specify confounding, selection, attrition, missingness, uncertainty and stopping rules. Null or adverse results, refusal and discontinuation are legitimate. Diagnosis H4 is separate from the Sandbox harm evaluation.
- **Public reporting:** annual interim and final reports with methods, uncertainty, limitations, aggregate authorized results and independent oversight. Protected knowledge remains protected even where reproducibility is restricted; report the limitation without disclosure.
- **Sunset:** five-year initial phase, renewable once only after justified review; stop or narrow earlier for harms, cost or poor evidence. At sunset submit results for competent authorities' consideration, not automatic adoption. Maintaining existing arrangements may be the preferred decision.

**The Conceptual Framework Review Procedure (formerly Review Trigger).**

The original proposed an automatic two-period Gap Score threshold obligating the IASB. That rule is withdrawn. Instead propose a reasoned evidence petition and review procedure that the competent bodies could adopt under their own authority. IASB, FASB and jurisdictions have distinct powers; a coalition cannot impose a review obligation. Task-specific validated triggers could be piloted with predeclared criteria, uncertainty and review rights; none automatically establishes amendment.

Retain the proposed expedited timetable (for example two years from authorized initiation), a public determination with reasons, and an independent advisory panel including relevant stakeholders and expertise beyond preparers and investors, such as ecological economists, human-capital specialists, civil-society and future-generations advocates. These are illustrative expertise categories, not a claim of authority to represent affected peoples. Establish appropriate representation without claiming the panel represents all communities. Require periodic evaluation of the procedure itself. Options include confirmation of the current objective, targeted amendment, a new consultation or further research. Any actual change follows the competent body's lawful procedure. No petition, majority, public-interest assertion or metric substitutes for relevant collective authorization. If the proposal cannot satisfy safeguards, reconsider or discontinue it.

### 5.2 Selection Criteria and Political Strategy

The Audit targets the possibility that existing evidence studies miss relevant alternatives (reflexivity) or that reliance becomes detached from task adequacy. Its value depends on the validity and usefulness of its evidence, not whether its findings compel reform. The Sandbox targets the question of whether alternative presentations and media produce better decisions at acceptable cost and harm. The Review Procedure targets the question of whether evidence receives timely, reasoned consideration under authorized procedures.

The initiative is intended as a complement to the IASB, not a finding that it has failed. A standard-setter's consensus process may have difficulty producing controlled evidence across alternative reporting designs; independent research might supply such evidence without claiming authority over amendment. The ISSB parallel sustainability-disclosure track is a comparison lead: whether integrated presentation, linked reports or existing separate presentation changes actual decisions is untested here. The Sandbox provides a way to learn about this question; either a benefit or no incremental benefit is informative, subject to identification and uncertainty.

The bypass-trap metaphor describes a possibility: a successful voluntary complement could reduce pressure for a broader reform even where the broader reform would be justified. Its converse is equally important: a sufficient complement may be exactly the desired outcome, and pressing for replacement could introduce needless burden or coercion. Transparent parallel research can generate evidence, but may not circumvent scrutiny or hide its reform intentions. Obtain prior approvals and make the intended comparisons clear. Independent evidence may support the IASB's current approach, not just challenge it.

Potential participants include long-horizon asset owners such as pension and sovereign wealth funds, entities already experimenting with integrated reports, interested jurisdictions and relevant civil society actors. Their participation is not evidence of representativeness or authority over communities. Assess selection bias, conflicts, costs and distribution of benefits, and include legitimate alternatives rather than assemble only advocates. §3.1 applies to each pathway.

### 5.3 Metrics

**Process indicators** (not independent outcome measures): first Audit report within two years and annually thereafter; citations in investor and regulatory documents, inclusion in a standard-setter's evidence base within three years where feasible, media coverage; Sandbox recruitment (target fifty within two years), annual reports, retention and voluntary diffusion; establishment or consideration of a Review Procedure, reasoned engagement and possible review within five years if adopted. The former Gap Score's recognition as a metric is not validation; that score is withdrawn.

**Outcome evaluation:** preregister tasks, comparison groups or counterfactual design, time horizon, tolerable costs and harms, uncertainty and minimum meaningful effects. Assess independently observed decision quality, prediction calibration, material risk detection with false positives, verified impacts, reporting and assurance costs, burden, trust and distributional effects. Cost of capital is an outcome to interpret cautiously, not a measure of total wellbeing. Document alternative evidence and action authority; no improvement could reflect an adequate baseline, an unsuitable metric, or other constraints. For community-authorized cases use community-defined knowledge control, authority, harms and benefits; retain rights to stop and withhold data. Do not trade away a violated rights constraint for a higher average benefit. Missing or refused observations remain unknown.

Use baseline and follow-up evidence with prespecified selection, confounding and attrition strategies; compare targeted, additive, integrated and existing alternatives. Report estimates, uncertainty and sensitivity to assumptions. Matched controls alone are not causal identification. A null, adverse or insufficiently identified result may justify no expansion, redesign or termination. H1–H5 diagnosis tests are independent of whether a proposal is adopted. A decline in a score, more categories, adoption or press coverage is not proof of benefit. Failure to adopt or lack of improvement does not establish immune-system absorption.

### 5.4 The Honest Caveat

The Audit, Sandbox and Review Procedure are proposals, not institutions already authorized or implemented. Independent research can proceed only within its legal and ethical scope; formal changes to financial reporting require the competent authorities' decisions, and protected knowledge requires the relevant collective authorization. It is possible that the IASB and FASB decline a petition because evidence is insufficient or existing arrangements meet the task; that can be a legitimate decision. There is also a distinct possible failure mode: genuine procedural absorption. Participants may revert after an initial commitment, or review provisions may be diluted or delayed in ways that prevent relevant evidence from receiving substantive consideration. Such observations are leads, not proof of obstruction: H2's predeclared coding of evidence, reasons, procedural steps and rival explanations must distinguish absorption from legitimate decline, resource constraints, better alternatives or adverse findings. A trial may fail to recruit or yield adverse effects. The response should be to examine evidence and redesign or stop, not classify every outcome as obstruction.

The fragments of the original proposal—integrated reporting, natural-capital accounts, double materiality and interoperable interfaces—are leads worth investigating, not proof of feasibility or desirability of the combined system. The wager is that specific changes may produce enough independent benefit to justify their costs, without reproducing the harms described in §2.14. Existing arrangements or a narrower intervention may be preferable. The question is whether a transparent, authorized comparison can help identify which path is warranted.
