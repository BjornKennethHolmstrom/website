## Appendix A: Value Systems and Policy Mindsets — A Guide for the Standard‑Setting Context

### A Note on This Appendix

This appendix offers an interpretive lens drawn from Spiral Dynamics integral theory, not a validated account of institutional development. The four portraits reconstruct the original author's report framing; they are unaudited, optional and not classifications of individuals, societies or Indigenous peoples.

### A.1 The Basic Insight

Different professional commitments can help explain why a proposal seems attractive or problematic. Order, efficiency, care and integration can coexist in one organization or person. The lenses are questions about values and practices, not measured stages or necessary constraints on institutional development.

### A.2 The Value Systems in the Standard‑Setting Arena

**Order and Stability (Blue) — the rule-based foundation.** Double-entry records, consistency, due process and independent assurance reflect values of order and accountability in the original portrait. They preserve memory, fairness and trust. Overemphasis on procedure or rigid categories could impede warranted changes, but careful procedure can also prevent errors and protect rights. The Conceptual Framework is a useful case for examining the relationship between stability and amendment.

**Achievement and Efficiency (Orange) — the capital-market orientation.** Decision-usefulness to capital providers, quantitative evidence and comparability can support allocation and innovation. The original portrait asks whether a focus on market-relevant information leaves other impacts inadequately represented. Prices may be useful but not sufficient measures for every purpose. This is not a finding that market-oriented institutions ignore all social or ecological concerns.

**Inclusion and Care (Green) — sustainability and stakeholder inclusion.** Concern for wellbeing, dignity and ecological integrity motivates proposals for impact reporting and wider participation. Such concern can correct omissions; additional requirements can also be duplicative, poorly specified or imposed on those they aim to help. The dual-reality hypothesis and §2.14 constrain this approach. Inclusion is not a substitute for collective authorization.

**Integrative and Systemic (Yellow) — the proposed multi-capital and networked architecture.** The original preference emphasizes interdependence, plural frameworks, flexible experimentation and uncertainty. These are advocated design values, not a formal warrant or higher developmental rank. Complexity, administrative burden and political feasibility are possible weaknesses. A shared taxonomy can impose categories; authorized partial mappings or no mapping may be preferable. §3.1 governs the proposed architecture, including refusal, non-legibility and community control. No value-system interpretation justifies collecting protected knowledge.

### A.3 The Standard‑Setting Variety Gap as a Value‑System Clash

The Blue–Orange–Green–Yellow framing suggests questions about stability, efficiency, inclusion and coordination. The ISSB and integrated reporting debates may be examined through those questions, but their creation does not establish a developmental clash or an inevitable transition. The proposed Audit, Sandbox and Review Procedure are opportunities to test designs on their merits, not mechanisms to advance a privileged stage. Existing arrangements or alternative designs may be justified.
