## Appendix B: International Analogues and Precedents

These five analogues are historical leads from the May 2026 report. Their exact status, scope, implementation and effects require verification. None is a certificate for transferring a design to a different institution.

### B.1 The European Union: Double Materiality and the CSRD

The original cites the EU's Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (2022) and European Sustainability Reporting Standards, including double materiality and assurance, as a regional example of mandatory impact and financial sustainability disclosure. Verify the applicable dates, scope, EFRAG role, assurance requirements and subsequent changes. Compare actual burden, use, decision quality and impact outcomes; do not infer feasibility at all scales or global authorization. Distinguish EU law from IASB standard-setting.

### B.2 The International Integrated Reporting Framework

The original traces the International Integrated Reporting Framework from the IIRC to the IFRS Foundation and identifies its six capitals: financial, manufactured, intellectual, human, social and relationship, and natural. Verify organizational status, adoption and claimed benefits. Integrated thinking and presentation may help connect strategy and performance, but a voluntary framework is not evidence that it should replace mandatory statements. Categories are optional candidates under §3.1, not authority over protected knowledge.

### B.3 The UN System of Environmental‑Economic Accounting

SEEA is a lead for environmental-economic statistical accounts, including physical and monetary measures of stocks, flows and ecosystem services. Verify UN Statistical Commission adoption, intended scales and SEEA Ecosystem Accounting, and the relationship of business-oriented extensions or the Natural Capital Protocol to entity-level use. Statistical maturity does not validate attribution to individual firms, ownership or monetary valuation of all relationships. Apply §3.1: Indigenous lands, knowledge and data require relevant collective authorization; no coerced classification or monetisation of sacred or relational values, and a right to refuse. Community-controlled or non-monetary alternatives and no transfer are legitimate.

### B.4 The TCFD and TNFD

The original identifies the Financial Stability Board's TCFD and the TNFD as leads for climate- and nature-related disclosure. Verify their dated status, recommendations, incorporation into ISSB standards and actual jurisdictional adoption. Verify how TNFD, ISSB and ESRS address Indigenous peoples and local communities, without assuming either endorsement or opposition by them. Compare decision utility, actual impacts and costs, respecting §3.1. A structured disclosure framework is not proof of effectiveness or legitimate data access.

### B.5 XBRL and Machine‑Readable Reporting

XBRL is a lead for structured machine-readable financial reporting. Verify adoption, filing requirements, taxonomy scope and actual frequency of updates. A tagged filing differs from a continuous API; machine-readable exchange does not establish semantic equivalence, data quality or authorization. Consider how versioned tags, audit trails, permissions and partial mappings might support particular tasks, alongside security, burden and confidentiality. No technical precedent establishes the full proposed architecture.
