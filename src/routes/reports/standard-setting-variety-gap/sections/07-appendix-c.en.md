## Appendix C: The Governance as Engineering Connection

### C.1 The Architectural Foundation

The report draws on the Governance as Engineering series. Papers I–V are historical research formulations, not validated premises for the present report; their scope has not been independently audited here. Corrected VI, VIII and XX supply limited formal results and counterexamples. These motivate research questions, not certification of institutions. The previously withdrawn VIII index, universal score and band are not reinstated; the proposed MCMGS was a distinct unvalidated count-based instrument and is also withdrawn as a diagnostic certificate.

### C.2 The Core Results in Brief

**Task-relative adequacy (W1).** In the static noiseless linear setting of corrected VI, x = d + u with disturbance d in D, observation y = Cd and acceptable residual x in L, let D be the disturbance subspace, C the observation map and L the tolerated residual subspace. Define K = D ∩ ker C. K ⊆ L is necessary for exact task adequacy and sufficient when all of −D is available as corrections under the specified model. The hidden gap is h = dim K − dim(K ∩ L). The count bound max(0, dim D − dim(D ∩ L) − rank(C restricted to D)) is a lower bound, not an equality in general; a zero count is no certificate. Equal observation rank can conceal different relevant distinctions: in R² with L = span(e₂), observing x₁ gives h = 0 while observing x₂ gives h = 1. A redundant observation row changes a raw count without changing h. In R³ with L = span(e₂,e₃), observing x₁ gives rank 1 and h = 0, while observing x₂ and x₃ gives rank 2 and h = 1. A non-nested change in sensing can thus increase rank and worsen adequacy. Corrected XX’s action-cover bound is necessary, not sufficient: exact adequacy requires at least as many available actions as the minimum number of action-compatible sets needed to cover the admissible states; the actual observation partition, action feasibility and objective also matter. Accounting category counts do not establish any of these conditions.

**Conditional representation loss (W2, W5).** A channel loses a distinction relevant to a task if different relevant states produce the same available observation and no permitted alternative evidence resolves it. A financial statement identical in two cases with different water exposures may be inadequate for a specified water-risk decision; if the task is indifferent to exposure or reliable hydrological evidence is available separately, the conclusion changes. A scalar can encode four discrete states (for binary x,y use 2x+y); a low-dimensional label is not by itself proof of loss. The former universal Legibility Compression Principle and inevitable crisis inference are withdrawn. Omission is neither necessary nor sufficient for material harm: response, authority and underlying physical processes are separate.

**Signal quality (W4).** In the real scalar additive white Gaussian noise channel under its power constraint, capacity is ½ log₂(1+SNR) bits per use. At SNR = 1 this is 0.5 bit, not a universal observability or decision failure threshold. Market-to-book residuals are not signal or noise powers. No 17%→80% market-value series establishes reporting fidelity.

**Compounding Failure Tax (W6).** In the specified sequential retention model with fractions fᵢ lost at each stage, retained fraction is ∏(1−fᵢ) and total fractional loss is 1−∏(1−fᵢ) ≤ ∑fᵢ for 0 ≤ fᵢ ≤ 1. Two stages retaining one half each retain one quarter and lose three quarters, not more than the sum of individual fractional losses. A task requiring one fifth passes at one quarter; one requiring one third fails. This is not a general law of institutional harm. Measure interactions in outcomes against a common baseline: the joint effect minus the sum of separate effects can be positive or negative (for baseline 10, separate outcomes 12 and 13, joint outcome 14 gives −1; joint outcome 17 gives +2). Do not infer multiplication of costs from sequential loss.

**Goodhart–Ashby (W3).** The objective and the observation map are distinct. A proxy can tie outcomes with different true values, and even deterministic ranking comparisons may not establish safety for lotteries. For the finite set of outcomes (0,0), (0,1), (2,0), (2,1), take proxy M = a and true target T = a + b. Every strict deterministic proxy improvement improves T, although ties can differ. If lotteries are feasible, mixing (2,0) with probability 1/4 and (0,0) otherwise gives expected M = T = 1/2; the deterministic outcome (0,1) gives M = 0 and T = 1, reversing the proxy and target preference. Corrected XX's lottery reversal witness concerns the specified objective and feasible choices; it is not a claim that a scalar objective necessarily destroys sensing. Safety depends on the allowed comparison class and alignment of proxy and true objective. A larger observation space does not by itself fix incentives, objectives or available actions. No formal endorsement of a multi-capital mandate follows.

**Measurement and procedure (W7–W8).** A count or process indicator is not an independently measured outcome. An aggregate would require new validation and explicit preference weights. Better trial results do not automatically authorize an amendment; non-adoption is not evidence of obstruction; a coalition petition is not an IASB rule. The five-axis prospective profile in Chapter 5 is a diagnostic record, not a scalar score.

### C.3 The Standard-Setting Domain as a Research Case

Standards can influence several downstream institutions where those institutions depend on common evidence. The magnitude and direction of the effect, the availability of substitute channels and actual action authority require empirical testing. No highest-leverage or limiting-case theorem follows. The formal results motivate but do not certify the proposed institutional designs. Epistemic adequacy, legitimate authority and proportionality remain separate requirements; even a lossless representation confers no entitlement to collect or use it.
