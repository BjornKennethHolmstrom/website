## Appendix D: Anticipated Objections

These are substantive objections to the proposed programme. The answer to an objection may favor retaining existing arrangements; disagreement is not evidence of an immune response.

### D.1 "Accounting standards must be neutral. Expanding the observation channel politicises financial reporting."

A specialized financial mandate may protect technical rigor and guard against arbitrary policy pressure. The original counterargument is that selecting users, purposes and definitions is not value-free. That is a reason to make choices explicit, not a reason to abandon neutrality within a specified mandate. Impact and financial purposes may be served separately. Compare a broader mandate against complementary arrangements and preserve independent assurance. Changes require competent authorization, not a mathematical justification.

### D.2 "Expanding the observation channel will make financial reporting impossibly complex and costly."

The objection is material: data collection, assurance, interoperability and transition can impose real costs, particularly on small entities and communities. Duplicate sustainability and financial reporting may also be costly. Integration might reduce duplication or might increase it; neither net effect is established. Evaluate targeted, additive and integrated options against existing reporting using independently measured benefits, costs and uncertainty. If complexity exceeds benefit, simplify, narrow or stop. Rights and harm constraints apply independently of aggregate cost savings.

### D.3 "Investors don't need this information. Markets will sort it out."

Investor needs research can be influenced by familiarity with current reports, as the reflexivity hypothesis suggests. But investors may already use independent evidence, and existing reports may be adequate for a decision. Test users' decisions and predictive performance with credible alternative data, including users with no demand for additional information. A null result is evidence against the scoped hypothesis, not proof of capture. Some impact information may serve other legitimate purposes even if investors do not need it; those purposes and their authority should be stated openly.

### D.4 "Natural and human capital cannot be measured with sufficient rigour for financial reporting."

The measurement objection deserves serious attention. Trust, tacit knowledge and ecosystems do not have the same evidence structure as a cash balance. Graduated measurement could use independently observed physical measures, qualitative accounts or intervals; fair-value uncertainty disclosures offer an analogy to verify. Poorly defined metrics can be misleading even when uncertainty is reported. Evidence that fails the task should not be mandated simply to fill a category. Not all values should be monetized or recognized as corporate assets. Community-controlled evidence, refusal and no additional reporting may be appropriate under §3.1. Evaluate reliability and costs; a measurement failure may favor other evidence or action.

### D.5 "Global standards require global consensus. This is a European agenda."

International comparability is valuable, and no regional framework has automatic global authority. The original offers the EU experience and networked translation as ways to explore diversity; the precedents do not establish global feasibility. Partial mappings may lose distinctions or impose one ontology, and an injective mapping does not authorize use. Preserve local frameworks and unmapped categories, record losses and provenance, and respect collective authorization, refusal, benefit-sharing and community control under §3.1. Affected peoples may contest who represents them. Neither a global consensus nor an investor coalition substitutes for relevant permission. Compare limited interfaces, common standards and no mapping; some purposes may require separate governance.

### D.6 "The IASB's due process ensures all voices are heard."

Due process can provide genuine safeguards through transparent exposure drafts, reasons, consultation and field testing. Resource asymmetries may influence whose submissions receive attention or affect final rules; this is a hypothesis to test with participation, influence and actual outcomes, not an inference from duration. Representation of non-investor interests depends on the relevant mandate and procedures; verify the status of actual stakeholder channels. A reasoned rejection can be a legitimate result. The Review Procedure proposes an authorized route for submitting evidence, not a means of overriding due process. Collective authorization and individual consultation remain distinct.
