## 6. An Architectural Response: The Global Governance Frameworks

### 6.1 Two lines of inquiry and a conceptual alignment

The original synthesis connected the country-report diagnostics to the Global Governance Frameworks (GGF), a design programme for planetary coordination. Both use ideas from systems thinking, control theory and other interpretive traditions. The Treaty for Our Only Home, Genesis Protocol, Integrated Meta-Governance Framework and Indigenous governance proposals are named in the earlier report.

The overlap between proposed design concerns and named components is a textual alignment. The available documents do not establish that the reports and the GGF were developed independently, or that one was designed before the other. Shared assumptions, methods and author involvement could contribute to the convergence. Recover dated versions, prompts and source records if a stronger historical claim is to be made. This revision neither establishes nor denies such independence.

The alignment makes it possible to pose concrete design questions. It does not establish that the proposed architecture works, is sufficiently specified or costed, or is compatible with all relevant laws and rights. The current versions of the GGF were not supplied for this audit; the table describes historical proposals rather than current specifications.

### 6.2 Mapping candidate checks to GGF proposals

| Candidate concern | Historical GGF design response | Question for evaluation |
|---|---|---|
| Distributed sensing | Societal Sensing Dashboards; distributed sensemaking | Does the proposed channel improve access to actionable evidence while protecting confidentiality, rights and data quality? |
| Translation layers | Bioregional Autonomous Zones (BAZs); Coherence Regions | Who can adapt policy, which standards remain binding, and how are cross-boundary effects handled? |
| Fiscal autonomy matched to responsibility | AUBI; Hearts and Love Ledger; proposed multiyear settlements | Which public revenue, lawful spending powers, transfers and resources actually fund the mandate? |
| Deliberative infrastructure | Citizens' Assemblies; Global Citizens' Initiative | Who participates, what authority exists, and how are decisions, dissent and review handled? |
| Nested governance | Multilevel councils; Meta-Governance Coordination Council; Subsidiarity Protocol | Does the allocation of tasks improve outcomes relative to simpler arrangements, with what coordination cost? |
| Cross-border coherence | Coherence Regions; shared budgets and execution; voting and crisis protocols | What powers and consent are needed, and how are affected outsiders and shared risks protected? |
| Peripheral capacity-building | Trailblazer Regions; Cohesion Administration Fund; Community Weaver training | What resources and competencies are needed, and how will persistent unequal capacity be addressed? |
| Protection against capture | Accountable Coordination Principle; Governance Integrity System; Legitimacy Drift Detector; Office of the Adversary | Can independent actors detect, challenge and remedy misuse of power, and do the safeguards work under conflict? |

A correspondence of labels is not a feasibility witness: a named component can match a concern while lacking legal permission, funding or a necessary complementary mechanism. To evaluate a specific version, operationalise the requirements, set acceptance criteria, trace resources and authority, test interactions and compare alternatives. Unknown must remain an admissible result.

### 6.3 What the GGF adds as a proposal

The GGF proposes specific institutional forms: BAZs, deliberative bodies and councils at several scales. These are possible arrangements within a wider design space. A deliberative body can contribute evidence or advice without having binding power; where binding power is proposed, its legal mandate and accountability require separate examination. Territorial authority likewise depends on recognized rights, consent and the actual constitutional arrangement.

The economic proposals include Adaptive Universal Basic Income (AUBI), Hearts, Leaves, the Love Ledger and a Sovereign Debt Transformation Protocol. Recognition of care or ecological contribution, local exchange, personal economic security and public fiscal capacity are distinct. Complementary currencies and personal income support do not by themselves create authorized public revenue or spending capacity. A design evaluation should specify how obligations are funded and fulfilled, and distinguish record integrity from enforceable authority. Exit rights must be practically usable, not merely stated.

The original report also identifies a normative commitment to Indigenous sovereignty and self-determination that cannot be derived from the country diagnostics or engineering results. That commitment is preserved independently of any claim about informational or ecological efficiency. Indigenous peoples and governance traditions are diverse; no general historical claim that they have all maintained one form of subsidiarity for millennia is warranted here. The Indigenous and Traditional Knowledge Governance Framework and Treaty of the Circle are historical GGF proposals, developed by a non-Indigenous author with AI assistance and offered as invitations to dialogue, not as assertions of representation or consent. Affected peoples' own institutions, rights, decisions and consent procedures must determine whether and how any such arrangements can proceed. Neither a design label nor a technical record can establish consent.

### 6.4 The Genesis Protocol as a proposed transition strategy

The original report describes a Pre-Genesis Demonstration Phase (Phase −1) involving willing municipalities and community initiatives, followed by a proposed Phase 0 constitutional activation process. The exact current documents and legal arrangements have not been verified. The historical description names BAZ pilots, Hearts implementations and Community Weaver training; three sources of consent (nation-state ratification, Indigenous consent and a popular referendum); a Power Dissolution Clause with a 30% limit on the subsequent Interim Secretariat; and Founding Intent Snapshots and Drift Reports. These are reported design features [HR/IP], not confirmed current protocol rules. They require versioned verification, including applicable thresholds, procedures, rights and remedies, before use.

Demonstrating useful outcomes can strengthen a case for additional authority; it cannot confer authority. Authorization and applicable consent must precede any intervention. Later constitutional authority requires separate legitimate procedures; different channels of consent are not interchangeable and their statistical independence is not established. Cryptographic records may assist auditing but cannot themselves enforce legal rights or ensure correction. Protections against capture are proposals to test, not guarantees.

Small, low-stakes experiments may reduce disruption and support learning. Their legitimacy does not depend on remaining below a threshold of political controversy. They require full applicable authorization, transparency, consent and review, including necessary federal or national coordination. Neither design secrecy nor avoidance of legitimate oversight is acceptable. Low salience is not evidence of legitimacy.

### 6.5 The path forward

For a particular proposed GGF component, identify the task and actors, compare existing arrangements, specify the minimum lawful authority and resources needed, and test whether the component improves outcomes while respecting constraints. Distinguish design coherence from demonstrated feasibility and efficacy. Section 7 examines a candidate transition pathway in that spirit; alternatives remain open.
