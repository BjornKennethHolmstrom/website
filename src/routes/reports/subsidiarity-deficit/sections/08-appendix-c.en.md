### Appendix C: The Governance as Engineering Connection

The original report drew on Papers I–V. Their specific derivations and simulations have not been independently audited in this correction cycle. The earlier latency, single-scale stability, representation-chain depth and commons-observation claims are historical model claims [HR], not general engineering proofs of the institutional architecture proposed here. The corrected VI, VIII and XX, and the accepted capstone correction, supply narrower statements that can be used without relying on those claims.

**C.1 Information and adequate action [M].** In the static noiseless linear model described in §1.5, two disturbances with identical observation differ by a vector in K = D intersect ker C. If a single correction is adequate for both, their difference must belong to the acceptable residual subspace L. Necessity follows. When every correction in −D is available, choose for each feasible observation y a representative d_y in D satisfying Cd_y = y, and apply u = −d_y. The residual belongs to K and hence to L if K is contained in L. This proves sufficiency only under these assumptions. Equal observation rank need not imply equal adequacy: for D = R² and L = span(e₂), C = (1,0) hides only acceptable residuals, whereas C = (0,1) hides a consequential direction.

For finite deterministic tasks, XX's action-cover bound uses the set A(w) of adequate actions for each feasible world w. The smallest number of actions whose set meets every A(w) is a necessary lower bound on the number of distinguishable observation messages in a deterministic adequate decision architecture. If any A(w) is empty no cover exists. For a fixed observation channel, each observation cell must share an adequate action. These statements do not establish that a particular geographic partition is required.

**C.2 Aggregate observation witness [M].** Let two disturbances d₁ and d₂ be in {0,1}, and observe s = d₁ + d₂. For the task of cancelling their sum, the scalar correction a = −s is adequate. For the task of cancelling each coordinate, the correction must be (−d₁, −d₂); (0,1) and (1,0) have the same observation but different unique corrections. A central observer receiving both coordinates could distinguish them. The problem is access to consequential distinctions and feasible action, not the location of the decision-maker alone.

**C.3 Signal quality [M/HR].** The early papers use models of noise, delays and aggregation. The condition SNR = 1 is not a universal observability threshold. In a scalar additive Gaussian channel with independent Gaussian signal and noise of equal positive variance, the mutual information between signal and observation is 0.5 log₂(1 + SNR) = 0.5 bit. A fixed many-to-one observation cannot distinguish its own fibres, but other evidence or histories can help. The original universal delay, representation-depth and necessary nesting claims are not imported here.

**C.4 Sequential retention [M].** Under a stipulated product model, retained capacity Q = Q₀ × ∏ᵢ(1 − fᵢ), where Q₀ is positive and each fᵢ is a fractional loss in [0,1]. Four half-retentions give Q/Q₀ = 1/16. The loss fraction 1 − ∏ᵢ(1 − fᵢ) is at most the sum of fᵢ: induct using x + y − xy ≤ x + y for x,y in [0,1]. The combined loss in the example is 15/16, not greater than the sum 2. A task requiring retention 1/20 is feasible by this scalar criterion, while one requiring 1/10 is not. Thus positive retained capacity alone does not imply categorical inability. This is a model implication, not proof that institutions obey the product model or that Paper V is empirically established.

**C.5 Diagnostic implications [IP/H].** The sensing–decision–delivery workflow can be investigated with the five axes of VI and VIII. Specify outcomes, evidence, causal explanations, decision authority and feasible execution separately. Do not infer a universal critical gap value, a ranking of governance systems, a necessarily irreversible loss or a unique scale allocation. The historical composite index, scores and threshold in VIII have been withdrawn; no such measure is introduced in this synthesis. The relationship between the engineering models and actual institutional cases remains a research question.
