### Appendix D: About this synthesis and how it was made

**How it was made**

This synthesis, and the country reports it draws upon, were generated by AI language models ([Claude, ChatGPT, DeepSeek — adjust]) from prompts by Björn Kenneth Holmström. For each diagnostic exercise, several models were prompted in parallel to approach the country or system from different strategic angles — institutional architecture, cultural dynamics, political economy, and comparative governance — and to apply the systems-thinking framework he had set out. He chose the topics, posed the questions and decided to publish the results, but wrote little of the text himself. The voice of the synthesis is the models', not his.

The synthesis has not been systematically fact-checked, and neither have the reports it builds on. Statements about specific countries, institutions, laws, events, figures and sources may be inaccurate or invented, and should be verified against primary sources before being relied on or cited. Because a synthesis compounds the claims of its sources, errors in the underlying reports may be carried forward here. Like any work of synthesis, it is provisional. It makes no claim to finality. It claims only that the lens it offers merits testing against reality — and that the testing, in the end, is what matters most.

**Perspective and limits**

Neither the models nor the person who prompted them write from within any of the institutional systems examined. Björn Kenneth Holmström has studied engineering physics, has worked with mathematical optimisation at TOMLAB Optimization, and has a long-standing personal interest in systems thinking, complexity and governance. He has no formal training or professional experience in public administration or political science, and is not an expert on any of the six countries examined. The framework applied here draws on complexity science, developmental psychology (Spiral Dynamics), governance theory, and control‑theoretic approaches to institutional design.

That distance limits access to the granular, day‑to‑day texture of each country's policy‑making — the unwritten norms, the informal power structures, the lived reality that no formal framework can capture. An outside lens can sometimes raise questions that insiders have learned not to hear, but it can just as easily miss what insiders know. The synthesis does not claim insider knowledge of any of the six systems it examines. It offers a lens that may or may not prove useful to those who hold institutional positions and are searching for frameworks that make sense of what they are experiencing.

The synthesis refers to related material on this website — the Global Governance Frameworks, the Governance as Engineering working papers and the EU Subsidiarity Protocol — which were produced in the same way and have not been reviewed by practitioners or experts. The synthesis is offered in the spirit of collaborative sense‑making, not definitive pronouncement. Feedback, criticism, and corrections are welcome.
