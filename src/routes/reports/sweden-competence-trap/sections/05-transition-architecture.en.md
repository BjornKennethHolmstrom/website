## 5. Working with the Grain: Transition Architecture for Sweden

### 5.1 The Principle: Start Where Trust Is High

The transition architecture is a candidate for working *with* existing trust. The Germany and France reports explore administrative modernisation and locally bounded learning respectively; their comparative mechanisms are hypotheses, not a basis for bypassing scrutiny. Low political salience can coexist with fully disclosed purposes and competent authorization.

Satisfied competence, a consensus filter and agency silos are proposed mechanisms, not a hostile adversary or established national diagnosis. Trust, evidence-based policy-making, professional autonomy and agreement can support adaptation. Honest conversations, better task-relevant evidence and transparent, bounded spaces for experimentation are possible ways to build on these strengths, subject to evidence and legitimate disagreement.

Where evidence warrants gradual development rather than emergency action, reform can be described as a continuous upgrade: calm, evidence-rich and forward-looking. Strategic honesty means accessible explanation, not minimising an actual emergency or concealing purposes. Available time, trust and the Competence Trap remain questions for evidence, and justified objections remain legitimate.

The following candidates — municipal innovation, cross-silo data covenants, safe-to-fail pilots and voluntary diffusion — aim to work with existing institutional capabilities. A low-salience initiative may be proposed only transparently, with all material purposes and powers disclosed before authorization. It must remain subject to scrutiny, complaints, oversight and stop/remedy; existing authority must be verified for each activity.

### 5.2 Municipal Innovation: The Historical “Trojan Horse” Metaphor

Municipalities are one candidate vehicle for examining feedback gaps because local services touch residents directly and responsibility may not match capacity. The earlier claim that confidence in local government typically exceeds national institutions requires empirical verification. Local experimentation must be compared with other channels and remains subject to actual authority and public scrutiny.

The earlier edition used the term "Trojan Horse" deliberately, not to imply deception, but to describe a strategy in which a culturally legible initiative would build a new feedback architecture through its deeper logic. It proposed municipal innovation and administrative modernisation as the wrapper, expecting these goals to fit the pragmatic tradition and attract cross-party support. That expectation remains to be tested; no party’s agreement is presumed. The adopted framing is transparent, opt-in incremental prototyping with all aims disclosed.

The no-deception boundary governs the metaphor: no material purpose, cost, data use, power or evaluation condition may be withheld from the bodies and people entitled to scrutinize it before authorization. Cultural legibility is not permission to bypass scrutiny.

Consider a concrete example. A group of municipalities — perhaps five to seven, diverse in size, geography, and economic base — could form a **"Framtidskommuner" (Future Municipalities) Network**. The stated purpose would be straightforward: to pioneer more integrated, data-informed approaches to complex local challenges, to share learning across municipal boundaries, and to provide a testbed for administrative practices that could eventually be adopted more broadly. The language is the language of *verksamhetsutveckling* — operational development — and *evidensbaserad praktik* — evidence-based practice. It is the language that Swedish public administration already speaks.

Five to seven is the historical planning illustration, not an adopted cohort size; see §6.2 for the selection criteria and unresolved size decision.

*Earlier edition:*

> Inside this wrapper, the Framtidskommuner would carry a more transformative payload. They would be authorised to implement cross-agency data-sharing agreements under the privacy-by-design framework described in Section 3. They would establish standing citizens' deliberative bodies with formal response obligations. They would receive the fiscal and legal flexibility to experiment with integrated governance approaches — treating housing, education, social services, and public safety as a single design challenge rather than separate silos. And they would be evaluated not primarily on traditional output metrics, but on feedback velocity: how quickly signals are detected, shared, and acted upon.

The corrected proposal discloses each of these aims openly. Data agreements do not confer disclosure power; use existing lawful aggregate learning first and separately authorize any necessary personal linkage (§5.3). Advisory deliberation may use a voluntary reasoned-response procedure; any statutory duty requires competent enactment. Integrated experimentation and budgets are limited to existing or expressly authorized sector powers and common service floors. Feedback velocity is secondary to appropriate response, outcomes, rights and harms (§6.5).

*Earlier edition:*

> The key is that the Framtidskommuner would be *voluntary*. No municipality would be required to participate. Those that choose to — and there are already municipalities, across the political spectrum, that are acutely aware of the gap between their responsibilities and their capacities — would be self-selected for readiness. Their participation would be a signal of local political will, which provides cover for the national government to grant the necessary exemptions and resources. The dynamics of scaling by attraction begin with the first movers.

Voluntary municipal participation may indicate readiness, not representative consent or legal authorization. National support means lawful resources, transparent authorization and protection of honest reporting, not political cover against scrutiny, interim harm stops or remedy. Self-selection limits generalization.

### 5.3 Cross-Silo Data Covenants

The Data Archipelago can persist despite expanded legal gateways. Since the 2025 reforms, the immediate task is not necessarily another nationwide permission to share: it is to map the actual legal basis, operational practices, technical barriers, and remaining analytical needs for each use case. Where a lawful basis already exists, agencies can explore carefully scoped implementation; where it does not, a voluntary agreement alone cannot create one.

A **cross-silo data covenant** would be an operational and accountability agreement among participating organisations—not a new disclosure power. It would specify a lawful purpose, legal bases, controllers, permitted fields or aggregate outputs, minimisation, access, retention, audit, and redress. Some cases may require only joint analysis of public statistics. Where personal data are necessary, legality and proportionality must be established independently of the covenant; an illustrative school–social-services–police linkage cannot be presumed permissible.

This precision is essential, but an agreement does not make an otherwise unlawful exchange lawful. The value of the covenant lies in testing a specific, authorised collaboration, recording its benefits and harms, and exposing operational barriers that remain even when sharing is permitted.

The covenants should start with narrowly scoped, legally assessed cases where a clearly documented gap prevents useful learning. Aggregate school-to-work transitions could be a lower-risk starting point than linking sensitive data about named minors. Proposals involving schools, social services, or police must undergo a separate necessity, proportionality, and rights assessment before any information exchange; the 2025 disclosure rules do not make all such uses automatically permissible.

The national government's role is to enable, not to control. It can provide a template legal agreement, model privacy protocols, and technical assistance. It can fund an independent integrity authority to oversee the covenants and build public trust. It can celebrate successful covenants and make their methods available to other municipalities. But the covenants themselves are initiated and governed locally, by the agencies that will use them. This respects the Swedish principle of agency autonomy while building the bridges that autonomy currently lacks.

### 5.4 Safe-to-Fail Municipal Pilots

Sweden, like most well-functioning states, has an aversion to visible failure. The pressure to deliver consistent, high-quality outcomes across all municipalities has produced a system in which deviations from national standards are treated as risks to be managed rather than experiments to be learned from. This is understandable. Citizens in Kiruna should not receive worse healthcare than citizens in Stockholm because their municipality decided to try something unconventional. But the understandable preference for consistency has a cost: it suppresses the variation that generates learning.

The Kiruna–Stockholm service-floor principle is retained: variation cannot justify lower required protections. Learning must be compared with actual effects and lawful alternatives.

*Earlier edition:*

> A **safe-to-fail pilot** is an experiment deliberately designed to overcome this aversion. It is bounded in scope — a specific municipality, a limited timeframe, a predefined set of activities. It is explicitly framed as a learning exercise, not a permanent policy change. Its participants — municipal officials, agency staff, citizen participants — are publicly assured that mixed results are expected and that honest documentation of what did not work is as valuable as evidence of what did. And it has political cover: a national government that authorises the pilot commits to protecting it from premature judgment.

A bounded pilot must identify a responsible decision-maker, permitted actions, service continuity, cost, independent evaluation, complaint and withdrawal routes, and interim stop or reversal for material harm. National support can protect honest documentation, never delay scrutiny, remedy or lawful intervention. Mixed results can yield learning without making a harmful pilot successful.

A pilot with specifically authorized fiscal or regulatory flexibility can learn from disappointing metrics without treating every disappointing result as a reason for punitive action. An unexpected obstacle, such as incompatible IT systems impeding lawful data exchange, can count as learning even when programme outcomes are mixed. Learning success is distinct from service success and cannot override independent oversight, service floors, complaints or interim stop/remedy. Independent evidence of serious harm, rights violations or loss of lawful authority requires stop/remedy; learning cannot excuse those consequences.

Existing *försöksverksamhet* routes are candidates to examine (§6.4), not blanket legal permission. A dedicated *Försöksverksamhetslag* is a historical design proposal for enumerated permissible experiments, governance, evaluation and possible extension or scaling; existing lawful pathways may suffice. Actual legal authority requires verification or competent enactment, with safeguards and independent intervention not displaced by national support.

The Framtidskommuner would be the first cohort of safe-to-fail pilots under this framework. Their experiences — documented transparently, evaluated independently, shared openly — would build the evidence base for broader adoption. Some experiments will succeed. Some will fail. Some will succeed in unexpected ways and fail in expected ones. The common output is learning, and learning is the foundation of the feedback upgrade.

Only independently authorized and bounded pilots may proceed; report adverse results and costs, and separate learning from public outcome and rights performance.

### 5.5 Scaling by Attraction

The tradition of *kommunalt självstyre* suggests a possible basis for voluntary adoption. Local choice following observed results might support genuine ownership, whereas a central mandate may in some settings invite merely formal compliance; neither effect is inevitable. Compare voluntary adoption with authorized common standards, existing arrangements and other routes, subject to evidence, equal protection and local suitability. Actual outcomes, costs, mandates and affected-party experience would determine whether local ownership helps.

Scaling by attraction means making successful experiments visible, accessible, and easy to replicate. The national government's role is not to command but to enable: to fund documentation and evaluation, to facilitate peer-to-peer learning networks, to provide technical assistance and seed funding for municipalities that want to adopt proven approaches, and to celebrate successes publicly. The Swedish Association of Local Authorities and Regions (*Sveriges Kommuner och Regioner*, SKR) is a natural partner — an existing institution with the relationships, the legitimacy, and the convening power to facilitate horizontal learning across municipalities.

*Earlier edition:*

> The dynamics are slow in the beginning, then accelerating. The first few Framtidskommuner demonstrate that integrated data sensing, citizen deliberation, and experimental autonomy produce better outcomes — not just on traditional metrics, but on the feedback velocity that prevents problems from compounding. A second wave of municipalities, observing the results, voluntarily adopts elements of the model. The national government, observing the second wave, adjusts the legal and fiscal framework to make adoption easier. Over time, what began as a small experiment at the periphery becomes the norm — not because anyone mandated it, but because enough actors chose it that the old way of working became harder to justify.

This is a hypothesized diffusion sequence, not a forecast or evidence of benefit. Publish favorable and adverse findings, compare existing alternatives, and require independent evidence of public outcomes, rights compliance, cost and local suitability before renewal or expansion. Adoption and speed alone are not success.

Voluntary diffusion may be slow and less visible within a single electoral cycle, but could embed learning in practice and increase ownership. Durability is an empirical question, not a unique property of voluntary reform; discontinuing an ineffective or harmful policy can be a success.

---

The transition architecture described here — transparent municipal innovation, cross-silo data covenants, safe-to-fail pilots, and scaling by attraction — is an expression of the same pragmatic tradition that built the *folkhemmet*. It does not require constitutional revolution. It does not demand that Swedes become a different kind of people. It requires that the existing strengths — trust, competence, rationality, local self-government — be directed toward the architectural upgrade that the 21st century demands. The next section translates this architecture into a concrete first step: the Framtidskommuner proposal in full.

The recap is conditional: material purposes and powers are disclosed, covenants confer no new access, experiments stay within actual authorization and service floors with stop/remedy, and scaling depends on independent evidence rather than attraction alone.
