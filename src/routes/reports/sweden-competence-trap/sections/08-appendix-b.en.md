## Appendix B: International Analogues and Precedents

The proposals in this report are not without precedent. The following examples illustrate existing implementations of feedback-oriented governance reforms.

### B.1 Finland: Futures Literacy and Anticipatory Governance

Finland offers possible comparison cases: the parliamentary Committee for the Future and its reported foresight exercises and long-term policy review, Sitra (the Finnish Innovation Fund)’s purported nationwide futures literacy programmes, and anticipatory governance zones in regional planning. The earlier edition attributed measurable gains in agency and complexity tolerance to those programmes; their scope and outcomes require verification. They may inform comparison of existing Swedish inquiry channels and optional challenge and futures-literacy arrangements, but establish neither necessity nor authorization.

### B.2 Estonia: Privacy-by-Design Data Infrastructure

Estonia’s digital identity and X-Road exchange architecture are possible comparison cases for cross-agency learning without one central database. The earlier claims of over 99% of public services online and full transparency about data access require verification, including actual privacy and trust effects. The example does not establish Swedish lawfulness, feasibility or effectiveness. Start with existing lawful aggregate evidence; any personal linkage requires independent authorization and oversight, lawful purpose, necessity, proportionality, security and redress (§3.1).

### B.3 Denmark: Municipal Autonomy and Asymmetric Devolution

Danish *kommuner* provide a comparison for fiscal autonomy, service design, local priorities and experimentation. The earlier assertion of broader powers than Swedish municipalities needs factual and legal verification. Context-sensitive adaptation may strengthen universal services, but no Swedish fiscal power follows; any tailored flexibility must preserve equal protection, common service obligations and solidarity.

### B.4 United Kingdom: Devolution Deals and Combined Authorities

UK devolution deals and Combined Authorities, including the Greater Manchester health and social care example, are potential comparisons for tailored powers over transport, housing, skills and economic development with accountable governance. Verify actual powers and effects in their distinct context. Asymmetric devolution is a possible design comparison, not a direct precedent conferring Swedish authority; equal protection and local suitability matter.

### B.5 Taiwan: Digital Democracy Infrastructure

Taiwan’s vTaiwan and Join platforms offer possible comparisons for open consultation, facilitated deliberation and consensus-seeking on contentious policy issues. The earlier claim that they produced legislation otherwise politically impossible requires verification. They illustrate a possible complement to representative democracy and a research question about participatory depth and scale, not a mandate, veto or proof of causal impact; existing Swedish channels and separately authorized participation remain alternatives.

### B.6 Germany and France: Cross-References in the Series

Germany’s Adaptive Governance Pilot Regions, France’s Territoires d’Intégration Adaptative and Sweden’s Framtidskommuner are historical candidate designs respectively exploring execution, integration and feedback. A European learning network might compare evaluated lessons, but none independently confirms a national diagnosis, establishes transferable powers or permits avoiding scrutiny.
