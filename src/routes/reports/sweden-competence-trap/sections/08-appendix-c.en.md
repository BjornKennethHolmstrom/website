## Appendix C: The Governance as Engineering Series Connection

### C.1 The Architectural Foundation

This report draws on the historical Governance as Engineering series, which models governance using ideas from control theory, information theory and cybernetics. Papers I–V are not certified by this correction. The original report treated their transfers as mathematical foundations for its diagnosis; the following distinguishes that historical argument from bounded formal results and local hypotheses.

### C.2 The Five Papers in Brief

*Earlier edition:*

> **Paper I — Governance Stability Simulator** demonstrates that centralised governance systems operate on aggregated signals that destroy spatial information. A central controller observing only the national average cannot see which municipalities are in distress and which are stable. Its interventions are simultaneously too weak for the crisis locations and too disruptive for the healthy ones. This is the formal basis for the spatial blindness that the Data Archipelago produces in the Swedish context: when agencies cannot share local data, the national level sees only aggregates, and local variation becomes invisible.

For the specified coordinate task, a controller observing only the sum cannot distinguish states such as (2,8) and (8,2); this is a supported conditional non-identifiability result. For a sum task the same aggregate may suffice. Whether national authorities in Sweden only observe aggregates is unverified and §2.2 notes registers supporting longitudinal analysis. Uniform intervention effects require evidence and actual feasible action sets; neither central inadequacy nor distributed superiority follows unconditionally.

*Earlier edition:*

> **Paper II — Fractality as Stability** demonstrates that no single-scale controller can stabilise a system facing simultaneous fast, medium, and slow disturbances. A controller calibrated for fast crises will oscillate in response to slow trends. A controller calibrated for slow trends will be too slow for fast crises. The only stable architecture is a fractal hierarchy of controllers, each matched to the timescale of its disturbance band. This is the formal basis for the Mean-Reversion Fallacy critique: Sweden’s institutions are calibrated for medium-frequency electoral cycles and slow consensus-building. They lack the fast sensing and response loops that exponential or cascading challenges require.

The universal single-scale impossibility and exclusive fractal stability conclusion are withdrawn. In a stipulated unrestricted memoryless example a controller can exactly cancel simultaneous disturbances; in another bounded scalar plant, unrestricted actuation permits boundedness, not arbitrary performance. These illustrations do not imply all single-scale controllers work. The relevant question is whether response times are matched to local disturbances and deadlines; Sweden’s electoral and deliberative cycles may matter, but legitimate deliberation and changed evidence are alternatives. Nested and ordinary arrangements should be compared under actual dynamics and feasible powers.

*Earlier edition:*

> **Paper III — The Observability-Democracy Connection** demonstrates that citizen preferences cannot be reliably transmitted through representation chains deeper than two or three layers. Noise variance exceeds surviving signal variance, and the policy layer governs a phantom signal. This is the formal basis for the deliberative infrastructure proposed in Section 3: shortening the chain between citizen experience and policy response requires direct participatory mechanisms, not just improved representation.

No universal two-to-three-layer threshold follows: identity channels can retain a bit through many layers, while a constant map loses it in one; a Gaussian channel at signal-to-noise ratio one has capacity 0.5 bit per use, not zero (under the stated Gaussian assumptions). Positive information is not exact recovery. Shorter channels may help or introduce shared bias, selection and nonresponse. Citizen experience and reason-giving are valuable design questions, not proof that direct participation is necessary or adequate; an advisory lottery does not confer authority.

*Earlier edition:*

> **Paper IV — Requisite Variety and the Commons** demonstrates that governance systems with low-dimensional observation cannot stabilise high-variety resource systems. Observation dimensionality—the number of independent signal dimensions the governance system can access—is the primary determinant of commons management outcomes. This is the formal basis for the cross-silo sensemaking infrastructure: connecting school data, health data, police data, and housing data increases the observation dimensionality available to municipalities, enabling them to govern the complex social systems they are responsible for.

Under the static noiseless linear model let D be the disturbance subspace, L the tolerated residual subspace in the ambient space, C the observation map, and K=D∩ker C. Exact task adequacy requires K⊆L; the hidden consequential dimension h=dim K−dim(K∩L) satisfies h≥max(0, dim D−dim(D∩L)−rank(C|D)). Thus rank(C|D) below dim D−dim(D∩L) precludes the specified task. Meeting the necessary bound is not sufficient; the condition K⊆L is sufficient in the stipulated model only if all −D corrections are available. Equal raw rank can conceal different task distinctions. This is not a dynamic-stability theorem or a national score. Linkage may expose relevant distinctions but does not suffice for action or confer lawful authority. The named Swedish cross-silo applications are hypotheses; compare aggregate and existing lawful routes, or noncollection, and test feasible action and actual outcomes separately.

*Earlier edition:*

> **Paper V — The Coordination Failure Tax** demonstrates that the four failure modes do not add—they multiply. A governance system exhibiting all four simultaneously is not four times worse than a well-designed one; it is categorically incapable of the functions it claims to perform. This is the formal basis for the urgency beneath the calm tone of this report: the Competence Trap is not a single vulnerability but a compounding set of structural constraints that, left unaddressed, will progressively erode the system’s capacity to govern.

A conditional product of fractional retentions rᵢ∈[0,1] gives total loss 1−∏rᵢ≤∑(1−rᵢ). Four half-retentions yield 1/16 retained: enough for a stipulated 1/20 scalar threshold, not enough for 1/10. Neither threshold certifies governance, and no categorical incapacity, superadditive loss or inevitable decline follows. Actual interactions can reinforce or offset one another. The Swedish narrative of compounding delay is a hypothesis requiring a defined task, evidence of interaction and outcomes, not a mathematical prediction.

### C.3 From Engineering Analysis to Institutional Design

The original report claimed mathematical proof that its Swedish architectural diagnosis was real and structural. That transfer is withdrawn. The model-conditional results above identify possible task-specific information and action constraints under their assumptions; they establish neither Sweden’s actual conditions nor the legitimacy, feasibility or sufficiency of a design. Will, competence, resources, lawful authority and other mechanisms remain relevant.

The Framtidskommuner design retains the original rationale as research questions: can lawful task-relevant observation reduce harmful latency, can cross-silo learning preserve needed distinctions, can deliberative channels improve fidelity and reason-giving, and can authority be matched to actual disturbances? Existing and alternative arrangements must be compared. Neither necessary information conditions nor the number of actions certify adequate response, and ownership or scale alone does not settle it. The pilot is a candidate to test, evaluate, revise or discontinue within authorized scope and rights protections, not a design shown to satisfy engineering constraints.
