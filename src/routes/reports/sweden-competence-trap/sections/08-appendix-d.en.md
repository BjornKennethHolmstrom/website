## Appendix D: Anticipated Objections

### D.1 “Sweden is already one of the best-governed countries in the world. Why fix what isn’t broken?”

*Earlier edition:*

> This is the most natural response to this report, and it is precisely the Competence Trap in action. Sweden’s high rankings are real, but they measure performance against metrics that were designed for the conditions of the late 20th century. A system can be top-ranked on yesterday’s measures while being progressively less capable of handling tomorrow’s challenges. The question is not whether Sweden is well-governed by historical standards—it is. The question is whether its governance architecture is being upgraded at the pace its environment is changing. The answer, across the three mechanisms documented in this report, is no.

The objection is legitimate, not evidence of the trap. High performance on relevant measures may count against the proposed deficit; new and historical tasks should be examined separately. The three mechanisms are hypotheses, not a demonstrated negative answer.

### D.2 “Doesn’t cross-agency data sharing threaten Sweden’s privacy traditions?”

The proposal is not unrestricted data sharing or centralised surveillance, but specific, transparent connections under independent oversight where lawful and necessary. The earlier edition argued that Sweden has chosen privacy over systemic visibility because it never had to choose: the technologies for combining the two were not available when the relevant arrangements were designed. This is an attributed historical interpretation, not an established account of the law or a claim that the trade-off has disappeared. Cryptographic techniques may support pattern detection without exposing identities but do not confer disclosure authority or automatically anonymize. Privacy and sensemaking may complement one another or conflict; begin with aggregate and existing lawful exchanges, not a presumption of first-mover status or guaranteed compatibility. Separate purpose, legal basis, necessity, proportionality, independent oversight and redress are required before personal linkage; suspend unsafe use.

### D.3 “Isn’t this just decentralisation by another name? We tried that and it didn’t solve the problems.”

The proposed rebalancing is not generic decentralisation: it concerns fiscal capacity matched to responsibility, bounded experimentation and support suited to different starting points. Whether residual mismatch exists after equalisation is task-specific. Compare ordinary arrangements, targeted support and precisely authorized sector experiments; no blanket departure or unequal basic protection follows.

### D.4 “The Swedish consensus culture is a strength, not a weakness. Why should we import adversarial politics?”

The goal is not to import adversarial politics but to explore whether structured disagreement, through red teams, advisory deliberation and futures literacy, can strengthen *saklighet* and make consensus earned rather than assumed. Existing challenge and consultation may suffice. Advisory bodies do not replace elected or rights-holder authority; red teams have no default veto, require actual protections and transparent authorization, and training remains within competent education authority. Evaluate benefits and risks, including retaliation and stop/remedy. Protection against retaliation and the absence of compelled agreement are necessary; harmful exercises must be stopped.

### D.5 “Won’t the Framtidskommuner just become another pilot project that goes nowhere, like so many others?”

Pilots may produce reports and fade away; the Framtidskommuner design proposes three features to test that risk: actual authorized decision capacity rather than solely advice, transparent independent evaluation to support voluntary learning, and a possible dedicated legal framework (*Försöksverksamhetslag*) for extension or scaling. Only existing lawful or specifically authorized powers may be exercised; no Act is presumed in force. National support cannot shield pilots from scrutiny, service floors, interim stop/remedy or independent evaluation. Independent evaluation must distinguish learning, public outcomes and rights, and renewal or expansion requires separate evidence and authority.

### D.6 “This report diagnoses problems but doesn’t offer a political strategy. How do you actually get this done?”

The transition architecture proposes voluntary participation, working with existing trust, and explaining institutional learning as a possible continuation of pragmatic reform. Willing municipalities and national support are not sufficient: disclose all material aims and powers, establish actual sector authority, service floors, rights, safe participation, cost and independent evaluation. Evidence, if favorable, may inform voluntary adoption, not replace authorization or justify scaling by attraction alone. No national permission alone overrides other competent bodies. Evidence may favor an alternative or no adoption.
