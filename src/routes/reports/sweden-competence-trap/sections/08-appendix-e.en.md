## Appendix E: About this report and how it was made

### How it was made

This report was generated by AI language models (Claude, ChatGPT, DeepSeek) from prompts by Björn Kenneth Holmström. Several models were prompted in parallel to approach Sweden's situation, and their outputs were combined into a single text using the systems-thinking framework he had set out. He chose the topic, posed the questions and decided to publish the result, but wrote little of the text himself.

The report has not been systematically fact-checked. Statements about Swedish institutions, laws, events, figures and sources may be inaccurate or invented, and should be verified against primary sources before being relied on or cited. Like any work of synthesis, it is provisional. It makes no claim to finality. It claims only that the lens it offers merits testing against reality—and that the testing, in the end, is what matters most.

### Perspective and limits

Neither the models nor the person who prompted them write from within Sweden's institutional core. Björn Kenneth Holmström lives in Sweden and knows Swedish society as a resident, but has no formal training or professional experience in Swedish public administration or political science. He has studied engineering physics and has worked with mathematical optimisation at TOMLAB Optimization. The framework applied here draws on complexity science, developmental psychology and governance theory.

Standing outside the institutions limits access to the granular, day-to-day texture of Swedish policy-making. A view from the periphery can sometimes raise questions that the centre has learned not to hear, but it can just as easily miss what insiders know. The report does not claim insider knowledge. It offers a lens that may or may not prove useful to those who hold institutional positions and are searching for frameworks that make sense of what they are experiencing.

Feedback, criticism, and corrections are welcome. The argument is offered in the spirit of collaborative sense-making, not definitive pronouncement.

### The Country Reports Series

This report was the third in a series of Country Reports for Systemic Change, all produced in the same way. The first examined Germany through the lens of a twin execution deficit and proposed Adaptive Governance Pilot Regions. The second examined France through the lens of an integration deficit and proposed Territoires d'Intégration Adaptative. Together, the first three reports propose a triptych on Europe's adaptive capacity gap: the inability to *decide*, *integrate*, and *sense* simultaneously. Later reports extended the series to India, the European Union, and beyond, each asking the same underlying question: *Is the real bottleneck money, decisions, or legitimacy—or is it the system's capacity to see what it is facing and adapt before the window narrows?*
