## 2. Structural Mechanisms

The following seven candidate mechanisms are not independent variables or proven causes. Section 2.5 synthesises two of them rather than adding an eighth. Different mechanisms can operate together, and an episode can exhibit none.

### 2.1 Asymmetric Observation: information pathways

Commercial institutions may have information that is relevant to public objectives but unavailable or unused in public decisions. The original report points to commodity trading, wealth management, multinational taxation and financed emissions. These domains do not establish that a sensor already exists. Trace what firms measure, who can lawfully access it, whether it measures harms rather than commercial exposure, the quality of attribution, and who has authority to respond. Workers and communities may have evidence unavailable to firms. Legitimate confidentiality is a constraint to respect, not automatically an obstruction. H1 distinguishes missing, disconnected, contested and unused information.

**Banking.** The original banking-secrecy case contrasts information about deposits, jurisdictions, instruments and returns with information about the origin of funds and the fiscal consequences abroad. Confidentiality can restrict disclosure of client information to public authorities or other jurisdictions; it does not imply that bankers were prohibited from knowing the provenance of assets. Due diligence and reporting obligations have changed over time. The proposed inquiry would establish, for a given period and jurisdiction, what was recorded, what was legally disclosable, what authorities actually knew, and whether a lost tax base or other harm can be attributed to specific practices. It would also distinguish legitimate confidentiality from concealment of unlawful conduct.

**Commodity trading.** The original names Vitol, Glencore, Trafigura, Mercuria and Gunvor and contrasts trading centres with extraction and production areas such as the Niger Delta, the Congolese copper belt and the Brazilian soy frontier. These are illustrative leads, not findings about individual firms. Commercial actors may monitor supply disruptions, prices, logistics and legal risks worldwide. A worker's exposure or the environmental effects of extraction may be recorded only partially or by different actors. The hypothesis is that information relevant to those effects is not adequately available or used in Swiss public deliberation, while employment and tax receipts are more visible. Trace actual reporting and local testimony rather than equating risk data with evidence of harm.

**Tax competition.** Cantonal tax policies can attract headquarters, investment and employment. The original asks whether some of these benefits coincide with reduced tax bases elsewhere. A multinational's registered headquarters, location of production, employees and markets need not coincide. Whether particular arrangements shift taxable profits, and whether that changes public revenues in France, Germany, Italy or elsewhere, requires counterfactual estimates and attention to treaties, other countries' policies and legitimate economic activity. Residents abroad generally do not vote on Swiss cantonal tax policy, but that alone says nothing about the effectiveness of international coordination.

These three cases make H1 operational. For each, compare the private and public records against independent evidence from affected people, and distinguish absent information, restricted disclosure, interpretation disputes, ignored evidence and lack of authority. If authorities already possess and use the relevant evidence, the proposed disconnection is not the explanation for that episode.

### 2.2 Neutrality and information pathways

The Faraday Cage is a historical metaphor for possible differences in international information channels, not a physical law or an argument against neutrality. Membership in an alliance or regulatory body can entail both information access and obligations; neither follows automatically from the other. Non-members may receive timely information through diplomacy, research, international organisations or agreements. Compare actual channels and response obligations, including security and peace-related benefits of neutrality. Verify legal duties and options before proposing changes. It is legitimate to maintain neutrality and still investigate information exchange.

The historical argument is that neutrality offered a small, landlocked, multilingual country a way to reduce exposure to military commitments and alliance entanglements while preserving diplomatic autonomy. The original describes this as adaptive. It is a historical interpretation to be tested against the actual conditions of different periods, not a claim that neutrality alone explains Swiss survival or prosperity. Its possible benefits for mediation and security belong in the comparison.

The changed-environment hypothesis concerns sanctions transmitted through financial networks, disruptions through interconnected energy grids, cyber threats, supply-chain dependencies and climate effects. Such disturbances can affect neutral and allied countries alike. The energy crisis after Russia's invasion of Ukraine is the original's illustrative episode. It cites an energy-import share of roughly 75 percent (unverified here; check year and denominator), and asks how access to European coordination and supply information affected Swiss choices. Establish actual information access, participation, agreements and physical constraints, not a presumed absence of signals. A better information pathway might help without any alliance commitment; a membership obligation might confer costs even where access improves. If existing pathways were timely and adequate, the filtering hypothesis is weakened.

### 2.3 Direct democracy: feedback and latency

Referenda and initiatives can communicate preferences on specified proposals, create accountability and constrain decision-makers. They do not measure preference intensity or guarantee adequate observation. Campaign information, eligibility, participation, the available alternatives and implementation matter. The original report contrasts referendum and consultation timelines with emergency response, citing the 2008 UBS rescue and pandemic measures. Compare actual authorised pathways, deadlines, deliberative benefits, emergency powers and review. A delayed response can be inadequate for one task and appropriate for another. No universal representation depth or signal cutoff follows.

A popular initiative, in the original account, requires 100,000 signatures within 18 months; campaigning, parliamentary treatment, voting and implementation can occupy several years (verify the procedural details and actual durations). This is not simply wasted time. Constitutional rights, fiscal arrangements, infrastructure and institutional design are examples of issues for which deliberation can expose conflicts and prevent hasty, costly changes. A rapidly developing pandemic, a financial disruption or a supply emergency can have a much shorter task deadline. For such tasks, a pre-authorised, proportionate delegated response with later review may be preferable to waiting for a new vote. The original cites the 2008 UBS rescue, in which the Federal Council and Swiss National Bank used executive mechanisms rather than initiating a new referendum process. Verify the specific powers, dates and accountability arrangements; it is a lead for comparison, not proof that internal emergencies are always handled effectively. Match the response pathway to the task's actual tempo and preserve democratic supervision.

### 2.4 Modularity and veto topology

Cantonal autonomy, the double-majority requirement and consultation (*Vernehmlassung*) may protect local interests and pluralism. They can also impose coordination costs. Distinguish legally binding approval requirements from influence or consultation, and actual refusal from an empty feasible agreement set. For a fixed proposal space, joint approval requires intersection of the relevant admissible sets; a failed proposal does not establish that the intersection is empty. Identify amendment, negotiation, appeal, exit and escalation paths. Neither removing vetoes nor adding central authority is an automatic remedy.

Modularity can contain failures: where a canton controls a service, an unsuccessful experiment need not become a uniform national failure, and other cantons may provide comparisons or alternatives. This advantage depends on interdependencies, resources and spillovers; a local failure can still propagate through shared networks. Conversely, reforms touching several competences may require negotiation across multiple authorities. The original hypothesis is that arrangements which protect minorities and constrain hasty centralisation in domestic affairs can make some system-wide reforms harder. Cantons, parties, unions and industry associations may exert different kinds of influence; only a legally binding approval rule constitutes a formal veto. Examine the fixed set of proposals, actual approval requirements and feasible amendments before concluding that no shared solution is available.

### 2.5 Synthesis: two distinct constraints

Latency concerns the time to an authorised, effective response; veto topology concerns which responses can be authorised at all. They may interact but must be recorded separately. A fast but unauthorised decision is not an adequate response. An admissible decision reached after a task deadline can also fail. Pre-authorised reversible measures, shared information or better coordination may improve timely response without changing rights. Compare these options and legitimate procedural safeguards before attributing a throughput constraint.

**For temporal mismatch**, consider bounded delegated authority in advance of an emergency: democratically approved objectives, trigger conditions, limited powers, time limits, transparency, review and remedy afterwards. The original points to the Swiss National Bank as an institutional analogy to verify. Delegation need not change who decides on long-term constitutional commitments. It can also fail when the task is misidentified or the delegated body lacks information or resources.

**For veto topology**, compare changes to the admissible proposal space, negotiated safeguards, experimental pathways, sunset clauses and, where constitutionally legitimate, changes to decision rules or majorities. A majority rule may ease agreement but weaken protection of a minority; a narrower proposal may instead make an existing rule workable. The characteristic policy error is offering speed remedies for structural problems: accelerating consultations cannot by itself make a prohibited action admissible. Conversely, changing majority rules does not necessarily provide a timely operational response. Establish which constraint binds before choosing a remedy.

### 2.6 Prosperity and externalization

A possible feedback mechanism is that benefits accrue within a constituency while some harms fall on people outside it. Actors who benefit may resist recognition or internalisation of costs. Identify the actual causal chain, beneficiaries, harmed groups, legal responsibility and evidence of influence. Commercial success does not quantify external harm, and harms may be known, contested, voluntarily reduced or regulated. Test whether disclosure, liability, changed incentives or enforcement changes independently measured outcomes; compare alternative causes. Proxy gaming and selective attention are distinct from absent information.

The proposed reinforcing loop has four links. Domestic benefits, such as jobs and public revenue, support confidence in the institutions associated with them. That confidence can favour maintaining the arrangements. If some costs are borne elsewhere and are not included in the evidence used to evaluate the arrangements, the assessment may remain favourable even as those costs grow. External pressure, for example a change in a trading partner's rules, can then arrive as a shock to domestic policy. Incremental adjustment may preserve the arrangements and start another cycle. Each arrow is an empirical proposition: domestic success may have other causes, external costs may already be recognised, and an adjustment can remove rather than perpetuate a problem. Distinguish observation, evaluation and action. Do not infer external harm from a favourable domestic metric or recurrence from a single episode. H1 and H2 can fail independently.

### 2.7 *Sonderfall* and political resistance

The original report proposes that the identity of Switzerland as a special case may make some international commitments harder to accept. It associates this with the SVP, sectors exposed to regulation and cantonal interests. These are historical hypotheses about particular arguments and incentives, not descriptions of every actor's motives. Examine statements, votes, actual authority and distributional effects. Opponents may legitimately defend autonomy, privacy, wage protection or accountability. Reserve the immune-system metaphor for a tested resistance mechanism rather than treating criticism as proof of dysfunction.

**Who benefits — named honestly.** The original proposes four interests to investigate: financial institutions whose business models may benefit from differences in regulatory regimes; commodity traders whose costs might change with supply-chain obligations; multinational companies that choose locations partly for taxation; and political parties or constituencies that gain support through defending sovereignty or existing arrangements. These are hypotheses about incentives in particular situations. They are not allegations of misconduct, uniform preferences or covert motives. Each actor may also favour common standards, stable market access or public accountability. Compare actual positions and the distribution of gains and losses, including between cantons and within sectors.

*Sonderfall* is not in itself cynical. Attachment to Swiss distinctiveness can be genuine and historically grounded. Federal autonomy, neutrality, direct-democratic rights and the accommodation of minorities are arrangements whose value deserves serious consideration. An objection to a particular reform may be evidence of a real design problem, not resistance to be overcome. The question is whether a given appeal to distinctiveness protects those values, sustains an avoidable external cost, or does both. The answer cannot be inferred from the label.

### 2.8 Cultural concerns: *Sonderfall*, *Konkordanz* and pragmatic neutrality

Exceptionalism, consensus and pragmatic neutrality may reinforce or challenge one another. Stability, autonomy, performance, solidarity and cross-border responsibility are values whose tensions warrant explicit deliberation. The historical cultural triad and Spiral Dynamics interpretation do not establish a national developmental stage or require cultural transformation. Institutional reform and changing values may interact, but neither follows necessarily from the other. *Vorbild* is an openly advocated possibility, not a prescribed endpoint. The participants can legitimately reject it.

As an interpretive hypothesis, the triad supplies three distinct functions. *Sonderfall* provides an identity frame: what is worth protecting and what counts as a threat to autonomy. *Konkordanz* provides a decision logic: negotiate a compromise acceptable across conflicting groups rather than rely exclusively on victory by one side. Pragmatic neutrality provides an external posture: cooperate selectively while preserving freedom of decision. The functions can reinforce each other when a proposal seen as threatening distinctiveness meets difficult internal bargaining and a preference for selective external engagement. They can also work in the opposite direction: a commitment to autonomy can favour anticipatory cooperation, a consensus can incorporate affected interests, and neutrality can support mediation and shared information. Test which relationship holds in actual episodes rather than treating the triad as a single cultural cause.
