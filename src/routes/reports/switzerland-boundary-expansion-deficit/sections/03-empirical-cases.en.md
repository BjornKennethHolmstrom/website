## 3. Empirical Cases

The following three narratives preserve the chronology and central questions of the June 2026 report. They are historical research leads, not independently checked facts, current-status reports or identified causal effects. Before relying on any claim, verify the relevant primary documents, dates, legal provisions, figures and alternative explanations. In particular, a June 2026 expectation is not a statement about the situation in November 2026.

### 3.1 The EU relationship

The original account starts with the EEA referendum of 6 December 1992 (50.3% against, 49.7% for). It describes a subsequent network of more than 120 bilateral agreements covering free movement, transport, agriculture, technical barriers and research, with seven major agreements linked under Bilaterals I. Modularity can permit selective cooperation; linkage can also create dependencies between domains. The issue is not whether a comprehensive agreement is intrinsically superior but which arrangements achieve which outcomes at what cost.

The account describes difficulties with updating covered rules in the mid-2010s, seven years of negotiations, the November 2018 draft Institutional Framework Agreement and the Federal Council decision of 26 May 2021 not to sign. Dynamic alignment, dispute resolution involving the European Court of Justice and state aid were among the questions. Wage protection (*flanking measures*), sovereignty and cantonal fiscal discretion were legitimate concerns. The role of trade unions, the SVP, business associations and cantons must be established from actual authority, positions and negotiation records; consultation is not necessarily a legal veto. The collapse of one proposed agreement cannot establish that all alternatives were infeasible.

The report associated the decision with changes in recognition of medical devices and cited an annual cost estimate of more than CHF 100 million. Verify the actual regulatory changes, timing, counterfactual and source of the estimate. Subsequent exploratory talks were dated March 2022, formal negotiations March 2024, conclusion December 2024, initialling May 2025 and signature March 2026 in the original account. It described parliamentary consideration in mid-2026, not a completed ratification. Bilaterals III was described as covering electricity, food safety, cross-border health, dynamic alignment, dispute settlement and a cohesion contribution, involving 36 Swiss laws and 94 EU acts. Verify the text, counts, parliamentary status and referendum requirements. The original report also mentioned the Compass initiative and the vote scheduled for 14 June 2026 on population limits; no current outcome is asserted here.

The reactive-loop hypothesis predicts poorer terms or diminished practical options in some delayed episodes. Compare actual treaty texts, reciprocal access, autonomy, wage protection, legal safeguards and changing bargaining conditions (including war, energy and supply-chain pressures). Earlier terms are not an observed counterfactual. A later package may constrain certain policy choices and expand others. Existing bilateral arrangements might be adequate for particular tasks; a more comprehensive agreement might improve some outcomes but impose other costs. This case should distinguish H2 from H3.

### 3.2 Immigration

The original report recounts the 9 February 2014 *Masseneinwanderungsinitiative* (50.3% in favour, 49.7% against), its proposed quotas and renegotiation of free movement, and the 2016 implementation legislation described as a domestic-preference/job-vacancy mechanism in sectors with above-average unemployment. It notes the rejection of the Limitation Initiative in September 2020 by 61.7% and the June 2026 proposal for a ten-million population limit with measures at 9.5 million. Its account of late-2025 polling and the scheduled June vote must be checked; those statements were prospective at the time of writing, not current status.

A ballot expresses a choice among specified options; it does not establish complete knowledge of immigration dynamics or a single preference of all voters. The constraints of free movement and linked agreements, as well as labour demand, housing, migration pressures and rights of migrants, must be investigated rather than inferred from national borders alone. Implementation can reflect lawful compromise among competing objectives, not necessarily evasion of a democratic signal. Compare actual obligations, options for reform, labour and housing outcomes, distributional effects and subsequent preferences. If existing mandates can achieve the specified outcome, a boundary deficit is not established. If they cannot, determine whether the constraint is authority, resources, coordination or a rival cause. Opposition or support for immigration is not a proxy for a developmental stage.

### 3.3 Banking secrecy

The June 2026 account refers to Article 47 of the Federal Banking Act of 1934 and the protection of client information by criminal sanction. Confidentiality and disclosure rules must be distinguished from knowledge of beneficial ownership, due diligence, anti-money-laundering obligations and criminal enforcement. Privacy can be legitimate. Neither commercial capital-flow data nor secrecy alone establishes that provenance and external fiscal harms were known or unknown to public institutions. Test H1 using the actual records and access rules, with lawful safeguards.

The account dates the US Department of Justice investigation of UBS to 2007–2008, cites an estimate of 52,000 US-taxpayer accounts, and describes a 2009 settlement of $780 million and disclosure concerning approximately 4,450 accounts. It reports over eighty banks entering the DOJ programme in 2013–2015. It also traces the OECD Common Reporting Standard and automatic exchange of information (AEOI): parliamentary approval in 2015, data collection beginning in 2017 and first exchanges in 2018; its estimate of partner coverage requires checking. These figures and descriptions are unverified, not newly established evidence.

The report discusses FATCA and the 2014 Model 2 agreement, direct reporting to the IRS, and a reciprocal Model 1 agreement signed in June 2024 with entry into force expected in 2027 at the time of writing. The timing, legal terms and reciprocity must be verified. It suggests that US market access, OECD standard-setting and EU blacklisting pressure shaped Swiss choices. Compare bargaining power, domestic law, public consent, privacy, international cooperation and enforcement, rather than attributing every change to coercion or assuming earlier self-designed rules would have produced better terms. Changes may preserve or increase some options while restricting others.

Commodity trading (the original report names Vitol, Glencore, Trafigura and Mercuria), corporate tax competition and wealth management remain possible research domains. Verify current practices and actual fiscal, environmental and labour impacts before inferring externalization. They are not inevitable repetitions of the banking sequence. Across all three cases, missing evidence is not evidence of an observation gap or resistance, and the selection of illustrative cases cannot establish how often the loop occurs.
