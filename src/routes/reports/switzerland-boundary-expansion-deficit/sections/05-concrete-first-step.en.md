## 5. A Concrete First Step: The Boundary Expansion Pilot

Three innovations are proposed for comparison with existing arrangements, separately or in combination. Their interaction is a testable complementarity hypothesis, not a proven minimum bundle. All timelines below are illustrative from a future authorising decision, not commitments already made. Begin with a disclosed mandate and applicable consent, then evaluate a limited intervention; expansion requires separate authority.

### 5.1 The Global Footprint Dashboard

**Form.** Consider an independent federal commission established by parliamentary act, drawing on the original report's analogies to the Swiss Federal Audit Office and the UK Climate Change Committee. The proposed expert board would be appointed for fixed, non-renewable terms; expertise from academia, civil society and international organisations should be balanced with affected-party testimony. Disclose conflicts, protect independence from regulated industries and ensure they can respond to findings without controlling them. Inventory existing reporting mechanisms first.

**Scope.** Four proposed domains: fiscal effects of tax and wealth-management arrangements (OECD and academic methods); environmental and social impacts of commodity supply chains (satellite data, audits and disclosures where suitable); financed emissions (consider PCAF methods); labour and human rights in production networks. Establish coverage, data quality, causal attribution and uncertainty independently for each. Do not combine these into an unvalidated score. Require lawful purpose, necessity, proportionality, minimal data, retention and access rules, correction rights and secure handling; do not proceed where safeguards cannot be met.

**Output and timing.** An annual multilingual report (four national languages and English) with public hearings, nonbinding assessments and no automatic regulatory mandate. The original proposed two years for drafting legislation after authorisation, and eighteen months from appointment to the first report. Treat these as planning assumptions subject to feasibility and review.

### 5.2 The Cantonal Boundary Expansion Pilots

**Form.** A federal enabling framework for willing cantons and partners, within defined competencies and treaty obligations. Coordination with the Federal Department of Foreign Affairs where federal powers are involved, regular reporting to Parliament, adequate funding and accessible remedies are conditions. Compare existing arrangements and alternatives without expansion.

**Basel:** build on the Upper Rhine cooperation lead to test environmental impact assessment and cross-border health coordination with French and German partners. Any health-data exchange requires separately verified legal basis, necessity and consent where applicable, security and remedy; a shared service does not automatically require shared personal data.

**Geneva:** examine how perspectives from international organisations (the original names the UN, Red Cross, WHO and WTO) could inform cantonal and federal decisions. Do not infer access, representation, mandate or authority from their physical presence.

**Zug:** explore digital finance and international regulatory coordination, comparing existing supervisory arrangements and narrower experiments. Protect consumers, assess financial integrity, costs and distributional effects, and obtain the necessary federal and partner mandates.

**Learning and timing.** Proposed biennial federal assessment, consultation of pilot cantons and five-year sunset requiring explicit renewal, modification, integration or termination. The initial schedule suggested framework legislation within two years and first authorisations within one year of enactment. No pilot is entitled to approval or continuity merely because it produces promising results.

### 5.3 The Citizens' Assembly on Switzerland's Global Role

**Form and question.** Consider 150–200 participants selected by stratified random sampling, six to nine months of part-time deliberation, professional facilitation and compensation to enable participation. Test representativeness and accessibility across linguistic, regional, age and socioeconomic groups. Ask how Switzerland should balance neutrality, sovereignty, self-governance, interests and responsibilities in an interconnected world. No preferred conclusion should be built into the mandate.

**Process and output.** Balanced evidence from institutional and international experts, affected communities and critics, including non-residents not represented by national sortition. Provide translation, accessible materials and a documented opportunity to contest evidence. Publish recommendations in four national languages for Parliament and the Federal Council. They are advisory, not a referendum or constitutional mandate; authorities may adopt, modify or decline them with reasons. Participants may recommend no expansion. The original schedule proposed authorising legislation within one year, convening within six months and recommendations within two years; test feasibility and costs.

### 5.4 Selection logic

The Dashboard concerns evidence, the pilots institutional action, the assembly deliberation. They may complement or substitute for each other. Compare components and combinations against existing arrangements, where feasible and ethical. For a specified outcome, define the interaction as the combined incremental effect minus the sum of the separate incremental effects against the same baseline; it can be positive, zero or negative. Disclose objectives and risks, compare harms, ensure independent evaluation, and do not use a pilot to evade oversight. Authorisation for one experiment is not authorisation for scale-up.

### 5.5 Metrics and stopping rules

Separate process measures from outcomes. Dashboard coverage, awareness, hearings and citations; pilot agreements, implementation and transmission of lessons; assembly participation, representativeness, deliberative quality and recommendation uptake are useful process records, not certificates of success. Compare independently specified outcomes: accuracy and use of evidence, actual environmental or labour outcomes, costs, distribution, service quality, rights and legitimacy. A recommendation can legitimately be rejected; a new agreement can make outcomes worse. Track formal competence, feasible options, reciprocal access and bargaining influence separately. A proactive/reactive ratio requires a locked coding population and definitions, not a sovereignty score.

Prespecify criteria, comparators, data access and review arrangements before authorisation. Missing information is unknown, not success or failure. If independent outcomes do not improve, assess implementation and the diagnosis separately; adverse effects, disproportionate cost or violations require revision or stopping. Neither lack of uptake nor adverse results are automatically immune absorption. No trial or hypothesis test in this report has been run.
