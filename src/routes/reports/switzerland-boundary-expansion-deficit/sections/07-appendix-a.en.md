## Appendix A: Value Systems & Policy Mindsets

### A.1 An optional interpretive vocabulary

Spiral Dynamics is an unaudited external framework [HR/H]. It is not a validated stage measurement or a causal explanation here. The original report's portrait of internal Yellow and external Blue-Orange is retained as a historical attribution, not an active classification of Switzerland. Political actors can hold several values, change positions and disagree within a party. No value label justifies withholding rights, concealing intentions or dismissing objections.

### A.2 Four historical portraits, not rankings

The original Blue portrait associated constitutional procedure, neutrality and continuity with order and stability; its proposed trade-off was rigidity when circumstances change. The Orange portrait associated banking, commodity trading, cantonal tax competition and industrial innovation with performance and competitive advantage; its proposed risk was neglect of harms. The Green portrait associated the 1937 *Friedensabkommen*, social partnership, humanitarian traditions and environmental movements with solidarity and inclusion; it raised questions about the scope of solidarity and consensus costs. The Yellow portrait associated federal distribution of authority, direct democracy, the *Zauberformel* and protection of minority cantons with integration and systems thinking. These are interpretive associations from the original report, not verified descriptions, causal mechanisms or a hierarchy of institutions.

The following longer portraits reconstruct the original interpretive vocabulary, not empirical classifications. They are unaudited historical hypotheses [HR/H].

**Order, rules and stability (Blue).** The original associated the constitutional settlement of 1848, respect for procedure, direct-democratic legitimacy and military neutrality with a preference for order and continuity. An actor reasoning in these terms might ask whether a new agreement respects the constitution, protects dependable rules and leaves citizens a meaningful decision. Such questions can prevent arbitrary action and protect minorities. The suggested limitation is that procedures appropriate to earlier circumstances might be difficult to change; this must be shown for particular decisions, not inferred from a colour label.

**Achievement and competition (Orange).** The original associated financial services, commodity trading, cantonal tax competition and industrial innovation with an orientation towards performance and opportunity. This perspective can motivate innovation, efficient provision and experimentation. It may also favour metrics of domestic competitiveness over costs that fall outside the accounts used to judge success. This is a hypothesis about measurement and incentives, not a claim that business interests necessarily neglect social consequences. Compare actual firms, regulations and decisions, including initiatives to reduce harm.

**Inclusion and solidarity (Green).** The original pointed to the 1937 *Friedensabkommen*, social partnership, humanitarian traditions and environmental movements as possible expressions of solidarity and negotiated inclusion. This lens asks who participates, how affected groups are heard, and what obligations extend beyond the immediate constituency. It also raises a possible tension between inclusive consultation and the cost of reaching agreement; inclusion can improve decisions rather than merely delay them. Neither the historical examples nor any particular party's place in the portrait has been verified here.

**Systems thinking and integration (Yellow).** The original associated federalism, linguistic accommodation, the *Zauberformel*, direct democracy and minority safeguards with attempts to integrate different interests without making them uniform. It invites inquiry into whether combinations of local discretion and shared rules can handle different tasks better than a single uniform solution. Such arrangements can also conceal inequalities or create coordination burdens. They do not measure a developmental stage, demonstrate national superiority or establish that a similar structure will work internationally. These portraits are optional interpretive aids; actual rights and evidence take precedence over the framework.

### A.3 Value tensions across boundaries

The contrast between domestic and external priorities is worth investigating using actual policy decisions, affected groups, mandates and documented trade-offs. A person may value procedural stability, competitiveness, solidarity and coordination at once. Neither stronger solidarity nor wider integration automatically yields a better outcome; specific rights and costs matter. The earlier colour attribution should not decide which claims or actors are legitimate.

### A.4 Political disagreement and *Sonderfall*

The original report linked the SVP's sovereignty and competition concerns to its Blue-Orange portrait, and mentioned distinct emphases among Social Democrats, Greens, FDP and Centre. These are historical interpretive attributions, not measurements of party members or evidence of their motives. Analyse specific proposals, stated reasons, votes and effects. The aim of deliberation is to hear legitimate concerns and evidence, including arguments against expansion, not to engineer a change of values without consent.
