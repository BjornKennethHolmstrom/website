## Appendix C: The Governance as Engineering Connection

### C.1 Model scope

The formal series motivates questions, not national rankings or certification of designs. [HR] denotes historical, unaudited model material; [H] denotes a hypothesis. Papers I–V have not been audited here. Their original results are not declared false, but the following governance transfers must be qualified or corrected in light of accepted VI, VIII, XX and the capstone Appendix E.5.

### C.2 The papers in brief

**Paper I — Governance Stability Simulator [HR].** Aggregation of local information is a model concern. Subsidiarity as a necessary topology is not established for Swiss tasks. **Paper II — Fractality as Stability [HR].** Models of multiple timescales motivate comparing nested and other arrangements; the claim of uniquely stable fractal architecture is not transferred. No formal endorsement of Swiss federalism follows.

**Paper III — The Observability-Democracy Connection [HR].** There is no universal cutoff at two or three layers or at SNR=1. For a scalar Gaussian signal with independent Gaussian noise at power SNR=1, mutual information is 0.5 bit per observation. An identity chain can preserve information at any depth; one lossy mapping may remove it. A ballot is not a measurement of full preference or intensity.

**Paper IV — Requisite Variety and the Commons [HR].** Count alone does not determine task adequacy. Consider the corrected VI static noiseless additive model: disturbance subspace D, acceptable subspace L, observation y=Cd and outcome x=d+u. The condition K=D∩ker C ⊆ L is necessary and, when all of −D is available, sufficient for exact adequacy. The hidden gap is dim K − dim(K∩L). The count dim D − dim(D∩L) − rank(C restricted to D) is only a lower bound. A nonpositive count is not a certificate. With D=ℝ² and L=span(e₂), C=(1,0) yields hidden gap 0; C=(0,1) gives 1, although rank and count are equal. These are stipulated examples, not Swiss data.

**Paper V — The Coordination Failure Tax [HR].** In a stipulated sequential retention model Q/Q₀=∏(1−fᵢ), 0≤fᵢ≤1, the fractional loss 1−∏(1−fᵢ)≤∑fᵢ follows by induction from a+b−ab≤a+b. Four half-retentions leave 1/16 and lose 15/16, below the sum 2. A task requiring 1/20 of the original capacity passes; one requiring 1/10 fails. The product is not a measured institutional law and does not certify categorical incapacity. Interactions in independently specified policy outcomes can be positive or negative; see Chapter 5.

### C.3 The revised Variety Gap approach

Paper VIII v0.2 withdraws the old composite method, estimates, bands and validation. The original Swiss internal, external and composite scores, interval, implied threshold trend and illustrative assignments are withdrawn. The audit's conditional substitution into the historical expressions cannot reproduce those scores from the printed common nominal assignments; it is not a new estimate or bound on real Switzerland. No separately estimated vectors, permeability value, justified weighting or interval method were provided.

Use a prospective, task-relative record: (1) evaluation — outcomes, rights and affected people; (2) observation — needed information, channels, access, reliability and missingness; (3) modelling — causal relevance, uncertainty and alternatives; (4) decision — authority, incentives, procedures and admissible choices; (5) actuation — feasible resources, actions, timing, enforceability and effects. Record source, date, comparison, definitions and unknowns for each. For example, a cross-border health service may need timely coordination but not indiscriminate patient data sharing. A supply-chain task may need evidence from workers rather than additional price records. No profile is filled with assumed Swiss values.

For a finite deterministic task, let A(w) be adequate feasible actions in world w. Each observation cell must admit a common action, otherwise no decision rule based on that observation can guarantee adequacy. If any A(w) is empty no cover exists; when a cover exists, the number of distinguishable observation cells must be at least the minimum cardinality of an action set intersecting every A(w). This XX action-cover bound is necessary, not sufficient for a given partition. In a simple two-region example, observing the domestic disturbance may suffice for correcting it while not distinguishing external harm. Better observation alone does not confer authority or resources for a joint task.

### C.4 The Goodhart–Ashby synthesis and Asymmetric Observation

Corrected XX treats proxy safety as an ordering property on a specified feasible set, with ties and the domain of lotteries considered separately. For `X={(0,0),(0,1),(2,0),(2,1)}`, proxy M=a and target T=a+b, every strict improvement in M raises T, but ties may differ in T. The lottery with probabilities 0.75 on (0,0) and 0.25 on (2,0) has expected M=T=0.5, versus M=0, T=1 at (0,1). Thus an ordering valid for deterministic strict improvements need not be safe for lotteries. Omitting b from the objective does not destroy its observation. Incentives to suppress evidence, missing information, legitimate mandates and failures to act must be tested separately. A universal crisis or irreversibility prediction is withdrawn.

### C.5 From model to design

The models motivate the Dashboard, pilots and citizens' assembly, but do not certify their constraints, feasibility, necessity or sufficiency. An explicit mapping to tasks, authority, data, resources and independent outcomes would be required. The three tests in §1.6 and the proposal evaluation in §5.5 are prospective. No empirical test has been run. The audit and original sources are retained in the research record.
