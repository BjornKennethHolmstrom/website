## Executive Summary

**Revision candidate GA-R02-R2 v0.2 (November 2026).** The June 2026 edition proposed internal, external and composite Variety Gap estimates without a reproducible measurement procedure. These estimates, their interval and threshold interpretation, and the illustrative parameter assignments are withdrawn. The correction draws on the revised Papers VI, VIII and XX and the corrected capstone and Subsidiarity Deficit; it is not an empirical re-evaluation of Switzerland. Historical figures, institutions, events and precedents in this report remain claims requiring source verification. The original edition is preserved separately.

Switzerland offers a particularly interesting setting for examining governance across boundaries: municipalities, cantons, federal institutions, direct-democratic processes and international agreements coexist with internationally connected economic activity. These arrangements may perform differently across tasks. Neither the existence of federalism nor the frequency of votes establishes national observational adequacy, optimality or comparative response capacity.

**Boundary Expansion Deficit:** the hypothesis that a mismatch between the footprint of consequential activities and the mandates or coordination arrangements of institutions is a binding cause of inadequate outcomes for a specified task. Crossing a border is not sufficient evidence of such a deficit. Adequate existing arrangements would count against it. Distinguish evaluation, observation, modelling, decision and actuation; lawful authority and actual capacity are not interchangeable.

**Asymmetric Observation:** the hypothesis that useful information exists in commercial institutions but is not adequately connected to public decisions. Price and flow data do not necessarily describe environmental, labour or fiscal consequences. Information may be absent even privately, already publicly available, contested, or known but not acted on because of authority, resources or political disagreement. These alternatives require separate tests.

**Internal Optimization–External Shock–Reactive Fudge Loop:** a proposed episode pattern in which domestic priorities, external pressures and incremental adjustments interact. It is not a law of sovereignty erosion. Reactive agreements can preserve or expand options. Compare the terms, obligations, reciprocal benefits, participation and feasible alternatives rather than treating delay or agreement counts as sufficient indicators.

Seven candidate mechanisms are developed in Chapter 2, with a separate synthesis of latency and veto topology. *Sonderfall*, *Konkordanz* and pragmatic neutrality provide interpretive questions about values, incentives and institutional rules, not fixed national motives. The historical Spiral Dynamics portraits in Appendix A are unaudited attributions; they do not determine stages, rights or causal conclusions. The three empirical narratives (EU relations, immigration, banking secrecy) are leads for comparison, not proof of the diagnoses.

The proposed Global Footprint Dashboard, cantonal pilots and citizens' assembly address informational, institutional and deliberative questions. Global Impact Assessments, foresight, international interfaces and federal learning may complement them. They require transparent mandates, appropriate consent and authorisation, resources, independent evaluation, remedy and the ability to stop. None is established as necessary, sufficient or superior to existing institutions.

The question is how to preserve valuable arrangements while investigating whether, where and in what form additional coordination would improve outcomes. The three diagnosis tests in Chapter 1 are separate from the proposal evaluation in Chapter 5. A legitimate conclusion may be that no expansion is needed.
