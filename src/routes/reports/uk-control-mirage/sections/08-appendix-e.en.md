## Appendix E: About this report and how it was made

### How it was made

This report was generated by AI language models (Claude, ChatGPT, DeepSeek) from prompts by Björn Kenneth Holmström. Several models were prompted in parallel to approach the UK's situation from different angles, applying the systems-thinking framework he had set out. He chose the topic, posed the questions and decided to publish the result, but wrote little of the text himself.

The report has not been systematically fact-checked. Statements about British institutions, laws, events, figures and sources may be inaccurate or invented, and should be verified against primary sources before being relied on or cited. Like any work of synthesis, it is provisional. It makes no claim to finality. It claims only that the lens it offers merits testing against reality — and that the testing, in the end, is what matters most.

### Perspective and limits

Neither the models nor the person who prompted them write from within the UK's institutional core. Björn Kenneth Holmström is not British, does not live in the UK and has no formal training or professional experience in British public administration or political science. He studied engineering physics at Uppsala University, in the systems engineering track (*systemteknik*), which included courses in control theory and signal processing, and has worked with mathematical optimisation at TOMLAB Optimization, his father's company. The framework applied here draws on complexity science, developmental psychology and governance theory.

That distance limits access to the granular, day-to-day texture of British policy-making. An outside lens can sometimes raise questions that insiders have learned not to hear, but it can just as easily miss what insiders know. The report does not claim insider knowledge. It offers a lens that may or may not prove useful to those who hold institutional positions and are searching for frameworks that make sense of what they are experiencing.

Feedback, criticism, and corrections are welcome. The argument is offered in the spirit of collaborative sense-making, not definitive pronouncement.

### The Country Reports Series

This report was the sixth in a series of Country Reports for Systemic Change, all produced in the same way. The first examined Germany through the lens of an execution deficit. The second examined France through the lens of an integration deficit. The third examined Sweden through the lens of a feedback deficit. The fourth examined India through the lens of a synchronisation deficit. The fifth examined the European Union through the lens of a coherence deficit. Together, the first six reports propose a diagnostic framework built around six dimensions of adaptive capacity: the ability to *execute*, *integrate*, *sense*, *synchronise*, *cohere*, and *deliver* — which the series suggests determine whether governance systems can meet the complexity of the 21st century. The series does not claim to be complete. It is meant as a foundation on which further analysis, deeper testing, and better design can be built.
