## Appendix A: Value Systems and Policy Mindsets — A Guide for the University Context

### A Note on This Appendix

This optional appendix preserves the original's complementary lens on institutional culture. Spiral Dynamics is used as a heuristic vocabulary for candidate concerns, not as a validated developmental taxonomy, a classification of people or evidence about universities.

### A.1 The Basic Insight

The four portraits distinguish possible emphases in institutional practices: standards and stability, measurable achievement, inclusion and care, and systemic integration. These are not personality types, ranks or disciplinary affiliations. An actor may draw on several concerns, and actual institutional practice requires evidence.

Each portrait suggests possible strengths and failure modes in context. No perspective is assigned epistemic privilege or treated as inherently better. The question is which concerns and arrangements serve a specified task while respecting legitimate plurality and rights.

The original draws on Spiral Dynamics integral theory. Its historical provenance and empirical status are not verified here.

### A.2 The Value Systems in the University Arena

**Order and Stability (sometimes called 'Blue') — the Disciplinary and Bureaucratic University.** The original associates this portrait with departments, tenure, peer review and procedural integrity. Possible strengths include rigour, methodological standards, continuity and due process protecting intellectual freedom. Possible failure modes are rigid boundaries, inappropriate paradigm enforcement and disproportionate procedural burden. Investigate actual arrangements; legitimate quality controls and effective administration are not evidence of a Disciplinary Imperative.

**Achievement and Efficiency (sometimes called 'Orange') — the Entrepreneurial and Managerial University.** Rankings, funding competition, commercialisation, enrolment and performance measures illustrate the original's concerns. Possible strengths include mobilising resources, innovation and practical contributions. Possible risks include distorted proxies, audit burden and prioritising credential revenue over learning. Test both sides, with actual quality and resource evidence rather than infer motives from commercial activity.

**Inclusion and Care (sometimes called 'Green') — the Collegial and Inclusive University.** Shared governance, diversity, student wellbeing, intellectual freedom and public mission are the original's examples. Accessibility, equity, community and meaningful participation may protect legitimate interests. A metric-only diversity scheme, consultation without decision rights or a purely symbolic public commitment could undermine those aims. Such outcomes require evidence; actual inclusion and effective governance are genuine alternatives. Dissent and refusal remain legitimate.

The original links the absorption of Green rhetoric into Blue and Orange administrative structures to the Performative Reform Trap. Retain this as a configuration hypothesis: inclusion commitments could become symbolic if decision rights, resources and actual outcomes remain unchanged. Effective inclusion, meaningful participation and justified administrative safeguards are genuine rivals. No value-system label establishes this mechanism.

**Integrative and Systemic (sometimes called 'Yellow') — the Adaptive and Integrative University.** The original associates this portrait with interdisciplinary institutes, translation and institutional design. Possible strengths are attention to functional fit, interacting mechanisms and uncertainty. Risks include superficial synthesis, detachment from implementation, shared blind spots, unvalidated metrics, compulsory integration and concentration of authority. The Audit and Pilot are expressions of the original's integrative aspiration, not evidence of superior perspective. Their designers and panels face the same scrutiny as existing institutions.

### A.3 The Integration Deficit as a Value‑System Configuration Problem

The original proposes that disciplinary standards, managerial efficiency and collegial inclusion can interact in ways that obstruct appropriate exchange. This is a configuration hypothesis; the labels themselves neither identify mechanisms nor establish causation. Successful combinations of these concerns, valid specialist work, substantive inclusion and durable learning are rival cases.

A translation mechanism may be useful where relevant perspectives need to interact, but no Yellow layer is formally necessary. Preserve legitimate disagreement and independent standards. The proposed spiral requires the actual links under H2/H3/H5, not the presence or absence of a value label.
