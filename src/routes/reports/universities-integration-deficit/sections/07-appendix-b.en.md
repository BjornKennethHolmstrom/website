## Appendix B: International Analogues and Precedents

### B.1 The Santa Fe Institute: Integration Through Institutional Design

The original describes the Santa Fe Institute (SFI), founded in 1984, as organised around complex adaptive systems and bringing together physicists, biologists, economists, computer scientists and anthropologists through shared research programmes. Its organisation, faculty composition, evaluation, founding date and degree of disciplinary separation are unverified historical leads. Claude's v0.1 audit review supplied the resident/external faculty and evaluation questions. Apply the same classification as in §3.7 (ownership, funding, degree-granting, governance/accountability, openness), permitting overlapping affiliations.

The original emphasises that SFI is small, well-funded and unusual relative to a comprehensive research university. Protected space, appropriate staffing and suitable evaluation may support cross-field inquiry. Existence of a design does not establish causal benefit, necessity or transferability. Compare independent quality, resources, institutional missions and legitimate alternatives. This is consistent with §3.2 and the Shadow University comparison; SFI is not assumed superior or a threat because of its category.

### B.2 Olin College of Engineering: Integration Through Founding Design

The original describes Olin College as founded in 1997 to transform engineering education, integrating design, entrepreneurship and humanities, team projects from the first year and collaborative faculty assessment. It describes a small undergraduate institution, not a comprehensive university. Verify those claims and outcomes. Claude's v0.1 audit review supplied charter versus entering-class dates and whether Olin uses tenure as unverified questions. Assess Olin under the same classification criteria as §3.7; founding design may help, but retrofit changes may also work. No efficacy or transferability follows from existence.

### B.3 The Institute for Advanced Study: Protected Space for Integrative Thought

The original cites the Institute for Advanced Study (IAS), Princeton, founded in 1930, as protected intellectual space, describing permanent faculty and visiting members with relief from teaching and administration and naming Albert Einstein, Erwin Panofsky and Clifford Geertz. Dates, obligations, appointments and claimed integrative contributions require verification. Claude's v0.1 audit review supplies IAS's organisation into Schools as an unverified lead complicating the characterization. Time for reading, thought and conversation may be useful for specific tasks; compare other arrangements, actual outputs and costs. Classify IAS symmetrically with universities and other Shadow examples; no blanket commitment or lack of commitment is inferred.

### B.4 The Leiden Manifesto: Metrics Reform in Research Assessment

The original cites the Leiden Manifesto (2015) and its ten principles as a research-assessment reform emphasising qualitative judgement and contextual metrics. Verify the original text, scope and evidence of implementation and effect. Claude's v0.1 audit review supplied DORA (2012) and CoARA (2022) as unverified additional comparators. Separate bibliometric use, evaluation and incentive effects; no reform instrument is validated by its existence. The original names the 'Specialisation–Fragmentation spiral' here; that variant remains a historical name pending author confirmation, not a silent change to the main label.

### B.5 Arizona State University: Institutional Redesign for Societal Impact

The original describes Arizona State University under Michael Crow as pursuing societal-impact redesign through transdisciplinary schools (School of Sustainability and School for the Future of Innovation in Society), broader tenure criteria and regional development. It cites a large public comprehensive university as a potential retrofit comparator. Verify school names, dates, actual authority, promotion criteria, resources and favourable and adverse outcomes; Claude's v0.1 audit review supplied primary sources on tenure and school reorganisation as unverified leads. Its existence is not evidence of a general deficit, successful causal redesign or transferability.
