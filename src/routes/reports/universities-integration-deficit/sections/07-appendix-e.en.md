## Appendix E: About this report and how it was made

### How it was made

This report was generated by AI language models (Claude, ChatGPT, DeepSeek) from prompts by Björn Kenneth Holmström. Several models were prompted in parallel to analyse university governance from different analytical angles, applying the Governance as Engineering framework that he had set out. He chose the topic, posed the questions and decided to publish the result, but wrote little of the text himself.

The report has not been systematically fact-checked. Statements about specific universities, funding systems, studies, figures and sources may be inaccurate or invented, and should be verified against primary sources before being relied on or cited. Like any work of synthesis, it is provisional.

### Perspective and limits

Neither the models nor the person who prompted them write from within the university. Björn Kenneth Holmström is not an academic and holds no university position. He studied engineering physics at Uppsala University, in the systems engineering track (*systemteknik*), which included courses in control theory and signal processing, and has worked with mathematical optimisation at TOMLAB Optimization, his father's company.

That distance from the university's internal structures restricts access to the granular, day‑to‑day texture of academic governance. An outside view is free of disciplinary and institutional loyalties, which can sometimes surface questions insiders no longer ask — but it is equally likely to miss what academics know, and why disciplinary boundaries that look like silos from outside often protect rigour and expertise. Feedback, criticism and corrections are welcome.

### The Organizational Reports Series

This report is the third in the Organizational Reports Series, an extension of the governance‑as‑engineering framework from nation‑states to the complex adaptive coordination systems that shape our world—healthcare systems, technology companies, universities, non‑governmental organisations, and the hybrid institutions that blur the boundaries between these categories. All reports in the series were produced in the same way. The first report examined frontier AI governance, proposing a Coherence–Velocity Trap. The second examined healthcare systems, proposing a Clinical Observability Gap. This third report proposes an Integration Deficit in universities. The series does not claim to be complete. It is meant as a foundation on which further analysis, deeper testing, and better design can be built. The university—the institution charged with producing the knowledge that societies need to understand themselves—may be the case where, if the report is right, the Integration Deficit's consequences are most profound. The question it raises is whether the university can build the integrative capacity to perceive what it already knows.
