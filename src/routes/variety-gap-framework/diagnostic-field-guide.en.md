#### A practical guide to task-relative institutional inquiry

**Version 2.0 — November 2026 · Revised following Papers VI and VIII**

---

### What this guide is

This guide helps policy professionals, institutional designers, citizens and researchers investigate structural constraints on adaptive capacity. It draws on the Country Reports for Systemic Change, organizational studies and the Governance as Engineering working papers. It can be used for a national jurisdiction, municipality, service pathway or organization, provided the task and unit of analysis are specified afresh at each scale.

**Research status.** This is a proposed methodology, not a validated diagnostic instrument. The earlier edition treated observation architecture as the primary explanation of governance failure. Revised Paper VI distinguishes evaluation, observation, causal modelling, decision and actuation; no one of these is a universal explanation. Revised Paper VIII replaces the composite index with a task-relative diagnostic profile. Its earlier country scores, intervals, critical threshold, bands, trajectory classes and pilot-validation claim are withdrawn from active use. Do not use them to rank systems or predict collapse. The earlier reports remain available as research material; their claims and recommendations require individual review.

### The core insight

Ask what an institution must achieve, for whom, under which constraints and within what time. Which distinctions in the world could change an appropriate decision? What can the institution observe, infer, decide and actually do? A system may value something without observing it, observe it without understanding it, or understand it without having the authority or resources to respond. Conversely, a narrow objective does not necessarily eliminate other observations. A dashboard is not the whole architecture.

A **core deficit** is a proposed explanation of a task-specific shortfall, not an essential property of a country. Several deficits may coexist; a well-functioning pathway or a non-architectural explanation is also a legitimate finding. Do not assume a single root cause.

### Quick start: research, including optional AI assistance

**Step 1 — Define the case.** Record the affected population, task, desired and unacceptable outcomes, rights, deadlines, decision pathway, feasible interventions, jurisdiction and period. Identify a comparator and document how the case was selected. Distinguish the analyst's proposed values from the institution's actual objectives and from the values of affected people.

**Step 2 — Gather evidence.** Identify primary records, dates, laws, budgets, service data, evaluations and relevant testimony. Include routine successes, failures and unresponded episodes; identify missing evidence and access limitations. Ask whose reports are recorded, heard and acted on, and whose interests or experiences are excluded. Public transparency and internal access are separate questions.

**Step 3 — Generate competing hypotheses.** If using LLMs, collect initial responses separately before cross-feeding. A systems-thinking or Spiral Dynamics lens may help generate questions, but is optional and is not a validated measure of people or societies. Use a prompt such as:

> Analyze the specified governance task in [jurisdiction and period]. Identify possible constraints in evaluation, observation, modelling, decision and actuation. For each proposed mechanism, name the evidence needed, a competing explanation, a counterexample or observation that would weaken it, and relevant affected groups. Separate facts, inferences and proposals. Do not invent citations or assume one deficit, a recurring loop or a developmental stage.

**Step 4 — Challenge and verify.** Compare outputs, including disagreements. Cross-feed only after recording the independent drafts; cross-fed outputs are not independent confirmations. Models may share data, assumptions and blind spots. Check cited sources directly, seek local expertise and affected perspectives, and track unresolved contradictions. Agreement among models is not evidence of truth. Record the model versions, dates, prompts and material supplied where possible.

**Step 5 — Synthesize and report.** Distinguish observations, scoped formal results, model illustrations, interpretive hypotheses and proposed interventions. Preserve alternative explanations and negative findings. A useful report structure is: case specification and executive summary → candidate deficit and evidence profile → signature pattern (if supported) → structural mechanisms and alternatives → incentives and resistance → cultural and distributional context → possible transition and first step → limitations and tests. Philosophical reflection may be included, marked as such.

### The diagnostic framework

#### Step 1: Identify the task and candidate deficit

Use the five axes from revised VI and VIII. These are questions, not independent components of a universal latent score.

| Axis | Questions to investigate |
|---|---|
| Evaluation | Which outcomes, interests and constraints count? Which are omitted or contested? |
| Observation | Are consequential distinctions accessible in time and at the relevant scale? |
| Modelling | Can the evidence distinguish the competing mechanisms relevant to the decision? |
| Decision | Does the decision respond appropriately to the evidence, authority and trade-offs? |
| Actuation | Can the chosen action be delivered with the available resources, authority and capacity? |

The earlier country reports used memorable deficit names. The table below is a reading index of report hypotheses (including the later reports), not a classification validated by independent country data. The labels do not imply one deficit per jurisdiction. See the live report listing for the current collection.

| Jurisdiction | Report theme to investigate |
|---|---|
| Germany | Spending mirage / execution and sensemaking |
| France | Decisiveness mirage / integration |
| Sweden | Competence trap / feedback pathways |
| India | Leap–lag cycle / synchronization across scale |
| European Union | Coherence mirage / alignment of implementation |
| United Kingdom | Control mirage / control–delivery mismatch |
| Brazil | Accumulation deficit / persistence of capacity |
| Russia | Legibility deficit / incentives around accurate information |
| United States | Integration deficit / coordination across institutions |
| Finland | Throughput constraint / ability to act on evidence |
| China | Calibration deficit / model correction |
| Japan | Continuity trap / paradigm revision |
| Nigeria | Substrate deficit / formal and informal provision |
| Israel | Boundary deficit / institutional and political settlement |
| Spain | Transition trap / integrative closure |
| Switzerland | Boundary expansion deficit / interdependence |
| South Korea | Tournament trap / consequences of competition under effective scarcity |
| Bhutan | Interface race / coupling objectives and actuation |
| Netherlands | Feasibility debt / commitments before durable feasibility |

For the organizational reports and cross-case syntheses, apply the same task specification. Treat the titles as research questions, not proof that the proposed mechanisms hold.

#### Step 2: Map a possible signature pattern

A recurring loop is a hypothesis about dated events. Check the temporal ordering, the mechanism at each link, and whether a loop actually recurs or changes its baseline. A one-off sequence, exogenous shock or stable equilibrium may fit the evidence better.

For example, the Sweden report proposes a feedback-and-delayed-correction pattern; the Japan report a pattern of incremental accommodation and deferral; the Israel report a relationship between emergency responses and political fragmentation; and the Nigeria report a pattern involving extraction and informal adaptation. These are historical report hypotheses, not causal findings certified by this guide. Look for episodes that do not follow the proposed pattern and compare what a rival explanation predicts.

#### Step 3: Identify structural mechanisms and measure the profile

Investigate relevant fiscal, electoral, constitutional, administrative, information and security arrangements, as well as parallel provision. Do not prescribe a fixed number of mechanisms. Separate formal authority, actual implementation, resource constraints, and behavior. Include plausible explanations involving leadership, incentives, shocks, resources or policy choices rather than attributing everything to architecture.

| Indicator family | Candidate evidence and caution |
|---|---|
| Observation and disturbances | Inventory indicators and dated events; counts or sample correlations do not establish task-relative rank or relevance. |
| Latency | Define start, response endpoint and deadline; include routine and nonresponded episodes and record right-censoring. |
| Fidelity | Compare transmitted evidence with a specified reference; distinguish external access from internal availability. |
| Implementation | For an eligible cohort, code enacted instruments, resources for the implementing body, and independent evaluation at least two years later confirming changed behavior or outcomes. Code each criterion separately: met, not met, unknown or not yet assessable. These are candidate criteria, not a universal definition. |
| Oscillation | Record named series, period, scale and shocks; volatility is not necessarily internal instability. |
| Bypass | Document alternative provision, coverage, reliability and effects; it may help or harm. |
| Symbolic reform | Describe outcomes for the same cohort; do not count the complement of an implementation proportion again. |
| Attention | Record sampling frequency, delay, resources and actual decision uptake separately. More sampling is not necessarily more relevant information. |

For a latency study, select episodes by documented start date in a fixed window, prespecify the endpoint and retain nonresponses up to the cutoff. Under appropriate non-informative censoring assumptions, estimate response-time distributions using Kaplan–Meier; report an estimable median or restricted mean over a stated horizon. Avoid selecting only completed responses or prominent crises. Do not assume the direction of selection bias.

Signal suppression, definition changes and symbolic transparency are possible mechanisms of misrepresentation; require dated evidence and consider alternative explanations. Metric attrition and proxy divergence are reasons to investigate, not evidence of approaching a universal threshold. Record uncertainty, missingness and competing accounts separately; never encode missing data as zero capacity.

#### Step 4: Investigate resistance and enabling conditions

The earlier reports describe a political immune system: possible incentives and arrangements that preserve a status quo. This is a hypothesis, not a presumption of intentional obstruction. Investigate bureaucratic delay, announcements without delivery, satisfied competence, stability incentives, extraction coalitions, security priorities and veto structures when relevant. Identify specific actors, resources, legal constraints, stated reasons, beneficiaries and affected groups. Legitimate safeguards, disagreement, inadequate implementation capacity or competing obligations may explain resistance. Record evidence of successful correction too.

#### Step 5: Examine cultural and distributional context

The earlier guide used the notion of a cultural anchor, including terms such as *saklighet* in Sweden and *wa* or *kaizen* in Japan. Such concepts may suggest questions about institutional narratives; they are not causal explanations of national character. Seek local accounts, counterexamples and differences within groups. Ask who defines normality, which values are disputed, whose knowledge is recognized and who bears costs. Cultural legitimacy, rights and consent matter independently of predictive or operational performance.

#### Step 6: Design and test a transition

Match the intervention to the diagnosed constraint. More information cannot by itself fix inadequate authority, resources, conflicting objectives or a poor decision rule. Candidate instruments include a transparent, voluntary pilot, a new reporting channel, independent evaluation, a changed decision rule, a capacity-building measure or a revised coordination boundary. Work with existing institutions where useful, but do not disguise a proposal or evade legitimate oversight. Agree on rights, consent, responsibilities, costs, evaluation criteria, stopping conditions and restoration or exit arrangements before testing. Scale only when evidence and receiving capacity justify it; central or shared provision may sometimes be appropriate.

A protected experimental space is one design option, not the only route to reform. Identify an actionable first step, its comparator, what would count as failure and a realistic timeline. Specify who can authorize the action, who bears the risks and how the outcome will be independently checked. A proposal may be revised or abandoned.

### Using this guide: diagnostic profile worksheet

**Case header:** case ID and codebook version; task and period; affected population; rights and outcome tolerances; unit and decision pathway; feasible actions and constraints; comparator and selection method.

Use one row per consequential distinction or intervention. Duplicate the worksheet for different tasks; do not aggregate them into a single country score.

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| Distinction or intervention | What could change the appropriate decision? |
| Axis | Evaluation / observation / modelling / decision / actuation |
| Operational definition | Unit, denominator, timing and measurement method |
| Provenance | Source, version, date, access and extraction method |
| Observation or estimate | Value or qualitative finding; unknown is allowed |
| Uncertainty | Sampling, measurement, model uncertainty and missingness |
| Relevance | Link to task, alternative explanation and affected groups |
| Verification | Independent check, conflicting evidence, unresolved status |
| Response | Proposed action, responsible actor, constraints and follow-up |

Add a narrative summary of the candidate deficit(s), possible loop, mechanisms and alternatives, resistance and enabling conditions, cultural context, first step, evaluation, exit conditions and timeline. No composite, threshold or rank is required.

**Fictional example (illustrative, not data).** An outbreak pathway must decide within 48 hours whether a cluster warrants investigation. A documented 72-hour delay may establish a missed deadline if the clocks are valid. It does not identify the cause. Compare availability of local records, the authorization process and staffing separately. Missing records remain unknown. This is a task-relative investigation, not a country diagnosis.

### From case descriptions to validation

A prospective study should freeze the task and outcome criteria, case selection and temporal cutoff, evidence coding, alternative explanations and predictions, and independent outcome assessment. Include adverse cases, successful pathways and nonresponse. Separate development and held-out cases, guard against outcome leakage into predictors, compare simpler baselines, and report coder disagreement, false positives, false negatives and unknowns. These are proposed steps from revised VIII, not completed validation. No sample size or universal threshold is established.

### Where the framework comes from

The first edition described a multi-model exploratory workflow for the Country Reports. The current guide retains structural inquiry and the report format, but no longer treats that workflow as independent evidence or attributes a validated cognitive stage to a model. Papers VI (*The Variety Gap*) and VIII (*Measuring the Variety Gap*) supply the revised conceptual and measurement basis. The [working papers](/working-papers), [report collection](/reports) and [synthesis brief](/syntheses/brief) provide the broader context. Other dependent papers, reports, the historical glossary and prior PDFs remain subject to their own review. A later correction of one paper does not automatically validate an earlier application.

The framework is a living body of work. Publish dated evidence, methods, counterexamples, limitations and corrections where possible, and respect privacy and consent. AI can support inquiry, but accountability for the report remains with its authors.
