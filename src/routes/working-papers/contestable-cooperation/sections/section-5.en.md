## 5. A defensible normative floor and a corrective trajectory

Assessment requires a declared normative standard. Our provisional standard is non-domination coupled with a basic rights floor and effective remedies. It is grounded in republican political theory [13,14] and informed by human-rights protections for remedies, expression, peaceful assembly and association [15]. The relevant legal duties depend on jurisdiction, actors and circumstances; this paper is not a legal determination of any particular dispute. Principles on effective grievance mechanisms can also guide institutional assessment: the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights identify legitimacy, accessibility, predictability, equity, transparency, rights compatibility, learning and engagement as effectiveness considerations [22]. Their direct legal and institutional application varies; we use them here as a relevant source of design criteria, not as a claim that every water partnership is a business enterprise.

A **two-tier structure** avoids pretending that every injustice can be resolved before essential cooperation continues.

**Tier 1: a rights and safety floor.** The arrangement and the assessment should not rely on serious threats to basic safety, arbitrary deprivation of essential goods, or retaliation against legitimately protected expression and access to remedy. Restrictions on conduct—including particular forms of protest—must be assessed under applicable law, with attention to legitimate purpose, necessity, proportionality and independent review. This is a threshold on *how cooperation may be governed*, not a claim that all disruption is protected or all state interference is domination. Credible evidence of an unchecked capacity to impose serious sanctions should trigger scrutiny before affected people are asked to provide dangerous testimony proving actual retaliation.

**Tier 2: a corrective trajectory above the floor.** An imperfect settlement can remain valuable and temporarily necessary, especially when immediate failure would cause grave harm. But its deficiencies should not be renamed success. A defensible transitional judgment asks: Which unequal powers are necessary for which functions? Who bears the resulting burdens? What review and sunset conditions exist? Who can initiate review, veto changes, appeal decisions and obtain resources for participation? Which outcomes or milestones must change, and what happens if they do not? Can the process be blocked indefinitely by the party benefiting from the status quo? An arrangement that makes real correction possible differs from one that merely promises eventual fairness while locking in unilateral control.

These rules do not solve every conflict. A consociational settlement can protect one set of groups against domination while empowering their elites over internal minorities. Emergency service delivery may demand centralized technical control before representative governance becomes feasible. The assessor should record such trade-offs explicitly, identify non-negotiable protections that remain in force, and apply a burden of justification to those exercising exceptional power. A *provisional, normatively deficient but correctable* assessment is a meaningful conclusion; it is neither uncritical endorsement nor a demand to shut down life-saving cooperation.

Corrective capacity does not give an institution a license to reinterpret protected opposition as pathology, irrationality or disloyalty. An operator may sincerely believe a challenge is misunderstood, strategically motivated or rooted in fear; that belief is not an authoritative substitute for examining the claimant's own account, independent evidence and relevant legal entitlements. Nor does the claimant's first-person report automatically settle a dispute over facts, resources or rights. **Interpretive pluralism is a procedural safeguard:** allow contestable competing accounts, require reasons for decisions and make framing errors reviewable without making subjective experience a compulsory disclosure requirement.

Finally, standards themselves must be open to reasoned contest. Affected participants should be able to challenge the interpretation of a rights provision or a non-domination claim, and competent assessors should disclose how rival normative frameworks would alter their judgment. Protected participation does not give any claimant an automatic presumption of truth. It gives them a safe and meaningful opportunity to have their claims tested against consistent criteria.
