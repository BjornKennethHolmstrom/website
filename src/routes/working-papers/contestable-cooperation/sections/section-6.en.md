## 6. The proposed Contestability Module

The **Contestability Module (CM)** is a provisional addition to an existing conflict-sensitive review, public-service evaluation or governance assessment. It is **not** a validated independent 'Relational Viability Audit', a substitute for expert DNH/PCIA practice, or a universal peace index. Its aim is to make four possible assessment failures harder to overlook: invalid inference from indicators, unexamined discretionary power, **non-contestable interpretation of grievances**, and the evaluator's own contribution to suppressing correction.

### 6.1 Minimal record and escalation

A lightweight initial review should record the arrangement, decision, affected people and possible nonparticipants, timeframe, operational results, decision authority, sources of evidence, how concerns are categorized, and the applicable normative floor. It should then address five tests; serious rights concerns, disputed representation, interpretive monopoly or retaliation risks trigger a more independent and protected assessment. In urgent situations the process may proceed in stages, without halting essential services while facts are gathered, provided immediate safeguards remain in force.

| CM test | Required record | What it prevents or makes contestable |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Specify the claim and signal** | Outcome, affected unit, direction, period, comparator, evidence strength; why the indicator is relevant to this decision | Promoting willingness into behavior, indicator movement into legitimacy, or a correct measurement into a relevant one |
| **2. Trace control and distribution** | Benefits and burdens; who can set terms, sanction, withhold goods, exit or compel review; internal group dissent | Mistaking exchange or visible silence for symmetric consent |
| **3. Trace the correction chain** | Safe expression, initial classification of concerns, independent adjudication, implemented remedies, review of actual rule changes | Equating complaint reception or favorable closure counts with recognized and corrected grievances |
| **4. Protect challenges to interpretation** | Alternative framing supplied by affected people or independent advocate; evidence, reasons for rejection or adoption, safe dissent record | Letting the commissioning or dominant party monopolize the account; treating every allegation as fact |
| **5. Audit the assessment itself** | Sponsor and funding, framing and publication control, metrics' effects, retaliatory or strategic use, bypass channels and follow-up triggers | Replacing a captured dashboard with a captured audit |

The output is a **three-property judgment**, accompanied by separate *evidence-status* and *normative-status* fields for each consequential proposition. It should name responsible decision makers, feasible corrective actions, unresolved issues and review dates; it does not generate an aggregate 'peace score'. Where safety prevents meaningful participation, the constraint and the structural evidence available must be recorded rather than falsely interpreting absent testimony as approval.

A protected adverse reading need not force vulnerable people to appear publicly or repeatedly relive trauma. Independent representation, secure channels, safe document review or an external advocate may be more appropriate. Crucially, the *affected person's interpretation of their own grievance* is not automatically correct about every fact, but it must not be silently replaced by an incumbent's supposedly neutral classification. The assessor should preserve disputed framings and give reasons that can be appealed or independently tested. The assessor must also examine whether factional actors are using the process to suppress internal rivals, manufacture allegations or hijack the framing. Competing claims should be assessed with comparable evidentiary standards, while acknowledging that unequal access to records and retaliation risk can make nominal symmetry unfair in practice.

### 6.2 What could this add to existing practice?

The relevant comparator depends on context. A municipal dashboard may report service and complaint counts but have little structured analysis of domination. A **competently performed** DNH or PCIA assessment is already much more sophisticated. The CM can be justified only if its explicit procedures improve decisions relative to the comparator actually used.

| Comparison practice | What it can already do | Proposed CM emphasis | Incremental value still unproven |
|---|---|---|---|
| Routine operational dashboard | Measure outputs, continuity, budgets, complaints | Separate performance from correctability; trace whether grievance classifications and remedies are reviewable; examine metric incentives | May reveal risks missed by output-only evaluation |
| Competent DNH/PCIA | Analyze conflict drivers, beneficiaries, intervention effects and power | Mandatory claim-typing, structural discretion and interpretive-monopoly checks, safe adverse reading and audit-reactivity record | Could add little beyond good existing practice |
| Participatory / bottom-up assessment | Bring affected perspectives into indicator definition | Track whether dissent changes decisions and whether auditors can be challenged | Must avoid replacing participation with another formal ritual |

The CM's distinctive **proposal** is less a new list of social goods than a rule about *how an evaluator must justify and reopen its judgments*. Even that procedural combination may already be present elsewhere. It deserves comparative testing, not an assumed monopoly on good assessment.
