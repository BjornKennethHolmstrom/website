## 8. Testing the proposal and setting limits

The CM's comparative design claim is testable. A preregistered assessor study could supply the same realistic, mixed-evidence case dossiers to evaluators using (a) a normal public-sector performance review, (b) competent DNH/PCIA, and (c) the same relevant method with the CM added. Fair comparisons require suitable training, balanced resources, transparent assessor selection and recognition that participants' prior expertise may affect results. The reference standard should be established independently, with uncertainty recorded rather than invented certainty about hidden motives.

Key outcomes would include accurate distinction of observed cooperation from stated willingness, identification of unchecked sanction authority even without reported punishment, detection of **interpretive misclassification without falsely imputing motives**, correct treatment of lawful protest and unlawful intimidation, recognition of legitimate operational benefits, appropriate recommendations for effective remedies, sensitivity to contested intra-group representation, inter-rater consistency, assessor burden and informant safety. Crucially, an intervention's success must not be measured only by **whether a grievance mechanism exists**; investigators should follow whether claims can be raised safely, adjudicated fairly and translated into enforced changes. Longer follow-up should examine whether the review itself alters reporting, retaliation or access to correction.

If the CM merely repeats findings of competent DNH/PCIA, produces more false positives or false accusations, compromises sources, or imposes costs disproportionate to any improvement, its separate identity should be dropped or its components incorporated into existing methods. A weak or null result would be informative. To test the new interpretation requirement specifically, the dossier set should include some cases where a technical classification is warranted and others where it conceals a rule-governance claim, with independent evidence available to assessors; otherwise the test would simply reward labeling every grievance political. The conceptual distinction between performance and correctability does not depend on the success of this particular module.

Several boundaries remain. There is no universal observational measure of threat, recognition, operator comprehension or domination; power may operate informally, and records can be strategically produced. The correction chain is a proposed diagnostic decomposition, not a validated process model. A human operator is a *potential* bottleneck, not necessarily the only channel through which people can communicate complex or interior experiences. The formal results invoked from GaE operate under model-specific assumptions; in linear dynamical systems a rank-deficient instantaneous sensor need not make the full system unobservable if subsequent observations and dynamics recover the state. Neither the Self trilogy's psychological mappings nor the Country Reports independently verify the institutional hypotheses advanced here. The B0 evidence includes cross-sectional studies, heterogeneous experiments and qualitative institutional analyses; no direct data analysis in this paper identifies cooperative persistence under sustained threat, causal institutional buffering or hysteresis. The feedback pathways in Section 4 are causal **hypotheses** requiring observational or experimental discrimination. Human-rights and non-domination judgments require competent contextual interpretation, including disagreements about whose safety and rights are affected. This paper does not resolve the optimal sequencing of peace and justice in particular conflicts.

The planned later research on group identity, perceived threat and antagonistic feedback can ask how social boundaries acquire hostile meanings and whether institutions interrupt that recursion. It should not be treated as a promised derivation of the present argument. Paper 1 stands or falls on the quality of its distinctions, its ability to clarify real evaluation decisions, and the feasibility of testing its procedural proposal.
