# Executive Summary

A governance system must sometimes act before it knows enough about the consequences of its actions. Those actions can also change what it will be able to learn. This paper uses adaptive control as a vocabulary for that problem, not as a proof that an institution is a linear plant or that deliberately increasing variability is always beneficial.

The distinction between sensing, learning and acting motivates the adaptation triad of Papers X, XIV and IX. In the corrected Paper VI, objectives, observation architecture, causal models, decisions and actuation are distinct. Information adequate for a decision need not identify every parameter. In this paper, finite-data identification, consistent estimation, uniform excitation and tracking drifting parameters likewise require different conditions.

Three contributions survive the revision:

- **Formal [M].** Actions can have an informational as well as an operational effect. A uniform lower bound on joint-regressor information yields an inverse-information bound for exponentially weighted least squares. It does not imply that a varying external reference or deliberate perturbation is necessary for all learning. In a static two-channel sunset model, residual incident records alone identify only a product of threat and residual effectiveness; an additional known-coefficient channel can restore joint identifiability.
- **Model evidence [HR/E].** The historical adaptive-controller comparison and a small fresh diagnostic suggest a trade-off between tracking performance and parameter error. The implemented controller uses certainty-equivalent LQR and Gaussian dither, not optimal dual control. The historical panels are not independently reproduced. Self-concealment was not measured. The sunset simulation is a model-specific decision-timing experiment, not a calibrated regulatory risk assessment.
- **Design interpretation [IP].** Protected experimentation, durable evidence, model review, safe-to-fail design and evidence-based sunset review are candidate mechanisms for maintaining adaptive capacity. Their feasibility, value, distributional consequences and legitimacy must be assessed separately.

**Correction note (November 2026, GA-F04).** This edition replaces the original universal excitation, survival and net-learning claims with scoped statements; corrects the simulation specification, memory formulas and probe-rate interpretation; and withdraws unverified institutional thresholds.
