# Part V — Empirical illustrations

The country material in the original edition was not a calibrated empirical test. Its causal narratives and numeric country scores are not retained as results. The following questions preserve the intended comparative research programme [IP/H]; each needs source verification, rival explanations and a defined outcome.

## 5.1 China: experimentation and scaling

Did variation across local reforms reveal information that a uniform national policy would have missed? Did selection of successful pilots distinguish genuine transferability from favourable local conditions, reporting incentives and selective scaling? Compare documented trial designs, selection rules and subsequent outcomes across periods, rather than assigning an exploration coefficient.

## 5.2 Japan: continuity and model revision

Could institutional continuity preserve useful capacity while making particular assumptions harder to challenge? A test would distinguish slow decision change from rational persistence, demographic and economic constraints, and failures of implementation. Continuity alone is not model lock-in.

## 5.3 Finland: memory and execution

Foresight and evaluation infrastructure can be studied as channels for producing and retaining evidence. Whether recommendations alter policy depends on authority, budgets and competing objectives. Trace particular evaluations into decisions and outcomes; the existence of a report is not evidence of either learning or execution.

## 5.4 Nigeria: institutional memory

Investigate whether turnover, reorganizations or fiscal constraints disrupt the reuse of prior evidence. Compare programmes and periods with different handover practices. These are hypotheses, not a quantified national forgetting rate.

## 5.5 Scientific communities

Replication, criticism and archival infrastructure provide candidate mechanisms for correcting models. Their performance depends on the reliability of evidence, access, incentives and the possibility of revising the hypothesis space. Publication volume and disagreement are not sufficient indicators of learning. Appendix C outlines a prospective coding protocol.
