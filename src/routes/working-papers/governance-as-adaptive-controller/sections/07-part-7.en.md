# Part VII — Connection to the series

## 7.1 Exploration, memory and learning from stress

These are related design problems, not three interchangeable mathematical quantities. A policy affects exposure to evidence, an estimator assigns weights to evidence, and a stress test may change the available evidence. Their effects depend on the actual model, observation and policy class.

## 7.2 The adaptation triad

The series' sense–learn–execute vocabulary (X, XIV, IX) is retained as a diagnostic organization. A task may need only a subset of these capacities at a particular time, or a different ordering. The corrected Paper VI explains why adequacy is task-relative. Paper XIX's candidate roles do not establish a necessary three-role institutional architecture. Paper XX's corrected proxy-ordering result cautions against replacing the distinct objectives by an unvalidated single score.

## 7.3 The architecture triad

Delegation (XI), jurisdictional boundary (XII) and legitimacy (XIII) are the other design questions in the original Cycle Two map. Their interplay with sensing, learning and actuation is a research programme, not a theorem of the two triads. Correction of a parameter does not necessarily repair a deficient observation channel, an omitted boundary interaction or an infeasible action.

## 7.4 Regulating the regulator

Second-order regulation asks how an architecture recognizes and revises its own assumptions, data channels and feasible policies. The ability to observe an error differs from the authority to correct it. The correction chain can itself be examined, including who can stop unsafe changes, how failures are reported and whether independent evidence survives.

## 7.5 AI and subsequent work

AI systems may also face informational constraints, distribution shifts and costly tests. The analogy does not establish formal identity or a solution to alignment. Paper XXI's stopping questions and XXIV's constrained excitation motivate separate analysis of when evidence is sufficient and what it costs to obtain. XVI examines replenishment and entrenchment under specified population dynamics; XXIII distinguishes behavioural dissimilarity from adaptation cost. Those results do not prove a universal learning architecture.

## 7.6 Engineering agenda

Cycle Three can turn the design vocabulary into auditable questions: define the task and evidence, test rival models, measure the feasible correction path, specify rights and costs, and validate intervention outcomes. The agenda remains open; this paper does not establish a calibrated governance simulator or a verified empirical law of institutional learning.
