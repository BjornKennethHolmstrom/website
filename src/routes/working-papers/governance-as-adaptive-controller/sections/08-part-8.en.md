# Part VIII — Limitations and conclusion

## 8.1 Limitations

The formal arguments concern specified regression, belief-state and Poisson models. They do not cover arbitrary nonlinear institutional dynamics, endogenous preferences or model discovery. The adaptive-controller implementation is not optimal dual control; its estimator is mismatched, and its self-concealment statistic is absent. The historical panels and sunset control experiment are unverified, and the three-seed replay is exploratory. No country sources or comparative coding were independently verified in this correction. The nine design principles require empirical testing and normative justification; they are not a necessary or sufficient architecture. Literature and specialist review remain advisable.

## 8.2 Conclusion

Operational success can coexist with inaccurate parameter estimates. An action can generate evidence as well as an outcome, but information has value only relative to decisions, costs and constraints. An operational record can fail to identify two causal factors without making a particular decision impossible. The aim of adaptive governance is not to maximize variance, but to keep adequate evidence and feasible correction available for the tasks that matter. The revised paper provides a conditional mathematical vocabulary and a limited simulation comparison, not a general theorem of institutional survival.
