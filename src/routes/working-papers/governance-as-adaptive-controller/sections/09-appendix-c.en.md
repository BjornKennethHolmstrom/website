# Appendix C — Empirical coding notes for learning metrics

## C.1 Prospective protocol [IP]

Use a specified policy domain, decision, time period and outcome as the unit of analysis. Record primary sources and exclusions. Separate existence of an institution from its actual operation and from consequences. Register definitions and comparisons before scoring outcomes; report missing data rather than turning missing evidence into a low score. Use independent coding and disagreement records where possible. Do not map the indicators directly to simulation coefficients.

## C.2 Exploration

Candidate indicators: pilot design and completion, credible comparisons, local variation that is evaluated, independent experimentation budgets, and documented incorporation of results. A large number of pilots is not necessarily informative; check whether design, measurement and follow-up distinguish rival explanations.

## C.3 Model lock-in

Candidate indicators: public predictions and errors, review frequency, available rival models, access for dissenting evidence, and documented revisions or reasoned retention. Distinguish justified retention from inability to revise. Test whether new evidence changes conclusions where it should, not whether a government frequently changes policy.

## C.4 Institutional memory

Candidate indicators: accessible versioned evaluations, handover documentation, personnel continuity, preservation of data and code, and evidence of actual reuse. Turnover and reorganization are possible causes, not direct measures of knowledge loss; retained but obsolete information can also be harmful. Record why a decision superseded an earlier one.

## C.5 Case comparison

The original numerical country ranges were heuristic, not calibrated estimates; they are withdrawn as results. The cases in Part V suggest comparative designs but no country ranking follows from the formal model. A suitable study would estimate inter-coder agreement, test alternative operationalizations, and compare predictive performance against competing explanations.

## C.6 Data sources and further work

Potential sources named in the original edition include legislative records, programme evaluations, administrative personnel records, OECD and World Bank administrative statistics, V-Dem and Comparative Political Data Set turnover indicators, and audit and review documentation. Their availability, relevance and validity must be checked for the specific jurisdiction and period. No new country evidence or bibliographic verification was undertaken in this correction.
