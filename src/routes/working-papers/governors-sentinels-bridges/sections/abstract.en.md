## Abstract

Paper 0 motivates preserving alternative factorizations when predictive adequacy changes across regimes. This paper distinguishes three candidate functions of a model ecology: governing (performance on a task), sentinel service (timely additional information about failure), and bridging (enabling transfer or communication). Selection on performance need not explicitly reward the other functions; their independence and loss under selection are empirical questions, not consequences of their definitions.

The historical study evaluated seven recurrent predictors on regime-shifting bouncing-dot streams across twenty reported zoos. Adaptive selection beat monoculture and was closer to a retrospective single-model oracle than to full pluralism in 20/20 zoos; the registered architecture prediction nevertheless failed. The top governor differed from the top nominal sentinel in 17/20, and the top graph-central model in 20/20. A coverage-selected portfolio won in 11/20, below the registered threshold. These are historical reported values [HR], not independently reproduced results. The loss feedback uses outcomes not yet available at forecast issuance, and the sentinel statistic additionally uses a centered window. Graph centrality is measured at a construction-dependent threshold, not by a translation task. The per-regime articulation criterion cannot be verified from the supplied code or recovered outputs.

Exact mathematical arguments [R] show that this graph can exhibit top-rank mismatch even when models differ only in scalar mean-loss level. The role triad survives as a diagnostic and experimental proposal [IP/H], not as a demonstrated functional dissociation. Paper XXIII studies related geometry and adaptation costs using a different distance construction and separately trained ecologies.
