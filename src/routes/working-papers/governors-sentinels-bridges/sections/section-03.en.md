## 3. Adaptive pluralism — historical offline comparison

[HR] Adaptive audit was reported closer to the oracle than to full pluralism in 20/20 zoos and better than monoculture in 20/20. Closed WTA was worst in only 2/20, and the full five-way ordering held in 1/20. P1 requires the median ordering and at least 12/20 closer-to-oracle comparisons; its explicit null clause fires when closed WTA is not worst in a majority. **P1 failed as registered.** The favorable adaptive subcontrast (later called P1a) does not rescue it; the code does not compute the registered median ordering.

The information clock further limits the subcontrast. A forecast at origin $t$ uses frames through $t-1$ and predicts outcomes at $t+4,t+9,t+19$. The complete loss can be known only after the last outcome arrives. `arch_mse` and `adaptive_active_series` use the loss at origin $t$ in rolling comparisons, and closed WTA reviews the preceding 30 origins without waiting for maturation. The adaptive persistence counter also need not track the same challenger throughout. A conservative online rule at decision origin $t$ would use only losses from origins no later than $t-20$, or explicitly handle separately matured horizons. A feasible policy must state the order of observation and action. No such re-score has been performed.

Monoculture and the initial incumbent are chosen by validation losses on each model's own regime, with an auxiliary term for the velocity model; these are not a common held-out task and loss. The comparison is therefore an origin-time descriptive contrast of the implemented rules, not evidence that periodic reopening is robustly superior online. Temporary closure with revisable commitments remains a design hypothesis [IP], subject to timing, evaluation cost and switching cost.
