## 1. Introduction: From diagnosis to measurement

This revised paper asks how to investigate the adequacy of a governance architecture for a specified task. It follows the corrected Paper VI: what is valued, observed, modelled, decided and implemented are distinct. Institutional problems may involve several of these relations, and an improvement in one need not repair another. The original edition proposed a composite Variety Gap Index and reported estimates for twenty systems. Those estimates, intervals, classifications and validation claims are withdrawn from active diagnostic use: the supplied parameter table does not reproduce the scores under the stated formula, and the calculation inputs and protocol have not been recovered. The original record is retained in the correction archive.

The replacement is a diagnostic profile [IP/H], not a new calibrated index. Its unit is a task, jurisdiction, decision pathway and period, not a country in the abstract. A profile can document relevant evidence, missing evidence, feasible interventions, and uncertainty without assuming a single latent quantity. Paper VI supplies one exact static adequacy condition; it does not supply a universal scale of institutional quality. This paper develops a proposed operational procedure, with no claim of empirical validation.
