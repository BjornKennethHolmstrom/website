## 2. Task-relative distinctions

In VI's static noiseless model, disturbances belong to a finite-dimensional real subspace \(D\), tolerated deviations to a subspace \(L\), and observations are \(y=Cd\). With \(x=d+u\), adequate regulation requires \(K=D\cap\ker C\subseteq L\); it is sufficient if all actions in \(-D\) are available. The hidden consequential dimension is
\[g_{\rm hidden}=\dim K-\dim(K\cap L).\]
The count bound \(g_{\rm count}=\dim D-\dim(D\cap L)-\operatorname{rank}(C|_D)\le g_{\rm hidden}\) need not be tight. [M] For \(D=\mathbb R^2,L=\operatorname{span}(e_2)\), observing the first coordinate gives hidden gap zero; observing the second gives one. Both have rank one and count gap zero. No reweighting of a score that sees only the counts can distinguish these cases.

An indicator catalogue or PCA estimates properties of a particular dataset, not the rank of this map or its alignment with tolerated deviations. A crisis inventory is not a basis of disturbance directions; correlated variables may still be causally distinct, and statistically independent variables may be irrelevant. No count is an automatic lower or upper bound. In dynamic settings, histories, feasible policies, observation errors and delays matter. Before measurement, specify the decision, tolerable outcomes (including rights), relevant counterfactual disturbances, available histories and actions. If the map or tolerated subspace is unknown, report the gap as unidentified, not zero or a guessed count.
