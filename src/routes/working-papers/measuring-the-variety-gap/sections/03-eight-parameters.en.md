## 3. The indicator families

The eight historical parameter families can be retained as candidate observations, not independent primitives of one measured quantity. Reform permeability and its complement are one quantity in the old coding; attention allocation is treated separately. The table proposes a measurement target and an explicit caution for each family [IP/H].

| Family | Candidate observation | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Observation | Catalogues, independent variation in a specified sample, actual decision use | Not rank of the task map |
| Disturbances | Dated event inventory, plausible mechanisms, unknowns | Categories are not dimensions |
| Latency | Time from a defined signal to a defined response | Record censoring and task deadline |
| Fidelity | Compare transmitted signal to a reference; transparency and audit access separately | Public access is not internal fidelity |
| Reform implementation | Eligible announcements, completed, partial, not implemented, unknown | Fixed denominator and follow-up period |
| Oscillation | Named time series, amplitude, autocorrelation, shocks | External volatility is not automatically internal instability |
| Bypass | Parallel provision, informal channels, coverage and effects | May supply capacity or cause harm |
| Symbolic reform | Documented outcome of the same reform cohort | Do not double-count a complement |
| Attention | Sampling allocation, timeliness, decision uptake | More sampling does not guarantee more relevant information |

The original distinction between structural and symbolic reform remains a useful question, but requires explicit partial and unknown categories. The historical protocol proposed three implementation criteria: enactment of the legal or regulatory instrument, allocated resources for the implementing body, and independent evaluation at least two years after enactment confirming measurable changes in behaviour or outcomes. Treat these as candidate criteria, not a universal definition. Code each separately (met, not met, unknown, or not yet assessable), rather than treating a partly met bundle as either fully structural or wholly symbolic. Under binary exhaustive coding, the proportions p and rho satisfy rho = 1 − p. With partial or unknown outcomes, a full vector of shares is more informative. Do not force binary coding to simplify a score.

Latency should be tied to the service or decision: the time between recognition, formal decision and effective delivery may have different causes. An announcement is not implementation. Where failure to respond is observed, use a censored time or report nonresponse rather than treating it as a fast response. Attention scheduling may be studied using sample counts and measurement error; see D.3 for the limited model.

For a latency study [IP/H], define the eligible episode cohort by documented start dates within a fixed window, and specify the response endpoint in advance. Include routine as well as crisis episodes, including those without a response by the observation cutoff. Record elapsed follow-up for each right-censored episode. Under appropriate non-informative censoring assumptions, Kaplan–Meier estimates can summarize the response-time distribution; report the median if reached, or a restricted mean over a prespecified horizon, with uncertainty and domain-specific results. Selecting only completed responses or only prominent crises can bias the estimate; the direction is not asserted without evidence.
