## 5. From composite index to diagnostic profile

The old composite score multiplies chosen penalty factors. Paper V does not establish their units, functional forms, weights or calibration. A low rate of reform is not necessarily a deficit where no reform is needed. Likewise, greater bypass activity may improve rather than reduce a service's capacity. The reference constants and threshold are not established; see Appendix D. No replacement scalar is proposed.

Use a profile with the following five questions, adapted from VI [IP/H]:

| Axis | Question | Evidence to seek |
|---|---|---|
| Evaluation | Which outcomes are valued or neglected? | Mandate, affected parties, rights, competing outcome criteria |
| Observation | Which relevant distinctions can the system access? | Coverage, fidelity, timing, histories and exposure |
| Modelling | Which explanations can be distinguished? | Competing hypotheses, validation and residuals |
| Decision | What happens when evidence changes? | Decision rules, contested cases, response records |
| Actuation | What can actually be done? | Legal authority, resources, coordination and delivery |

Use the worksheet in Appendix A, one row per consequential distinction or intervention. Record data quality separately from estimated performance. A profile may yield a partial comparison (one channel has shorter delay, another greater coverage); disagreements are informative. If a user explicitly needs an aggregate, define its decision purpose, normalization, weights, compensability and missing-data policy in advance. Report sensitivity and leave rights or essential service constraints as constraints, not automatically compensable losses. Such an aggregate is a new preference-weighted score, not VI's hidden dimension and not a validated universal index.
