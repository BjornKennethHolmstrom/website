## 6. Tracking change without predicting collapse

The former equation \(\dot G=\alpha-\eta A\) does not describe the derivative of the composite index. For a deliberately defined dimensional difference one may stipulate a change equal to new dimensions minus acquired dimensions; it is not a law of hidden gap evolution. In a fixed task, record changes separately in disturbances, available observations, decision rules and intervention capacity. A change in task or tolerance can change adequacy even with an unchanged sensor. Counts of newly recognised issues or enacted reforms are not counts of independent directions. If A already measures realised observation change, multiplying it by an implementation fraction discounts it a second time.

For a longitudinal profile, freeze the definitions and denominator, date each observation, and distinguish remeasurement, changes in underlying conditions, and changes in coding. Track censored response times, lost channels, unresolved disagreements, and delivered actions. These are possible leading indicators [H], not validated forecasts of a threshold. No historical trajectory class or time-to-failure estimate is retained.

Two monitoring leads from the historical proposal are metric attrition (a dated inventory of discontinued or restricted indicators) and proxy divergence (a widening discrepancy between independently specified measures intended to track related outcomes). Treat these as prompts for investigation, not evidence of approaching a critical threshold. Check changes in definitions, coverage and external conditions before interpreting a discrepancy.
