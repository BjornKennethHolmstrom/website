## Appendix A: Diagnostic profile worksheet

### A.1 Case specification

Record task, affected population, rights and outcome tolerances, unit of analysis, decision pathway, period, feasible actions, comparator and known constraints. If more than one task is studied, keep separate profiles. Register a case ID and codebook version.

### A.2 Evidence rows

| Field | Entry instructions |
|---|---|
| Distinction / intervention | What would change a decision? What alternative is feasible? |
| Axis | Evaluation, observation, model, decision or actuation |
| Observation | Operational definition, unit, denominator, timing |
| Provenance | Source, version, date, extraction method, access restrictions |
| Estimate | Value or qualitative finding; unknown is allowed |
| Error / uncertainty | Sampling, measurement, model and missingness separately |
| Relevance | Link to specified outcome; alternative explanation |
| Verification | Independent check, contradictory evidence, unresolved status |
| Response | Proposed action, owner, constraints and follow-up |

### A.3 Coding instructions

Maintain an inventory of expected and actually available indicators. Separate dataset correlation from task relevance and decision uptake. For event latency, preserve start/end dates and censoring. For implementation, record each criterion and outcome category separately; keep unresolved cases in the denominator or explicitly justify exclusion. For volatility, report scale, period and shock context; avoid coefficient of variation when the mean is near zero or signed without a meaningful ratio interpretation. For bypass, describe service function, reliability and distributional effects. For attention, record sampling frequency, delay, staffing and actual use, not just budget. Avoid duplicate weighting of related indicators.

### A.4 Worked example [H]

For a fictional outbreak-reporting pathway, the task is to decide whether a cluster requires investigation within 48 hours. One candidate observation is elapsed time from first recorded cluster signal to investigation authorisation. A second is whether local case records are distinguishable after aggregation. A third is the availability of staff to investigate. A 72-hour observation is evidence of missed deadline if the clock definitions and records are valid; it does not identify whether the cause was observation, decision or implementation. Missing local records remain unknown, not a score of zero. The profile therefore suggests testing the reporting and authorisation pathway separately from staffing. The numbers are illustrative, not data.
