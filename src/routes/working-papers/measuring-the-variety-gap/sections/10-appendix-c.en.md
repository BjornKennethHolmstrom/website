## Appendix C: Candidate data sources

The original source catalogue remains a useful starting point. The following are leads from the previous edition, not checked for current coverage, reliability, availability or fitness for any given task.

| Family | Candidate sources |
|---|---|
| Observation | National statistical catalogues; World Bank statistical capacity; Global Data Barometer |
| Disturbances | National risk registries; IMF Article IV reports; World Bank country diagnostics; INFORM |
| Latency and reforms | Legislative and administrative records; Comparative Agendas Project; OECD regulatory policy; evaluation repositories such as Campbell and 3ie |
| Fidelity and access | Independent audit reports; WGI, V-Dem, Freedom House, RSF and INTOSAI as possible contextual leads |
| Oscillation | National accounts; IMF IFS; World Development Indicators; policy-rate histories |
| Bypass | ILO informal economy data; satellite night lights; service-specific records |
| Attention | Sampling calendars, decision logs, budgets, staff interviews where ethically appropriate |

Triangulate against independent records, record shared sources and dependencies, and distinguish a proxy's construct from the proposed measurement target. Night lights, for example, are not an independent measurement of every form of informal provision. Do not assign default confidence by country category. When eliciting expert ranges, publish the questions, assumptions, aggregation rule, conflicts and uncertainty; withhold estimates that cannot be supported. No country source has been newly verified in this correction.
