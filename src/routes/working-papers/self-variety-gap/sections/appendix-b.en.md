## Appendix B: Changing tasks and the limits of timing

The former time-varying dimensional difference is not an evolution law for the hidden gap. Disturbances, tolerances, histories and actions can change independently. In a finite-dimensional linear model exact rank is integer-valued; a continuously varying effective dimension requires a different definition. Even for a deliberately stipulated continuous surrogate with \(\dot g=\alpha(t)-\beta(t)\), a positive derivative need not reach a threshold. The increment is
\[g(t)-g(0)=\int_0^t[\alpha(s)-\beta(s)]\,ds.\]
For excess rate \(e^{-t}\), the increment is \(1-e^{-t}<1\); it never reaches 2. Constant positive slope gives a hitting time only if the target lies ahead and the slope remains constant until that time. These are mathematical statements about an assumed surrogate [M]. Neither the surrogate nor a threshold is validated for personal experience. The earlier time-to-dissolution formula is withdrawn as a prediction. Life changes may reduce as well as increase demands; no inevitable widening is implied.
