## Part I: The self as a conditional feedback analogy

A person can reflect on consequences of action, interpret experience and change course. Control theory provides a language for examining such relations, not a complete model of a person. What matters to someone is not identical to what they notice. Someone can focus on one goal while remaining attentive to many experiences, or care deeply about several things while having limited information or opportunities. Neither case can be diagnosed by counting values.

The corrected VI separates evaluation, observation, modelling, decision and actuation. The personal analogy [IP] asks, for a particular concern: what matters here; what evidence is accessible; what interpretations compete; what response is considered; what choices are actually available? Fatigue, economic constraints, illness, coercion, relationships and institutional conditions may affect any of these. They need not arise from the person's priorities. Awareness does not guarantee control; limited control does not demonstrate limited awareness. The word self here names a subject of lived experience and reflection, not an identified finite-dimensional controller. We do not equate a person with an engineered objective.
