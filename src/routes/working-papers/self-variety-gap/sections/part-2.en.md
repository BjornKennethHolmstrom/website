## Part II: Values, observation and feasible action

A narrow performance measure can become misleading when pursued at the expense of what it was intended to represent. The corrected XX makes the ordering requirement explicit: a strict improvement in a proxy accompanied by a deterioration of the target must be feasible for strict reward hacking to occur. An omitted variable alone does not imply such a reversal. A career goal may conflict with rest or connection, but it may also support them; the question is how particular actions, circumstances and outcomes relate. This is not a universal Goodhart–Ashby law.

The legacy text used three different quantities as the same gap: the number of reality dimensions minus value dimensions, a disturbance-minus-goal-minus-observation count, and five minus the tracked coordinate count in the simulator. They are not equivalent. The formal repair in A instead uses a tolerated deviation subspace and an independent observation map in a toy task. A real person's goals are not necessarily a subspace and their tolerances are not its dimension. The formal quantity is not a psychological index. The simulator's tracked-coordinate count is just a description of its policy. An unnoticed distinction might be inconsequential for a particular decision, while a recognised concern may remain unaddressable. The terms “gap” and “blind spot” are retained as questions about specific mismatches, not as estimates of personal adequacy.
