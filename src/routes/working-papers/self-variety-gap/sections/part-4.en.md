## Part IV: Psychological hypotheses and evidential limits

The original edition drew on self-regulation, self-complexity, cognitive dissonance, developmental psychology, acceptance-based practices, flow and contemplative neuroscience. These are research leads (C and E), not independent tests of the proposed construct. The supplied evidence includes no clinical dataset, measurement validation, or experiment identifying a causal relationship between number of values and mental health. Neural activity or a change in experienced self-boundaries cannot be equated with observation rank. A subjective experience of clarity is not evidence of greater predictive accuracy.

Burnout, depression, trauma, illness and suffering have diverse possible causes and are not diagnostic signatures of a gap. Pain or fatigue may warrant attention but does not uniquely reveal its cause or the correct response. No practice is shown here to reduce crisis risk or treat a condition. Clinical questions belong to appropriate care and evidence; social and material constraints deserve their own analysis. This paper makes no clinical recommendation. Future research would need a clearly operationalised construct, credible alternative explanations, ethical oversight where applicable, independent measurement, and validation before any psychological prediction.
