## Part VI: The speculative horizon — transcendence and nonduality

The contemplative question can be asked independently of the engineering analogy: what is experienced when habitual self-description becomes less central? Some traditions invite inquiry into the relation between the observer and the observed, and into whether experience requires a separate narrative centre. These questions are philosophical and phenomenological. They need not be framed as a more successful controller or a project of expanding its dimensionality.

A less prominent self-narrative may be experienced as openness, intimacy or freedom; it may also have other meanings or no special significance. No mathematical limit of the hidden dimension establishes nonduality, enlightenment, cessation of suffering or complete access to reality. The toy model's tolerated deviation set and observation map do not represent the metaphysical status of the self. Conversely, a philosophical interpretation of experience does not validate the model. The distinction protects both inquiries: formal claims can be tested, while contemplative inquiry can remain open without becoming a clinical or computational assertion.

There is also a tension worth retaining from the original: if reflection itself becomes another metric of achievement, it can cease to serve what motivated it. This is a possible interpretation, not an inevitable Goodhart effect. Nothing here requires a person to undertake contemplative practice or to interpret difficult experience as a necessary phase of transformation.

The original discussion distinguished two registers of consciousness. The cybernetic register concerns information, representation, attention and feedback in a specified system. The metaphysical register asks what consciousness is and whether the distinction between subject and object is fundamental. The former permits operational questions; the latter is not settled by a control-theoretic result. Advaita Vedanta, Zen, Christian mysticism and Sufism offer diverse contemplative accounts that can be read as phenomenological testimony, not measurements of a hidden dimension or proof that their metaphysical interpretations coincide.

A separate philosophical thought concerns continuity through influence. A person’s actions, relationships and ways of attending may continue to affect others after that person dies. This possibility of continuity does not establish personal survival or immortality, and the feedback analogy cannot adjudicate claims about consciousness beyond death.
