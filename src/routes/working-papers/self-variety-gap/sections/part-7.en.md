## Part VII: Conclusion — adequacy without an imperative

The surviving contribution is a set of distinctions and voluntary questions, not a theorem about the self. Values can guide attention and action without defining the observation channel. Learning can matter without universal excitation requirements (XIV); accessible information can coexist with limited choices. The replay in D shows that even a small simulation must distinguish policy from plant and outcome measure from what it purports to represent. The old crisis and dissolution claims are withdrawn, not replaced by a new personal threshold.

Self II and Self III remain separate papers whose imports must be checked. The original script, figure and text are preserved for transparency; their obsolete labels are not current claims. This revision makes no claim to have validated psychological theory, contemplative metaphysics or a practice for wellbeing. It offers a way of asking more precise questions while leaving the meaning of a life to the person living it.

Open research questions remain: can values, attention, interpretation and action constraints be measured separately without conflating them? How do those relations change over time, and what do voluntary reflective practices change relative to suitable comparison conditions? What can phenomenological accounts contribute without becoming clinical evidence? The authenticity boundary also remains a philosophical question about who may define a meaningful life. None of these questions presumes a measurable universal personal gap or a required development trajectory.
