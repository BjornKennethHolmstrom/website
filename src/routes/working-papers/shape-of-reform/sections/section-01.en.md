# §1 — A promised geometry, audited before it was built

## 1.1 The promise

The original XIX asked whether bounded model ecologies have a useful geometry and whether preserving central models matters. Its revised edition distinguishes graph centrality from functional translation. XXIII asks two narrower questions: how behavioral dissimilarities change across regimes, and whether they predict the measured cost of adapting a model. Neither question assumes a manifold, a translation network or independent role dimensions.

## 1.2 The audit

The original geometry v2 panel fixes a roster and compares distance matrices, their split-half baselines, bridge rankings at slack and graph changes across a stress sweep. The connectivity threshold is an exact derived quantity (§3.2), not an independent measurement. GA-F03-A subsequently recovered 73 output files and replayed Tests 1 and 2, the cost reports and the mechanism report from stored tables. The historical training and registration chronology are not authenticated. Several prose statistics match the first three zoos rather than the full panel; a likely small-panel mix-up is corrected without claiming knowledge of the drafting history.

## 1.3 What replaced the promise

This edition retains the typed distinction between model-model dissimilarity and model-task adaptation cost. A statistical failure is reported at its actual criterion rather than as a proof of geometric nonexistence. The cost benchmark and staging candidate-set limitations are explicit. A failure of the descriptive map to predict a particular cost proxy does not forbid a geometry defined over actual update states.

## 1.4 The shape of the paper

Sections 2–3 define the quantities and report the three geometry failures; §4 reports all five transition-cost gates and the staging mechanism diagnostic. Sections 5–6 distinguish a typed mathematical object from institutional interpretation; §§7–8 delimit scope and dependencies. Appendix A supplies exact arguments; Appendix B records measurement history and replay limits. [R] denotes scoped mathematics, [TR] replayed table-level findings, [HR] unrecovered historical reports, [E] post-hoc checks, [IP/H] institutional interpretation or hypotheses. No training is reproduced.
