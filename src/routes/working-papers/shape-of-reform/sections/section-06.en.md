# §6 — Institutional readings, and what does not survive

## 6.1 Directional reform [IP]

Moving from one arrangement to another and returning may require different capabilities, coalitions and costs. The model makes asymmetry of a specified adaptation proxy concrete, not asymmetry of all institutional reforms. Institutional tests should measure forward and reverse interventions on comparable outcomes, including who bears costs, resource constraints, and whether the target is actually achieved. Rights and consent are not quantities that this simulation resolves.

## 6.2 Staged reform [IP]

An intermediate arrangement may build capacity useful for a later task. It may also consume resources, entrench interests or remove alternatives. The selected-route contrast motivates comparing staged and direct reform with equal resource budgets and a specified endpoint. The historical mechanism study cannot distinguish destination proximity from curriculum or route structure. The proposed oblique-reform principle is withdrawn as an established result; it remains a testable possibility. A staging policy must be selected on information available before the transition, evaluated independently, and compared with direct target investment and home warm-up.

## 6.3 What does not survive from the evolution analogy

The graph sweep is not evidence for speciation, phase transitions or their impossibility in institutions. Training and switching in recurrent predictors are distinct from reproduction, political bargaining and institutional design. The loss space is not a network of actual communication or transfer. Biological language may motivate hypotheses but cannot identify mechanisms without measurements.

## 6.4 The shape of the institutional layer

The practical distinction is between description and intervention: measure how arrangements behave, specify what a feasible reform changes, and assess the route-dependent consequences on actual intermediate states. A shared target does not fix the costs of arrival. This reading connects to transition feasibility in VII, IX and revised VI, without establishing a necessary sequence of reforms. The empirical research question is which interventions improve viability and correctability under constraints, not whether a metaphorical map looks compelling.
