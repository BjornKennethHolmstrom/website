## Abstract

The Governance as Engineering series examines the limitations of institutions as bounded controllers. This paper extends that perspective to the relationship between objectives, observations, causal models, decision policies and feasible interventions. These are distinct components: a scalar objective may be served by rich observations, and a multidimensional objective need not confer adequate sensing. Objectives and incentives can nevertheless shape which evidence is collected, which states a policy encounters, and which alternative explanations can be tested. This conditional mechanism, rather than an identity between value and observation, is the central interpretive proposal.

The *variety gap* denotes a family of task-relative inadequacies, not a universal scalar measure. A restricted static linear regulation model yields an exact condition on hidden, consequential disturbance directions and a necessary rank bound (Appendix A). Dynamic observability, noise, action constraints and normative choices require separate analysis. No universal dissolution threshold is established. Appendix C documents a recovered historical simulation of two bundled investment policies: both cross the environmental collapse threshold, but the environment-aware rule partially recovers. This is not an experiment isolating objective dimensionality. Country cases are interpretive hypotheses, not independent validation; the measurement and test proposals in Appendices G–H remain unvalidated.

The final part considers value audits, deliberation and revisable institutional arrangements as possible responses to changing tasks and neglected evidence. These are design proposals, not mathematical requirements for the inclusion of particular values.

**Contributions**
- Separates evaluation, observation, modeling, decision and actuation; identifies policy-dependent exposure as one conditional link.
- Preserves a static necessary rank bound and specifies a stronger adequacy condition under explicit assumptions.
- Documents the legacy simulation and distinguishes its model-specific findings from general claims.
- Recasts the country interpretations and measurement proposals as a research agenda, and considers meta-governance as an institutional design question.
