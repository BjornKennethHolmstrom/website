## Appendix B: Effective Gap Dynamics and Conditional Thresholds

### B.1 Time-Varying Adequacy

Changing disturbances, sensors and tasks can change the static quantities in Appendix A, but their exact dimensions are integers. A continuously varying effective-gap score $g(t)$ is a separate phenomenological construct [H], not an exact rank. Its scale and measurement must be specified before fitting dynamics.

### B.2 Emergence of New Requirements

A term $\alpha(t)$ can represent the rate of increase of a specified effective deficit attributable to changing demands, not a universal rate of dimensional growth. Changes may also remove requirements.

### B.3 Adaptation

A term $\beta(t)A(t)$ can represent a rate of reduction by a specified adaptation process. This separation is a simplifying assumption: interactions, delays, costs, substitutions and depletion of correction capacity may violate it.

### B.4 Why Rank Does Not Determine Signal Quality

Let $x,z,\epsilon$ be independent and $y=x+\epsilon$, with $x$ and $\epsilon$ centered Gaussian of equal positive variance. SNR is one and $I(x;y)=\tfrac12\log_2(2)=0.5$ bits. Appending independent unobserved coordinates $z$ leaves $I((x,z);y)=I(x;y)$ and SNR unchanged. Their relevance to regulation is separate. Neither SNR one nor a rank deficit implies zero information or inevitable failure. The universal $G_{\mathrm{crit}}$ and estimated range 2–3 are withdrawn; Paper III's chain-depth result does not calibrate them.

### B.5 Conditional Crossing Calculation

Under the illustrative equation $\dot g=\alpha(t)-\beta(t)A(t)$,

$$g(t)=g(0)+\int_0^t[\alpha(s)-\beta(s)A(s)]\,ds.$$

If a fixed threshold $g_*$ has been independently justified, $g(0)<g_*$, and net rate $v=\alpha-\beta A$ is constant and strictly positive, crossing time is $(g_*-g(0))/v$. With varying rates, crossing requires integrated net growth to attain the difference. Positive growth alone does not suffice: $\dot g=e^{-t}$ and $g(0)=0$ give $g(t)=1-e^{-t}<1$, never reaching two. A score threshold must be calibrated to the task and does not imply institutional dissolution.

### B.6 Relationship to Empirical Phenomena

The ansatz may organize hypotheses about delayed adaptation, competence loss and recovery. It does not establish a causal explanation of any country crisis. Alternatives include changing action constraints, distorted feedback, model error and changes in outcome criteria.

### B.7 Caveats and Open Problems

No general mapping from rank, entropy or objective count to a continuous deficit has been established. No common score or threshold is validated across institutions. Future work may fit a task-specific score and test predicted paths against alternative models with uncertainty.
