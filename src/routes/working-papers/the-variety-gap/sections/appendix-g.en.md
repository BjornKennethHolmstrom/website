## Appendix G: Operational Definitions and Measurement Protocols

### G.1 The Operationalization Challenge

The formal gap in Appendix A is not a country index. The earlier protocols treated objectives, action variation and compression ratios as estimators of one dimension. This revision separates constructs. All protocols below are proposals, not validated instruments.

### G.2 Evaluative and Observational Architecture

Code declared goals, constraints, actual selection criteria and whose outcomes count, using laws, budgets and decision records. An objective count is a descriptive coding, not sensor rank. Separately inventory signals, access, independence, timing, measurement error and their use. For a specified static linear task with independently justified $D,L,C$, estimate rank and hidden directions with uncertainty; report sensitivity to rank tolerance. Otherwise describe task-relevant information using suitable observation models and tests of discrimination, not a claimed dimension count.

### G.3 Disturbances, Models and Actions

Define relevant disturbances relative to a task and horizon. Identify plausible causal dependencies, alternative models, actual feasible interventions and implementation constraints. PCA on action/output series describes covariance under the sampled policy; it is not sensor rank or the dimension of all possible disturbances. A log compression ratio is a dimensionless statistic, not a count of directions. Compare observed and counterfactual trajectories only under explicit modeling assumptions.

### G.4 Measuring a Gap

Report separate observations of evaluative, observational, model, decision and actuation inadequacy (Part III). Use $g_{\mathrm{hidden}}$ and the signed $g_{\mathrm{count}}$ only within Appendix A's model. In empirical settings define specific deficit indicators before examining outcomes, record missing information and alternative interpretations, and do not sum disparate constructs without an independently justified aggregation rule. Crisis recurrence cannot both define the score and validate it.

### G.5 Performance Thresholds

The earlier $G_{\mathrm{crit}}$ range and unexplained-outcome-variance ratio are withdrawn as estimators of a universal collapse threshold. Residual outcome variance can include model error, process variation and interventions, not just sensor noise. A task-specific threshold requires an independently chosen acceptable loss or failure probability, calibration and held-out validation.

### G.6 Emergence Rate

For a specified effective score as in Appendix B, track new task requirements and their dates independently of the outcome. Report the units, observation intervals, uncertainty and sensitivity to coding. Do not assume that novelty always increases a deficit or that the score equals rank.

### G.7 Adaptation Efficiency

Measure changes attributable to a specified response relative to its resource use, delay and counterfactual. Distinguish adding a signal, improving a model, changing a policy and increasing feasible actions. A rate in Appendix B is a fitted or stipulated model parameter, not automatically a general efficiency measure.

### G.8 Usage Guidelines

Specify the decision episode, stakeholders, acceptable deviations and lawful actions. Document observation availability separately from use; test whether a claimed blind spot could be inferred from other evidence. For policy-dependent exposure, compare which informative events occur under admissible alternatives. Preserve negative cases where fixed precautions or substitutes work. Report model assumptions and normative judgments separately.

### G.9 Validation Criteria

Predefine coding and outcome windows, competing explanations, reliability checks, a practically meaningful effect and uncertainty analysis. Use held-out cases or a prospective design, avoid leakage between predictor and outcome, and assess construct validity before prediction. Non-significance without sufficient precision is inconclusive. No cross-country measurement instrument is validated here.

### G.10 Research Priorities

Begin with a narrow service or decision pathway, independent measurements and credible rival mechanisms. Validate one construct before attempting a composite. Compare simple buffers, better decision rules and independent audits against new sensing or broader evaluation at comparable costs. The original numerical country rankings remain historical, not target values for calibration.
