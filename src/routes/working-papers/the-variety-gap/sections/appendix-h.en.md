### Appendix H: Testable Hypotheses and Evaluation Protocols

These replace the earlier prediction list. They are candidate designs, not preregistrations or results. Specify population, exposure, outcome, competing explanations, smallest meaningful effect and precision before collecting test data. A well-powered equivalence or directional test may count against a scoped hypothesis; a non-significant result alone does not falsify it.

#### H.1 Task-Specific Deficits and Service Failure

Hypothesis: an independently coded observational or response deficit predicts later failure in a specified service pathway beyond baseline capacity and demand. Compare held-out episodes matched on resources and disturbance severity. A precise absence of incremental predictive value counts against that particular operationalization. Do not use past crisis counts to construct the predictor and validate it on the same crises.

#### H.2 Changing Requirements and Institutional Rigidity

Hypothesis: under specified changes in requirements, restrictive revision procedures delay correction. Measure time to diagnosis and implementation separately; compare similar changes with different procedures, controlling for conflict, resources and legal constraints. Faster or equally effective correction under the restrictive procedure is a counterexample to a universal claim.

#### H.3 Threshold Prediction — Withdrawn in Universal Form

No common critical dimension gap is established. A task-specific performance-threshold study could be proposed after selecting a justified outcome criterion and calibrating on independent data. Do not fit a threshold to crisis episodes and then claim those episodes validate it.

#### H.4 Evaluation, Observation and Policy

Hypothesis: adding a consequential signal or changing evaluation may improve a specified outcome under identifiable constraints. Separate the interventions; include a comparator retaining the objective but improving sensing or policy and a simple buffer where feasible. Measure costs and unintended effects under common evaluations. An unchanged objective can support an effective policy; more metrics need not help.

#### H.5 Audits and Correction

Hypothesis: an independent audit improves detection and correction of a specified inadequacy. Compare against a matched-cost alternative and no additional audit; measure actual correction and not just newly recorded problems. A costly audit that supplies unused evidence may have no benefit. Predefine decision deadlines and recovery resources.

#### H.6 Legacy Simulator Calibration — Withdrawn

The previous calibration proposal depended on the misdescribed Appendix C simulator. The recovered script is a synthetic fixed-policy comparison, not a calibrated country model. No empirical calibration claim survives. Any new model would require independent justification, data, parameter identification and validation; further factorial runs are optional future work, not necessary for this correction.

#### H.7 Representation Chain Depth and Democratic Satisfaction

This remains a distinct Paper III research question, not a test of a universal gap threshold. Specify the actual transmission mechanism, signal quality, institutional context and alternative causes of satisfaction. Chain length alone is not an observation-rank measure. No new validation of Paper III is provided here.

#### H.8 Research Priority Ordering

First establish construct validity in a bounded decision pathway (G), then evaluate predictive or causal claims with independent evidence. Compare simple alternatives before increasing architecture complexity. Register hypotheses, inclusion rules and analysis before testing; report negative and inconclusive findings separately. None of H.1–H.7 is claimed to have been confirmed by the country illustrations or the recovered simulation.
