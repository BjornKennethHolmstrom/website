### Part III — The Variety Gap: Task-Relative Adequacy and Dynamics

#### 3.1 Defining the Gap

The original edition used incompatible dimension differences. Here *variety gap* is an umbrella term for an inadequacy relative to a specified task, not an index aggregating incommensurable quantities. Distinguish evaluative adequacy (which consequences count), observational adequacy (what distinctions are available), model adequacy (what causal structure is represented), decision adequacy (how information is used), and actuation adequacy (which interventions are feasible). A gap in one may matter without a gap in another.

In the static model of Appendix A define $K=D\cap\ker C$ and $g_{\mathrm{hidden}}=\dim K-\dim(K\cap L)$. This is the dimension of hidden disturbance directions modulo tolerated ones. The signed count deficit $g_{\mathrm{count}}=\dim D-\dim(D\cap L)-\operatorname{rank}(C|_D)$ is a lower bound on $g_{\mathrm{hidden}}$, not the same quantity. A nonpositive count deficit does not establish adequacy. The exact hidden gap is nonnegative and zero exactly when observational adequacy holds in this model with unrestricted additive correction. Neither measure counts declared values or measures welfare losses.

#### 3.2 Dynamics of the Gap

Tasks, observation capacities, policies and environments can change. An institution may neglect a previously useful channel, or discover a substitute. It may learn, rebuild, or adjust its actions to avoid a disturbance. Hence a gap may grow, shrink, remain constant or change kind.

For illustration only, the earlier smooth effective-gap ansatz can be written $\dot g=\alpha(t)-\beta(t)A(t)$, with $\alpha$ an effective emergence rate and $\beta A$ an effective adaptation rate. It is not an equation for exact integer rank and it is not derived from the static bound. Appendix B states its assumptions and conditional crossing calculation.

#### 3.3 Performance Thresholds, Not a Universal Dissolution Threshold

A rank deficit does not determine SNR, mutual information, stability, or the probability of institutional failure. Adding independent unobserved coordinates can raise an ambient dimension count without affecting an existing signal. Even an SNR of one leaves positive information in a Gaussian channel. The earlier numerical proposal of a critical gap around 2–3 is withdrawn; the representation-chain model in Paper III is a different setting and cannot calibrate such a threshold by analogy.

A task-specific threshold may still be useful, for example a specified unacceptable probability of service failure under a particular disturbance distribution. It requires independent calibration of the performance criterion and uncertainty. It is not a universal number of missing dimensions.

A further hypothesis is that unmodeled causes can appear as unexplained shocks, inviting responses to symptoms rather than to causal mechanisms. This possibility is related to boundary drift (Paper XVIII) and stock confounding (SO-WP0), but requires a specified causal model and tests against competing explanations.

#### 3.4 Reform, Recovery and Dissolution

Severe inadequacies can be consequential and persistent, particularly where loss of competence or feedback impedes correction. But no result here establishes dissolution as a structural necessity. Reform, redundancy, substitution, external support, changes in demands and even adequate fixed buffers can change the outcome. The design question is whether a system can recognize and correct the particular inadequacy in time, at acceptable cost and under legitimate authority. Dissolution or transfer of functions is one possible response, not a consequence of the rank bound.
