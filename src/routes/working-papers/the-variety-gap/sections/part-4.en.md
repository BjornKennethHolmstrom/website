### Part IV — Neglected Dimensions: Truth, Meaning, Connection and Wellbeing

#### 4.1 Reframing Through the Gap

The earlier argument identified truth, meaning, belonging, wellbeing and relational integrity as important aspects of collective life. That inquiry survives, but these are not interchangeable with observation channels. A social condition can be a valued outcome, a causal contributor to other outcomes, a signal of an underlying condition, or all three. Which role it plays depends on the task, model and measurement process. Slow changes can escape short-horizon decisions even when their measurements are available.

#### 4.2 Candidate Dimensions in Detail

**Truth and signal fidelity.** Incentives to embellish reports or suppress independent evidence can distort feedback. Distinguish deliberate distortion, measurement dependence, noisy transmission, and neglect of available evidence. A report that pleases its recipients is not necessarily accurate. Independent verification, source diversity and protected dissent are possible safeguards; they need not require a larger objective vector.

**Meaning, connection and belonging.** Their intrinsic importance is a normative question; their relationship to cooperation, participation and long-term orientation is an empirical question. Surveys, qualitative testimony and longitudinal studies may help illuminate these relationships. Neither low economic output nor a short questionnaire uniquely measures meaning. Participation and nonparticipation may reflect many competing causes.

**Wellbeing and ecological integrity.** Economic production is not equivalent to welfare. Health, distribution, leisure, ecological conditions and security may be valued in their own right or matter instrumentally for future production (Stiglitz, Sen and Fitoussi, 2009; Raworth, 2017). The distinction matters: a long-horizon wealth-only decision rule might protect a productive ecosystem without attaching intrinsic value to it, while a plural evaluation could still underinvest in sensing. Appendix C establishes neither the necessity of intrinsic valuation nor optimality of either controller.

**Relational integrity.** Trust, reciprocity and local knowledge can facilitate collective action, but their effects and measurement depend on context (Ostrom, 1990). Institutional trust can also conceal failures or reflect unequal power. Independent evidence about delivery, participation and who bears costs is needed to discriminate explanations.

#### 4.3 The Architectural Claim Restated

The revised framework asks whether relevant consequences are represented in evaluation, information is available and credible, causal relations are modeled, decisions use that information and interventions can be implemented. Adding a metric is one possible intervention; improving incentives, information access, models or response capacity may be more effective. No universal ordering of these remedies follows from the mathematics. The choice of what to value and whose welfare and rights count remains a normative and political question. These candidate dimensions warrant investigation rather than a claim of demonstrated necessity.
