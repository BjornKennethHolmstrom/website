### Part VII — Conclusion: From Diagnosis to Research and Design

#### 7.1 The Argument in Brief

Bounded institutions can lack consequential distinctions or effective responses. Objectives, observation, causal models, decision rules and actuation are not identical. Priorities can influence sensing or exposure, but whether this generates persistent harm depends on the task, dynamics and available alternatives. A single dimension count cannot establish all these relationships.

#### 7.2 Summary of Contributions

- **[R, within model]** A necessary static rank bound and an exact observational adequacy condition with unrestricted additive correction (Appendix A). These follow from elementary linear algebra, not a new general theorem of institutional collapse.
- **[R within the model]** A documented rerun (one fixed-seed, non-preregistered run) of the recovered Appendix C script: both fixed rules cross the environmental threshold; the environment-aware rule partially recovers. The comparison is bundled and wealth rankings depend on the scoring horizon.
- **[H]** Policy-dependent exposure, neglected consequences and correction capacity are useful research questions; their empirical prevalence is not established here.
- **[IP]** Interpretations of country reports and proposals for revisable, deliberative and multiscale governance. No measured country rankings or universal threshold are claimed.

The prior objective–observation identity, general Goodhart–Ashby synthesis and numerical dissolution threshold are withdrawn. The original edition and supplied legacy script are retained as historical material.

#### 7.3 Open Questions and the Research Frontier

Which tasks require distinctions that current observations cannot provide? Are alternative signals available? Does policy suppress exposure, distort the likelihood, or merely neglect existing evidence? Do causal models and action constraints explain failures better than value choice? Can low-cost reserves, audits or feedback improve decisions without changing the objective? These questions invite explicit competing explanations and bounded tests.

Transition feasibility is a separate research question: institutions that would benefit from reform may resist adopting it, or lack the means to implement it. Papers VII, IX and XXIII motivate examining incumbent incentives, feasible transition paths and directed adaptation costs rather than assuming that diagnosis makes reform achievable.

### 7.3.1 The Measurement Gap

The formal static result specifies a mathematical quantity under particular assumptions. Objective counts, output PCA, independent observation rank, entropy and information value are not interchangeable estimators. Appendix G outlines a construct-specific measurement approach. Application requires independently defined tasks, predictors and outcomes, uncertainty and validation on cases not used to construct the diagnosis. A country-level composite index is not validated by this paper.

#### 7.4 The Civilizational Bet

An institution that remains open to criticism and capable of revision may be better placed to handle novelty. This is a defensible design aspiration, not a prediction that all narrow objectives lead to dissolution. Respect for plural values and legitimate choice matters even when it does not increase measured efficiency.

### 7.5 Testable Predictions

Appendix H restates the original research agenda as conditional hypotheses. The critical-gap prediction is withdrawn in its universal form; the legacy simulator calibration prediction is withdrawn pending a separately justified model. The representation-chain hypothesis belongs to Paper III. The remaining hypotheses require explicit measures, comparison cases, confound controls and adequate precision. Failure to reject a null hypothesis is not, on its own, falsification.

#### 7.6 Invitation

The invitation is to test, correct and refine this framework, not to treat it as a comprehensive theory of social value. Independent replication, negative cases, source-based country analyses and human specialist review of the formal claims remain welcome. A framework concerned with institutional perception should itself remain open to correction.
