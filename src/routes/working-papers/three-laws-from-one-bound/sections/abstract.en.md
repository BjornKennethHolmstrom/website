## Abstract

The Governance as Engineering series uses Ashby's law of requisite variety, Goodhart's law, and the certification cost introduced in Paper XVII. This paper examines their relationship through the premise used in Paper 0: a finite controller partitions the task-relevant world into a bounded set of internal states and assigns a response to each state.

*Ashby* is treated through a deterministic counting bound: if the controller has fewer internal states than the minimum number of actions needed to cover all task conditions, it cannot respond adequately in every condition. Capacity alone is insufficient; the controller must distinguish the right things and revise its distinctions when task demands shift. *Goodhart* is treated through the ordering of proxy and target on a specified reachable intervention set. A strict increase in the proxy is guaranteed not to decrease the target exactly when the set contains no strict order reversal. This is the established reward-unhackability criterion, not a new theorem. A discarded target-relevant dimension may be reachable without generating a reversal; an explicit counterexample establishes this. In the registered budget-allocation demonstration across thirty worlds, a specific resource trade-off makes degradation from a target-optimal starting point increase with the permitted reduction of the omitted dimension. *Certification cost* is treated as a cumulative accounting quantity under the assumptions of §5, not a conservation law for representational complexity.

The contribution is a scope-bounded synthesis: holding a representation constrains adequate action, optimizing a proxy requires scrutiny of feasible trade-offs, and maintaining a representation entails resource accounting. These are related questions, not three consequences of finitude alone. The reported demonstration and negative conservation-law result are retained; the earlier claim of a novel general Goodhart theorem is withdrawn.
