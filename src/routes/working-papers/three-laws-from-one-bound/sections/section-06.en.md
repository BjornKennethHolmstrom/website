## 6. How the three faces relate

The three arguments can now be read together. They share the setting of bounded representation, but they do not constitute a single theorem. Each asks a different question about the relationship between a factorization, its task and its environment.

Ashby is the *static* face: a finite controller must distinguish enough of the right conditions to act adequately. The counting bound is necessary under §3's assumptions. Goodhart is the *optimization* face: when a controller improves a proxy, safety against strict target degradation depends on the proxy–target ordering over the specified feasible set. A reachable omitted dimension alone does not determine that ordering. The budget trade-off in §4.3 illustrates how the ordering can fail. Certification cost is the *dynamic* face: when a controller checks and revises its factorization, the cumulative cost is an accounting quantity under §5's assumptions.

The unity is methodological. The static face asks which distinctions are required for adequate action; the optimization face asks which feasible changes are compatible with the target; the dynamic face asks what must be checked and maintained when conditions shift. Each question requires its own premises: the task's response requirements, the target and proxy functions and feasible interventions, or the costs and accounting rules. Neither the ordering criterion nor the cost accounting follows from a finite state count alone. The paper's contribution is to place these established and conditional results in a common framework, without claiming a new general theorem.
