## 7. What this re-grounds, and what it does not show

### 7.1 Re-grounding

The arguments organize three constructions in the series. Paper XVII's certification floor motivates the per-event terms in §5's accounting; Paper XVI's source terms motivate examining whether evidence of discrepancies remains available when the task changes; Paper XIX's role triad proposes mechanisms for detection and adjustment. The counting bound does not require that particular triad, and Paper XXIII motivates caution about interpreting graph connectivity as functional translation. These are links between research questions, not proofs of the necessity of the proposed institutional designs.

### 7.2 What this paper does not show

**The paper's value is consolidation.** Ashby (§3) is close to definitional within the stated controller class. The ordering criterion (§4.2) is established prior art and the certification argument (§5) is conditional accounting. The earlier general theorem claiming that loss plus reachability implies degradation is withdrawn: the explicit counterexample shows why a feasible adverse ordering is an additional requirement. Even a strict reversal does not guarantee that a particular optimizer realizes it. The synthesis in §6 is not a derivation of all three results from finitude alone.

**The stronger conservation conjecture did not survive the exploration.** Section 5.3 distinguishes a proposed heuristic lower-bound accounting argument from a fixed complexity budget. It does not establish a universal law about the quantity of representational complexity.

**The Goodhart demonstration is minimal and analytic in part.** The $r=0$ endpoint and monotonic target loss along the stipulated budget path follow analytically. The thirty registered worlds describe the magnitude within the chosen parameter ranges. They do not test the universal sufficiency or necessity of omitted-dimension reachability, nor establish safety from arbitrary starting states. The original reported results are unchanged.

**The premise is imported at Paper 0's tier.** The finite-state deterministic controller is the specified class for §3, with **[R within the model]** for Paper 0's demonstration and **[IP]** for the institutional reading. The proxy criterion and accounting argument also apply in settings not described by this controller class, subject to their own assumptions. No claim is made that leaving the class escapes all three phenomena.

**The institutional reading is [IP] throughout.** Applying the controller argument, proxy ordering and certification accounting to ministries, agencies or metric regimes requires a justified mapping from the real case to the corresponding assumptions. The mathematical statements do not by themselves establish that the analogy holds.
