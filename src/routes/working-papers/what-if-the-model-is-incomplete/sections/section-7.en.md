## 7. Unanswered questions and limits

### 7.1 Limits

- **Synthetic generator.** The confirmatory worlds are independent draws from one investigator-defined generator, so inference is conditional on it. The $t$-interval at $n=40$ is an approximation.
- **Hypothetical weighting.** The equal weighting of retention present and absent is a hypothetical prior. Other weights would change the estimand.
- **One confirmed mechanism.** Only retention was tested confirmatorily, from one baseline, at exact parameters and no rain shift.
- **Strategy set.** REP is an assumption, not discovery. MIX is a heuristic. The robust ambiguity-averse planner from A1, which WP0 proposed comparing (Iyengar, 2005; Nilim & El Ghaoui, 2005), was not evaluated here.
- **Learning studies.** These used selected development worlds, noise-free full-state observation, a known two-hypothesis problem and one-time commitment.
- **Criteria.** U2 is a simple distributive variant. Wetland deficit and stock are model quantities, not field indicators.

### 7.2 Hypotheses for future work

The hypotheses are numbered after WP0's H1–H4.

**[H5 — Reversal is predictable.]** The sign of the re-model versus re-value contrast in a world will be predicted by the ratio of the ecological objective's price to the re-modelled plan's misspecification cost. Worlds like t18 should be those where the price is small. This requires new worlds and a new declaration, not a post hoc search of the 40.

**[H6 — Cheaper insurance.]** Two alternatives may keep most of the ecological objective's insensitivity to omitted retention at a lower U1 price:

- an objective that values wetland conditionally on its downstream relevance;
- a correct belief-state plan over the two hypotheses.

The MIX result motivates this, but does not establish it.

**[H7 — Parameter error favours robustness.]** As planning-parameter error grows, the advantage of re-modelling over re-valuing shrinks. A robust ambiguity-averse planner may then dominate both on U1. This should be tested with the A1 robust planner included from the start.

**[H8 — Exposure-aware planning.]** Planners that account for the observability their own policy creates will avoid self-maintained traps where the omitted mechanism is decision-relevant. Their net value will depend on probe cost and remaining horizon.

**[H9 — Observation channels beat longer probes.]** In line with WP0's H4 (integrated learning), adding an observation channel that responds to retention directly, such as a downstream flow measurement, will raise realised learning value more than lengthening probes.

### 7.3 Relation to the programme and to Governance as Engineering

The Systemic Optimization series asks how an optimization problem should be *constructed* and *evaluated*, not only solved. *Governance as Engineering* (GaE) concerns the institutional architecture through which such problems are formulated: who observes, who decides, what is valued, and how feedback returns.

**[IP]** This paper adds one architectural observation. What an institution can learn about the world depends on what it does, and what it does depends on what it values and what it believes. Monitoring arrangements, objectives and model assumptions are therefore not separable design choices.

A planning body that never acts on a mechanism may never be in a position to observe it. An objective adopted for one reason may, as a side effect, create the conditions for learning something else.

These are interpretive bridges. No result here validates a governance arrangement, and no recommendation for a real catchment follows directly.
