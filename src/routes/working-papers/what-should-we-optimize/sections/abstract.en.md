## Abstract

Optimizing a coupled system presupposes answers to different questions: what outcomes matter, how actions change the world, which future goals may arise, and what it costs to learn. A planner may optimize exactly against an inadequate representation. This paper synthesizes three synthetic computational experiments in *Systemic Meta-Optimization*. **A1** [confirmatory] investigates model errors in coupled flood management. A wetland-augmented objective does not establish a general hedge against unknown-direction parameter error. Its *difference-in-differences* advantage under an incomplete rather than full model equals 34.3% of the narrow model's regret; the narrow model jointly omits downstream retention effects, peak synchronisation, and rainfall correlation. Ambiguity aversion [confirmatory] recovers 83.1% of the direct planner's regret under a wetter evaluation shift **with exact planning parameters**; the benefit weakens substantially with parameter misspecification and reverses under a drier shift. **B** [confirmatory] keeps dynamics known but makes future goals uncertain. Reachability and diversity correlate with incremental option value (both approximately 0.73), yet at the frozen possibility weight no measure passes the joint test against premium-matched reserves. A uniform prior over an exactly correct 30-goal support recovers 69% of the value of the true goal distribution [exploratory]. **B5** [confirmatory] finds that support knowledge recovers an estimated 59.1% of full-information value (inconclusive against a 50% threshold), while period-displacing polling has negligible value. When surveys accompany ordinary actions [exploratory], they recover 20.9% of the full-information gap when free and 10.5% at a marginal cost of 0.075 reward units. A1's exploratory fixed-programme map finds no tested pair both mutually beneficial across nine strict accounts and synergistic. These findings neither establish a universal optimization theory nor validate institutional interventions in the field. They motivate a narrower programme: diagnose the *type of mismatch*, distinguish measures of possible futures from decision objectives, and model information-acquisition opportunity costs.

**Keywords:** systemic optimization; model misspecification; robust decision-making; option value; uncertain preferences; value of information; adaptive institutions; multiobjective interactions; computational preregistration.
