## 6. What the three experiments jointly say—and do not say

### 6.1 No universal remedy follows from a legitimate concern

One can advocate ecological consideration, robustness, diversity and learning for excellent reasons. The experiments test a different proposition: does translating that concern into a particular objective term, penalty, or action improve a specified evaluation criterion under a specified uncertainty?

A1 answers **sometimes and conditionally**. An ecological augmentation can partly substitute for a bundled omission of three hydrological features, but it can be costly when the modeled risk is overestimated. Ambiguity aversion helps under the adverse evaluation shift explored in A1, even though that shift lies outside its nominal ambiguity ball, but costs under the opposite shift. A generic safety-looking reserve floor can lose badly by delaying necessary protection.

B answers **not necessarily**, even when a measure is statistically informative. Counting accessible states or diversity captures some properties of incremental option value; optimizing those quantities can still be inferior to an informed belief about future objectives or a reserve policy with comparable current-goal sacrifice.

B5 answers **not at any cost**. The information source is useful, yet the originally specified learning action almost never passes the opportunity-cost test. Allowing concurrent learning changes the answer, without changing the laws of Bayesian inference or the set of possible goals.

These are results of different models. One should not place the A1 figure of 83%, B's 69%, and B5's 21% on a common league table: each has a different reference regret, opportunity set, and experimental task.

### 6.2 Three separations the evidence requires

**Separation A: causal model and objective.** An augmented reward may compensate for a missing causal pathway, but it remains distinct from explicitly representing that pathway. Treating the two as interchangeable would conceal the directional dependence of A1's ecological objective. A model improvement and an incentive change can generate similar behavior for different reasons; their response under a new shift may differ.

**Separation B: option measurement and option valuation.** In B, HB3's ranking validity does not imply HB1's policy value. Reachable options must be interpreted through possible future goals, their probabilities, their conditional payoffs, and the costs of preserving them. This is a reason to measure structural capability and decision value separately.

**Separation C: information and acquisition architecture.** In B5, an observable signal and a practical learning channel are not equivalent. A theoretically informative survey does not guarantee beneficial learning if it consumes time, resources or institutional attention before a decision can be revised.

### 6.3 A provisional diagnostic framework [IP]

The following is a **proposal for future operationalization**, not a validated four-factor theory:

| Question | Diagnostic object | Failure example | Candidate response to test |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| What matters? | Evaluation accounts and explicit trade-offs | Mutually beneficial totals obscure losses in a habitat account | Multi-account reporting; legitimate preference elicitation |
| How does the world work? | Causal pathways and uncertain transitions | Omitted upstream retention in A1 | Improve representation; compare targeted augmentation and robust planning |
| What might matter later? | Goal support and distribution | B's possibility counts are weak objectives | Represent plausible future goals; value retention conditionally |
| Can we learn in time? | Signal quality, delay, costs, policy updates | B5's exclusive poll interrupts productive activity | Compare integrated feedback, protected inquiry, and adaptive timing |

The table is not a checklist that guarantees success. It is a way to prevent selecting remedies on the basis of slogans. It asks the analyst to identify the exact information loss, model error or incentive mismatch before choosing a formal intervention.

### 6.4 Negative results that should survive publication

A credible synthesis must retain four inconvenient observations.

First, **the worst-off auxiliary term F1-eq barely moves A1 outcomes** in most relevant cells. Including an equity-sounding term does not establish equity improvements; here, its induced policy changes were small under the selected normalization and environment.

Second, **B's possibility measures did not pass the primary joint test**. Strong correlations of state ranks with future option value coexist with poor action incentives and failure against reserve-matched controls.

Third, **B5's first polling channel was effectively unused**. The amended channels are informative exploratory contrasts but cannot retroactively rescue H5-2.

Fourth, **A1's preferred adaptability metric sometimes rewards unfinished work**. Any subsequent paper that uses value of future corrective action as “capacity” must account for accumulated liabilities. A large capability-to-recover number may indicate a fragile, underprepared state.
