## Abstract

Can an optimizing system preserve alternatives that are not presently rewarded? This paper revisits a historical four-discipline elicitation and several finite-population simulations. The elicitation suggests analogies among excitation, genetic variance, entry threat and strategy or posterior weights, but does not establish formal isomorphism or universal monotone depletion. Source-term locality is retained as a diagnostic question about who can influence replenishment, depletion and protection, not as a scalar order parameter or a collapse theorem.

The strongest revised result concerns the switching-barrier model. For innovation strictly between zero and one, barrier in [0, 1) and finite population of at least two agents, its partition chain is irreducible and aperiodic. With linear copying it admits an exact reversible product-form stationary law. At the sampled cell \(N=120\), \(B=0.99\), \(r=0.35\), this law has substantial mass in two widely separated regions, and a bottleneck bounds the relaxation time below by more than 18,388 sweep equivalents. The previously claimed irreversible fold is withdrawn. First innovation from consensus, mixing, and reaching a diverse population are different events.
