## 1. The object that keeps moving

The investigation started from a concern that improvement might consume the alternatives by which it could later be challenged. A selected practice can displace rivals; a controller can avoid informative histories; an incumbent can alter entry conditions. Yet these are different mechanisms. A system can also create or retain alternatives, and a narrow objective does not by itself imply an observation deficit (corrected Paper VI).

The difficulty is partly conceptual. The alternatives may be existing actions, candidate models, unobserved states, or possibilities not represented in the current model. Their existence, weight, accessibility and value are not the same. Paper XXIII distinguishes descriptive distance from adaptation cost; Paper XIX distinguishes graph centrality from a tested connective function. This paper examines replenishment and loss without assuming that a single scalar captures all of them.

The original strong thesis is now a research question [H]: under which update rules, constraints and time horizons does selection make alternative configurations harder to reach, and which interventions preserve useful alternatives at acceptable cost?
