## 2. Method: adversarial narrowing as qualitative evidence

### 2.1 The historical decay curve [HR]

The original account reports five successive formulations: universal coupling of improvement and contestability; coupling within a fixed hypothesis space; pricing the generator rather than an unconceived outcome; externally protectable alternatives with possible endogenous protectors; and source-term locality. The narrowing was prompted by objections from language-model interlocutors. It is a useful example of revision, not a controlled demonstration that observer decorrelation causes graceful convergence to truth. No full round log, raw prompts or returns were available for this correction.

### 2.2 The four-lens elicitation [HR]

The historical method asked questions in four native vocabularies, forbade shared cross-domain terms, withheld the proposed answer and requested a five-field output including a minimal formal statement and unresolved disagreement. Different model families were assigned within lenses; the integrator was excluded from lensing. The stated criterion distinguished identity from resemblance. Appendix A records the supplied summary rather than claiming a full prompt transcript.

Role separation and different providers do not establish statistical independence. Training overlap and integration choices can influence agreement. The described chronology and criterion are historical reports, not authenticated preregistration. The returned summaries are therefore treated as hypotheses and analogies. A formal equivalence would require an explicit map preserving the relevant relations, not merely matching words such as source or decay.
