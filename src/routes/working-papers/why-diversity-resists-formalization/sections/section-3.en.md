## 3. Four distinct formal objects

### 3.1 Control theory — persistent excitation

The lens concerned richness of the joint regressor, represented by positive definiteness of a window information matrix. Tracking can restrict information under particular policies. However, the original iff condition on the external reference is false. Constant input with a transient can identify unknown parameters, and information may grow without uniform persistent excitation. The controller can generate informative actions subject to costs and constraints. See corrected XIV, Appendix A.2. The object is regressor information, not a stock of unused options.

### 3.2 Evolutionary biology — standing genetic variance

Directional selection and drift can deplete standing additive variance under specified conditions; mutation, recombination and fluctuating selection can change it. A schematic balance \(\Delta V_A\approx V_m-aV_A\) requires assumptions about the trait, genetic architecture, environment and rates. Mutation rate can itself be subject to selection, but this does not prove selection-stability of a particular diversity protection. Neither monotone depletion nor inevitable collapse follows in general.

### 3.3 Institutional economics — threat of entry

The original lens used a relation \(\partial T/\partial B<0\) between entry threat and barriers in a specified market. This derivative is distinct from the incumbent's optimization over \(B\). A cost or legal ceiling can constrain barrier choices without changing the sign of the relation. Entry may also depend on technology and resources. The model must say which variables the incumbent controls, the costs and benefits of altering them, and how constraints are enforced. No universal reduction to a source of alternatives follows.

### 3.4 Decision theory — strategy and posterior weights

Action probabilities, posterior probabilities and support sets are distinct. A deterministic policy may choose a single action while retaining uncertainty about hypotheses. A posterior can increase the weight of an alternative as evidence arrives. The value of information concerns improved decisions under a model; it is not literally a flow of posterior mass. Nonstationarity, ties, learning incentives and a revised hypothesis space can all matter. The original elicitation's competing responses about an internal hedge are not proof of a bistability or unresolved theorem in decision theory.
