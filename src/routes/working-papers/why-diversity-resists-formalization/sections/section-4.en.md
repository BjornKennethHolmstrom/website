## 4. Replenishment, depletion and control authority

The four lenses have related questions but different mathematical types: a matrix information condition, a variance evolution equation, a derivative sign and a statement about action or belief weights. Their isomorphism has not been established. A decay-plus-source model is a candidate abstraction only when the relevant quantity and dynamics are actually specified.

### 4.1 Locality is not a collapse criterion [M/IP]

Even for a simple external positive source \(s>0\) and loss rate \(a>0\),

\[
\dot Q=s-aQ,\qquad Q(t)=s/a+(Q(0)-s/a)e^{-at}.
\]

The equilibrium \(s/a>0\) refutes the implication that exogenous replenishment guarantees collapse. A protected external source might instead sustain the quantity. Conversely, endogenous replenishment is not automatically safe or sufficient.

| Question | What must be specified |
|---|---|
| Replenishment | How options or evidence are created and retained |
| Depletion | Which updates reduce availability, weight or access |
| Control authority | Which parameters and policies can be changed by whom |
| Protection | Costs, constraints, enforcement and ability to repeal |

Source-term locality is the last two questions applied to a particular source, not a universal order parameter. Different constraints may act on loss rather than adding new alternatives. The original two-tier classification of robust versus contingent collapse is withdrawn.

### 4.2 Unawareness and model revision [H]

A hypothesis assigned zero prior mass remains at zero under ordinary Bayesian conditioning when the conditioning is defined. A possibility absent from the hypothesis space is not the same as a represented zero-mass hypothesis. Expanding the space, revising priors or acquiring a new observation model changes the problem. The decision-theory seam remains worth investigating; no universal impossibility of preserving or discovering alternatives follows.
